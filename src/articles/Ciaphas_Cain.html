Ciaphas Cain was a [[commissar]] of the [[Officio Prefectus]] who served with the [[Astra Militarum]] for most of the last century of the [[41st Millennium]]. He is remembered as one of the most celebrated heroes of the [[Imperium of Mankind]], although his own accounts describe a man more concerned with his survival than with glory. He was recalled to service during the [[13th Black Crusade]] of [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] and lived for more than a quarter of a century into the [[42nd Millennium]].

Cain was unusual among [[commissar]]s. He avoided the savage discipline for which the Commissariat was known and would not readily sacrifice his soldiers unless it served his own survival. Imperial propaganda nonetheless credited him with many victories. Cain tried to avoid combat, but his reputation as a hero repeatedly placed him in the most dangerous situations of his career. Records describe him as a skilled dissembler whose candour about his own failings was matched by an unusual modesty about his victories.

Early life and training

Cain's record places his birth on an unnamed [[Hive world]]. He claimed that his parents died at the hands of [[Kroot]] while serving in the [[Astra Militarum]], which was the reason he was sent to be trained as a commissar. Inquisitorial scholars doubt this account. No Hive world in the nearby [[sector]]s is known to have raised mixed-gender regiments in that period, and orphans from a petty criminal family were not normally admitted to a [[Schola Progenium]]. Cain's marks at the Schola were average, and his clean disciplinary record probably reflects skill at avoiding detection rather than good conduct.

Service history

Cain began his service in 919.M41 as a commissar attached to the Valhallan 12th Field Artillery, and he soon took responsibility for the whole regiment. His first battle was on [[Desolatia]], where the regiment held against [[Ork]]s and then against a [[Tyranid]] splinter [[hive fleet]]. During that fighting Cain attempted to desert in a stolen [[Salamander (Armoured Vehicle)|Salamander]] and ran into a [[Hive Tyrant]]. His panicked report was later counted among his heroic deeds.

In 924.M41 his escape pod crashed on [[Perlia]] behind Ork lines, and he was presumed dead. Cain raised an army from freed slaves and survivors of the planetary defence forces. He fought his way to the Imperial front and killed the Ork [[Warlord]] in single combat. The victory was recorded as the March of the Liberator.

In 931.M41 Cain founded the 597th Valhallan Regiment from the remains of the 296th and 301st Valhallan regiments, one male and one female. He ordered that troopers from both be mixed in every squad. The same year he took part in the [[Gravalax]] Incident, where he first met [[Inquisitor]] [[Amberley Vail]]. Cain later described this as the affair in which his aide was found to be a [[Psychic Blank]].

In 942.M41 the 597th was sent to suppress an Ork outbreak on [[Nusquam Fundumentibus]]. Cain found a [[Tyranid]] swarm that had lain dormant in the permafrost for millennia. Imperial accounts describe this as a turning point in the defence of the world. Cain's later service included missions against the [[Necron]]s on [[Interitus Prime]], the [[Craftworld Aeldari]], and the reclamation of [[Archipelaga]].

Later career and death

Cain was kept on active duty throughout the [[Era Indomitus]] and is said to have died of old age. He was buried with full honours as a Hero of the Imperium. Several reports of his death had reached the [[Administratum]] before, most notably after his crash on Perlia, and the [[Departmento Munitorum]] issued standing orders that he should be kept on the active roster regardless of any report. Imperial accounts state that the Imperium does not make mistakes, so Cain remains listed as on duty.

Cain spent his final years as an instructor of new commissars at the [[Schola Progenium]].

The Cain Archive

Cain's private notes were organised by Inquisitor Amberley Vail and sequestered by the [[Inquisition]] in the early [[42nd Millennium]]. Vail edited them with information from other sources, including the memoirs of [[Jenit Sulla]], and the result is known as the Cain Archive. Cain wrote the notes only to organise his own thoughts and expected them to be destroyed after his death. Vail's footnotes note that the archive is more candid than Cain's official published memoir, which was written for public consumption.
