The Grand Annulus is a huge ring of stone bounded by two concentric circles, set in the floor at the centre of the Hall of the Great Wolf at the summit of [[The Fang]], the fortress-monastery of the [[Space Wolves]] on [[Fenris]]. It is a map of the Chapter's order. Each outer segment is a separate slab carved with the sigil and runes of one [[Wolf Lord]].

At feasts in the hall the Wolf Lords and their [[Wolf Guard]] sit in the part of the floor marked by their own name-stone. The central stone, carrying the Wolf That Stalks Between Stars, marks the place of the [[Great Wolf]]. It is turned to show which Wolf Lord holds that rank.

Construction and custom

Each slab is as wide as a battle tank, and it takes a dozen of the strongest warriors to lift one. Great care is taken in carving and setting them, for the Space Wolves say that to shatter a name-stone is to doom its Great Company to a dishonourable end.

When a Wolf Lord dies his stone is taken from the hall to the Grove of Heroes. There the stones lie in widening rings around the oldest stone of all, that of the primarch Leman Russ, as a record of the Chapter's leaders across the ages.

The name-stones

The Wolf Lords carry totemic sigils drawn from Fenrisian myth, and each is carved on the Annulus. The twelve companies recorded in the Chapter's current order, with their sigils, are:

The Great Wolf's own company is fanatically loyal to its leader. The sigil of the Red Moon belongs to a Wolf Lord who favours his [[Long Fangs]], and the Bloodied Hunter to a lord who is looked on with suspicion by the other eleven, because more Wulfen serve in his company than in any other.

The 13th stone

A blank obsidian stone is set into the ring. It was once the name-stone of Jorin Bloodhowl's Great Company, the Wulfenkind, who hounded [[Magnus the Red]] into the [[Warp]] at the time of the [[Horus Heresy]].The [[Burning of Prospero]] is dated 004.M31 in the Imperial record. The Codex does not date the Wulfenkind's part in it separately. It now stands for every Great Company that has been destroyed in battle, lost on campaign or has broken its oath. Few outsiders have seen it. To say that a Great Company "walks to the 13th stone" is a grave accusation.
