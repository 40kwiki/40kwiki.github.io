The Ordo Hereticus, known across the Imperium as the Witch Hunters, is one of the three [[Ordos Majoris]] of the [[Inquisition]]. Its charge is to protect [[Mankind]] from itself. Its agents combat the internal threats to the [[Imperium of Mankind]]: treason, [[Mutant|mutation]], [[Heresy|heresy]], apostasy among the clergy of the [[Ecclesiarchy]], and the [[Unsanctioned Psyker|unsanctioned psykers]] who are called "[[Witch|witches]]" in the common speech of most worlds. Mutants, rebels and [[Heretic|heretics]] fall within its remit as well, and it has never hesitated to act against the other Adepta. A [[Renegade]] cardinal of the [[Adeptus Ministorum]] or a corrupt [[Tech-priest]] is as likely to meet a Witch Hunter as any cultist.

The Ordo is the youngest of the three Ordos. The Ordo Malleus and the Ordo Xenos trace their origin to the order that the [[Emperor of Mankind]] gave to [[Malcador the Sigillite]] at the opening of the [[Horus Heresy]] in 005.M31. The Ordo Hereticus was set up only after the [[Age of Apostasy]] in the 36th millennium, when the anarchy of the [[Reign of Blood]] and the heresies that grew from it, above all the [[Plague of Unbelief]], had shaken the Imperium's government as nothing had since the Heresy. Its founding purpose was to check the power of the Ecclesiarchy, so that another prelate like [[Goge Vandire]] could never again hold the machinery of state.

Witch Hunters are counted the darkest and most unforgiving of the Inquisition, because their prey is Humanity. Like all [[Inquisitor|Inquisitors]] they hold ultimate authority over every organisation of the [[Adeptus Terra]] and may demand help from any source of authority in the Imperium. Their Chamber Militant is the [[Adepta Sororitas]], the Sisters of Battle, and they may also call on the Ecclesiarchy's [[Frateris Militia]] or requisition regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]]. The arrival of one of them on a world is met with fear and awe, since only the Inquisitor knows on whom the gaze will fall.

History

Origins

Main article: [[Age of Apostasy]]

The Ordo Hereticus exists because of the [[Reign of Blood]]. In the 36th millennium the High Lord [[Goge Vandire]], Master of the [[Administratum]], seized the office of [[Ecclesiarch]] of the Adeptus Ministorum as well, and so held the two greatest bureaucracies of the Imperium at once. He thereby threatened the rule of the Emperor and of the [[Senatorum Imperialis]], which governs in the Emperor's name. His tyranny ended around 378.M36, when the reformer [[Sebastian Thor]] led an army against [[Terra]] and Vandire was killed by his own guard.The Imperial record of the Apostasy gives no single year for each event. The date of Vandire's death is the estimate used in the article on the [[High Lords of Terra]].

The Inquisition had to answer how a single churchman had come so close to supplanting the Throne. It had always watched the organs of state, the Ecclesiarchy among them, but the Ecclesiarchy now held an extraordinary place in the daily life of the Imperium's people. Nothing moves the men and women of the Imperium like faith in the Emperor, and the Inquisition feared a repetition by other corrupt men, or the perversion of honest faith into a new [[Chaos Cult]] that could drive a planetary rebellion. The Ordo Hereticus was shaped as the permanent watchman over that danger.

From myth to open shadow

For its first standard millennia the Ordo was a rumour even to other Inquisitors, so fanatically was its secrecy guarded. After the Apostasy the leaders of the Holy Ordos judged that it would serve better as a shadow that frightened than as a myth too easily ignored, and it became openly feared. It grew with the Ecclesiarchy it was meant to watch, for nothing arouses an Inquisitor's suspicion faster than a holy man who claims to speak with the Emperor's voice.

The Sisters of Battle

Under the reforms that followed the Apostasy the ecclesiarch [[Sebastian Thor]] issued the [[Decree Passive]], which forbade the Ecclesiarchy to keep "men under arms". Later ecclesiarchs found a loophole in the wording and refounded the Daughters of the Emperor as warrior-nuns, merged into the Adepta Sororitas. Soon afterwards the reformed Sisterhood and the Ordo Hereticus made an agreement of mutual aid at the [[Convocation of Nephilim]]. Its terms are still shrouded in mystery, and some Inquisitors suspect that only the canonesses of the Sororitas and a few [[Inquisitor Lord|Inquisitor Lords]] understand what the two bodies are working toward.

Remit

Watching the Ecclesiarchy

The Ordo's original task remains its first. Its Inquisitors monitor the [[Wars of Faith]] declared by the Ecclesiarchy, to see that they stay within the aims set by the Ecclesiarch and the [[High Lords of Terra]]. They test that the doctrine preached by the priests of the [[Imperial Cult]] remains true to the orthodoxy of the Ministorum, and they check the wealth and the stellar territory claimed by bishops and cardinals, so that the princes of the church never again gather temporal power on the scale of the Reign of Blood. Many Witch Hunters pose as servants of the Ecclesiarchy, feigning obedience to a cardinal until they have proof of his guilt. It is said that a rare priest has no fault that an Inquisitor of the Ordo cannot find.

The Ordo's gaze also falls on the Adeptus Arbites, the Space Marines, the Sisters of Battle and its fellow Inquisitors. Only the Emperor is beyond its suspicion. Simply to oppose a Witch Hunter is to risk being declared a heretic and condemned as Extremis Diabolus.

Heresies of faith and thought

Main article: [[Heretic]]

In the Ordo's working definition, heresy is to adhere to, promulgate or act on a theological or political doctrine contrary to the orthodoxy of the Ministorum or to the [[Lex Imperialis]], which is itself held to be of divine origin. In practice the crime covers everything from the denial of the Emperor's divinity and rebellion against the [[Adeptus Terra]] to blasphemy and irreligious practice. Imperial confessors use the word more loosely still, for any sin of thought that the Imperium's moral guardians dislike, and the zeal with which a given world treats heresy varies enormously, since the Imperium holds countless cultures and doctrines that do not fit well together.

Corruption of the body and the witch

The Ordo regards the mutant and the [[Psyker|psyker]] as threats to the nature of Humanity itself. The mutant, corrupted in flesh, is held to be a possible sign of the taint of [[Chaos]], and to be a mutant under the Lex Imperialis is to be removed from Humanity. Many so-called mutants are really [[Abhuman|abhumans]], deformed by generations of industrial pollution or alien biospheres and blameless of any Warp taint, yet they are still often the victims of zealots. More measured factions of the Ordo do draw the distinction, preferring not to waste effort on a few chem-dregs when real corruption hides behind a wealthy facade. Others of a [[Puritan]] mind would destroy all of them, to be safe. That course meets stiff resistance on worlds whose economies depend on an abhuman underclass, and on the whole the pragmatist's policy of control and repression prevails, until Warp taint or rebellion appears and the Holy Ordos loose the Emperor's hounds, and whole worlds are heaped with pyres.

The witch is held to be more dangerous than any other traitor or heretic. An unsanctioned psyker is a door by which the Warp may reach the unsuspecting, and every untrained one is a possible planetary catastrophe, capable by accident or design of starting fires, spreading nightmares, enslaving others as living puppets or killing by a stray thought. Hunting them is the central task of many Witch Hunters, and the [[Black Ships]] carry the captured to Terra. The Ordo has no objection to psykers who have been properly sanctioned, and a surprising number of its Inquisitors are psykers themselves.

Sins of the past

Rarer crimes also concern the Ordo: the veneration of false gods and the pursuit of forbidden lore that recall the sins of Humanity's ancient past. Some are so proscribed that to know of them is cause for summary execution. The worship of "progress" and the pursuit of technologies banned since the Emperor walked among his people are treated with the contempt reserved for mutant cults. In these matters the Ordo has clashed with the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] and with the [[Ordo Xenos]], which dispute both its involvement and its methods.

Methods and organisation

Witch Hunters

Witch Hunters are by repute figures of strong will. Some wear [[Psychic Hood|psychic hoods]] against hostile witchery. Others trust in litanies of protection and hatred. A Hereticus Inquisitor may specialise in the identification of mutants and command cells of [[Acolyte|Acolytes]] that range from hive-hardened flamer squads, trained to purge whole infected districts, to assassination cells that remove and replace political figures who have succumbed to the Dark Gods. Such an Inquisitor is usually less concerned with the mutants than with the cause of an outbreak, which may herald a rise in Chaos or xenos cult activity. An investigation may thus end with the removal of a head of state, the cleansing of a polluted region, or a war.

When an individual poses a severe threat, such as a psyker of unprecedented power, a mutant with terrible abilities or a [[Planetary Governor|planetary governor]] unwilling to carry out a necessary pogrom, the Ordo excommunicates the offender from the human species and hunts him down, calling on the Sisters of Battle, other Adepta or another Ordo. It will send the [[Astra Militarum]] against the Imperium's own people if needed, holding it better to end a thousand innocent lives than to let corruption reach millions. The Ordo thinks in sectors and star systems, not in planets or individuals, and its masters regard the billions of the Imperium as a flock in which every soul may be a psyker, a mutant or a rebel.

The art of investigation

Because the heretic, the witch and the traitor lurk unseen among the masses, the Ordo's work is detection before it is destruction. Its records sort the clues an investigator follows into three kinds. Physical clues are the remnants of dark rituals, markings and pendants of allegiance, and blasphemous works of craft, from weapons that use xenos technology to paintings laced with fine heretical iconography. Mutation is also a physical sign, though cultists sometimes mark their allegiance on their own flesh with scars, brands or [[Electoo|electoos]], which can be a trap for an Acolyte infiltrating a cult.

Informational clues are less reliable. They include heresies of thought, found by covert monitoring or, for those who employ psykers, by telepathy; heresy of tolerance, the failure to act on knowledge of a heresy; and the accusation of a neighbour, which the Adeptus Terra and many governments encourage by reminding citizens to report heresy. Correlational clues are patterns that link the others: a series of murders that proves to be the work of a cult of the [[Khorne|Blood God]], dark patterns such as a lingering smell or nightmares at regular intervals, ciphers and secret signs such as a feather of a certain colour, and heresy by association. The strictest Witch Hunters hold that friends and family of a heretic should simply be purged with him, since innocence proves nothing.

Chamber Militant

Main article: [[Adepta Sororitas]]

The Ordo's Chamber Militant is the Adepta Sororitas, also called the Sisters of Battle or the Daughters of the Emperor. The Orders Militant form the military arm of the Ecclesiarchy and answer when an Inquisitor of the Ordo needs a more forceful resolution. The two bodies differ in temper. Hereticus Inquisitors tend to be analytical, methodical and suspicious, while the Sisters are zealous and unquestioning of the Imperial Creed, yet both are committed to rooting out threats from within. Through the Sisterhood the Ordo has a standing armed force that is, in theory, owned by the very church it watches.

Notable members

The Ordo's most famous Inquisitors are known by repute more than by record, and many of its cases are sealed. [[Fyodor Karamazov]], an Inquisitor Lord, gained his name on Salem Procor when he executed a popular servant of the Ministorum. The Ecclesiarchy and the Thorian Inquisitors cried out, but the preacher proved to be a true heretic, and Karamazov's reputation was redeemed, though his relations with the church and his peers never mended. He rides into battle on the war-machine called the [[Throne of Judgement]].

In the [[Calixis Sector]] the Witch Finder Rykehuss descends on a city, sets up a Court of Ordeals and puts the accused to the flame, holding that fear itself suppresses witches. Some call him a hero and others a butcher, and both are right. The same sector holds Vownus Kaede, a swordsman and psyker of Puritan leanings who spirits promising young psykers away from the pogroms of Rykehuss, and Astrid Skane, a former Arbitrator of Scintilla, who hunts corruption among the nobility and the Adepta. Both belong to the Tyrantine Cabal, which watches the [[Tyrant Star]].

Others are known from the Imperium's wider wars. Inquisitor Ligea, who studied texts and ancient writing and served beside the [[Grey Knights]] on the trail of [[Ghargatuloth]], fell to the taint of the daemon prince she hunted and was captured by her own Inquisition. In her ravings she let slip the true name of Ghargatuloth, which gave the Grey Knights the means to destroy it once in every thousand years. Inquisitor Adrastia was sent to the Aurelia sub-sector in the late 41st millennium with Lord General Castor of the [[Cadian Shock Troops]] to investigate charges against [[Azariah Kyras]], Chapter Master of the [[Blood Ravens]], while an [[Exterminatus]] fleet approached. Inquisitor Agmar, working with the Adeptus Arbites on Ichar IV, helped to break a [[Genestealer]] cult and then, through his astropaths, to detect the approach of [[Hive Fleet Kraken]].

Not every Witch Hunter is a zealot. Hettesh Kane, a sanctioned psyker and former teacher of the [[Scholastica Psykana]], came to believe that psykers of moral conviction could achieve great things, and trained those he caught instead of killing them. Victoria Aldrich, trained by the cunning Inquisitor Yusuph Trevar, tempers an Acolyte's verve with her master's planning. Havelock Blackheel of Maccabeus Quintus has waged a one-man war on psykers for a standard century in the [[Koronus Expanse]].
