A Chaos cult is an often secret organisation that worships [[Chaos]] or one of the Chaos Gods. Imperial scholars regard cults as the most dangerous of the organisations that plot against the rule of the Imperium from within. Cults can be found on almost any Imperial world, and they recruit from every class, from hive gangers and soldiers to nobles and officials.

Cults aim to survive and to take control of the society around them. Their members usually hide behind legitimate bodies such as trade unions, charities, guilds and political parties, and they gather power until they can seize a township or a whole world. Imperial records describe cults that rose in open revolt and cults that worked with a Traitor Legion's invasion, so that the rebels and the invaders struck together.

The Imperium has never been able to stamp out cults entirely. The [[Ordo Hereticus]] and the [[Inquisition]] treat them as the chief enemy within, and their agents have hunted cults since the [[Horus Heresy]]. [[Imperial Truth|The Imperial Truth]] denies that the Ruinous Powers are real, and so many Imperial officials have been slow to recognise a cult until it has grown large. Cults are also suppressed as a matter of policy, and Imperial records on them are often sealed.

History

Warp cults before the Heresy

[[Warp]] cults were known during the [[Great Crusade]], but most were small and found only on worlds near [[Warp storm|Warp storms]] or with high rates of [[Psyker|psyker]] birth. The Great Crusade's commanders usually ordered such worlds destroyed by [[Exterminatus]] rather than fight the cults on the ground. Many Imperial records treat this order as the Emperor's own, though later accounts show that some Legions quietly ignored it.

The [[Word Bearers]] changed this. Before the Heresy, the Legion seeded hidden Warp cults on the worlds it had brought to Compliance, while outwardly those worlds seemed orderly and loyal. Imperial records call these worlds the "Holy Worlds", a name used by the cults themselves.Imperial sources differ on how many worlds the Word Bearers seeded. The count of "Holy Worlds" comes from a single sealed summary.

Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

The Heresy, which ran from 005.M31 to 014.M31, turned cults into an army. At the [[Battle of Calth]] in 007.M31, the Word Bearers committed cult forces for the first time in large numbers, and the cultists fought alongside the [[Traitor Legions]] in the attack on the [[Ultramarines]]. The cults were cheaper and easier to raise than regular troops, and they obeyed any order, however cruel. Imperial accounts record that the Traitor Legions relied on cultists to hold ground after the Legions had moved on.

The rising spread after the betrayal at [[Isstvan III]] in 013.M31. Across the Imperium, workers destroyed their machines, rebels slaughtered their officers, and scholars revived old idolatries. The Imperial Truth held that these were the failures of Imperial Compliance. Later Inquisitorial reports blame both the Word Bearers and the regimes that trusted them.

Post-Heresy suppression

After the Heresy, the High Lords of Terra and the Inquisition suppressed all knowledge of what the Warp cults believed. The cults' terms, such as the "Primordial Annihilator" and the "Eightfold Path", were classed as anathema. The Ordo Hereticus was given the task of hunting cults in the centuries that followed, and the Ordo developed the methods used against them ever since.The date of the Ordo Hereticus' formation is not recorded. The Ordo is placed in the early Imperial period from the order of its early purges.

The Hollow Cult and the Grey Knights

In 290.M34, the [[Grey Knights]] fought a [[Long War|long war]] against the Slaaneshi Hollow Cult. The cult ambushed their Brotherhoods by bending reality so that each Battle-Brother was cut off and overwhelmed. The Grey Knights lost almost the whole of the 7th Brotherhood. Their retribution was total, and the cult was recorded in a sealed chronicle that survives only in faded ink.

Era Indomitus

Further information: [[Era Indomitus]]

In 855.M41 the Cult of the Red Talon rose on Antraxes, serving a [[Daemon|daemon]] called M'Kachen, the Lord of Change. Brother-Captain Arvann Stern of the Grey Knights led the counterattack and drove the daemon back into the Warp. Early in the [[41st millennium]] the Cult of the Angel of Fire took power in the Krask System. Its sect leaders believed they spoke with a messenger of the Emperor, and they overthrew the [[Planetary Governor|Planetary Governors]]. The cult was later encountered by the forces of [[Lord Solar]] [[Macharius]] during the [[Macharian Crusade]].

During the [[Gothic War]] (139–160.M41), the troopers of the 969th Cadian Regiment were quarantined on Certus Tertia after contracting a plague from its water. They refused to return to [[Cadia]], turned to [[Nurgle]], and became the Unbound, a cult that still lives in the [[Eye of Terror]]. Their former colonel styles himself the "False Castellan".

The [[Thirteenth Black Crusade|13th Black Crusade]] of [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] in 999.M41 drew the largest body of cultists since the Heresy. Cultists of every kind followed the [[Warmaster]]'s banner, and the term "The Lost and the Damned" came to mean all human and [[Mutant|mutant]] forces of [[Chaos]] that were not [[Chaos Space Marines]].

Organisation

Recruitment

Cults form where the Imperium's rule is most harsh. [[Hive world|Hive worlds]] are the most fertile ground, since their enormous populations live in poverty, and many of their underhive dwellers never learn the Imperial Truth. Feral and [[Death world|Death worlds]], where native superstition is strong, also produce cults. On the Imperial worlds that have been Compliant for longest, cults often grow from the families of officials who keep old beliefs alive.

New members are brought in by demagogues, who promise a better life or a place in a new order. Recruits are marked with sigils that are said to hurt the eyes of the faithful. Members who gain the favour of their gods are granted [[Mutant|mutations]], which may take the form of living steel muscles or a tongue of sucker-tentacles.

Types of cult

Imperial scholars divide cults into several types. Blood cults sacrifice victims to the gods. Cults of change serve [[Tzeentch]] and seek forbidden knowledge. Plague cults serve [[Nurgle]] and spread disease. Pleasure cults serve [[Slaanesh]] and seek sensation. Death cults follow the dead, and mystery cults and secret societies hide their goals behind legitimate fronts. Many cults combine several of these forms.

Notable cults

Brotherhood of the Knife

The Brotherhood of the Knife was the largest and most effective of the Warp cults the [[Word Bearers]] brought to [[Battle of Calth|Calth]]. It was mustered under the guise of second-line [[Imperialis Auxilia|Excertus Imperialis]] Levy Auxilia, so that its numbers were hidden from loyal commanders.

Cult of the Red Talon

The Cult of the Red Talon arose on Antraxes in 855.M41, serving the daemonic [[Daemon|Lord of Change]] M'Kachen. It was destroyed by the [[Grey Knights]] under Brother-Captain Stern.

Cult of the Angel of Fire

The Cult of the Angel of Fire ruled several worlds of the Krask System in the early [[41st millennium]]. It executed those who spoke against it by burning them alive, and its theocratic regimes held the systems until [[Macharius]]'s forces arrived.

The Unbound

The Unbound was a cult devoted to [[Nurgle]] in the [[Eye of Terror]]. It was formed by the Cadian 969th Regiment, which turned its allegiance to the Lord of Decay after a plague from Certus Tertia.

The Lost and the Damned

The Lost and the Damned is the general name for the cultists that follow [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] in the [[Black Crusade|Black Crusades]] of [[Chaos|Chaos Undivided]]. In later usage, it also means any human or [[Mutant|mutant]] force of Chaos that is not a [[Chaos Space Marines|Chaos Space Marine]] warband.
