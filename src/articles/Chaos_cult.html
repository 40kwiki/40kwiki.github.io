A Chaos cult is an often secret organisation that worships [[Chaos]] or one of the Chaos Gods. Imperial scholars regard cults as the most dangerous of the organisations that plot against the rule of the Imperium from within. Cults can be found on almost any Imperial world, and they recruit from every class, from hive gangers and soldiers to nobles and officials.

Cults aim to survive and to take control of the society around them. Their members usually hide behind legitimate bodies such as trade unions, charities, guilds and political parties, and they gather power until they can seize a township or a whole world. Imperial records describe cults that rose in open revolt and cults that worked with a Traitor Legion's invasion, so that the rebels and the invaders struck together.

The Imperium has never been able to stamp out cults entirely. The [[Ordo Hereticus]] and the [[Inquisition]] treat them as the chief enemy within, and the duty of hunting heresy has been theirs since the days of the Age of Apostasy. The business of the Ordo Hereticus is largely the discovery and destruction of heretical cults, and the Inquisition uses the word "cult" for every kind of sect, secret society, party, guild and conspiracy in the Imperium, not only for those that serve Chaos.

History

Warp cults before the Heresy

[[Warp]] cults were met, though rarely, during the [[Great Crusade]]. They were usually found on worlds near [[Warp storm|Warp storms]] or with a high rate of [[Psyker|psyker]] birth, and were often tainted by xenos lore or led by a rogue psyker with messianic pretensions. From the beginning of the Great Crusade, any world showing substantial Warp breaches was destroyed from orbit by [[Exterminatus]] without a confrontation. This was a direct order of the Emperor and was upheld almost without exception until the [[Word Bearers]] began to subvert it.

The [[Word Bearers]] changed this. In the five solar decades before the Heresy, the Legion seeded hidden Warp cults on the worlds it had brought to Compliance, while outwardly those worlds seemed orderly and civilised. These worlds were called the "sons of Lorgar" and, by many of the cults themselves, the "Holy Worlds". Pilgrims from all over the Imperium travelled to join them, until countless billions rose together and hailed the Warmaster [[Horus]] as their lord.

Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

The Heresy, which ran from 005.M31 to 014.M31, turned cults into an army. At the [[Battle of Calth]] in 007.M31 the Word Bearers used Warp cults in large military actions for the first time. The cultists engaged the mortal defenders of the world, the native defence units, the Army regiments and the Solar Auxilia, while the Traitor Astartes dealt with the [[Ultramarines]]. The cults were easier to raise and more disposable than regular troops, and they would obey any order, however cruel. As the war went on, the Traitor Legions used them as occupation troops, hunter-killer squads and overseers.

After the betrayal at [[Drop Site Massacre|Isstvan V]], across the Imperium, previously law-abiding peoples rose against those in authority, workers smashed the machines they had tended, and educated men and women resurrected the idolatries of their ancestors. Imperial scholars suspect either gross incompetence among the [[Iterator|Iterators]] whose task was to replace such beliefs with the Imperial Truth, or a conspiracy on a monumental scale.

Post-Heresy suppression

At the time of Calth few of the Imperium's rulers knew what the Warp cults believed. Investigation showed terms that appeared again and again, among them the "Primordial Annihilator", a title taken to mean the Warp itself, and the "Octed" and the "Eightfold Path". Later, the High Lords of Terra and the Inquisition suppressed all knowledge of these beliefs and declared it anathema.

The Hollow Cult and the Grey Knights

See also: [[Grey Knights]]

In 290.M34, the [[Grey Knights]] fought a [[Long War|long war]] against the Slaaneshi Hollow Cult. The cult ambushed their Brotherhoods by bending reality so that each Battle-Brother was cut off and overwhelmed. The Grey Knights lost almost the whole of the 7th Brotherhood. Their retribution was total, and the cult was recorded in a sealed chronicle that survives only in faded ink.

Later cults

In 855.M41 the Cult of the Red Talon rose on Antraxes, serving a [[Daemon|daemon]] called M'Kachen, the Lord of Change. Brother-Captain Arvann Stern of the Grey Knights led the counterattack and drove the daemon back into the Warp. Early in the [[41st millennium]] the Cult of the Angel of Fire took power in the Krask System, where its sect leaders overthrew the [[Planetary Governor|Planetary Governors]]. It was met by the forces of [[Lord Solar]] [[Macharius]] during the [[Macharian Crusade]].

During the [[Gothic War]] (139–160.M41), the troopers of the 969th Cadian Regiment were quarantined on Certus Tertia after contracting a plague from its water. They refused to return to [[Cadia]], turned to [[Nurgle]], and became the Unbound, a cult that still lives in the [[Eye of Terror]]. Their former colonel, Abner Varicuss, styles himself the "False Castellan".

The hordes of cultists that follow [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] in the [[List of Black Crusades|Black Crusades]] are called the Lost and the Damned, a term also used for all the human and [[Mutant|mutant]] forces of [[Chaos]] that are not [[Chaos Space Marines]].

Organisation

Recruitment

Cults form where the Imperium's rule is most harsh. Rebellion festers beneath almost every civilised world. [[Hive world|Hive worlds]] are especially fertile ground, since many of their underhive dwellers live in lawless resentment and never learn the Imperial Truth. On [[Feral world|feral worlds]], native superstition is never far from the surface, and the blood-soaked sacrificial cults of such worlds are as typical as the philosophical secret societies of advanced ones. Cultists may be soldiers, hive gangers, nobles, deserters, persecuted abhumans or mutants.

New members are brought in by demagogues, who promise a better life or a place in a new order. Beneath their clothes, cultists are tattooed and branded with sigils that hurt the eyes of any faithful who look on them. Members who gain the favour of their gods are granted [[Mutant|mutations]], which may take the form of living steel muscles or a tongue of sucker-tentacles.

Types of cult

Imperial scholars divide cults by their purpose and structure. Blood cults serve [[Khorne]] and offer blood and skulls in exchange for power and vengeance. Cults of change serve [[Tzeentch]] and seek mortals in positions of power. Plague cults serve [[Nurgle]]. Pleasure cults serve [[Slaanesh]] and are marked by excess and obsession. Apocalypts believe that mankind is doomed and work for the collapse of civilisation. Death cults treat every death as an act of worship, often of the Emperor himself, and are among the most dangerous heretical cults the Inquisition meets. Mystery cults are built around a secret held by an inner circle. By structure, cellular cults are divided into small cells that know nothing of one another, and charismatic cults rest on the authority of a single leader.

Notable cults

Brotherhood of the Knife

The Brotherhood of the Knife was the largest and most effective of the Warp cults the [[Word Bearers]] brought to [[Battle of Calth|Calth]]. It was mustered under the guise of second-line [[Imperialis Auxilia|Excertus Imperialis]] Levy Auxilia, so that its numbers were hidden from loyal commanders.

Cult of the Red Talon

The Cult of the Red Talon arose on Antraxes in 855.M41, serving the daemonic [[Daemon|Lord of Change]] M'Kachen. It was destroyed by the [[Grey Knights]] under Brother-Captain Stern.

Cult of the Angel of Fire

The Cult of the Angel of Fire flourished on the worlds of the Krask System in the early [[41st millennium]]. Its sect leaders believed they were in contact with the Emperor's messenger, who was in truth a Greater Daemon of [[Tzeentch]]. They overthrew the Planetary Governors and burned those who spoke against them. In the [[Macharian Crusade]] the forces of [[Macharius]] met fierce resistance, the cult opened a Warp rift that let the [[Lord of Change]] through, and the daemon was defeated and the rift closed. The cult then fell apart.

The Unbound

The Unbound was a cult devoted to [[Nurgle]] in the [[Eye of Terror]]. It was formed by the Cadian 969th Regiment, which turned its allegiance to the Lord of Decay after a plague from Certus Tertia.

The Lost and the Damned

The Lost and the Damned is the general name for the cultists that follow [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] in the [[List of Black Crusades|Black Crusades]] of [[Chaos|Chaos Undivided]]. In later usage, it also means any human or [[Mutant|mutant]] force of Chaos that is not a [[Chaos Space Marines|Chaos Space Marine]] warband.
