Nurglings, also called the Mites of Nurgle, Gleeful Castoffs, Pus Spores, Tiny Plagues and Little Lords, and Khan'gurani'i in the Dark Tongue of [[Chaos]], are small and mischievous [[daemon]]s of [[Nurgle]]. They are among the most numerous of his servants and among the most favoured. Each one is a miniature copy of the Plague God, which pleases him.

A nurgling stands little over a Terran foot high and is no real danger alone, since a grown man can kick one aside. The danger is in the swarm and in the claws and teeth. Wounds from them fester and often will not heal.

Origin

Nurglings grow in the guts of a [[Great Unclean One]], the greater daemon of Nurgle, and burst from the boils and pustules in its hide. They start as small clots of foul matter. They feed on the juices of the greater daemon's organs, grow into copies of Nurgle, and drop out as plump, spiteful imps.

Most remain with the greater daemon that bore them. They climb across its body, seek warm pools under its folds of flesh and chatter to it without end, hoping for a fatherly belch of approval. Some pretend that their parent is Nurgle himself. Great Unclean Ones sometimes swallow a nurgling to see where on their great bulk the creature will come out.

Nature and behaviour

Nurglings are jovial and playful, like their master. They hop about the larger daemons and giggle as they play, and they bite, claw and tear whatever interests them. Mortal servants of Nurgle learn never to leave anything valuable in a nurgling's keeping, because it will come back ruined and filthy.

The [[Plaguebearer]]s, the daemonic footsoldiers of Nurgle, find them a nuisance. A plaguebearer who is nearly done counting the drips of pus needed to fill a pool may see a swarm run through it, splashing, and the count must start again. Nurglings do not seem to understand why this matters, and plaguebearers do not complain within earshot of a Great Unclean One.

Gifts to champions

A Champion of Nurgle who has earned the god's favour may be given nurglings as a sign of it. They infest his wounds, orifices and armour, and fight to protect him. [[Death Guard]] warriors often find them an annoyance, because they steal blight grenades, climb armoured limbs to lick discharge and mimic shouted orders in squeaky voices. The Death Guard still treat them as a mark of the god's regard, since Nurgle does not hand them to any mortal.

When the Death Guard go to war, nurglings appear from nowhere. They slip through the plague ships, drop out of ventilation grates and play in the cesspools of the bilges. On the battlefield they come out of ammunition crates and cling to the hulls of advancing tanks. They are drawn above all to the vectoriums of the 4th Plague Company, especially those ruled by sorcerers. Warbands such as the Filth-favoured, the Prophets of the Seven and the Bilious Ones go into battle on a living carpet of them. The Malignant Plaguecaster Dulthrox Globbergor, the "Father of Mites", is carried to war on a mountain of nurglings.

In battle

Nurglings are seldom a formal part of the daemonic Plague Legions. They follow behind the commanding Great Unclean One and run among the Heralds and troop formations. They are protective, and throw themselves on anyone who threatens their friends. Their usual attack is to overrun one victim and pull him down under the weight of many bodies. They hide in tank wrecks, sewers and collapsed ruins, then erupt from cover as the enemy passes and bury it in biting, scratching little bodies.

The wounds are small, but the contagions from which the nurglings are made infect and rot even a scratch. The soldier's scream is muffled by the mass of them, and his comrades' panicked fire does little.

Palanquin of Nurgle

Nurglings are sometimes given to a Daemon Herald or champion as a living litter, to carry him in a Palanquin of Nurgle. This is a daemonic steed in the form of an ornate, mobile throne. The nurglings treat the new master as they treated the greater daemon that bore them, whether he wants it or not. The palanquin is decorated with mouldered finery and wrapped in a cloud of flies. The flies fly into the eyes and ears of anyone fighting its rider, and each one carries a fragment of protective magic. The palanquin and its rider cannot be harmed by sorcery or psychic attack.

Behind the palanquin the bearers leave pools of sticky filth. Anyone who steps in one may contract the plague called Nurgle's Rot.

Since the Great Rift

After the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41, nurglings have become far more common in realspace. Some new diseases, including late strains of the [[Zombie Plague]] such as the Walking Pox, are so virulent that nurglings form inside the sick. They eat their way out or burst from a cyst, and spread yet more disease as they go.
