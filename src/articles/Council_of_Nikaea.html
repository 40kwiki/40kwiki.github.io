The Council of Nikaea, also the Edicts of Nikaea or, in the archives of the Blood Ravens, the Hearing of the Thousands, was a conclave called by the [[Emperor of Mankind]] on the world of Nikaea in 001.M31, in the first year after the [[Ullanor Crusade]]. It met to decide whether the psychic gift, which the [[Great Crusade]] met on almost every world it reached, was a resource or a mortal danger. Its judgment, the Decree Absolute, sanctioned the Navigators and astropaths under strict Imperial control and forbade sorcery, defined as any bargain with the intelligences of the Warp. It disbanded the Librarius of every Space Marine Legion, and it created the office of Chaplain.

The council is remembered chiefly as the trial of [[Magnus the Red]], primarch of the [[Thousand Sons]], and as the moment when the Emperor's anxiety about the Warp became visible to his sons. Magnus obeyed the decree and did not mean to keep it. His Legion's continued studies on [[Prospero]] led to the destruction of that world in the opening year of the [[Horus Heresy]]. The ban on Librarians stood until the Heresy showed the Imperium that it could not face the powers of the Warp without psykers of its own, and it was lifted in the age of the [[Codex Astartes]].

Background

Psykers in the Great Crusade

The Emperor was the most powerful psyker Mankind has produced, and he distrusted the growth of the gift in the people he was gathering. In the [[Age of Strife]] the Warp had been the great enemy of human settlement. Worlds had been lost to possession, to cults and to mutant psyker dynasties, and the sight of a child who could move objects had meant a killing. The Great Crusade found those worlds again. Expeditionary fleets landed on planets held by cabals of sorcerers, or by communities that revered their psykers as priests, and the fleet's Remembrancers recorded such scenes in dispiriting numbers.

The Emperor held that the greater part of humanity was not yet evolved enough, in body or in spirit, to use the power without being used by it. The [[Imperial Truth]] held that the supernatural was only the natural not yet understood, and the Iterators who preached it found the psychic gift a difficult subject. Practice ran ahead of doctrine. Under the [[Treaty of Olympus]] the Mechanicum had been promised new astropath choirs and six Navigator houses, and astropaths already carried the messages of the crusade. A sorcerer was a different thing, and the line between them was not always clear to the officers who had to draw it.

The Librarius

Psychic mutation appeared in the Legions themselves. Some Legions, notably the Thousand Sons, the [[Space Wolves]], the [[White Scars]], the [[Blood Angels]] and the [[Salamanders]], found it common among their recruits. Rather than lose such warriors, the primarchs, led by Magnus, proposed a training programme that would add a combat psyker corps to the ordinary Legion structure. The Emperor sanctioned the first experiments as a way of containing spontaneous outbreaks. The psykers were called Librarians, because they soon became the keepers of their Legion's records and lore, and they proved loyal and useful warriors. By the end of the first decade of the 30th millennium several Legions kept a Librarius, and each primarch used it according to his temperament.

The quarrel of the primarchs

The Librarius divided the primarchs. [[Leman Russ]] thought Librarians no better than witches and held that any contest settled by trickery rather than by blood was dishonourable. [[Mortarion]], who had been raised among the sorcerers of Barbarus and had seen what Warp-bargains did to a world, accused Magnus openly. [[Corvus Corax]] and [[Rogal Dorn]] refused to let their Legions fight alongside those that used Librarians. Others, among them [[Sanguinius]], [[Jaghatai Khan]] and [[Vulkan]], thought the gift could be used in the open and disciplined. The Space Wolves and the Thousand Sons had come to blows in the field, and the quarrel threatened the cohesion of the fleets.

The Emperor, then preparing to leave the crusade after Ullanor and return to Terra to begin the work of the [[Webway]] beneath the [[Imperial Palace]], decided to settle the matter before he left. He summoned the primarchs, the Master of the Mechanicum and the officers of the Council of Terra to Nikaea.

The council

Assembly

Nikaea was chosen for the survival of an ancient amphitheatre that seated tens of thousands. The Emperor sat above the dais as arbiter, and [[Malcador the Sigillite]], his Regent, acted as convener and keeper of the order of speech. [[Constantin Valdor]] and a delegation of the [[Adeptus Custodes]] stood watch. The Legions sent their primarchs and chief officers, including the Chief Librarians of several Legions, and the Mechanicum sent the Fabricator-General, [[Kelbor-Hal]], who spoke strongly against psykers. The Imperialis Armada, the Auxilia and the Ordo Astra Telepathica sent their ranking officers, and the Occulex-Magister of the astropaths affirmed a neutrality that satisfied nobody.

Along one wall were ranged cantilevered boxes screened by a technology of reflected light, which the Acta call the falsehoods. Their occupants could be seen only as shadows and creases of light. The Emperor ordered the boxes so that no witness's testimony would be bent by the fear of those watching, and the Thousand Sons' Chief Librarian [[Ahzek Ahriman]] later guessed that several of the hidden figures were primarchs who did not wish to be seen taking a side.

The case against

The case against the gift was opened by the Sisters of Silence, the blank-souled women of the Emperor's own court, who recited long records of human suffering caused by sorcerers: mutant breeding lines, daemon-ridden lords, cults that fed on their own congregations, and the psychic plagues of the Age of Strife. The primarchs most hostile to psykers followed them. Russ spoke for the Space Wolves, and his Rune Priest Ohthere Wyrdmake, who had made himself a friend of Ahriman in order to understand the Thousand Sons, now spoke against them. Mortarion gave the plainest testimony, since he knew from experience what the Warp could do to a world and its people.

Magnus

Magnus answered. The Acta describe the effect of his voice on the hall as one of fear and admiration in equal measure. He held that no knowledge was tainted of itself and no pursuit of it wrong, so long as the seeker mastered what he learned instead of becoming its servant. His Thousand Sons, he said, had done so. There was nothing the Legion could not learn and turn to the service of Mankind, and he asked the Emperor to support the study of the Warp rather than ban it. The speech moved many present, and the debate became more evenly divided than the Sigillite had expected.

The third way

The compromise was proposed by a body of Librarians from several Legions, who came before the dais together and stood in a half circle to show that they spoke as one voice. Their Epistolary argued that a psyker was like an athlete, a gifted person whose talent should be trained and put to use, and that the gift was no more evil in itself than a sword. Sorcery was something different, a knowledge that had to be sought and bargained for with beings whose price was never certain. They proposed that the Imperium educate all its psykers to serve Mankind and that sorcery be forbidden as the worst of offences against the Imperial Truth. The Stormseer Targutai Yesugei of the White Scars made the same proposal in the name of his Legion.

The Emperor rejected it. The accounts agree that the compromise, which was in the end the policy the Imperium would adopt after the Heresy, was set aside because of what the council had revealed about Magnus. The council had become, in effect, his trial.

The Decree Absolute

Judgment on Magnus

Evidence was brought that Magnus had gone on practising sorcery long after the Emperor had told him to stop. The Emperor had trusted him alone with certain secrets of the Warp and the existence of the powers later named the Chaos Gods, on condition that he leave such studies alone, and now it seemed that the primarch had gone further. The exchange between father and son is preserved in the Grimoire Hereticus, an Inquisitorial compilation, and the testimony agrees that the Emperor's anger was great and was held in check only with difficulty.

The ruling

The Decree Absolute was delivered in a speech that the Imperial Cult later called the Edicts of Nikaea. In it the Emperor said that power is a living thing and that its danger is obsession, that the attempt to learn from the Warp is an attempt to learn from a place of shifting shapes and lies, and that false knowledge is more dangerous than ignorance. He had walked paths that no man could know and understood the secrets that lay hidden there, and those secrets were not for lesser minds. He now saw that he had allowed his sons to delve into matters he should never have let them know existed. No one present would be censured, for the council served unity. But he decreed that no Legion should keep a Librarius, that every Astartes with a psychic gift be returned to the ordinary battle companies and never use it again, and that any who defied him should earn his enmity and bring ruin on his followers.

The rulings of the council as the Imperium has since recorded them were:

  • Navigators and astropaths, trained, controlled and licensed by the Imperium, were permitted, because the Imperium could not hold together without them.
  • Sorcery, the bargaining with the Warp's intelligences, was forbidden to all.
  • The Librarius of every Legion was closed, and the combat psykers of the wider armed forces were to be discharged from that service.
  • Every Legion was to create a new office, the Chaplain, modelled on the practice of the [[Word Bearers]], to keep its warriors faithful to the Imperial Truth and to the Emperor's commands.

The Chaplains

The institution of the Chaplain is the least discussed provision of the council and one of the most lasting. The Word Bearers had long kept a priesthood of their own under their primarch [[Lorgar]], and their chaplains were the model. The Emperor wanted a corps of officers charged with the morale and fidelity of the Legion, and he intended them to preach the Truth rather than a creed. The irony, which the later Ecclesiarchy has noted, is that the Imperial Cult would be served in later millennia by an order whose first model came from the Legion that fell to Chaos first.

Aftermath

The Legions

The Librarius of every Legion was closed. In the loyal Legions it was done without much difficulty, though the Salamanders and the White Scars kept their psychic warriors under other names. In the Thousand Sons the effect was severe: the Legion's culture was built around its Librarians and its libraries, and a large part of its strength was psychic. Magnus accepted the judgment, and in public the Legion obeyed.

Prospero

In practice Magnus did not stop. He moved his studies deeper into the Legion's homeworld of Prospero, closed its libraries to outsiders, and told his captains that the Emperor had not understood the work. The mistake was nearly fatal to the Imperium. When he saw in a vision that Horus was moving against the Throne and sent a warning across the Warp, the working broke the Emperor's wards in the Webway, and the Emperor responded by sending the Space Wolves to bring Magnus to Terra. Prospero burned, and the survivors turned to the powers they had been forbidden to touch. The reasons for the fall of the Thousand Sons are discussed in the article on [[Magnus the Red]].

Legacy

The Edicts of Nikaea were the law on psykers for ten thousand years in everything but name. The prohibition on Librarians was reversed in the Great Scouring, when the loyal Legions found that the Traitors fought with sorcerers and daemons and could not be matched by an army that denied itself the gift. The compromise that the Librarians offered at Nikaea became the standing policy: trained and disciplined psykers in the service of the Imperium, with the practice of sorcery and daemonology remaining the gravest of heresies. Each Chapter of the [[Space Marine Chapter|Adeptus Astartes]] now decides for itself whether to keep a Librarius, and some, such as the Salamanders, regard the old strictures as obsolete, while others keep a deep distrust of psykers.

The Chaplain remained. The office survived the Second Founding and appears in the Codex Astartes as the keeper of a Chapter's faith. In the 42nd millennium a Chaplain is a priest of the Emperor in all but title, a fact that would have appalled the Emperor of the Imperial Truth.
