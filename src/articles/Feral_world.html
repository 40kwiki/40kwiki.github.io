A feral world is a class of planet of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] whose human population lives in a primitive, usually tribal condition, without industry and often without writing. Feral worlders may be hunters, herders, raiders or nomads. Many such worlds were settled in the [[Dark Age of Technology]] and fell back into savagery during the [[Age of Strife]], keeping only legends of their past.

The [[Administratum]] usually leaves feral worlds in their primitive state. Their tithe is light or nothing, and the Imperium's interest in them is chiefly military: feral worlders are fierce and hardy, and are recruited by the [[Astra Militarum]] and above all by the [[Adeptus Astartes]]. Several of the most famous Space Marine Chapters, including the [[Space Wolves]] of [[Fenris]] and the [[White Scars]] of [[Chogoris]], recruit from feral or near-feral peoples.

Characteristics

Feral worlds vary greatly. Some are temperate and fertile, others are also [[death world]]s, and the [[Administratum]] frequently records both classes for one planet. What they have in common is a population without industrial technology, organised in tribes, clans or warbands under chiefs, shamans or warlords. Feral worlders often worship gods of their own, and Imperial preachers of the [[Imperial Cult]] are sometimes sent to bring them to the faith, though many [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] forbid interference in the customs of their recruiting worlds.

A feral world differs from a [[feudal world]] in that it has no settled kingdoms, castles or agriculture on any great scale. The line between the two is not always clear: [[Chogoris]], for example, has been recorded as feral, as feudal and as a Legion homeworld at different times.

Relations with the Imperium

On most feral worlds the Imperium is represented only by a small outpost of the [[Planetary Governor|planetary governor]], who may rule in name alone. The governor's task is to keep the world loyal, to protect it from [[Xenos|xenos]] raiders and heresy, and to supply recruits when the tithe falls due. Off-worlders are usually forbidden to trade advanced technology with feral peoples.

[[Space Marine Chapter|Space Marine Chapters]] prize feral worlds as recruiting grounds because their warriors are used to danger and hardship from birth. Some Chapters live openly among the tribes and are revered as gods or spirits; others watch from orbit and take aspirants only after trials. The [[Codex Astartes]] recommends a feral or [[Death world|death world]] as the ideal Chapter homeworld.

The [[Astra Militarum]] raises feral regiments for assault and irregular warfare, though their soldiers often have to be taught to use basic Imperial weapons. Some feral regiments are kept together for generations and develop traditions of their own.

History

Most feral worlds were once more advanced. Imperial surveys have found the ruins of Dark Age cities on many of them, and the tribes often keep garbled memories of their ancestors. In the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31) many were brought to compliance with little bloodshed, since their peoples could not resist the [[Imperial Armed Forces|Imperial armies]]; some were found to have been raised by a lost [[primarch]].

Since the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 feral worlds have suffered heavily. Lacking defences of their own, they depend on the [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] and garrisons that watch over them, and several have been depopulated by invasion. The losses suffered by [[Baal|Baal Secundus]] in the [[Devastation of Baal]] in 999.M41 are the best known.

Culture

Feral worlders commonly live by oral tradition, keeping the history of their people in songs, sagas and genealogies. Honour, strength and courage in battle are the virtues most valued, and tests of manhood or womanhood are common. On [[Fenris]] the tribes honour great deeds in sagas, and those taken by the [[Space Wolves]] are believed to have gone to the halls of the Allfather; on [[Chogoris]] the clans of the steppe honour the horse, the bow and the open sky. Imperial observers note that feral peoples who have long contact with a Chapter often worship its warriors as gods or spirits, a belief the Ecclesiarchy tolerates where it does not contradict the [[Imperial Cult]].
