Koriel Zeth was a magos of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicum]] of [[Mars]] and the mistress of the Magma City, one of the great forge-cities of the Red Planet, in the last years of the [[Great Crusade]]. She was an innovator in a priesthood that distrusted innovation: she developed the noosphere, a wireless network that connected the minds of the adepts who used it, and she began the building of the Akashic Reader, a machine that was meant to give its user access to all knowledge. Her networks protected her forge and its allies from the scrapcode that the [[Fabricator-General]] [[Kelbor-Hal]] released at the start of the [[Schism of Mars]] in 005.M31. She then refused to bow to the Fabricator-General's faction, and Magma City was attacked and destroyed. She died in the siege.

The Mechanicus of the Imperium regards her as the first martyr of the loyalist forges. Its annals note that she was also a [[Heretic|heretic]] in the eyes of the Cult Mechanicus of her own time, for she declared that she no longer believed that the Machine God existed. The annals have not decided how to resolve the two facts.

Life

Magma City and the noosphere

Nothing is recorded of Zeth's birth or early life. She was an adept of the Martian forges when the [[Treaty of Mars]] was signed in 739.M30, and by c. 900.M30 she was the mistress of [[Tharsis Montes|Magma City]], which she ruled as a [[Techmarine|master of the forge]]. She was the pupil of the adept Cacye, who was himself the pupil of the [[Explorator|explorator]] Laszlo. Laszlo had found, in a sealed tomb complex under the sands of [[Terra]], the plans of a communication system that the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicus]] of [[Mars]] knew only from legend, the noosphere.Estimated; no record gives an exact year.

The noosphere is a wireless network that joins the minds of the adepts who carry its implants. For them, information and communication become the same thing, and knowledge appears in the mind as shoals of light. Zeth took the plans from her teacher, improved them, and shared them with her allies, among them Ipluvien Maximal and the Fabricator Locum [[Zagreus Kane]]. She offered the system to the [[Fabricator-General]], who declined to give it his trust. The noosphere was dangerous to those who could not control it, for the realm of thought that it created, called the aether, was open to anything that could think.

The Mechanicum of the Great Crusade

The forges of Mars made the armour, weapons and ships of the Crusade, and the Legions' primarchs often visited them. Mechanicus records name two as friends of Zeth's work. [[Ferrus Manus]] of the [[Iron Hands]], who valued the Mechanicum and its inventions, and [[Fulgrim]] of the [[Emperor's Children]], who sought perfection in all things, are both said to have discussed with her the ways in which a machine could be joined to a mind. The record of these meetings, which the Mechanicus keeps under seal, is thin.

Her best-known project of this period was the Mechanum unit, a mind-machine interface that she designed for use in the forge. The noosphere was developed from it. Another, begun in the last years of the Crusade (c. 003.M31), was the Akashic Reader. She brought to Mars a young menial of the Mechanicum named Dalia Cythera, who had been accused on Terra of heresy for the belief that technology should advance by new invention, and not only by the recovery of old designs. Together they built the Reader, a throne-like device that was meant to give its user access to all knowledge through the Warp.Estimated; no record gives an exact year.

The first test used the light of the [[Astronomican]] to power the device, and an empath was seated in it. The empath is recorded to have gained "all knowledge" and to have died as a result of it, since the mind could not hold it. The Reader overloaded, and Dalia, in the moment of the failure, saw a vision of something that was not human stirring beneath the surface of Mars. The Mechanicus's record of what followed is sealed.

The Schism of Mars

Main article: [[Schism of Mars]]

The scrapcode

In 005.M31, [[Kelbor-Hal]] had pledged the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicum]] to the [[Warmaster]] [[Horus]], on the promise that the Emperor's prohibitions on knowledge would be lifted. His envoy to Mars was Regulus, who had won the [[Fabricator-General]] over. The Fabricator-General opened the [[Vaults of Moravec]] beneath Olympus Mons, and a corrupted code, the scrapcode, spread through the machines of Mars and drove them and their operators mad. The forges that used Zeth's noosphere were not affected, because its structure was unlike that of the old network, and the scrapcode could not enter it. These were [[Tharsis Montes|Magma City]], the forge of Ipluvien Maximal, and [[Zagreus Kane]]'s forge at Mondus Occulum, and they became the centre of the loyalist resistance.

Kelbor-Hal sent an emissary, Melgator, to Magma City to learn how it had survived, and to take the noosphere. Melgator reproached Zeth that if she had shared the technology freely, Mars might have been spared the "Death of Innocence". Zeth replied that if the Fabricator-General had put more faith in the noosphere when she had offered it to him, that might have been so.

The defence of Magma City

The pretext for open war was supplied by Zeth. She declared that she no longer believed in the Machine God, though she remained loyal to the Emperor and to his aims, since technology came from reason and science and not from faith. The Dark Mechanicum declared her and her followers hereteks and attacked Magma City, and the war spread over the planet within hours.

[[Legio Tempestus]] and the Knights of [[House Taranis]] came to her aid. The forges of Zeth, Maximal and Kane were soon surrounded, and the assault was led by the [[Legio Mortis]] and its Imperator Titan, the Aquila Ignis. It was blunted when a flow of lava overran the Traitor ranks, and the Tempestus destroyed most of the Mortis. The victory could not be followed up, for the loyalists were outnumbered everywhere else. Magma City was besieged for some months.

Death

Zeth was killed in the fall of Magma City (005–006.M31). Accounts of her death differ. The annals of Kane's followers say that she died at the hands of the troops of Regulus when the Dark Mechanicum broke into the forge, and that she refused until the last to give them the noosphere. The Traitors' records say nothing of her. The Mechanicus agrees that the forge was destroyed, either by the Dark Mechanicum or, as some accounts say, by the loyalists themselves, who chose to destroy their forge-cities rather than let them fall.Estimated; no record gives an exact year.

The Akashic Reader

The fate of the Akashic Reader is uncertain. The device was damaged in its first test, and it was in [[Tharsis Montes|Magma City]] when the siege began. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicus]] of the Imperium believes that it was destroyed with the forge. A tradition among the followers of Kane holds that Dalia Cythera took the machine's core out of the city before the end and hid it, and that she went on to serve a guardianship on Mars that lasted ten thousand years; this account is held by the Mechanicus to be a legend, and the facts are sealed.

Legacy

The noosphere survived Zeth. Kane's loyalists, who used it, formed the core of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] recognised by the [[Council of Terra]] in 012.M31, and the network, in a much altered form, is the ancestor of the data-systems of the [[Forge world|forge worlds]]. Zeth herself is remembered in the Mechanicus with ambivalence: as a founder of the loyalist forges, as a [[Dark Mechanicum|heretek]] who denied the Machine God, and as a magos who would have given her art to anyone who asked. Her denial is a matter on which the Priesthood has never reached agreement, and the tech-priests who follow [[Belisarius Cawl]] cite her as a precedent for innovation.
