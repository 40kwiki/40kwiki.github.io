A sector is the principal territorial division of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] below the [[Segmentum|segmentum]]. Each of the five segmenta is divided into sectors, each sector into sub-sectors, and each sub-sector into star systems and their worlds. The sector is the level at which most of the Imperium's government is actually carried out: tithes are assessed, battlefleets are based, the [[Inquisition]] gathers in conclaves and the [[Ecclesiarchy]] appoints its cardinals. It is the scale at which Imperial scholars speak of a theatre of war or a province of the Imperium.

No two sectors are alike. The size of each is set by local need and by the density of the stars within it, so that a crowded sector in the heart of the Imperium may be small while one on the frontier is vast. A "typical" sector, if there can be such a thing, spans some seven million cubic light years, equal to a cube almost two hundred light years on each side. Within it the sub-sectors are clusters of worlds, and the space between them, called inter-sector or [[Wilderness space|wilderness space]], may hold unknown worlds, nebulae, [[Xenos|xenos]] empires or nothing at all.

The Imperium assigns each sector a battlefleet of the [[Imperial Navy]], usually named for the sector it guards: Battlefleet Gothic is based in the [[Gothic Sector]] and [[Battlefleet Calixis]] in the [[Calixis Sector]]. The sectors are also the unit of the Imperium's wars. Since the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 many sectors have been divided between the [[Imperium Sanctus]] and the [[Imperium Nihilus]], and in the [[Era Indomitus]] the [[Administratum]] has had to recognise sectors it can no longer reach.

Structure

Size and shape

The size of a sector is governed by practical needs, above all by the patrol ranges of Imperial starships. The Navy cannot guard what its ships cannot cross in a reasonable time, and the [[Warp travel|warp routes]] between stars decide which worlds are neighbours and which are far apart. The boundaries of a sector are therefore drawn along those routes and not as lines in space, and a sector's worlds may lie in several clusters joined by warp lanes while much of its volume is empty or uncharted.

Sub-sectors

Main article: [[Sub-sector]]

Each sector is divided into a variable number of sub-sectors. A sub-sector usually comprises two to eight star systems within a radius of about ten light years, although some hold more and others only one. Its systems share warp routes, trade and defence, and it is generally named after its principal world, which often serves as its capital and naval base. A sub-sector is a division of worlds and not of volume, so that the large regions of space that do not fall within any sub-sector remain beyond the official reckoning.

Inter-sectors and wilderness space

Main article: [[Wilderness space]]

The systems that belong to no sub-sector are known as inter-sectors, and are more commonly called wilderness space, forbidden zones, empty space and frontier space. They may hold gas or dust nebulae, regions that ships cannot reach, [[Xenos|xenos]]-held systems, unexplored or uninhabited systems and worlds that cannot support life. Imperial planners treat them as a reserve for expansion and as a hazard, since raiders and hostile fleets often cross them unobserved to strike at the nearest sub-sector.

Government and institutions

Civil government

Main article: [[Sector Governor]]

Each sector is ruled in name by a Lord Sector, also called a [[Sector Governor|sector governor]], drawn from the [[Planetary Governor|planetary governors]] of its worlds or appointed by the [[High Lords of Terra]]. He governs from a sector capital, collects the tithes owed to [[Terra]] and answers to the Administratum. The real power of the office varies a great deal: in a rich and well-connected sector such as [[Calixis Sector|Calixis]] the Lord Sector is a great lord with a court and a fleet at his command, while on the frontier he may control little more than his capital and the worlds within a few weeks of it.

Military

Every sector, or group of sectors, has its own battlefleet of the [[Imperial Navy]], commanded by a Lord Admiral who answers to the [[Lord High Admiral]] of the segmentum. The [[Departmento Munitorum]] raises regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]] sector by sector, and a sector's regiments often serve together on campaign. In time of war, the High Lords may appoint a [[Lord Commander Militant|Lord Commander Militant]] to direct every Imperial force within one or more sectors, and for the greatest wars a [[Lord Solar]] may command the forces of many sectors at once.

Other Adepta

The other great [[Adeptus Terra|Adepta]] also follow sector divisions. The [[Adeptus Arbites]] keeps a fortress precinct in each sector capital. The [[Inquisition]] organises its members in sector conclaves, such as the Calixian Conclave and the great Ordos often work through local circles that take their names from a sub-sector, as the Ordo Malleus did in the [[Helican Sub-sector|Helican Sub-sector]] of the [[Scarus Sector]]. The [[Ecclesiarchy]] appoints a [[Cardinal]] for each sector or group of sectors, and the [[Adeptus Astra Telepathica]] usually maintains an astropathic relay at each sector capital, so that a sector can speak across the warp to Terra and to its neighbours.

History

The Great Crusade

The [[Great Crusade]] (798.M30 to 005.M31) had no fixed provinces. Worlds conquered by an expedition fleet were placed under the officers who had taken them, and the [[Administratum|Imperial Administration]] grouped them for tithe by their distance and direction from [[Terra]]. The late [[30th millennium|30th-millennium]] records already speak of groupings that later ages call sectors, although the form was not yet fixed.

The post-Heresy reforms

The present system took shape after the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31). The reforms of [[Roboute Guilliman]], in roughly 015–021.M31, divided the old Imperial Army into the [[Astra Militarum]] and the [[Imperial Navy]] and fixed the five segmenta and the sectors within them. Each sector was given its own battlefleet and made responsible for its own defence, and no sector governor was to command both a fleet and an army.The dates of the reform are estimated from the sequence of events. No Administratum record of the first division into sectors survives.

The middle millennia

In later millennia sectors were redrawn many times as worlds were lost or gained. The [[Age of Apostasy]], in the early 36th millennium, left whole sectors cut off, among them the Jericho Sector of the [[Eastern Fringe]], which was not re-entered until the [[Achilus Crusade]] of 777.M41 began the reconquest of the [[Jericho Reach]]. New sectors were created by conquest, such as [[Calixis Sector|Calixis]], founded after the [[Angevin Crusade]] of 322–384.M39. The [[Sabbat Worlds]] were lost to [[Chaos]] in the 41st millennium and have been the object of the [[Sabbat Worlds Crusade]] since 755.M41.

The Great Rift and the Era Indomitus

The [[Great Rift]], opened in 999.M41, split many sectors along its length. In the [[Era Indomitus]] the Administratum has had to recognise sectors that it can no longer reach, and the [[Indomitus Crusade]] and its successors have redrawn others as they have been reconquered.

Many more sectors are known by name. The registers list dozens, among them Askellon, Caligari, Octarius, Korianis, Narasima, Zeist and Ullanor, and each has its own record of its sub-sectors, its fleets and its wars. The Ultima Sector has the best-ordered records, since the [[Ultramarines]] keep them as part of the Realm of [[Ultramar]].

Navigation and astrography

The Imperium does not map its sectors as a traveller would map a coast. Every journey between stars passes through the [[Warp]], and the length of a voyage depends on the state of the warp more than on the distance in realspace. Two worlds a few light years apart may be separated by a storm that closes the route for decades, while worlds many sectors apart may be joined by a stable current. A sector is therefore partly a political fact and partly a chart of the warp lanes that bind its worlds together. The [[Adeptus Astronomica]] lights the [[Astronomican]] across the whole of it, and without the guidance of [[Navigator]] houses no fleet could keep to those lanes.

For the same reason sector boundaries are not fixed. Warp storms can close a route and isolate a sub-sector, and the opening of a new route can bring distant systems into a sector that had never known them. The Administratum revises its charts when it is able, but a sector that has been cut off for generations is often found, when it is reached again, to have gone its own way entirely. The loss and reopening of the Jericho Reach, and the long years during which the Sabbat Worlds fell out of contact with Terra, are the best-known cases.

Defence of a sector

The defence of a sector falls on three bodies that rarely have the same interests: the battlefleet, the regiments raised by the Departmento Munitorum, and the planetary defence forces of each world. The battlefleet is the sector's reach, able to carry war from its capital to any sub-sector. Its commander ranks with the Lord Sector, and the two often disagree on how much of the sector's wealth should go to the fleet. A sector that is ill-served by its fleet, such as one whose battlefleet has been recalled to a war elsewhere, is exposed to raiders, [[Orks]] and [[Tyranids]], and its governor has little means of calling for help beyond an [[Astropathy|astropathic]] plea to Terra that may go unanswered for years.

The Imperium also places the strongest works at the borders of sectors rather than in their centres. A fortress world or a [[Blackstone Fortress]] stands at the most dangerous approach, and the sub-sector next to it bears the burden of its supply. The [[Gothic Sector]], beside the [[Eye of Terror]], was guarded in this way until the [[Gothic War]], and the [[Cadian Sector]] was guarded by the fortress world of [[Cadia]] until its destruction in 999.M41.
