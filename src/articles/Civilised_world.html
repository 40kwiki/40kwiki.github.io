A civilised world is a class of planet of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] with a developed, mixed economy of industry, agriculture and trade, and a population ranging from a few million to several billion. It is the most common class in the registry of the [[Administratum]], and the one that most closely matches the ordinary picture of an Imperial world: a planet with cities, farmland, manufactories, a planetary government and a defence force, which pays its tithe and is otherwise left to its own affairs.

Civilised worlds lack the vast populations of [[hive world]]s and the specialised economies of [[forge world|forge]] and [[agri-world]]s. Their tithe is usually in the middle Decuma band, paid partly in goods and partly in regiments for the [[Astra Militarum]]. Most are governed by a planetary governor, often a hereditary lord, with wide freedom in local matters.

Characteristics

Civilised worlds vary widely in climate, culture and wealth. Many have one or more large cities, which may resemble small hives, surrounded by farmland and industrial regions. They typically produce enough food for themselves, manufacture their own everyday goods and export a surplus to the wider Imperium. Their technology is that of the Imperium at large: power, transport, voidships and weapons of Imperial pattern, maintained with the help of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]].

Society on a civilised world is commonly divided between an aristocracy or merchant class, a professional class of clerks and officials, and a large working population. The [[Imperial Cult]] is the official faith, the [[Adeptus Arbites]] enforce Imperial law, and a [[Planetary Defence Force]] guards against raids and rebellion.

Government and tithe

The Administratum treats civilised worlds as the backbone of the tithe. They pay regularly, raise regiments in steady numbers and supply the sectors around them. Their governors are often members of old local dynasties, confirmed in office by the Imperium. A civilised world whose tithe falls short may be reassessed, and a governor who fails or rebels can expect to be removed.

A civilised world may change class over time. Population growth may turn it into a hive world, war or famine may reduce it to a feudal world, and its fall to [[Chaos]] may lead to its destruction or its transformation into a [[daemon world]]. [[Isstvan III]], recorded in the Great Crusade as a compliant civilised world of several billion people, rebelled with the encouragement of [[Horus]] and was virus-bombed in 005.M31, after which it was reclassified as a [[dead world]].

Culture

The culture of civilised worlds is as varied as the Imperium itself. Each keeps its own customs, dialects, festivals and local saints alongside the observances of the Imperial Cult. Many have histories reaching back to the [[Dark Age of Technology]], and their nobility preserve genealogies and heirlooms from before the [[Age of Strife]]. Education, beyond what is needed for work, is usually the privilege of the wealthy, though the [[Schola Progenium]] and the [[Administratum]] recruit gifted children from all classes.

History

The class dates from the census of the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31), when the Administratum's adepts needed a category for worlds that were neither specialised nor primitive. Most civilised worlds were brought into the Imperium during the Crusade; others have been settled since. In the [[Era Indomitus]] many civilised worlds in the [[Imperium Nihilus]] have been cut off by the [[Great Rift]] (999.M41), and the [[Indomitus Crusade]] has had to reassess their condition one by one.

Defence

Civilised worlds are expected to defend themselves against minor threats. The governor must raise and equip a [[Planetary Defence Force]], maintain fortifications and orbital defences, and watch for cults and heresy. In the event of a major invasion the governor may appeal to the sector command for aid from the Astra Militarum, the [[Imperial Navy]] or the [[Adeptus Astartes]], but help may take years to arrive, and many civilised worlds have fallen before it did. Imperial doctrine holds that only worlds of proven loyalty and full tithe may expect such aid.
