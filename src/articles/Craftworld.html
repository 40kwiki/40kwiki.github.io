A craftworld is one of the vast world-ships of the [[Aeldari]], on which the people called the Asuryani fled the [[Fall of the Aeldari]] in M30 and on which they still live. Each is a city the size of a moon, grown from the psychically shaped material called [[wraithbone]] and powered by the light of stars. At its heart lies an infinity circuit, a psychic network in which the souls of the craftworld's dead are kept safe from [[Slaanesh]], the god born of the Fall.

The craftworlds drift slowly through space, linked to one another and to the rest of [[Milky Way|the galaxy]] by the [[webway]]. Their people live by the Path, a discipline in which each Aeldari devotes himself wholly to one calling at a time, in order to master the passions that led their race to ruin. Each craftworld is ruled by a Seer Council of [[Farseer]]s, who read the future and direct its wars. The [[Imperium of Mankind|Imperium]] knows of several hundred craftworlds, but only a few dozen have been seen by human eyes.

History

In the last centuries of the [[Aeldari]] empire, a part of the race foresaw where its excesses were leading. These Exodites left for distant worlds, and others built the craftworlds and set out in them. The ships were originally trade and colony vessels; once the Fall began, they were filled with refugees and pushed away from the core. When [[Slaanesh]] was born, in about M30, the psychic shock killed nearly every Aeldari in the galaxy and tore open the [[Eye of Terror]]. The craftworlds that were far enough away survived; many that were not were destroyed or drawn into the Eye, where some, such as Altansar, survived for ten thousand years.

The survivors have since fought to preserve the remnants of their race. The Aeldari of the craftworlds are few and dying, and a craftworld will sacrifice millions of other lives, human or otherwise, to save a handful of its own. They are among the most dangerous and least trusted of the Imperium's neighbours.

Life on a craftworld

The Path

The Path divides life into callings such as the Path of the Warrior, the Path of the Seer, the Path of the Artisan and the Path of the Outcast. An Aeldari may walk one Path for decades and then move to another. Those who become trapped on a Path become Exarchs (on the Path of the Warrior) or [[Farseer|Farseers]] (on the Path of the Seer). Those who reject the Path entirely leave as Outcasts, Rangers or Corsairs.

The infinity circuit

Every Asuryani wears a spirit stone, which catches the soul at death. The stones are taken to the infinity circuit, where the souls join the craftworld's dead and protect it with their power. In great need, souls are placed in [[Wraithbone|wraithbone]] bodies to fight as ghost warriors. The infinity circuit is the craftworld's most precious possession, and its loss is a disaster from which a craftworld may not recover.

War

Craftworld armies are made up of Guardians, the citizen militia; Aspect Warriors, who follow the shrines of the Path of the Warrior; Rangers; grav-tanks and war walkers; and, in the gravest wars, ghost warriors and the Avatar of Khaine, a fragment of the war god [[Khaine]] held in each craftworld. An Autarch, an Aeldari who has walked several Paths of war, may command.

Ulthwé

Ulthwé, also called Ulthwé the Damned, drifts close to the Eye of Terror and the [[Cadian Gate]], and has fought the forces of [[Chaos]] more often than any other craftworld. It has more seers than any other, and its Seer Council was long led by [[Eldrad Ulthran]]. Its army relies on its Guardians, the black-armoured Black Guardians, more than on Aspect Warriors. Ulthwé's seers are blamed by Imperial records for raising the Ork Warlord [[Ghazghkull Thraka]] to power in 941.M41 to divert the [[Orks]] from another craftworld, which led to the [[Second War for Armageddon]]. In 838.M41 it joined Biel-Tan in destroying the Imperial xenologists who plundered the Maiden World Kiliak. In 999.M41 it fought in the [[Thirteenth Black Crusade]], and after the [[Rise of the Ynnari]] its Seer Council exiled Eldrad for acting for all the [[Aeldari]] without its consent.

Iyanden

Iyanden was once among the largest craftworlds. In 812.M41 it helped destroy [[Hive Fleet]] Naga with Malan'tai and Idharae. In 992.M41 the tendrils of [[Hive Fleet Kraken]] reached it, warned of in advance by Eldrad Ulthran. Its exiled son Prince Yriel returned with his pirate fleet, took up the cursed Spear of Twilight and killed the Norn Queen leading the attack, but more than two-thirds of the craftworld was destroyed and most of its people killed. Since then Iyanden has fought with its dead, under the Spiritseer Iyanna Arienal, and its armies are made largely of ghost warriors. In the [[Era Indomitus]] it survived an attack by the forces of [[Nurgle]] with the help of the [[Ynnari]], and [[Yriel]], whom the Spear was slowly killing, was saved by them.

Alaitoc

Alaitoc keeps the Path more strictly than any other craftworld. Many of its young people find the discipline too harsh and leave, so that Alaitoc has more Outcasts and Rangers than any other, and its Rangers, among them the famed Pathfinder Illic Nightspear, act as its eyes across the galaxy. In 798.M41 the warhost of its [[Farseer]] Eldorath Starbane was destroyed by [[Imotekh the Stormlord]] at Somonor. In 894.M41 an Alaitoc Ranger squad found the lost craftworld Mymeara, and in 991.M41 Starbane's Rangers fought beside the [[Crimson Fists]] at Rynn's World.

Biel-Tan

Biel-Tan was the most warlike of the craftworlds, home to many Aspect shrines, and dedicated to rebuilding the Aeldari empire by clearing the old Maiden Worlds of other races. It fought the Imperium often, including at Kiliak in 838.M41, and in 999.M41 it fought beside Iyanden against Hive Fleets Kraken and [[Hive Fleet Leviathan|Leviathan]] on the Maiden World Dûriel. Later that year the craftworld was attacked by [[Daemon|daemons]] of [[Slaanesh]] and [[Khorne]]. The battle broke its infinity circuit, and the craftworld split apart in the event Aeldari call the Fracture of Biel-Tan. In the battle the [[Ynnari]] emissary [[Yvraine]] drew the second of the Croneswords from its [[Wraithbone|wraithbone]] and the Yncarne, avatar of [[Ynnead]], emerged. Many of Biel-Tan's survivors joined the Ynnari.

Saim-Hann

Saim-Hann is a craftworld of clans, whose people prefer speed and pride to strict adherence to the Path. Its warriors fight on jetbikes and Vypers, and its Wild Rider clans are famed across the galaxy. Its clans keep feuds and codes of honour, and its Wild Riders follow Wild Lords such as Nuadu Fireheart. Its warhosts have raided Imperial worlds, among them Gnosis Prime in 786.M41.
