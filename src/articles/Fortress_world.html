A fortress world is a class of planet of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] that has been fortified and garrisoned to guard against some constant and persistent threat to Imperial space. Its cities are fortresses, its industry is given over to war, and its people are trained as soldiers from childhood. Fortress worlds are placed at the gateways of the Imperium: at stable routes through the [[Warp]], at the borders of hostile empires, and around the [[Eye of Terror]] and other warp rifts. They are as much roadblocks as bastions, and several of them linked together form a barrier that prevents an enemy from spreading into Imperial space.

Their garrisons are very large and are usually made up of regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]], [[Planetary Defence Force|planetary defence forces]] and squadrons of the [[Imperial Navy]]. A fortress world is as valuable for its soldiers as for its position, since its armies are trained and equipped to the standards of the Imperial Guard and are always raised in the expectation that they will be called to serve elsewhere. The doctrine of the Tactica Imperialis holds that such worlds are a ready source of skilled and disciplined men, and few of them raise their armies without this purpose.

The most famous fortress world was [[Cadia]], which guarded the [[Cadian Gate]], the only stable passage out of the Eye of Terror, against the forces of [[Chaos]] and the [[Black Crusade|Black Crusades]] of [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] for ten thousand years, until its destruction in 999.M41 in the [[Thirteenth Black Crusade]]. Its fall was followed by the opening of the [[Great Rift]]. Not all fortress worlds belong to the Imperium: several, among them [[Medrengard]], have fallen to the [[Traitor Legions]], and the [[Iron Warriors]] have turned a number of them into strongholds. In the [[Era Indomitus]] the Imperium has fortified new worlds along the edges of the Rift, and the class has grown in number and importance.

Characteristics

Defences

A fortress world may be any kind of planet, from a temperate [[Agri-world|garden world]] such as [[Cadia]] to a barren [[Dead world|dead world]] such as Belis Corona. What makes it a fortress world is its purpose, and many are established on habitable planets that are then built up for war. The surface is covered with fortress-cities, bunker lines, missile silos, [[Void shield|void-shield]] generators and landing fields. The orbit is guarded by defence platforms, star forts and minefields, and the moons are often fortified as well. Stockpiles of munitions and supplies are kept in armoured vaults deep underground, so that the world can withstand a siege lasting years.

Government

Fortress worlds are generally ruled by a military governor and not by a civil one. On Cadia supreme authority lay with the [[Lord Castellan]], who was also the commander of the planet's regiments. The civilian economy, where one exists, is directed to the support of the garrison, and many fortress worlds also house the headquarters of a sector or [[Segmentum|segmentum]] fleet. The ties with the [[Imperial Navy]] are close, since a fortress world that cannot be reached by sea or by void is of little use.

Society

Life under arms

Life on a fortress world is steeped in military discipline and doctrine. From the hour they wake to the hour they sleep the citizens train and work as a military unit, and each is raised to prepare for the day when he is called on to push back the darkness beyond the walls. Those old enough to enlist in the Astra Militarum or the planetary defence forces are often required to do so, and even those too young or infirm find ways to serve. Whole generations of one family may be in service at one time, and many regiments of the Imperial Guard boast a long and proud history.

On [[Cadia]] every child was taught to handle a [[Lasgun|lasgun]], youth units took the field as Whiteshields before they were admitted to a regiment, and every adult served or stood in reserve. Military rank, drill and discipline governed public life. Fortress-worlders are known throughout the Astra Militarum for discipline and professionalism, and their officers and drill instructors are sought by other regiments.

Faith

The [[Imperial Cult]] on fortress worlds is martial and austere. It honours the Emperor as protector and the dead of past sieges as martyrs, shrines to fallen soldiers are common, and the defence of the world is treated as a sacred duty.

Armed forces

A fortress world serves three purposes. It is a barrier, holding a route an enemy must pass; a base, from which fleets and armies can strike; and a reservoir of soldiers, raising regiments for service elsewhere. Its soldiers are raised to war, and by the time they are grown they know the arts of war and the doctrines of the Tactica Imperialis. They are long trained to destroy the enemies of the Imperium, above all those whose threat first caused their world to be fortified, and are known as disciplined, honourable and loyal, each a proficient fighter long before he is taken into the Imperial armed forces.

The [[Cadian Shock Troops]] were raised on Cadia in greater numbers than any other regiments in the Imperium, and their doctrine became the model for much of the Astra Militarum. Fortress worlds that fall are a disaster for the Imperium, and their capture is a prize for its enemies. The Iron Warriors in particular seek them, and have turned several into strongholds, among them [[Kasr Partox]] in the [[Cadian Sector]].

History

Origins

The Imperium fortified strategic worlds from the time of the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30 to 005.M31), but the fortress world as a class took shape after the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31). When the defeated traitor Legions fled into the Eye of Terror during the [[Great Scouring]], [[Cadia]] and its neighbours were fortified to hold the Cadian Gate. The reforms of the [[Codex Astartes]], written c. 014–021.M31, fixed the principle that the ground and the void should not be held by one commander, and the [[Astra Militarum]] came to hold fortress worlds as fixed garrisons.Date of the reforms estimated from the sequence of events after the Great Scouring.

The long watch

Over the following ten thousand years fortress worlds were raised around the [[Maelstrom]], along the [[Eastern Fringe]] and on the borders of the [[Orks|Ork]] empires. Their defence was tested many times, above all in the Black Crusades of Abaddon, the first of which broke through the Cadian Gate in 781.M31. In the 41st millennium the [[Gothic War]] (139–160.M41) showed that Abaddon could bypass the Gate altogether, and some Imperial fortress worlds in the Jericho Reach, among them Alphos, Hethgard, Karlack and Pyrathas, held out against the forces of Chaos in the [[Achilus Crusade]].

The Era Indomitus

The destruction of Cadia in 999.M41 and the opening of the Great Rift changed the Imperium's defences entirely. The Rift could not be held at a single gate, and fortress worlds have since been built or reinforced at the few stable crossings, such as the [[Nachmund Gauntlet]], and on both sides of the Rift. The Cadians who survived have been settled on many of these worlds, and they teach the arts of war to a new generation of soldiers.

Notable fortress worlds

The registers record dozens of fortress worlds, in the keeping of the Imperium and of its enemies. The greatest of the Imperial fortress worlds after Cadia is Coronis Agathon in the Segmentum Obscurus, a world of some 120 billion people, and the Cadian Sector held Kasr Partox. In the Jericho Reach the Imperium holds Alphos, Karlack, Hethgard, Pyrathas, Spite and Wrath, with populations of between 50 and 340 million where they are recorded. In the Sabbat Worlds Jago and Morlond stand as fortress worlds, and the Inquisition holds two of its own, Talasa Prime in Ultramar and the moon Titan.

Some are lost. Khirab, Mortrex and Sybari are now dead worlds, and Mortain was destroyed. Others serve the enemy: the Iron Warriors hold Brigannion IV, Burjan's World, Castellax, Forgefane, Malodrax and Lutoris Epsilon, along with Medrengard in the Eye of Terror and Kasr Partox, which they took in 999.M41, while the world of Magog in the Jericho Reach is held by Chaos.

Fortress worlds of the Inquisition and the Chapters

Not every fortress world answers to the Astra Militarum. The [[Inquisition]] keeps fortress worlds of its own, held by its own troops and by the Chapters allied to it. The moon [[Titan (moon)|Titan]], in orbit about Saturn in the [[Sol System]], is the stronghold of the [[Grey Knights]] and the seat of much of the [[Ordo Malleus]], and its defences guard secrets that the Imperium has never been willing to put in the open. [[Talasa Prime]] in [[Ultramar]], a fortress world given by the [[Ultramarines]], serves the [[Deathwatch]] and the [[Ordo Xenos]] as a fortress in the war against the xenos threats of the Ultima Segmentum.

These fortress worlds differ from the others in what they hold rather than in how they are built. They are meant less to stop an invading fleet than to keep an enemy within, or to store what must not be lost. Their garrisons are small and chosen, their defences are directed inwards as well as outwards, and the rest of the Imperium is seldom allowed to know what they contain. The Chapters of the [[Adeptus Astartes]] raise their own fortress-monasteries in the same way, but these belong to the Chapter and not to the class of planet.
