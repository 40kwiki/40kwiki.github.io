The Old Ones were an ancient xenos race that ruled much of the [[Milky Way|galaxy]] tens of millions of years before mankind. They were masters of the [[Warp]] and of the shaping of life. They made the [[webway]], seeded countless worlds with life, and are believed to have made the [[Aeldari]] and the [[Krork]], from whom the [[Orks]] descend, as soldiers for their last war. Almost nothing of their own culture survives, and what the Imperium knows of them comes from Aeldari myth, Necron records and a handful of relics.

Their downfall came in the [[War in Heaven]], a war against the [[Necrons|Necrontyr]] and the star-gods called the [[C'tan]] that ended about sixty million years before the 41st millennium. The Old Ones lost. Their last great works, the Warp-born races they made, brought on the storms and predators of the [[Immaterium]], and some savants trace the first [[Daemon|daemons]] to the psychic powers unleashed in that war.

Nature

No Imperial record contains a reliable description of an Old One. Some Aeldari texts describe them as reptilian, and earlier Imperial accounts identify them with a vanished race the Inquisition once called the Slann; the Ordo Xenos now treats this link as unproven. What is agreed is that they were long-lived, patient and psychically gifted, and that they used the Warp the way mankind uses fire. Their lifespans were so long that the short-lived Necrontyr looked on them with envy and hatred.

Works

The webway

Main article: [[Webway]]

The Old Ones built the [[webway]], a labyrinth of tunnels between [[realspace]] and the Warp that let them cross the galaxy without the dangers of open Warp travel. It is still used by the Aeldari, the [[Harlequins]] and the [[Drukhari]] of [[Commorragh]], and the [[Emperor of Mankind]] tried to take part of it for mankind in the [[Webway War|Imperial Webway Project]] of the early 31st millennium.

Seeding of life

Imperial and Aeldari scholars agree that the Old Ones spread life to many worlds, either by planting it or by guiding species already there. Several races later met by the Imperium may owe their existence to them, among them the [[Jokaero]] and, some say, mankind's own distant ancestors, though the [[Ecclesiarchy]] rejects that view.

The warrior races

As the War in Heaven turned against them, the Old Ones made new races to fight for them. The best known are the Aeldari, a psychically gifted people, and the Krork, a war-race whose degenerate descendants are the Orks. Imperial texts also credit them with the [[Blackstone Fortress]]es, star-killing stations later found near the [[Eye of Terror]].

Fall

The Necrontyr, who had fought the Old Ones for a long time without success, accepted the C'tan as their masters and were made into the deathless [[Necrons]]. The new enemy was immune to the Old Ones' psychic arts, and the war turned against them. Aeldari tradition holds that their use of the Warp grew more desperate, that it filled the Immaterium with storms and predators, and that a plague of [[Enslaver]]s, psychic parasites that came through their own gates, finished their empire. The surviving Old Ones disappeared. Whether any still live, in some far region or beyond the galaxy, is unknown.

The Aeldari inherited the Old Ones' place and ruled the galaxy for millions of years, until their own [[Fall of the Aeldari|Fall]] in M30.

Legacy

Old One relics are among the most dangerous objects the Imperium knows of. The Blackstone Fortresses were used by [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] in the [[Gothic War]] (139–160.M41) and in the [[Fall of Cadia]] in 999.M41. The [[Harlequins]] are said by their own lore to guard Old One secrets in the [[Black Library]]. The Ordo Xenos keeps its knowledge of the race under seal.
