The Iterators were the orators, teachers and propagandists of the [[Imperial Truth]] during the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30 – 005.M31). The [[Emperor of Mankind]] created the corps to carry his secular [[Ursarkar Creed|creed]] to every conquered world: to explain why the Crusade was just, to break the hold of old religions, and to make new subjects into willing citizens of the [[Imperium of Mankind|Imperium]]. Iterators travelled with every Expeditionary Fleet, lectured soldiers and [[Space Marine]]s, and addressed the crowds of newly compliant worlds.

The [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31) destroyed the creed they served. The most famous Iterator, Kyril Sindermann of the 63rd Expedition, saw [[Daemon|daemons]] with his own eyes, gave up the Imperial Truth for faith in the Emperor, and became one of the first [[Inquisitor]]s. The corps did not survive the war. Its work of morale passed in part to the [[Commissar]]s of the later [[Astra Militarum|Imperial Guard]].

History

Purpose

The Emperor taught that the gods and superstitions of the [[Age of Strife]] had nearly destroyed humanity, and that a single rational doctrine would end the quarrels between peoples. The Iterators were the voice of that doctrine. They showed conquered peoples the benefits of Imperial rule and explained to soldiers why the conquest of tens of thousands of worlds was needed, whatever its cost. Above all they worked to replace religious belief with reason and faith in progress. Their success also served a purpose the corps itself did not know: worship of any kind feeds the powers of the [[Warp]], and a godless Imperium starved them. Those few who knew this later called the Imperial Truth the "[[Imperial Truth|Great Lie]]."

Selection and training

A saying of the Crusade held that one man in a thousand might become a Legion warrior, but only one in a hundred thousand was fit to be an Iterator. Candidates needed insight, eloquence, political judgment and quick intelligence. They were schooled in history, ethics, politics and rhetoric, and in both sides of persuasion: placed in a crowd, Iterators could stir it to enthusiasm, or turn it against a speaker. They often worked in teams, one speaking and others among the listeners. Most lived in the archive chambers of the Legion battle-barges, among records reaching back to the [[Dark Age of Technology]].

Service with the Legions

[[Horus]] valued the Iterators highly. Believing that the Crusade would one day end and his warriors would need a purpose in peace, he asked them to teach his captains and legionaries. The [[Raven Guard]] and other Legions kept Iterators of their own. The corps was led by an Iterator Prime; in the last years of the Crusade the office was held by Hugost Wacim.

Kyril Sindermann

Kyril Sindermann was Primary Iterator of the 63rd Expeditionary Fleet and lived aboard the [[Vengeful Spirit]], where he was a friend and tutor to the Luna Wolves captain [[Garviel Loken]]. On the world called Sixty-Three Nineteen c. 001.M31 he and the remembrancer [[Euphrati Keeler]] saw a [[Daemon|daemon]] possess a Luna Wolves sergeant. Shaken, he searched the archives for a reason why the Emperor, the greatest of [[Psyker|psykers]], would teach that no such beings existed. Reading from the Book of [[Lorgar]], he summoned a daemon into the archive by accident; Keeler banished it by faith.

The experience broke his belief in the Imperial Truth. He joined Keeler in the [[Lectitio Divinitatus]], escaped [[Isstvan III]] with her in 005.M31 aboard the Eisenstein, and on [[Terra]] visited her in her prison beneath the [[Imperial Palace]]. During the [[Siege of Terra]] (013–014.M31) he refounded a new order of [[Remembrancer Order|remembrancers]] to record the war, and recruited Keeler to it. Imperial records name him afterwards as one of the first [[Inquisitor|Inquisitors]], and he called [[Nathaniel Garro]] the first martyr of the [[Imperial Cult]].

End of the corps

[[Horus Heresy|The Heresy]] discredited the Imperial Truth. The war proved the existence of the Warp's powers, and on Terra the faithful of the Lectitio Divinitatus grew in number while the Iterators lost their hearers. No act abolishing the corps survives. Its members were absorbed into the offices of the [[Administratum]], or into the new [[Inquisition]], or vanished, in the years of reconstruction after 014.M31. Their task of guarding soldiers' morale and loyalty passed to the [[Commissar]]s of the Officio Prefectus, and their task of persuasion to the preachers of the [[Ecclesiarchy]].The end of the corps is estimated from the absence of Iterators in records after the [[Great Scouring]] (014–021.M31).
