A hive world is the most populous class of planet in the [[Imperium of Mankind]]. Its people live almost entirely within enormous city-structures called hives or hive cities, many-layered arcologies that rise kilometres into the sky and descend as far beneath the surface, and each of which may hold tens of billions of souls. The [[Administratum]] lists some 32,380 hive worlds, and they provide the greater part of the Imperium's manufactured goods and of the soldiers of the [[Astra Militarum]].

Most hive worlds began as habitable planets and have been ruined by constant industry. Outside the hives lie ash wastes and poisoned deserts, and the world depends on [[Agri-world]]s for food. A hive is ruled from its summit by a hereditary governor and the noble houses whose wealth rests on the labour of billions, while the lowest levels, the underhive, are held by gangs, mutants and heretics. The people of the middle hive may never see the sky.

The worlds are valued as much for their people as for their factories. A hive city produces soldiers and colonists as surely as steel, and the Administratum draws on hive populations to settle new worlds and to fill the regiments of the Astra Militarum. The Imperium will fight for them at almost any cost, as in the [[Wars for Armageddon]], and the loss of a hive world to rebellion, [[Genestealer Cults|Genestealer cult]] or invasion is among the gravest blows the Imperium can suffer.

In the Era Indomitus the hive worlds have become both battleground and reservoir. The opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 isolated many of them in the [[Imperium Nihilus]] and exposed others to invasion, while the Imperium's need for troops has made their tithes heavier than ever.

Structure of a hive

A hive is built in layers, and its society follows the same order. At the summit is the spire, the home of the governor and the noble houses, where the air is filtered and natural light still falls. Below it lies the hive city proper, a warren of habitation blocks, manufactoria and transit tunnels in which the working population lives and labours, often never seeing the sky. Beneath that lies the underhive, older levels abandoned or collapsed under the weight of the hive above, where outcasts, [[Mutant|mutants]], gangs and [[Heretic|heretics]] live beyond the reach of the law. At the very bottom is the sump, a lake of toxic waste.

Between the hives the surface is commonly an ash waste, a desert of industrial dust, slag and acid rain crossed by armoured convoys and roamed by nomads and raiders. Some hive worlds have a single hive and others dozens, linked by highways, rail and orbital shuttles. Some are wholly built over, covered by hundreds of layers of arcologies. [[Terra]], the Throneworld, is the greatest example of this kind of "super hive world", an ecumenopolis in which the whole of the world's surface is city.

Life in a hive

Class and labour

Hive cities are old, each growing wider across the barren ground and higher into the polluted air with every century. They are nearly sealed ecosystems, in which billions of people live stacked in a structure that is in essence a great metal tomb. A hive-dweller may live a whole life on the level where he was born and never see the sky or know what the surface of the world is like. Many are born into a house or family with their vocation already settled, and they work at it endlessly in the manufactoria and hab-blocks, producing goods for export or tending the machinery that keeps the hive alive.

Almost everything is recycled. The air, the water and the food of a hive-dweller have all passed through other bodies, and when a hive-dweller dies the hive recovers the corpse for its resources, among them corpse starch. Possessions pass down from the dead to the living, so that a room or a coat may have had a dozen owners. The population of a world roughly doubles every hundred years, and a hive city is in effect a factory for the most useful of all resources, people.

The class system follows the geometry of the hive. The nobility holds the spires, the bureaucratic middle class the middle levels and the workers the lower ones, and each level is poorer and more dangerous than the one above. The very bottom is where those without a place are sent, where law fails and gangs rule. Some hold that the underhive is allowed to exist in some form as a sink for the hive's malice and discord.

Unrest and calamity

A hive city is three missed meals from anarchy, and its millions depend on a vast infrastructure for food, power, light and clean air. If unrest is allowed to spread, the threads that hold a hive together can break and millions die. In the [[Calixis Sector]], for example, the Tyburn food-tax riots on [[Fenksworld]] almost doubled the population of the neighbouring Volg hive within a standard year, and two solar decades of petty revolt and near anarchy under the misrule of House Koba on [[Malfi]] left nearly a billion dead and ruined one of the sector's most powerful noble families.

Even the greatest hives are not safe. The Reinholt Blackouts of Hive Sibellus, the greatest city of [[Scintilla]], began when the Magistratum cracked down too hard on the city's gangs, and they spread into the hive's infrastructure. Power was cut to several dozen middle-hive districts for solar days, tens of thousands died, and whole districts burned. Sections of the hive remain as burnt-out ruins that are collapsing into the underhive, and the Magistratum has become more brutal as a result.

Tithe and military role

Hive worlds pay some of the highest [[Imperial tithe|tithe grades]] in the Imperium, in the ''Exactis'' band. They supply manufactured goods, labour for other worlds, ratings for the [[Imperial Navy]] and, above all, regiments for the Astra Militarum. It is said that the loss of a million soldiers is worth no more than one day's output of a hive world in arms and armour. Though hive-made goods are far less advanced than the products of a [[Forge world]], they are plentiful, and a hive recruit commonly already knows how to handle a weapon.

The soldiers of hive worlds are used to cramped, confused fighting, and hive regiments such as the [[Necromundan Spiders]] and the [[Armageddon Steel Legion]] are noted for city fighting and mechanised war. A hive world is also a hard place to conquer. Defenders can hold every level and attackers must clear the city floor by floor, which makes fighting for a hive among the costliest kinds of war.

The defence of [[Hades Hive]] on [[Armageddon]] under [[Commissar]] [[Sebastian Yarrick]] in 941.M41 is the best-known example. In the Third War for Armageddon in 998.M41, Hive Helsreach was besieged by the [[Orks]] of [[Ghazghkull Thraka]] and defended by the [[Black Templars]] and the hive's own defenders.

Society, crime and heresy

A hive world is ruled by a hereditary governor drawn from the most powerful noble house. The other houses control particular industries or districts and compete for the governor's favour, sometimes with violence. The workers belong to guilds or industrial clans whose loyalties are bound to their employers. Crime, gang war and cult activity are endemic in the lower levels, and the planetary enforcers, assisted by the [[Adeptus Arbites]], keep order only in part.

The overcrowded lower levels are fertile ground for heresy. [[Genestealer Cults]] in particular spread unnoticed through the underhive for generations, and many hive worlds have been lost to cult uprisings. The [[Inquisition]], and in particular the [[Ordo Xenos]], keeps watch on hive worlds for this reason.

History

Most hive worlds began as hospitable planets. Over many centuries their cities grew wider and higher as populations rose, and the land outside them was ruined by mining, harvesting and waste. The Imperium's populous core worlds cluster around regions first settled in the [[Age of Technology|Dark Age of Technology]], and the oldest hives are very old indeed.

In the 41st millennium the hive worlds were the centre of some of the Imperium's largest wars, from the three wars for Armageddon to the siege of Vigilus.

Since the opening of the Great Rift in 999.M41 the hive worlds have been among the main targets of the Imperium's enemies. Many in the Imperium Nihilus have been cut off, some have been lost to uprisings or invasions, and the [[Departmento Munitorum]] has demanded levies far above the usual grade to supply the [[Indomitus Crusade]].
