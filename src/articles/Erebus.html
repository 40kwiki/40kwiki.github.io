Erebus, who calls himself the Hand of Destiny, is a [[Chaos Space Marine]] of the [[Word Bearers]] Legion. He was First [[Chaplain]] of the Legion at the end of the [[Great Crusade]], and Imperial historians count him, with [[Kor Phaeron]], among the chief authors of the [[Horus Heresy]]. He helped turn his [[Primarch|primarch]] [[Lorgar]] to the [[Chaos|Ruinous Powers]], arranged the wounding of the [[Warmaster]] [[Horus]] on [[Davin]], and presided over the rites that delivered Horus to the gods of [[Warp|the warp]].

During the Heresy he served Horus as spiritual counsellor and accomplice, planned the purge of loyalists at [[Isstvan III]], and raised the warp storm called the Ruinstorm after the [[Battle of Calth]]. He made many enemies on his own side. He survived the [[Siege of Terra]], where he fought aboard the [[Vengeful Spirit]] and murdered the loyalist [[Sons of Horus|Garviel Loken]] after Horus's death. Since then he has served as a Dark Apostle on the Dark Council of the Word Bearers, contending with Kor Phaeron for control of the Legion.

Early life

Erebus was born to common parents on [[Colchis]] in the late [[30th millennium]]. [[Word Bearers]] lore, which he does not dispute, holds that the name was not his. As a boy he was compared unfavourably with a pious and studious child of his village named Erebus; he strangled that child, copied his facial tattoos and left to enter the priesthood of the Covenant of Colchis in the capital, Vharadesh, under the dead boy's name.

The Covenant worshipped four Great Powers that were the [[Chaos|Chaos Gods]] under another guise. Erebus at first cared little for its teachings, but later recounted a moment of revelation before a cracked mirror, in which he saw his face divided into four and understood the gods had always guided him. When [[Lorgar]] overthrew the Covenant and preached the coming of a single god, Erebus went into hiding and then reappeared as a follower of the new faith. He was, by his own account, the only man on Colchis unmoved when the [[Emperor of Mankind]] arrived; he saw the being the gods called the Anathema and resolved to bring him down.

Great Crusade

Further information: [[Great Crusade]]

Erebus was among the first Colchisians made into [[Adeptus Astartes|Space Marines]] of the XVII Legion, then called the Imperial Heralds. He rose through the Legion's chaplaincy, a rank [[Lorgar]] created, and had passages of the Book of Lorgar tattooed across his scalp.

After the Emperor rebuked the Legion at [[Monarchia]] on Khur for worshipping him as a god, Erebus and [[Kor Phaeron]] told the humiliated Lorgar that there were other gods willing to accept worship. They guided him to the warp realm he later called the Eye, and in the decades before the Heresy the Word Bearers raised secret temples to the [[Chaos|Dark Gods]] on the worlds they conquered. Erebus was the Legion's agent in the wider Crusade, and his mission was to turn the [[Warmaster]].

The Interex and Davin

See also: [[Interex]] and [[Davin]]

Attached to the 63rd Expedition, Erebus cultivated the warrior lodges of the [[Sons of Horus|Luna Wolves]]. In contact with the [[Interex]], he stole the [[Kinebrach Anathame]], a daemon-bound blade, from the Hall of Devices on Xenobia, and laid evidence to make the theft appear the work of the Imperium. The resulting war ended Imperial hopes of peaceful union with the Interex.

He gave the blade to Eugen Temba, the corrupted commander on [[Davin]], so that [[Horus]] would be wounded by it in combat on Davin's moon. When the [[Apothecary|Apothecaries]] could not heal the Warmaster, Erebus persuaded the Mournival to take him to the Serpent Lodge on Davin. There Erebus guided Horus through visions, acting for the gods, which showed a future in which the Emperor was worshipped as a god while his sons were forgotten. Horus accepted the gods' offer and was healed.

Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

Erebus remained at [[Horus]]'s side as his counsellor. With the [[Warmaster]] he planned the virus bombing of [[Isstvan III]] to rid the traitor Legions of their loyal elements, and he made sure that [[Sons of Horus|Garviel Loken]], who suspected him, was sent to the surface. He was present when the conspiracy struck at the [[Drop Site Massacre]], and the [[Vengeful Spirit]] became, under his direction, a temple in which sacrifices were made to the warp.

Calth and the Ruinstorm

See also: [[Battle of Calth]] and [[Ruinstorm]]

Erebus accompanied [[Kor Phaeron]]'s fleet to [[Battle of Calth|Calth]], where the [[Word Bearers]] attacked the [[Ultramarines]]. While Kor Phaeron's host fought on the surface, Erebus completed a great sacrifice that tore open the warp across the region. [[Ruinstorm|The Ruinstorm]] that followed divided [[Milky Way|the galaxy]] for the rest of the war, cutting off [[Ultramar]] and the eastern Legions from [[Terra]].

The Shadow Crusade and Signus

See also: [[Shadow Crusade]] and [[Signus Campaign]]

During the [[Shadow Crusade]] against Ultramar, Erebus killed the Word Bearer [[Argel Tal]], commander of the possessed Gal Vorbak, with the [[Kinebrach Anathame|athame]]. He claimed to have foreseen that Argel Tal would prevent the World Eater [[Khârn the Betrayer|Khârn]] from becoming the chosen of [[Khorne]], and that the [[Traitor|traitors]] would lose the war if he did. Khârn, Argel Tal's friend, beat Erebus almost to death on the Conqueror, and Erebus escaped only by teleporting off the ship.

At Erebus's urging, Horus sent the [[Blood Angels]] to the [[Signus Campaign|Signus Cluster]], where the [[Daemon|daemons]] of the warp were to break [[Sanguinius]] and win him for [[Chaos]]. The plan failed. When Erebus blamed the failure on Horus, the Warmaster cut the tattooed skin from his face with his own athame.

Siege of Terra

Main article: [[Siege of Terra]]

Erebus came to Terra with the traitor host. In the final days of the Siege, when the Vengeful Spirit and Terra overlapped in the warp-born Inevitable City, his sorcery aided [[Abaddon the Despoiler|Ezekyle Abaddon]]'s Justaerin against the [[Adeptus Custodes|Custodes]] of [[Constantin Valdor]]. He attacked the group of the old soldier Ollanius Persson, killing most of them, and on another occasion halted Abaddon's warriors with a single word of the Enuncia, the primordial language of power.

After Horus's destruction Abaddon spared Erebus for the knowledge he held about the warp, though survivors of the Sons of Horus recorded that Abaddon meant to kill him once he was no longer useful. Before leaving the ship Erebus stabbed Garviel Loken in the back with his athame and killed him. He claimed that Loken would have betrayed him in turn, and explained that the killing was needed to create the daemon Samus and close a loop of fate begun years before on Isstvan. Other testimony holds him responsible for the deaths of Erda, the perpetual known to the Emperor, and of the Word Bearer Barthusa Narek.

After the Heresy

Erebus fled with his Legion into the [[Eye of Terror]]. He is a Dark Apostle and one of the most influential members of the Dark Council that rules the [[Daemon world|daemon world]] of [[Sicarus]] in the absence of [[Lorgar]], who has withdrawn into contemplation. His long rivalry with [[Kor Phaeron]] for control of the Legion continues. Kor Phaeron once organised the secret society called the Brotherhood to remove him; the Dark Apostle Marduk warned Erebus, and Kor Phaeron disowned the plot.

During [[Abaddon the Despoiler|Abaddon's]] Thirteenth [[Black Crusade]] in 999.M41, Erebus led a [[Word Bearers]] army across the [[Cadian Sector]]. He sacrificed millions of captives to summon [[Daemon|daemons]] against the Imperial defenders of the [[Fall of Cadia|Cadian Gate]]. His activities since the opening of the [[Great Rift]] are not well recorded.

Character

Erebus regards himself as the instrument of the gods' will in the material world, and his methods are deceit, patience and murder of allies as readily as enemies. Many [[Traitor|traitors]] who worked with him came to hate him, among them [[Horus]], [[Khârn the Betrayer|Khârn]] and [[Abaddon the Despoiler|Abaddon]]. He tends to explain his acts as steps in a pattern of fate that only he can see, and Imperial analysts note that many of his schemes failed or turned against him even as the larger plan succeeded.
