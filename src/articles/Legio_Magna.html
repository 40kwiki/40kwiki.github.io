The Legio Magna, whose name means "Flaming Skulls", is a [[Titan Legion]] of the [[Mechanicum]]. During the [[Horus Heresy]] it repudiated its oaths to the [[Emperor of Mankind]] and followed the [[Warmaster]] [[Horus]] into the service of the [[Ruinous Powers]]. Imperial records suspect that the Legio was founded before the [[Age of Imperium]], during the [[Age of Strife]].

The Legio's homeworld was [[Estaban VII]], in the [[Ultima Segmentum]]. Its Mechanicum forges were isolated and beset by political infighting, and for most of the Great Crusade the Legio was a minor force.

The Great Crusade

During the Great Crusade the Flaming Skulls campaigned alongside the [[Sons of Horus]] (the XVI Legion) and the [[Word Bearers]] (the XVII Legion). The Legio was first sent to minor warfronts. By the middle of the second standard century of the Great Crusade it had been deployed with twenty-one expeditionary fleets, in units ranging from two dozen Titans to a single machine. The losses this caused outstripped the ability of Estaban VII to replace them.

The Mechanicum of Estaban VII then converted the world to supply wargear for the Imperial Army. The Legio was ordered to serve alongside Army units, on the threat that its resupply would be cut off if its princeps refused. Within a decade more than half of the Legio was on more prestigious assignments, acting in the service of the primarchs [[Horus]] and [[Lorgar]] and their Legions. Its princeps, who once cared little for honour, came to revel in the praise they received. The Legio's tactics changed to favour spearhead assaults and the use of fusion weapons, and only the [[Legio Krytos]] was more renowned for bringing rebel civilisations to ruin.

The Heresy

After Horus became Warmaster, a demi-legio of the Flaming Skulls was sent to Mars. Because of their close ties with Horus, Lorgar and [[Kelbor-Hal]], the [[Fabricator-General]] of Mars, the Legio followed its allies into treachery. The first blow of the [[Schism of Mars]] was struck against [[Mattias Kefra]], a Magos whose forge lay in the Sinus Sabaeus region of Mars. Titans of the Legio marched from the southern Noachis region and within minutes had broken the gates of his forge city in Madler Crater. Imperial histories record that the engines, painted in red, orange, yellow and black and marked with flaming horned skulls, destroyed the forge's libraries and weapon shops. The Legio's Martian contingent earned a name as butchers during the purging of the Martian loyalists. Over the course of the Heresy most of the Legio's traitor Titan crews went insane.

Later campaigns

At Ice World Tralsak in 011.M31 the Legio fought beside the [[Legio Victorum]] against the loyalist [[Legio Atarus]] and [[Legio Agravides]], while the loyalist [[Shattered Legions]] and the traitor [[World Eaters]] fought across the ice floes. Neither side won outright, and both withdrew under the fog thrown up by the destruction. At the [[Defence of Ryza]] in 012.M31 the Legio joined the [[Legio Vulturum]], a decad of the [[Legio Mortis]] and the Knights of [[House Morbidia]] and [[House Oroborn]], some 140 Titans in all, against the forge world. The loyalists beat the attack back at the cost of nearly half their number. The Legio sent eight Titans to the [[Siege of Cthonia]] (013–014.M31). It also fought in the Beta-Garmon campaign (006–013.M31) with the support of the Knights of [[House Atrax]], and in the [[Siege of Terra]] in 014.M31.

After the Heresy

The part of the Legio that had not been sent to Mars stayed loyal to the Imperium after the Heresy. It fought for the Imperium in the [[Orask Wars]] in the 41st millennium. The traitor elements clashed with the [[White Scars]] on the world of Barac, at an unrecorded date in M38, and have not been encountered by the Imperium since. Imperial records do not say whether they survived. The loyalist elements took part in the [[Angevin Crusade]] (322–384.M39) into the [[Calyx Expanse]], and in 977.M41 stood with the [[Red Talons]] and the Orask defence forces against a Tyranid splinter fleet in the Orask Wars.
