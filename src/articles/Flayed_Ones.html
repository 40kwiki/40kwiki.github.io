Flayed Ones are mad and flesh-hungry [[Necron]]s, the result of an ancient affliction called the [[Flayer Curse]]. They are close combat troops who march ahead of the regular Necron armies and spread terror through the enemy ranks before the main force arrives. Imperial accounts describe them as Necrons that kept some of their original consciousness and are driven by a hunger for flesh that can never be satisfied.

Flayed Ones appear from a pocket dimension of their own kind to join Necron armies in battle. They do not come by the invitation of the Necron commanders, who are said to fear and hate them as insane versions of themselves. Their arrival is therefore treated by the [[Necron dynasties]] as an unwelcome but unavoidable part of war.

The Flayer Curse

The Flayed Ones were once Necrontyr who kept some of their original consciousness when they were transferred into living metal bodies of [[necrodermis]]. A disease then took hold of them, which the Necrons call the Flayer Curse. The nature of the curse is little understood, even by the wisest [[Cryptek]]s, but its results are unmistakable. A victim first loses its sanity. In the case of [[Necron Warriors]] the change may go unnoticed for a long time, since a single malfunctioning warrior among thousands is easily overlooked. The madness shows first as a drive to kill that can make a Necron ignore orders to cease fire. Unlike the [[Necron Destroyer]]s, who kill for the sake of killing, the Flayed Ones kill to sate a hunger for flesh and blood that a machine cannot satisfy.

Necron tradition holds that the curse was laid on them by the C'tan [[Llandu'gor the Flayer]]. When the Necrons turned on the C'tan in the last days of the [[War in Heaven (Necron)|War in Heaven]], the Flayer was utterly destroyed, not merely splintered into shards like his brothers, and in his dying moments he tainted the Necrons with an echo of the hunger of his own essence. For aeons the curse went unseen. By the time its madness showed, the afflicted had spread it to a thousand worlds and dozens of dynasties. The necrodermis of the victim warps: the fingers become knife-like claws, an extra joint often grows in the legs, and in the end the Flayed One is drawn by an unknown instinct into a pocket dimension of its own kind, to live among charnel palaces.

Other Necrons fear that the curse is contagious. A Necron suspected of carrying it is banished or destroyed. The Flayed Ones act outside the chain of command, and they are never invited to battle, but can materialise from their pocket dimension at any time. Overlords and [[Nemesor]]s rarely adapt their plans to the Flayed Ones and accept any advantage they bring, though an Overlord often orders the survivors executed once the battle is won. The Destroyers, themselves shunned, tolerate the Flayed Ones more readily than other Necrons.

Combat and appearance

Flayed Ones are stooped, thin and quick. They are capable melee fighters who use blades of necrodermis that extend from their fingers and can flay a man alive in seconds. They wear the flayed skin of their victims, and sometimes leave the skinned corpses behind them. Enemy soldiers lose their nerve when they see the remains of their comrades hanging from these machines as they approach. Some Flayed Ones carry [[Disruption field]] technology that can tear apart armoured vehicles.

Their infiltration is as important as their combat. They spread fear through the enemy ranks before the Necron battle line closes, and this effect is often more decisive than their physical strength.

Valgûl and the Times of Bounty

Imperial records describe a charnel kingdom where a large population of Flayed Ones fights over rotting meat and bone. The records name the Necron fleets that serve its ruler as the fleets of [[Drazak]]. The only sane figure recorded there is [[Valgûl]], the Fallen Lord, who rules from a throne of bone and skin. Valgûl seems untouched by the Flayer Curse, and Imperial scholars have never agreed on whether he is sane by choice or by a subtler form of madness.

Valgûl keeps his subjects loyal by providing the bounty they crave. Every few months he announces a "Time of Bounty", and the Necron fleets of Drazak raid nearby worlds for tithes of blood and gore. These raids are not for plunder in the usual sense.
