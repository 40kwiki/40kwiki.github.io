A Commissar is an officer of the Officio Prefectus, also called the Commissariat, who serves with the regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]] and the ships of the [[Imperial Navy]] to keep their morale, discipline and loyalty to the [[Emperor of Mankind]]. A Commissar stands outside the regiment's chain of command. He may take command of a unit whose officers have failed, and he has the power to execute summarily any soldier or officer, of any rank, for cowardice, desertion or heresy.

Commissars are trained in the [[Schola Progenium]], the institutions that raise the orphans of Imperial servants, and are chosen from its most zealous pupils. They serve first as Cadet Commissars and are then assigned to regiments, and the most distinguished rise to the rank of Lord Commissar, who advises a general or an army group. The Commissariat traces its foundation to the reform of the [[Imperial Army]] after the [[Horus Heresy]], when the [[High Lords of Terra|High Lords]] sought to prevent the treachery of whole armies that had marked the Heresy.

Commissars are feared by the soldiers they serve with, and often hated. Many are also respected and some are loved: Commissar Sebastian Yarrick of [[Armageddon]] and Commissar Ciaphas Cain, called the Hero of the Imperium, are among the most celebrated soldiers of the [[41st millennium]].

History

During the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30 – 000.M31) the armies of the Imperium were held together by the presence of the Emperor and the [[Primarch|primarchs]], and by the [[Imperial Truth]]. In the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31) much of the [[Imperial Army]] followed its commanders into treachery. When the Imperium was rebuilt, the Imperial Army was divided into the [[Astra Militarum|Imperial Guard]], now the Astra Militarum, and the [[Imperial Navy]], in about 021.M31, and the regiments were placed under separate commands so that no general could again lead armies and fleets together into rebellion. The Officio Prefectus was created in the same reforms to place in every regiment an officer whose only loyalty was to the Imperium.

Over the following millennia the Commissariat grew with the Astra Militarum and gained its own traditions. Its officers have served in every great war of the Imperium, from the [[Gothic War]] (139–160.M41) to the [[Sabbat Worlds Crusade]] (from 755.M41) and the [[Wars for Armageddon]] (941.M41 and 998.M41). In the [[Era Indomitus]] Commissars serve in the armies of the [[Indomitus Crusade]] and in the isolated regiments of the [[Imperium Nihilus]], where their duty to hold troops to their posts without hope of relief has become harder.

Duties and powers

  • Morale. The Commissar inspires the troops by speeches, example and the reading of the [[Imperial Infantryman's Uplifting Primer]], and fights in the front line.
  • Discipline. He punishes breaches of discipline and has the authority to execute any soldier, including the regiment's own officers, for cowardice or failure. The execution of a fleeing soldier before his comrades is the act for which Commissars are most widely known.
  • Loyalty. He watches for heresy, [[Mutant|mutation]] and sedition in the ranks and reports to the Officio Prefectus and, where necessary, to the [[Inquisition]] or the [[Imperial Cult|Ecclesiarchy]].
  • Command. Where a regiment's officers are killed or found wanting, the Commissar may take command.

A Commissar's authority extends to regimental commanders and in principle to generals, but not to the [[Adeptus Astartes]], over whom the Officio Prefectus has no power.

Insignia and wargear

Commissars wear a black greatcoat, a peaked cap bearing the [[aquila]] or the winged skull, and often a red sash. Their badge is the skull between spread wings. They carry a [[Bolter|bolt pistol]] and a power sword or chainsword, and many keep a personal standard. The uniform is the same in every regiment, which marks the Commissar as an outsider to the regiment's own traditions.

Reputation

The figure of the Commissar is one of the most familiar in the Imperium. Propaganda of the [[Departmento Munitorum]] presents him as the soldier's guardian and the embodiment of the Emperor's will, and regimental songs and votive pictures honour famous Commissars as heroes. Soldiers often see him differently, as the officer who stands behind the line with a drawn pistol. Both images are true. The Officio Prefectus teaches that a Commissar must be feared before he is loved, but its most successful officers have been those who won their regiments' trust by sharing their dangers. Some regiments, such as those of [[Cadia]] and the [[Death Korps of Krieg]], hold their Commissars in high regard; others have murdered them.

The relation between a Commissar and the regiment's own colonel is delicate. The Commissar may overrule or execute the colonel, but in most regiments the two work together, the colonel commanding and the Commissar enforcing.
