The Great Crusade, also called the Crusade of Reunion in older Mechanicus texts, was the war of reconquest that carried the [[Emperor of Mankind]] and his armies from [[Terra]] into the galaxy, beginning in about 798.M30 and lasting a little over two centuries. Its stated purpose was to find every human world that had survived the [[Age of Strife]], bring it under a single rule, and destroy those alien powers judged a danger to Mankind. Its instruments were the [[Space Marine Legions]] and their [[primarch]]s, the soldiers of the Imperial Army, the war-engines of the [[Titan Legions]] and the vast fleets of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicum]] of [[Mars]].

At its height the crusade fielded 4,287 primary Expeditionary Fleets and more than 60,000 secondary groups, with a further 372 fleets refitting in the Sol System and the hub worlds of the inner Imperium. Each fleet carried, besides soldiers, a corps of Iterators, whose task was to persuade conquered peoples of the [[Imperial Truth]], and a train of [[Remembrancer Order|Remembrancers]], artists and chroniclers charged with recording the war for posterity. In the last years of the crusade some 4.3 million Remembrancers were serving with the fleets.

The crusade is remembered in Imperial tradition as a golden age, the one period in which the Emperor ruled in person and Mankind believed itself to be marching towards a brighter future. Historians of the [[Logos Historica Verita]] are more guarded. The Legions' appetite for recruits, the haste with which [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] was cultured to feed them, and the growing distance between the primarchs and the Emperor planted flaws that [[Chaos]] later exploited. The crusade ended in the [[Horus Heresy]], when the [[Warmaster]] Horus turned on the Emperor in 005.M31.

Background

The Emperor's purpose

By the end of the [[Unification Wars]] the Emperor held [[Terra]], the [[Mechanicum]] of [[Mars]] had accepted the [[Treaty of Olympus]], and the first prototype Legionaries were in the field. The Emperor's aim was not conquest for its own sake. Imperial sources record his judgment that Mankind, divided and unprotected, would not survive the hostile [[Milky Way|galaxy]], the predations of the [[Warp]] or the avarice of other species, and that only a single human realm could withstand them. The crusade was to be the first stage of a longer plan, whose completion lay in the [[Webway|Imperial Webway]] project begun beneath the [[Imperial Palace]] and left unfinished by the Heresy.Older Imperial accounts say the [[Golden Throne]] was an ancient machine unearthed beneath the Palace. The accepted account, from the testimony of the Custodes and the Sigillite's archive, is that the Emperor and the Mechanicum built it for the Webway project, and that it was repurposed to keep him alive only after the Siege of Terra.

The [[Age of Strife]] had left human space in ruins. [[Warp storm|Warp storms]], which had isolated worlds for some five thousand years, were ebbing, and the Emperor chose this moment to move. [[Navigators]] and [[Astropathy|astropaths]] could again carry fleets and messages across the void, and the first reports of lost colonies began to reach Terra.

Preparations

The first fleets were built in the orbital foundries of Terra, in the great ring of shipyards around Mars and at the docks of [[Sol System|Saturn]], under [[Eye of Terror|the eye]] of the Mechanicum's forge-wrights. Their numbers were swelled when the ship-masters of the Saturnyne Dominion joined the Imperium after their alien overlords were overthrown, and again as new yards were established at Voss, Lorin, [[Cypra Mundi]] and elsewhere. The earliest Legions were raised from the people of Terra. Recruits were taken from the survivors of the Unification Wars, and from them the first companies of what the sources call the "Terran Legions" were formed.

Alongside the Legions the Emperor raised the [[Imperialis Auxilia|Imperial Army]], a force of ordinary soldiers drawn from the conquered peoples of Terra and, later, of every world brought into the Imperium. It was meant to garrison and support rather than to lead the assault. The oldest regiments, descended from the genetically engineered infantry of the Unification Wars, were later remembered as the "Old Hundred".

The [[Thunder Warriors]], the first gene-engineered soldiers of the Unification Wars, did not march. The accepted account, set down in the later testimony of the [[Adeptus Custodes]], is that they were culled on the Emperor's order once Terra was won, because their bodies and minds were failing and they could not live in peace. Older Imperial records, and the popular legend, say instead that the last of them died to a man at Mount Ararat. Chroniclers now treat the Ararat story as an official fiction. See [[Unification Wars]].

Organisation

The Expeditionary Fleets

At first there was a single fleet, the Principia Imperialis, commanded by the Emperor himself. As the crusade widened, the War Council on Terra authorised more. Each was given a number, and the number was stamped on every world it found. A planet first recorded as 63-19, for example, was the nineteenth world discovered by the 63rd Expedition, the fleet commanded by Horus. In this way the [[Administratum]] could trace a fleet's course from the order in which its worlds had been registered.

The composition of a fleet was never fixed. Regiments of the [[Imperialis Auxilia|Imperial Army]], auxiliaries, squadrons of the [[Imperial Navy|Armada Imperialis]], [[Titan Legions]] and detachments from the [[Space Marine Legions]] could be attached or withdrawn as strategy demanded. A typical fleet was led by a [[Lord Commander of the Imperium|Lord Commander]] drawn from the Army, with a Master of the Fleet commanding its ships. Where [[Adeptus Astartes|Space Marines]] were present, the senior Legionary assumed overall command, whatever the rank of the mortal officers beside him.

The great fleets that carried a [[Primarch|primarch]] and the main body of his Legion were rare. Most Expeditionary Fleets had no Space Marines at all and followed in the wake of the great thrusts, bringing worlds that offered little resistance into compliance. Records preserve a few of the better known. The 3rd Expedition, a force of the [[Imperial Fists]], took the void cities of the Consus Drift; the 4th and 7th, under the [[Dark Angels]], cleared Ork holdings on the edge of the Grail Abyss; the 28th was commanded by [[Fulgrim]]; and the 63rd, the most closely chronicled, was the host of [[Horus]] and the [[Sons of Horus|Luna Wolves]].

Rogue Traders Militant

See also: [[Rogue Trader]]

Ahead of the main fleets ranged smaller flotillas led by the [[Rogue Trader|Rogue Traders]] Militant. Many were former rulers of Terran or colonial realms, offered a choice between submission and death. Those who accepted took the Emperor's Warrant of Trade and went out to scout the dark beyond the frontier, accompanied by their own troops and by whatever the Emperor had ceded them. They could expect no relief if they met a stronger enemy, and they were forbidden to return to Terra. Many vanished unrecorded.

Legions and primarchs

The Legions were the spearhead. Each Legion was created from the [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] of one primarch, and each was organised into [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] of roughly a thousand warriors, subdivided into battalions and companies; the exact structure varied from Legion to Legion and with the fortunes of war. Until its primarch was found, a Legion was led by a commander appointed by the Emperor and fought in the Terran manner. On rediscovery, the primarch commonly remade it in his own image, changing its name, its colours, its tactics and often the world from which it drew recruits.

Compliance

Doctrine

Imperial doctrine described the process of bringing a world into the Imperium as compliance. A fleet that discovered a human world first opened contact and presented its terms: the world was to acknowledge the Emperor's rule, accept the Imperial Truth, pay an agreed tithe in soldiers and materiel, and allow an Imperial garrison. A world that complied peacefully was left much of its own government. A world that refused was warned, and then assaulted.

The Emperor is recorded as having preferred persuasion, and the [[Iterators]] in each fleet were charged with offering the benefits of union: security, technology and an end to the isolation of the [[Age of Strife]]. In practice many Legions had little patience for argument. Compliance by arms was usual wherever a local ruler would not yield, and campaigns against such rulers were remembered as some of the most savage of the crusade.

The Imperial Truth

Main article: [[Imperial Truth]]

The ideology of the crusade was the [[Imperial Truth]], a doctrine of rationalism and progress. It rejected all gods and spirits, treated religion as a relic of the Age of Strife, and presented the Emperor as a leader rather than a divinity. Conquered worlds were expected to close their temples and replace their priests with the Iterators' lectures. The Emperor repeatedly disclaimed godhood, and the Legions that revered him in secret were reprimanded. The suppression of faith made the crusade's moral claim inseparable from its military one, and it brought unrest to worlds where the old rites had been all that had held society together.

Pax Imperialis

Illumination was understood as the price of peace, and the price was often paid in blood. Alongside open war the Imperium used quieter tools. Agents of [[Malcador the Sigillite]], the assassin temples that later became the [[Officio Assassinorum]], informants and a handful of forbidden weapons held in the Legions' armouries were used against those who plotted treachery under the guise of loyalty. The Emperor judged that the Chaos powers would unite against him if humanity were not ready, and that time was short.

Xenos policy

See also: [[Xenos]]

The Emperor took the view, shared by most [[Primarch|primarchs]], that nearly every alien species Mankind had met had proved hostile, and that extermination was the usual remedy. Expeditionary Fleets were authorised to exterminate any [[Xenos|xenos]] power that threatened human settlement, and to seize its worlds for resettlement. The few exceptions are noted in the archives as curiosities: the [[Interex]], a human realm that had chosen to live alongside aliens, was in the end destroyed for that choice.

Rediscovery of the primarchs

The twenty [[Primarch|primarchs]] had been scattered across [[Milky Way|the galaxy]] as infants shortly after their creation. The Emperor's efforts to retrieve them occupied much of the crusade. They were found in widely different conditions, some as rulers, some as outcasts, and none as the Emperor had intended. When each was rediscovered he was given command of the Legion made from his [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]].

The order of discovery is not wholly clear, and Imperial sources disagree about several dates. Horus was the first. [[Rogal Dorn]] is recorded as the seventh. [[Alpharius Omegon|Alpharius]] was the last, and the circumstances of his return are contested. [[Space Marine Legions|The Legions]] of two primarchs, the II and the XI, are expunged from every record.

The effect of each discovery on the crusade was immediate. A primarch was both a general and a source of gene-seed: with his genetic material the Emperor could culture organs for far larger numbers of Legionaries than the Terran stock had allowed, and a Legion that had been starved of recruits could now expand rapidly. In the later decades of the crusade a new Legionary could be made and trained in as little as a year. This speed was a necessity, but it also meant that recruits were chosen with less care, and Legions that raised their warriors in haste accumulated flaws in their gene-seed and in their discipline.

Horus was the first of the Emperor's sons to return and the one who stood closest to him. The records of the 63rd Expedition show a bond of close friendship between them, and during the first decades of the crusade Horus often held the command of the Principia Imperialis when the Emperor was away searching for another son. It was widely understood, and not only by Horus, that this made him the first among the primarchs.

Course of the crusade

Early years, 798–850.M30

The first decades pushed outward from the [[Sol System]] in a series of short campaigns against isolated human worlds. Many had slid into barbarism, some had retained scraps of the technology of the Dark Age, and a few were ruled by cults whose rites disturbed the [[Iterators]]. [[Space Marine Legions|The Legions]] grew as [[Primarch|primarchs]] were found, and the first Expeditionary Fleets were launched under their command. The crusade now moved in three great thrusts across the galaxy: one under the Emperor, one under [[Horus]] and one under [[Ferrus Manus]].

The Rangdan Xenocides

Main article: [[Rangdan Xenocides]]

The most terrible war of the early crusade was fought against the [[Rangda]], a xenos power whose domain lay at the edge of the galaxy, among the Halo Stars. The three Rangdan Xenocides, fought between about 839.M30 and 890.M30, called for the intervention of the Emperor in person and for at least nine Legions, more than a dozen Titan Legions, houses of the Imperial Knights and hundreds of thousands of Army troops. The sources record the loss of over eighty thousand Legionaries in the war and of uncounted millions of mortal soldiers. The Third Xenocide, about 890.M30, cost the Imperium some fifty thousand Space Marines in preventing the destruction, it is said, of much of the northern Imperium.

Our knowledge of the war is poor. The Dark Angels, who bore the heaviest losses, ordered the records sealed or destroyed for the good of the Imperium, and what survives is fragmentary and contradictory.Imperial accounts differ even over what the Rangda were. A few blurred picts of fallen warriors and some Mechanicum fragments survive; the First Legion's own archives were withheld at the time and have not been opened since. The heavy losses sustained by the [[Dark Angels]] were a factor in the later growth of the Ultramarines, which by 899.M30 was nearing the position of the largest Legion.

Compliance campaigns

Between the great wars the Legions carried out a continuous series of compliance campaigns. The records list many. In the mid-800s the [[Iron Warriors]], [[Imperial Fists]], Dark Angels and [[White Scars]] jointly broke the fortresses of the Araaki Spiral. In 865.M30 the Imperial Fists under [[Rogal Dorn]] fought a vast Ork host at Rennimar, while the [[Death Guard]] and the [[Space Wolves]] besieged the Ork Over-Tyrant on a neighbouring front. In 903.M30 the [[Word Bearers]] brought Khur into compliance; their conduct there, including the raising of the great city of [[Monarchia]] in the Emperor's honour, was rebuked by the Emperor, an episode that deeply wounded [[Lorgar]].

Not every campaign was a conquest of an enemy. In 848.M30 the world of Sarosh, brought into the Imperium by the White Scars, was later found to be failing every benchmark of compliance under a self-serving bureaucracy, and the Imperium moved against it. At [[Davin]], in 941.M30, the [[Sons of Horus|Luna Wolves]] chose to leave the warrior tribes mostly intact after they had impressed Horus with their courage, a decision later to have fateful consequences.

The Interex and the Kinebrach Anathame

See also: [[Interex]]

The 63rd Expedition first encountered the [[Interex]], a prosperous human civilisation of about thirty systems, after the Luna Wolves cleared the prison world the Imperium called Murder. The Interex had chosen to live alongside aliens, and were allied with the simian Kinebrach, whom they regarded as junior partners. Wary of the Imperium's intentions, they suspected the crusaders of allegiance with Chaos.

While a delegation led by Horus negotiated with the Interex on the world of Xenobia, a Kinebrach blade known as the Anathame was stolen from the museum of the Hall of Devices. The Interex held the Imperials responsible, and fighting followed. In fact the blade had been taken by [[Erebus]], First [[Chaplain]] of the Word Bearers, who had already turned to Chaos and had been told that Horus could not be won without it. The Interex were destroyed by the Luna Wolves in what is called the First War of Xenobia (003.M31), their Kinebrach-trained sorcery and psychic weapons leaving even Legionaries dead within hours of contact.

Ullanor

Main article: [[Ullanor Crusade]]

The greatest triumph of the crusade was won on Ullanor, the seat of the largest Ork empire that Mankind had yet met. Its overlord, [[Urlakk Urg]], ruled many worlds from Ullanor Prime. The assault was commanded by Horus and drew on several Legions, among them the Luna Wolves, the White Scars and the Ultramarines, together with millions of [[Imperialis Auxilia|Imperial Army]] soldiers, a hundred Titans of the [[Legio Mortis]] and hundreds of ships of the [[Imperial Navy|Armada Imperialis]]. The Ork empire was broken and the survivors scattered, though many drifted into distant stellar regions where they long remained a source of trouble. The [[Alpha Legion]] followed the remnants into the Kayvas Belt in 002.M31.

The victory was celebrated in the Triumph of Ullanor, in which the Emperor declared it the greatest of the crusade. There, according to the Triumph rolls, he announced that he would no longer lead the crusade in person and would return to Terra to undertake a work of great importance. He did not tell the primarchs what it was.

Council of Nikaea

Main article: [[Council of Nikaea]]

In 001.M31 the Emperor summoned a conclave on Nikaea to settle a dispute over the use of psychic power. Libraries of evidence had been gathered. Expeditionary Fleets found, again and again, worlds in the grip of sorcerous cults, and the Legions had themselves begun to enlist [[Psyker|psykers]] as [[Librarian|Librarians]]. The primarch [[Magnus the Red]] spoke for the use of psychic gifts. [[Mortarion]], who knew from his own world the danger of [[Warp]] sorcery, and [[Leman Russ]], who thought it dishonourable, spoke against.

The Emperor's judgment, the Decree Absolute, sanctioned the [[Navis Nobilite|Navigators]] and [[Astropathy|astropaths]] under strict centralised control but forbade the use of psychic power as sorcery, which is to say bargaining with the entities of the Warp. The Librarius of the Legions was disbanded and its warriors returned to the line. The same council is also recorded as ordering each Legion to establish the office of Chaplain, on the pattern of the Word Bearers, to keep its warriors faithful to the Imperial Truth. Magnus, whose Legion was affected most, accepted the edict, but his [[Thousand Sons]] continued to study the Warp in secret.

The Warmaster

Main article: [[Warmaster]]

At [[Ullanor Crusade|Ullanor]] the Emperor named [[Horus]] Warmaster, a new rank that gave him supreme authority over the armed forces of the Imperium, Legions and Army alike, including the other [[Primarch|primarchs]]. The appointment confirmed Horus's standing as the first of the Emperor's sons and was greeted as a deserved honour by the Luna Wolves and by most of their brothers. Some primarchs resented it, among them [[Angron]], [[Konrad Curze]] and [[Perturabo]]. In 003.M31 the Luna Wolves were renamed the [[Sons of Horus]] at the Warmaster's own request.

The Emperor's withdrawal to Terra left the crusade in Horus's hands. In his place he established the Council of Terra, a body of senior officials that became the [[High Lords of Terra]] and the model of the later [[Administratum]], with [[Malcador the Sigillite]] as its first among equals. To many Legionaries the change meant that the Imperium they had won was being handed to Terran bureaucrats. A widespread, erroneous belief grew that the Legions would be disbanded or reduced to the status of peacekeepers once the crusade was done. With the Emperor out of sight, many Legionaries began to speak of loyalty to their primarch rather than to the Emperor.

Fractures

The faults in the crusade had begun long before [[Horus]]'s fall. The [[Night Lords]] and the [[World Eaters]] were notorious for the severity of their compliance actions. The [[Thousand Sons]]' study of knowledge gave way, under the influence of [[Tzeentch]], to the open use of sorcery. The [[Word Bearers]]' belief in the Emperor's divinity, which he rejected, led to the rift with [[Lorgar]]. Distances of years and the interval between orders and their execution allowed the Legions and their commanders to act with a great deal of independence.

The loss of the Emperor's presence meant there was no authority to hold the [[Primarch|primarchs]] together. The [[Imperialis Auxilia|Imperial Army]], essentially a force of ordinary soldiers, garrisoned the conquered worlds, leaving the Legions free to push on. Imperial scholars note that this arrangement, though efficient, bound the Army's loyalty to the individual Legions it served.

End of the crusade

The [[Warmaster]]'s undoing began on [[Luna|the moon]] of [[Davin]] in 004.M31, where he was struck by the [[Kinebrach Anathame]], stolen from the [[Interex]] and wielded by the Nurgle-tainted governor Eugen Temba. The blade's poison was beyond the power of his Legion's surgeons, and in desperation the captains of the [[Sons of Horus|Luna Wolves]] took him to the Serpent Lodge on Davin's moon, where its priests healed him by sorcery. During the ritual his mind was taken into the Warp, where [[Erebus]] showed him a vision of an Imperium in which the Emperor was worshipped as a god, and persuaded him that his father's work would end in ruin. Horus returned healed and corrupted.

The rebellion followed quickly. In 004.M31 the [[Thousand Sons]]' attempt to warn the Emperor by psychic means breached the wards of his secret work beneath the Palace, and the [[Space Wolves]] were sent against [[Burning of Prospero|Prospero]]. In 005.M31, on [[Isstvan III]], the Warmaster's forces carried out the atrocity that marked the formal beginning of the [[Horus Heresy]], and in 007.M31 the Ultramarines were attacked by the [[Word Bearers]] at [[Calth]]. The Imperium that had been built over two centuries was divided in a matter of months.

The war that followed lasted nine years and ended with the [[Siege of Terra]] in 014.M31. The Emperor was mortally wounded and placed on the [[Golden Throne]]; the surviving loyalist Legions were reorganised in the [[Great Scouring]] and the [[Second Founding]], and the crusade, in the sense of a steady advance, was never resumed.

Legacy

The Imperium of the [[41st millennium]] is a creature of the crusade. Its borders, its great [[Fortress world|fortress worlds]], the sector and [[Segmentum|segmentum]] divisions of the [[Administratum]] and much of its military tradition derive from the compliance campaigns of the era. The numbering of the Expeditionary Fleets survives in the names of many worlds. [[Space Marine Legions|The Legions]], though broken, are remembered in the [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] that descend from them.

The crusade also left a darker inheritance. [[Imperial Truth|The Imperial Truth]] was abandoned, and its absence gave rise to the [[Imperial Cult]]. The extermination of [[Xenos|xenos]] realms and the destruction of human societies that refused the Emperor's terms, whatever their justification at the time, set a precedent that later rulers have not hesitated to invoke. Estimates of the human lives lost or enslaved in the crusade vary by orders of magnitude, and the Administratum has never attempted a count.

The [[Era Indomitus]] has also reopened the crusade's records. The [[Lord Commander of the Imperium|Lord Commander]]'s archivists have corrected several earlier Imperial histories against the testimony of the [[Adeptus Custodes|Custodes]] and the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicum]], and [[Belisarius Cawl]]'s [[Ultima Founding]] of [[Primaris Space Marines|Primaris]] warriors is described in his own notes as the first mass creation of Astartes since the crusade itself.

In the Era Indomitus the Lord Commander [[Roboute Guilliman]] has called for a new crusade, the [[Indomitus Crusade]], and has compared it openly to the first.
