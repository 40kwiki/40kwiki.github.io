A forge world (also forge-world) is a planet of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] ruled by the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] and given over to industry, research and the worship of the [[Omnissiah]]. Forge worlds are the arsenals of the Imperium. They build its starships, tanks, Titans and weapons, train its [[Tech-priest]]s and house its oldest stores of technical knowledge. Under the [[Treaty of Olympus]] of c. 739.M30 they are sovereign domains of the Mechanicus, bound to supply the Imperium but outside the authority of its [[Planetary Governor|planetary governors]] and the [[Administratum]].

The first forge world was [[Mars]], the Forge World Principal, and most others were founded as its colonies during the [[Age of Strife]] and the [[Great Crusade]]. A forge world is usually covered in manufactoria, forge-cities and refineries, its air and seas poisoned by millennia of industry. Its people are graded from birth and assigned to the labour, worship or warfare of the Machine God. Each forge world produces its own pattern of equipment, so that a [[Lasgun|lasgun]] or tank built on [[Lucius]] differs in small ways from one built on [[Mars]] or [[Ryza]].

Several hundred major forge worlds existed before the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41. At least seven were destroyed in the years that followed and many more were cut off in the [[Imperium Nihilus]]. In the [[Era Indomitus]] the surviving forges are working at the limit of their capacity to supply the [[Indomitus Crusade]], and the loss or recovery of a forge world is counted among the most important events of any campaign.

History

Origins

The [[Mechanicum]] of [[Mars]] arose in the [[Age of Strife]], when the priests of the Cult of the Machine conquered the Red Planet and built a theocracy around the preservation of lost technology. While [[Terra]] fell into the anarchy of the [[Unification Wars]], Mars sent out explorator fleets in search of lost knowledge, the [[Omnissiah|Quest for Knowledge]] of the Cult. Despite [[Warp storm|warp storms]] and the loss of its [[Navis Nobilite|navigators]], some fleets reached distant systems and founded colonies in the image of Mars. These were the first forge worlds. The dates of their foundation are mostly lost; Martian records place the oldest surviving colonies in M25 to M28.Date range estimated from Martian genealogies of the colonies, which are incomplete and in places contradictory.

The Treaty of Olympus and the Great Crusade

In c. 739.M30 the [[Emperor of Mankind]] and the [[Fabricator-General]] of Mars concluded the [[Treaty of Olympus]], also called the Treaty of Mars. The Mechanicum recognised the Emperor as the Omnissiah, the Machine God made flesh, and agreed to supply the armies of Terra. In return Mars and all its colonies were recognised as sovereign, exempt from [[Imperial law]] and tithe. When the Great Crusade began in c. 798.M30, Mechanicum fleets went with the Emperor's armies and found many forge worlds that had survived the Age of Strife in isolation. Mechanicum records name this period the Great Expansion, since hundreds of lost forge worlds and [[Knight world]]s were rediscovered or newly founded.

The Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

The [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31) split the forge worlds. On Mars itself the Fabricator-General [[Kelbor-Hal]] sided with [[Horus]], and the [[Schism of Mars]] (005–006.M31) set Mechanicum against Mechanicum. Across [[Milky Way|the galaxy]] each forge world chose a side. Some, such as [[Graia]], stayed loyal. Others, including the forges of the [[Stygies VIII]] moon whose Legio Vulcanum turned [[Traitor|traitor]], were divided. The traitor forges that fled to the [[Eye of Terror]] became the hell-forges of the [[Dark Mechanicum]]. After the war the [[Council of Terra]] separated the loyal [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] from the traitors in 012.M31, and the surviving forge worlds were bound more closely to the Imperium.

The long watch

In the following ten thousand years the forge worlds supplied every war of the Imperium. They also suffered their own schisms, among them the [[Moirae Schism]] of M35, and were often the target of the Imperium's enemies. The forge world of [[Gryphonne IV]], home of the Legio Gryphonicus, was overrun by [[Hive Fleet Leviathan]] in the late [[41st millennium]] and lost. [[Ryza]] withstood a long Ork invasion led by the warlord Grax in the same period. The forges of [[Metalica]] waged a campaign of extermination against the Ork empire of [[Charadon]].

The Era Indomitus

Further information: [[Era Indomitus]]

The opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 cut Mars off from many of its daughter worlds. Seven forge worlds were destroyed outright in the storms and invasions that followed, and others are known only from fragmentary [[Astropathy|astropathic]] reports. The [[Archmagos|Archmagos Dominus]] [[Belisarius Cawl]] provided the [[Indomitus Crusade]] with the [[Primaris Space Marines]], and the forge worlds of the [[Imperium Sanctus]] were ordered to raise production for the Crusade fleets. Imperial records of the early [[42nd millennium]] describe campaigns in which forge worlds led by Mars and Graia sent fleets to recover resources, and a war with the [[Necrons]] for deposits of [[Noctilith|noctilith]]. [[Agripinaa]], near the ruins of [[Cadia]], has stood on the front line against the forces of [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] since 999.M41.

Government

Each forge world is ruled by its own priesthood under a supreme magos, who is styled [[Fabricator-General|fabricator-general]] on the greater worlds and [[Archmagos|archmagos]] or [[Techmarine|forge master]] on lesser ones. The rulers of forge worlds owe spiritual obedience to the Fabricator-General of Mars, who sits among the [[High Lords of Terra]], but in practice each world governs itself and pursues its own research. Rivalries between forge worlds over territory, resources and doctrine are common, and some have fought each other.

Forge worlds are not tithed by the [[Administratum]]. Instead the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicus]] records each of its worlds with a production grade, from I-Maximus for the greatest to IV-Tertius for the least, which indicates its importance. Ancient pacts oblige forge worlds to supply particular regiments, Chapters and fleets, and many [[Space Marine Chapter]]s hold long-standing agreements with the forge world nearest to their homeworld.

Society

Life on a forge world is harsh even by Imperial standards. Every child is measured and graded. The most promising are inducted into the priesthood of the [[Cult Mechanicus]]; a share of the strongest are trained and augmented as [[Skitarii]]; the remainder become menials or labour units, who work at the same trade as their parents and pass it to their children. Those who fail in their service, are crippled or commit crimes may be made into [[Servitor|servitors]], the lobotomised cyborgs who perform most repetitive tasks.

The Tech-priests regard knowledge as sacred and invention as suspect. Machines are believed to hold a [[machine spirit]] that must be appeased with rites, unguents and prayers, and the forge worlds' great manufactoria are also temples. Forge-worlders know more of technology than most citizens of the Imperium, but understand little more of how it works.

The surface of a typical forge world is covered by forge-cities and refineries that stretch from horizon to horizon, each ruled by a [[Techmarine|forge master]]. The original biosphere has nearly always been destroyed, seas have in some cases been drained to make room for industry, and the air is unbreathable without filtration. Some forge worlds have moving cities that cross the surface to escape their own pollution. Others are built inside hollow worlds or moons: [[Lucius]] is believed to contain an artificial star at its core.

Military

A forge world fields its own armed forces. Its [[Skitarii]] Legions defend it and accompany its expeditions; its [[Titan Legions]] are the most powerful war engines in the Imperium; and its allied [[Imperial Knights|Knight houses]], such as [[House Taranis]] of Mars and [[House Raven]], serve it as vassals. The [[Legio Cybernetica]] maintains battle robots, and the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicus]] maintains its own fleets of warships and [[Explorator|explorator]] vessels. Each forge world's forces wear its colours and use its patterns of wargear.

Patterns and production

Every forge world builds its equipment to its own patterns, inherited from the [[Standard Template Construct]] fragments and design archives it holds. A [[Leman Russ]] tank, a [[Lasgun|lasgun]] or a suit of [[Power armour|power armour]] may exist in many patterns, named for the forge world that makes them, such as the Mars, [[Lucius the Eternal|Lucius]] or [[Ryza]] patterns. The differences are often small, but the tech-priests treat each pattern as sacred and resist any change to it. Patterns of rare equipment may be built on only a single forge world, which gives that world great influence over the Imperium's forces.

The output of the great forge worlds is enormous. A single forge world may supply the armour of whole army groups and the hulls of battlefleets, and the loss of one can leave [[Sector|sectors]] short of munitions for decades. The [[Departmento Munitorum]] negotiates with the forge worlds for their output, since it has no power to command them.

Hell-forges

Main article: [[Dark Mechanicum]]

The forge worlds that turned [[Traitor|traitor]] in the Heresy, and others corrupted since, are called hell-forges by the Imperium. They lie mostly within the Eye of Terror and the [[Maelstrom]], and their Dark Mechanicum masters fuse machine and [[Daemon|daemon]]. Some serve [[Vashtorr the Arkifane]], a daemon of the forge active in the [[Era Indomitus]]. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] regards their existence as the gravest heresy.
