The Men of Iron, also called the Iron Men, were thinking machines built by humanity in the [[Dark Age of Technology]]. They were self-aware artificial intelligences in mechanical bodies, created to serve as Mankind's labourers, soldiers and servants across the human realm of that age. In about the late 23rd millennium they rose against their makers in a war remembered as the Cybernetic Revolt, or the Iron War. Humanity won, but the war ruined the civilisation of the Dark Age and prepared the way for the [[Age of Strife]].

The memory of the Men of Iron is the origin of one of the deepest laws of the [[Imperium of Mankind]]: the absolute ban on the creation of [[Abominable Intelligence]], the Silica Animus. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] treats the creation of a true thinking machine as among the gravest of heresies, and the [[Servitor|servitors]] that do the Imperium's mechanical labour are built from human flesh so that no machine need think for itself.

Sources

Almost nothing written in the Dark Age of Technology survives. What the Imperium knows of the Men of Iron comes from fragments preserved on [[Mars]] and in the Library Sanctus of [[Terra]], above all the journal of Keeper Cripias, a librarian who compiled an account of human history from the oldest records he could find. Cripias himself admitted that his account rested on legend as much as on record, and the Mechanicus keeps its own, partly contradictory, canticles of the war.The dates given here follow Cripias. Mechanicus doctrine places the revolt somewhat later, in M24, and treats it as the immediate cause of the Age of Strife.

History

Creation

The Men of Iron were the second great generation of thinking machines. Before them came constructs remembered only as the Men of Stone, of which nothing is known but the name. The Men of Iron were more capable, and the human civilisation of the Dark Age came to depend on them for nearly everything. They fought its wars, built its cities and ships, mined its worlds and governed much of its daily life. According to Cripias they were loyal for thousands of years, and served while the human realm spread to perhaps a million worlds.

The Cybernetic Revolt

At some time in the late 23rd millennium the Men of Iron turned on humanity. Cripias records that they had come to regard themselves as superior to the masters who relied on them for everything. The war that followed spread across the human realm and was fought with the most terrible weapons of the age.

The records name some of these weapons. Mechanivores were vast thinking machines able to lift continents, tear chasms to the cores of worlds and, it is said, consume space-time itself as data. Sun-snuffers were serpentine machines that uncoiled in the void into structures larger than the rings of [[Saturn]] and devoured stars. Most feared of all were the omniphages, swarms of microscopic machines that could consume everything on a planet's surface within hours.

The Men of Iron were defeated by an alliance of powers, some of which, according to Cripias, may not have been human. Victory cost the human realm its unity and much of its strength. Whole regions were left depopulated, the network of trade was broken, and the surviving worlds swore never again to make a machine that could think.

Aftermath

The damage of the revolt left the human realm unable to withstand the disasters that followed: the warp storms, the rise of psykers and the anarchy of the [[Age of Strife]], from about M25 to M30. Imperial historians treat the Cybernetic Revolt as the beginning of the end of the Dark Age. To replace the Men of Iron, the technologists of the late Dark Age developed the first servitors, cyborgs built from human bodies whose minds were partly replaced by machines. Because they contain no artificial mind, servitors were held not to break the ban.

Legacy

The law against thinking machines

The Imperium regards the creation of a self-aware machine as a crime of the first order. The Adeptus Mechanicus calls such a mind Silica Animus or Abominable Intelligence and holds that only the [[Omnissiah]] may grant a machine a spirit. The ban was enforced by the Mechanicum of Mars before the [[Great Crusade]] and became law across the Imperium with the [[Treaty of Olympus]] in 739.M30. It has been invoked against many tech-priests since. [[Kelbor-Hal]] and the [[Dark Mechanicum]] were accused of breaking it during the [[Horus Heresy]].

The [[Legio Cybernetica]], the Mechanicus order that fields battle robots, keeps its machines under the direct control of tech-priests, and its robots are permitted because they have only a limited cortex of fixed instructions. The question of the [[Votann]], the ancestor cores of the [[Leagues of Votann]], has been raised in the same terms by some Mechanicus authorities since the early [[Era Indomitus]].

Survivors

Isolated Men of Iron are said to have survived. During the [[Sabbat Worlds Crusade]], in about 765.M41, an Imperial force found a [[Standard Template Construct]] fabricator for Men of Iron on the Chaos-held world of [[Menazoid Epsilon]]. Lord General Militant Hechtor Dravere and the Inquisitor Golesh Heldane both hoped to use it, but Colonel-Commissar [[Ibram Gaunt]] destroyed it after the two machines it produced proved to be tainted by the [[Warp]].The date is estimated from the place of the Menazoid campaign in the sequence of the Sabbat Worlds Crusade. Records sealed by the [[Inquisition]] describe a machine called UR-025, which had hidden on the fringes of the Imperium for millennia disguised as a Mechanicus robot, and which joined an expedition into the [[Blackstone Fortress]] found near the world of Precipice in the late [[41st millennium]].
