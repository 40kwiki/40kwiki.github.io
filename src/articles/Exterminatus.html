Exterminatus, in [[High Gothic]], is the sanctioned destruction of all life on a planet by the forces of the [[Imperium of Mankind]]. It is the most extreme power the Imperium possesses and is used only when a world is judged to pose a threat to the Imperium that cannot be contained by any other means: when it has fallen to [[Chaos]] beyond recovery, when it is infested by [[Tyranids]] or [[Genestealer Cults|genestealers]], when a plague or mutation threatens to spread, or when the cost of retaking it would be greater than the Imperium can bear.

The order may be given by a full [[Inquisitor]] on his own authority, by the [[High Lords of Terra]], and by certain senior commanders of the Imperial armed forces, including some Chapter Masters of the Adeptus Astartes. Those who order it are expected to answer for the decision. An Inquisitor who is judged by his peers to have ordered an unnecessary Exterminatus may himself be declared a heretic.

The act has been carried out many times in the Imperium's history, and the [[Administratum]] keeps records of the worlds destroyed. It was also used as a weapon of treason at the start of the [[Horus Heresy]], when [[Horus]] ordered the virus-bombing of [[Isstvan III]] in 005.M31 to destroy the loyalists of four Legions.

Methods

Most methods are delivered by warships from orbit. The choice depends on the nature of the world, the threat and the weapons available.

Virus bombs

Virus bombs release the life-eater virus, an engineered pathogen that destroys all organic matter it touches, reducing plants and animals alike to a flammable sludge. The gases released by the decay are then ignited, usually by a lance strike, and a firestorm sweeps the planet's surface. A virus-bombed world is left barren but its crust intact. Those sheltered deep underground may survive, as part of the population of Tallarn did when the [[Iron Warriors]] virus-bombed it in 010.M31.

Cyclonic torpedoes

Cyclonic torpedoes are weapons of planetary destruction launched from orbit. They ignite the atmosphere or unleash plasma fire on a scale that burns away the air, boils the oceans and may crack the crust and destabilise the core. A more exotic two-stage weapon bores into the planet with a melta charge before detonating at its core; it is used against airless worlds such as [[Tomb world|Necron tomb worlds]].

Other methods

Simpler means include sustained orbital bombardment with nuclear or conventional weapons. During the [[Macharian Crusade]] (392–399.M41) Lord Solar [[Macharius]] had the tech-priests move the moon of the world of [[Loki]] into a new orbit with plasma drives so that its fall would devastate the planet. In 999.M41, during the [[Fall of Cadia]], the Lord Castellan [[Ursarkar E. Creed]] ordered a generatorium on the prison world of [[St. Josmane's Hope]] to be overloaded when it was about to fall, destroying the planet.

Authority and procedure

Exterminatus is never undertaken lightly, though Imperial doctrine accepts it as a necessary sacrifice. An Inquisitor has the absolute right to order it, and his authority is usually enough to command the warships of any Imperial fleet. Senior commanders of the Navy, the Astra Militarum and the Adeptus Astartes may also order it in war. In practice the order is often accompanied by evacuation of valuable persons, relics and archives, if time allows.

The Inquisition reviews each instance. Some Inquisitors have been censured or condemned for orders judged excessive. The Inquisitor Kryptman ordered the destruction of a ring of worlds in the path of [[Hive Fleet Leviathan]] in the late 41st millennium, so that the Tyranids would find nothing to consume. The measure, known as Kryptman's Cordon, was condemned by his peers, though its supporters held that it saved many more worlds than it destroyed.

Consequences

A world that has suffered Exterminatus is reclassified by the [[Administratum]], usually as a [[Dead world]], and removed from the tithe rolls. Some are recolonised after centuries. Tallarn, virus-bombed in 010.M31, became a desert world whose people later raised the [[Tallarn Desert Raiders]]. Others remain barren and are used as fortresses or bases.

Imperial thinkers have debated the act. Some writers of the Ecclesiarchy hold that the destruction of a world without trial of its people is a sin; the Inquisition answers that it is the lesser evil when the alternative is the loss of a sector. In the [[Era Indomitus]] the scale of the wars against Chaos and the Tyranids has led Imperial commanders to order Exterminatus more often, and the records of the [[Indomitus Crusade]] list many worlds destroyed to deny them to the enemy.
