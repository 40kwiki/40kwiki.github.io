Exterminatus, in [[High Gothic]] the extermination of an entire world, is the sanctioned destruction of all life on a planet by the forces of the [[Imperium of Mankind]]. It is the most extreme power the Imperium possesses. The order is given only when a world is judged to pose a threat to the Imperium that no lesser means can contain: when it has fallen to [[Chaos]] beyond recovery, when it is infested by [[Tyranids]] or by a [[Genestealer Cults|genestealer cult]], when a plague or [[Mutant|mutation]] threatens to spread, or when the price of holding or retaking it would be greater than the Imperium can bear. Imperial scholars treat it as a last resort and describe it in the same terms as the amputation of a limb to save a body.

The authority to order Exterminatus belongs to the [[High Lords of Terra]], to any full [[Inquisitor]] on his own judgement, and to certain senior commanders of the [[Astra Militarum]], the [[Imperial Navy]] and the [[Adeptus Astartes]], among them some [[Chapter Master|Chapter Masters]]. Those who order it are expected to answer for the decision. An Inquisitor whose peers judge that he ordered an unnecessary Exterminatus may himself be declared a [[Heretic|heretic]] and hunted down.

The weapons used vary with the world. Most are delivered from orbit by warships: sustained bombardment, [[Virus bomb|virus bombs]] that consume every living thing and leave the planet's crust intact, and cyclonic torpedoes that burn away the atmosphere and may crack the planet itself. Other worlds have been destroyed by more improvised means, such as the dropping of a moon on [[Loki Sector|Loki]] during the [[Macharian Crusade]] or the overloading of a reactor on [[St. Josmane's Hope]] in 999.M41. The act was also used as a weapon of treason in the [[Horus Heresy]], when the Warmaster [[Horus]] ordered the virus-bombing of [[Isstvan III]] in 005.M31, and again when the [[Iron Warriors]] virus-bombed [[Battle of Tallarn|Tallarn]] in 010.M31.

A world that has suffered Exterminatus is reclassified by the [[Administratum]], usually as a [[Dead world]], and struck from the tithe rolls. The Imperium has carried out the act many times, and the Administratum keeps records of the worlds destroyed. In the [[Era Indomitus]], when the [[Great Rift]] has multiplied the dangers that a single sector must face, Exterminatus remains the ultimate answer available to an Imperial commander, and the debate over its use has not been settled.

Doctrine and authority

The justification

Imperial doctrine accepts that the cost of Exterminatus may be billions of lives, and holds that it is justified when the alternative is the loss of a sector or the spread of a corruption that could not be stopped. A saying attributed to the Exterminatus Extremis tells those who must give the order that they have no right to let such a world live, and that those who understand this will bear the weight of the choice. The [[Ordo Hereticus]] teaches the same in its doctrinal manuals, which hold that there is no place for hesitation and that no sacrifice is too great.

In practice Exterminatus is very rarely used against worlds of great value to the Imperium. A [[Forge world]] or a prosperous [[Hive world]] is more likely to be retaken at any cost, and the Administratum counts the tithe lost when a populous world is destroyed. The order is therefore usually given against worlds that have little strategic worth, or against worlds so far gone that nothing can be saved from them.

Who may order it

The [[Inquisitor]] has the absolute right to order Exterminatus at any time, and his authority is usually enough to command the warships of any Imperial fleet within reach. The [[Inquisition]] is the only body within the Imperium known to hold stocks of cyclonic torpedoes for this purpose, along with the Adeptus Astartes, and a number of Chapter Masters have carried out the act on their own authority. Senior commanders of the Navy and the Astra Militarum may also give the order in time of war, and the High Lords of Terra may command it for any world in the Imperium.

The decision is never without risk to the one who takes it. An Inquisitor who orders Exterminatus will have his course examined by his fellow Inquisitors, and if they find the act unnecessary he may be condemned and pursued by other members of the Inquisition. The reviews are held in secret, and many of their verdicts have never been published.

Evacuation and preparation

Where time allows, the order is accompanied by an evacuation of what the Imperium values most: rulers and officials, relics and archives, and sometimes selected workers. The evacuation is brief and ruthless, and no more people are saved than the available ships can carry. Fleets assigned to an Exterminatus usually arrive with the means to prevent escape, since a single ship that reaches the warp may carry the contamination that the order was meant to end.

Methods

Main articles: [[Virus bomb]], [[Cyclonic torpedo]]

All of the following, with the exceptions listed under other methods, are delivered by warships from orbit. The choice depends on the nature of the world, the nature of the threat and the weapons available to the fleet.

Orbital bombardment

Bombardment by the guns of an Imperial fleet is the simplest means of Exterminatus, since it needs nothing beyond the weapons already on board. Its force can range from small precision strikes to the release of full thermonuclear weapons. Fleets of the Navy and the Adeptus Astartes have used nuclear weapons in the megaton range to scour worlds of life and leave them radioactive husks on which nothing can live. Where a more thorough result is wanted, specialised munitions such as cyclonic torpedoes are used.

Virus bombs

Virus bombs release the life-eater virus, a pathogen engineered to spread at great speed and to destroy every organic cell it touches. Plants and animals alike are reduced to a sludge of biochemicals and gases, both highly flammable. The protocols of Exterminatus direct that a [[Starship weapons|lance]] strike or an incendiary device be used to ignite these products, so that a firestorm sweeps the planet and destroys whatever life remains. Only those who have retreated into deep and sealed shelters can survive. The speed of the virus is notorious: on [[Isstvan III]] in 005.M31 it destroyed a population of some sixteen billion in a matter of minutes, leaving a barren world that could just be inhabited. The Space Marines on its surface had to build airtight shelters in haste, and the loss among them was still very heavy.

The population of [[Battle of Tallarn|Tallarn]] survived the Iron Warriors' virus bombardment of 010.M31 only because it had built vast underground shelters, but its once green world was changed for ever into a desert.

Cyclonic torpedoes

A cyclonic torpedo is a weapon of planetary destruction, launched from a ship into low orbit. The class includes many variants, among them the atmospheric incinerator torpedo, but all can destroy the whole biosphere of the target. Some ignite all the free oxygen in the air in a thermonuclear reaction, and others deliver a bombardment of plasma boosted by nuclear fusion. The result is always the same: the atmosphere is burned away, the oceans boil to vapour, and the bare rock of a dead world remains. The blast can be seen from space, and is often strong enough to crack the crust and destabilise the core.

A more exotic class, the two-stage cyclonic torpedo, is made for worlds with no atmosphere or life, such as the tomb worlds of the [[Necrons]]. The first stage is an unusually powerful melta charge that bores through the crust and the mantle to the core, and the second stage is a modified plasma charge that destabilises it, so that in most cases the planet is destroyed from within.

Other methods

During the [[Macharian Crusade]] (c. 392–399.M41), [[Macharius]] ordered the destruction of the world of [[Loki Sector|Loki]]. His tech-priests set huge plasma drives on the surface of Loki's moon and moved it into a new orbit. The moon struck the planet and did not destroy it outright. It caused great earthquakes and waves of lava, and the only survivors were the heretics who sheltered in the largest fortresses.

During the [[Thirteenth Black Crusade]] (999.M41), the Lord Castellan [[Ursarkar Creed|Ursarkar E. Creed]], commander of the whole [[Cadian Sector]], ordered the generatorium on the prison world of [[St. Josmane's Hope]] to be overloaded when it seemed that the forces of Chaos would take it. The reactor did not melt down. It exploded with such force that the planet was blown apart.

Kryptman's Cordon

In the late 41st millennium the Inquisitor Kryptman of the [[Ordo Xenos]] ordered the destruction of a ring of worlds in the path of [[Hive Fleet Leviathan]], so that the [[Tyranids]] would find nothing to consume. The measure, known as Kryptman's Cordon, was condemned by many of his peers, and he was expelled from the Inquisition. Its supporters held that it saved many more worlds than it destroyed.

Consequences

A world that has suffered Exterminatus is reclassified by the [[Administratum]] and removed from the tithe rolls, usually as a [[Dead world]]. Some are recolonised after centuries. [[Battle of Tallarn|Tallarn]] became a desert world whose people later raised the [[Tallarn Desert Raiders]], some of the finest armoured regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]]. Others stay barren, and are used as fortresses or bases by the Imperium or its enemies.

Imperial thinkers have debated the act. Some writers of the [[Imperial Cult|Ecclesiarchy]] hold that the destruction of a world without a trial of its people is a sin. The Inquisition answers that it is the lesser evil when the alternative is the loss of a sector. The debate is sharpest when the world destroyed was loyal, as in the cordon of Kryptman. Since the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 many Imperial commanders have had to weigh the same question under the threat of daemonic invasion, and the denial of worlds to the enemy has been a part of the Imperium's defence ever since.

Exterminatus also bears on the way the Imperium treats other threatened worlds. The Inquisition will move swiftly and with great severity against any world where the forces of Chaos have gained a hold, so that it does not become a [[Daemon world]]. A daemon world cannot easily be destroyed, because the reality-shifting effects around it prevent the Imperium from trying.
