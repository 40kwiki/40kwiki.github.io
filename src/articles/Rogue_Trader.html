A Rogue Trader is the holder of a Warrant of Trade, a charter that frees its bearer from most of the laws of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] and licenses him to travel beyond its borders: to explore, to trade with whomever he finds, including [[xenos]], to claim new worlds for the Emperor and to make war on those who resist. Rogue Traders command their own fleets of starships and armies, and are among the most powerful and least accountable individuals in the Imperium.

The first Rogue Traders, called the Rogue Traders Militant, served in the [[Great Crusade]], ranging ahead of its fleets in independent flotillas. Warrants are hereditary and pass from one generation of a Rogue Trader dynasty to the next; some of the most ancient were signed by the Emperor Himself, and are the most valued. Their authority is held to come from the Emperor and so cannot be appealed.

The Imperium's frontiers, such as the Koronus Expanse beyond the [[Calixis Sector]], have been explored and settled largely by Rogue Traders. Their freedoms make them both useful and suspect, and the [[Inquisition]] keeps close watch on dynasties suspected of heresy or of alien sympathies.

History

The Great Crusade

Further information: [[Great Crusade]]

The [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31) pushed outward from [[Terra]] in great expeditionary fleets, and ahead of each ranged smaller independent flotillas led by the Rogue Traders Militant. Many were former rulers whose realms the Emperor had cast down in the Unification Wars or among the lost colonies, and they were offered a stark choice: swear service to the Crusade, or die by His hand. Those who chose service took up the Emperor's Warrant of Trade and were expected to scout ahead of the Crusade with their own armies, with little hope of aid. They were forbidden to return to Terra, and many vanished, slain or enslaved by xenos far from the light of the Throneworld. Their fleets became ramshackle, built of captured ships, some of them xenos vessels of strange form.

Later ages

In later ages Warrants are granted in the name of the [[High Lords of Terra]] through the [[Adeptus Terra]], and the grant is an occasion of great pomp, for it founds a new noble line. Warrants are given in reward for service, but also for political ends: powerful lords of the Adeptus Terra have been known to offer one to a rival as a bribe, to send him far from Terra. More recent Letters of Marque are similar but more restricted, and are not hereditary.

The Rogue Trader Solomon Haarlock was the first to chart the Calyx Expanse, in c. 760.M36; it became the [[Calixis Sector]] after the [[Angevin Crusade]] of the 39th millennium. In 997.M40 the Rogue Trader Purity Lathimon discovered and plotted a safe passage, which she named the Maw, from the Calixis Sector into the [[Koronus Expanse]], and the dynasties that followed her have been the principal powers of that frontier.

Era Indomitus

After the opening of the [[Great Rift]], nearly one hundred Rogue Traders were summoned to Macragge for an emergency symposium by [[Roboute Guilliman]]. He asked them to act as pioneers, to find new worlds for mankind to settle in the unexplored reaches of the Eastern Fringe and the edge of the Ultima Segmentum, using the new Warp routes the storms had opened, and he offered them the chance to withdraw without dishonour. None did, and within solar days their fleets left [[Ultramar]], many carrying Ultramarine colonists in cryogenic sleep.

The Warrant of Trade

The Warrant is an ancient legal document, a letter of appointment rather than a merchant charter, that sets the limits of the operations of its holder and his descendants across the galaxy under Imperial law. It raises its bearer to a status held to equal that of a Space Marine Chapter Master, an Inquisitor or a planetary governor. It entitles him to travel freely within the Imperium and beyond, and to deal with xenos and non-Imperial human worlds that ordinary citizens are forbidden to approach, as he sees fit, provided it serves the Imperium.

Every Warrant carries conditions, usually of military service or intervention: a rebellious world to be visited, a world to be settled, or an expedition to be made beyond the frontier, sometimes with an Administratum prefect or an Inquisitorial agent aboard to see the terms are kept. A Warrant may be revoked and forfeited when its holder's interests come into conflict with the Imperium's. It does not protect a Rogue Trader guilty of heresy or treason from the [[Inquisition]].

Dynasties and households

A Rogue Trader needs a considerable force of voidcraft, troops and staff: often dozens of ships, huge lumbering cargo vessels crammed with a small army, a full crew of technicians and volunteer settlers to found colonies, with fighting troops above all to deal with any threat. Many Rogue Traders come from positions of power within the Imperium, from the Adeptus Terra, the [[Imperial Navy]], the [[Astra Militarum]] or even the [[Inquisition]], and a few are influential civilians, of whom the [[Navigator]]s are the most famous. Some are poor, with a single ancient and dilapidated ship, while others hold whole fleets and private armies.

Rogue Traders vary widely. Some are pious, bringing the Emperor's light beyond the edge of His rule; others are little better than legitimised pirates, and the most infamous of them, Jan van Yastobaal, plundered whatever world he found. Many gather an entourage that may include alien warriors and mutants, which the puritans of the [[Inquisition]] are quick to call heresy. Certain favoured individuals may call on detachments of the [[Adeptus Astartes]] through pacts with individual Chapters, and some Rogue Traders hire out their ships to pirates, to other Rogue Traders, or to the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]], the Astra Militarum or the Inquisition.

Relations with other powers

Rogue Traders wield great political, economic and military power beyond the Imperium's frontiers, but once back within its borders they do not have free rein. They usually return to Imperial space every few years to unload their wares and resupply, and may then come into conflict with the [[Adeptus Terra]] or the Inquisition over their latest expedition. Having wandered among alien stars, they are held to be open to every heresy, from wayward philosophy to alien infection or possession, and many dispute the Imperium's right to rule them. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] sometimes sends [[Explorator|Explorator]] fleets with them as a condition of their Warrants, in search of pre-Imperial human artefacts.
