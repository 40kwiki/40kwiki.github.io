A servo-skull is a small drone made from a human skull fitted with an anti-gravitic impeller, sensors and tools, which floats through the air on the business of its owner. Servo-skulls are among the most familiar sights of the [[Imperium of Mankind]]: they take dictation in the offices of the [[Administratum]], carry lights and incense in the cathedrals of the [[Ecclesiarchy]], scan the decks of warships and attend [[Inquisitor|Inquisitors]] as spies and familiars.

The skulls used are those of loyal servants of the Imperium. To be chosen as a servo-skull after death is held to be an honour, a sign that one's service in life was good enough to be continued beyond it. The [[Imperial Cult]] teaches that something of the servant's devotion remains in the skull, and many who work with servo-skulls believe that they have something of the character of their former owners.

History

No record states when the first servo-skull was made. Mechanicus texts describe the practice as ancient, and accounts of the [[Great Crusade]] (798.M30 – 000.M31) mention servo-skulls in the service of remembrancers and fleet officers.The origin of the practice is estimated from the oldest surviving mentions. Some tech-priests trace it to the [[Age of Strife]], when the Mechanicum of Mars began to reuse the remains of its own adepts. In the [[36th millennium]], during the [[Reign of Blood]], the visits of [[Goge Vandire]] were recorded by hundreds of servo-skulls and the images shown on every world that could receive them. In the 41st and 42nd millennia, servo-skulls are found throughout the Imperium.

Manufacture

A servo-skull is built by the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] or by licensed tech-adepts. The skull is cleaned and consecrated, and a small anti-gravitic generator is fitted inside it or beneath it so that it can float and move. To this are added whatever tools its purpose needs: an optical lens or auspex in an eye socket, a vox-caster, a lamp, a quill or data-probe, a manipulator arm or a medical injector. A simple logic engine allows it to carry out a set of tasks. Higher-ranking magi and officials own cogitators that bind a servo-skull to a single master.

Servo-skulls are common in the great offices and shrines of the Imperium but are costly to make, and on most worlds they are owned only by the nobility, the clergy, military and naval officers and agents of the Adepta. Antique servo-skulls are sometimes sold in the underhives of worlds such as [[Necromunda]] by traders unwilling to say where they came from.

Uses

Administration and record

Scribe servo-skulls record conversations, confessions and proceedings, and are used in courts, interrogations and the offices of the Adeptus Terra. Lumen-skulls carry lights, and vox-skulls relay messages and sermons.

Religion

In the shrines of the Ecclesiarchy, servo-skulls carry censers, candles and holy texts, and broadcast hymns. Pilgrims regard skulls from great cathedrals as relics.

War

Military servo-skulls scout ahead of troops, relay targeting data and map fortifications. The [[Adeptus Astartes]] use them to survey battlefields, and [[Apothecary|Apothecaries]] and [[Techmarine|Techmarines]] take them as assistants. Gun-skulls, also called guardian-skulls, carry a single small weapon and guard their owners. Planetary enforcers on poorer worlds use cruder servo-hunters, often built from the skulls of hunting hounds.

Inquisition

Main article: [[Inquisition]]

[[Inquisitor|Inquisitors]] keep servo-skulls as familiars, often linked to their masters by psychic or cybernetic means so that the Inquisitor sees and hears what the skull does. They are used for spying, for keeping records of interrogations and as guards.

Belief and custom

The making of a servo-skull is a religious act as well as a technical one. The skull is blessed by a priest of the [[Imperial Cult]] or a tech-priest before it is fitted, and its owner is expected to keep it clean and to observe the small rites due to its [[Machine spirit|machine spirit]]. Many adepts of the Administratum leave instructions in their wills that their skulls be offered for the work, and some departments keep registers of the servants whose skulls they hold, so that the dead may be remembered by name.

Imperial custom treats a servo-skull with a certain respect. To strike or deface one is an insult to the dead, and on some worlds a crime. Soldiers and sailors who serve alongside servo-skulls often give them names and credit them with moods and preferences. The Mechanicus neither confirms nor denies that anything of the dead remains in the skull, but it accepts that a skull that has served long has a machine spirit of its own.

Servo-skulls have been in continuous use throughout the history of the Imperium, from the remembrancers of the Great Crusade in the late 30th millennium to the fleets of the [[Indomitus Crusade]] in the early 42nd, where they are used in great numbers to survey the worlds reclaimed from the [[Imperium Nihilus]].
