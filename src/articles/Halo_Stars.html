The Halo Stars are an ancient formation of stars that encircles the outer edge of the [[galaxy]]. They are the last stellar clusters met before the traveller enters the cold [[intergalactic void]]. Imperial astronomers hold that they are among the oldest stars in the galaxy. Their ring extends for some 200,000 light years and is thought to be partly wrapped in the dark matter that spreads beyond the visible disk. The Halo Stars are also called the Halo Zone in some Navy registers.

The Halo Stars have a dark reputation among Imperial starfarers. Many [[Rogue Traders]] and [[Explorator]]s of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] believe that those who go out into them do not return. Their fears are not wholly unfounded: [[Pirates]], [[Warp]] entities and new species of hostile [[xenos]] are reported in the outer dark. Imperial charts record few habitable worlds, and it is not known what worlds, if any, circle the oldest stars.

History

Early charts

The Halo Stars were first recorded in the late [[30th millennium]], when the [[Great Crusade]] began to chart [[Milky Way|the galaxy]] beyond the settled [[Sector|sectors]]. Their position at the rim was noted on the earliest navigational charts, but no fleet of the Crusade was sent to them. Imperial scholars treat that absence as a sign that the Crusade's commanders judged the region to be of no use.The date of the first chart is estimated from the order of the Crusade's survey expeditions. No single record gives it.

The Calixis frontier

The [[Calixis Sector]] was created in the [[39th millennium]] after the [[Angevin Crusade]], and it lies within the [[Segmentum Obscurus]] next to the Halo Stars. The sector's [[Rogue Trader]] houses and Inquisitorial conclaves have since been the Imperium's chief contact with the Halo Stars. Their expeditions have produced most of the surviving records of the region.

Koronus Expanse

Main article: [[Koronus Expanse]]

The most important part of the Halo Stars is the [[Koronus Expanse]], a region adjacent to the Calixis Sector. Exploration of the Expanse began in earnest in the 41st millennium, led by the fleets of Rogue Traders who were guided by old [[Explorator]] star-charts recovered from earlier expeditions. Access to the Expanse is through the [[Koronus Passage]], which is closed beyond the way station of Port Wander to all but licensed vessels.

Ynnari and Necrons

The [[Aeldari]] [[Ynnari]], a cult of the dead Aeldari god Ynnead that works to resurrect him and to defeat [[Slaanesh]], are known to operate in the outer dark, and Imperial reports place their raiding craft at the edges of the Halo Stars. The [[Necrons]] are also reported at the rim of the region. The Imperium has not confirmed how many of their tomb worlds lie within the Halo Stars, and the Administratum records no survey that counts them.The Ynnari and Necron reports are drawn from Imperial scouting accounts of the 41st millennium and have not been verified by a full survey.

Geography

The Halo Stars form a broad ring, with the [[Calixis Sector]] and its neighbouring [[Sector|sectors]] on the galactic inside and the outer dark beyond. Their stars are old and dim, and many of their systems are wrapped in dark matter. The light of the [[Astronomican]] does not reach most of the ring, so Imperial [[Navis Nobilite|navigators]] must travel there by dead reckoning. The region's borders are the least-known part of the Imperial chart.

Notable worlds

  • '''[[Port Wander]]''': a way station at the approach to the Koronus Expanse, where licences for passage through the [[Koronus Passage]] are issued.
  • '''[[Koronus Expanse]]''': the explored core of the Halo Stars, with its scattered human worlds and dangerous [[Warp travel|warp routes]].

Governance

No Imperial authority governs the Halo Stars as a whole. The [[Administratum]] and the Navy claim the region through the [[Calixis Sector]], and [[Rogue Trader|Rogue Trader dynasties]] hold charters to explore and trade in parts of the Expanse. Inquisitorial conclaves in the Calixis Sector maintain watch on the traffic that enters the region.
