The Imperial Navy, or Navis Imperialis, is the void fleet of the [[Imperium of Mankind]]. Its ships patrol the Warp routes, escort the convoys that carry the tithe, carry the regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]] to their wars and give them orbital cover when they arrive. Each sector has a battlefleet of its own, normally between fifty and seventy-five capital ships with escorts and support craft, and the five fleets of the great segmentae are headed by the Lord High Admirals. The Navy does not control the stars, which no force in the Imperium could do, but it holds the lanes by which the Imperium is joined together.

The Navy was born in the [[Great Crusade]], when the fleets that carried the [[Space Marine Legions]] and the armies of the Emperor were the first expression of Imperial power in the void. In its present form it dates from the reforms that followed the [[Horus Heresy]] (c. 015–021.M31), when the ships were separated from the ground forces and placed under a distinct service. It has been kept distinct ever since. An admiral cannot order an army, a general cannot order a ship, and both are deliberately made dependent on the other.

The Navy is also bound closely to the [[Navis Nobilite]], the Navigator houses without which no ship can cross the Warp. Every vessel of the fleet carries at least one Navigator, and the Navy's fate is tied to theirs. In the early [[42nd millennium]] that dependence has become a source of fear as well as strength, for the darkness of the [[Imperium Nihilus|Noctis Aeterna]] showed how quickly a fleet fails when its pilots cannot see their way.

History

The fleets of the Great Crusade

See also: [[Great Crusade]]

[[Great Crusade|The Great Crusade]] could not have been fought without ships. A single Legion needed hundreds of vessels to move its warriors, its armour and its [[Imperialis Auxilia|auxiliaries]], and the great expeditionary fleets that went out from [[Terra]] in the late [[30th millennium]] numbered hundreds, sometimes thousands, of hulls. The first of them were built in the orbital foundries of Terra and in the Ring of Iron about [[Mars]], under [[Eye of Terror|the eye]] of the Emperor and the forge-wrights of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicum]], and later at the shipyards of [[Sol System|Saturn]], Voss, Grulgarod and [[Cypra Mundi]], which grew in time to rival Mars itself. Without the alliance sealed in the [[Treaty of Olympus]] the whole undertaking would have been impossible.

During the Crusade the fleets and the armies were a single service. The Excertus Imperialis, the Imperial Army, held the ground regiments and the ships assigned to them, and the whole was commonly called the Armada Imperialis. A regiment's colonel commanded both his troops and the cruiser that carried them, which made a ship a self-contained force and limited the damage should it be lost in the Warp. [[Space Marine Legions|The Legions]] had fleets of their own, built to the taste of their [[Primarch|primarchs]]: the black raiders of the [[Raven Guard]], the crimson and gold cruisers of the [[Blood Angels]], the unadorned siege-barques of the [[Iron Warriors]].

Far ahead of the main expeditions went the Rogue Traders Militant. They were often former rulers of the realms the Emperor had cast down, who were offered the choice of kneeling or dying and who chose to take his [[Rogue Trader|Warrant of Trade]]. They scouted the unmapped dark with their own fleets, and they were forbidden to come home. Many vanished alone. Some, in their last decades, returned fleets of ramshackle ships that included captured [[Xenos|xenos]] hulls and vessels of unknown origin, and their descendants founded the Rogue Trader dynasties of the later Imperium.

The Great Crusade also recovered ships that the Mechanicum could not build. The Terminus Est, the Nicor, the Mirabilis and the [[Phalanx]] are all said to be relics of an older age, and no forge has succeeded in copying them. Their patterns, and those of the first ships of the line, passed into the fleets of the later Imperium and gave the Navy its inheritance of designs.

The anchorage fleets

As the Crusade reached the Eastern Fringe the Imperium formed fleet anchorages at points on and near its borders, with at least one in each of the great segmentae. These were base ports from which permanent armadas could patrol and respond to sudden threats. They were composed largely of the Imperialis Auxilia, and their admirals answered to Terra and not to any primarch. When the Heresy began the anchorage fleets proved less open to the [[Warmaster]]'s agents than the expeditionary armies, and the [[Port Maw]] Armada, though made the target of repeated subversion, remained for the most part loyal. Where its ships did defect, it was commonly through a polluted officer cadre or a mutiny by a minority.

The Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

The Heresy broke the old system. Almost every Army regiment attached to a [[Traitor Legions|Traitor Legion]] followed it into rebellion, and the ships under the regiments' command went with them. Officers of the Army who answered to no Legion used the ships that carried them to carve out private empires from the [[Chaos|chaos]], and a good part of the Imperium's long reconquest was spent undoing their work. The loyalty of the anchorage fleets, which had been raised to defend and not to conquer, was one of the few firm points the Loyalists could rely on.

The Reformation

After the [[Siege of Terra]], [[Roboute Guilliman]] and the surviving leaders of the Loyalists remade the Imperium. The Navy was made a service of its own. By the [[Tactica Imperium]] the link between the ground armies and the fleet was cut. An officer of the [[Astra Militarum]] could no longer command a ship, and an admiral had no authority over the regiments he carried. Fleets were divided among the five segmentae and into battlefleets for each sector, a structure that has lasted ten thousand years.

Before he was interred, the Emperor himself is said to have decreed that starships should be commanded only by officers of their own service. The decree is cited in the Navy's articles of command, though no copy has been found in the Palace archives.

Ten thousand years

The Navy's ships have fought on every front. Battlefleets hold the lanes about the Eye of Terror, escort the tithe fleets and defend the worlds of the Imperium against the raiders that prey on them. The greatest naval war of the later Imperium was the [[Gothic War]] of 139.M41, in which Battlefleet Gothic was assailed by the fleets of [[Abaddon the Despoiler|Abaddon]]'s Twelfth [[Black Crusade]]. The Navy drove the Despoiler's fleets from the sector at great cost, though Abaddon carried away a captured [[Blackstone Fortress|Blackstone fortress]] that he later hurled against [[Cadia]].

The Navy also fights in the shadow of the Warp. In the [[Age of Apostasy]] fleets were cut off for generations by the Warp storms, and in the [[Era Indomitus]] the opening of the [[Great Rift]] and the loss of the [[Astronomican]] for a time left many ships lost or stranded. The fleets of the [[Indomitus Crusade]] have fought through storms that wrecked whole battlefleets.

Organisation

Segmentae and battlefleets

The Imperium is divided into five great fleet zones, the [[Segmentum|Segmentae Majoris]]: Solar, Obscurus, Pacificus, Tempestus and Ultima. Every ship of the Navy belongs to one of them and answers to the [[Lord High Admiral]] of that segmentum. The Lord High Admiral of the Segmentum Solar holds the oldest and most senior post, and he may sit among the [[High Lords of Terra]]. Each segmentum has a [[Segmentum Fortress]] as its base. The fortress is under the control of an official of the [[Administratum]], the [[Master of the Segmentum]], who reports to the [[Master of the Administratum]], and not to the admiral.

Each segmentum is divided into [[Sector|sectors]], and each sector has a battlefleet, named for the sector it guards. Battlefleet Gothic is in the Gothic Sector, Battlefleet [[Cadia]] was in the [[Cadian Sector]], and [[Sol System|Battlefleet Solar]] guards the Sol System itself. A battlefleet is commanded by a Lord Admiral. It is divided into battlegroups of one or two cruisers with a squadron of escorts, which patrol independently, and a larger force can be formed by concentrating several battlegroups.

Ranks and crews

The Navy's officers are commissioned from the noble and military families of the Imperium. Beneath them are warrant officers, the ratings and a great mass of indentured workers, conscripted or bonded for life to the ships. Most are drawn from the same recruitment pools as the regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]]. The crew of a large ship numbers in the tens of thousands, and its lower decks hold generations of families that have never been planetside. The [[Commissar|Commissariat]] supplies political officers to the Navy as it does to the Guard.

Vessels and weapons

The Navy's ships are many kilometres long and built like cathedrals, with armour concentrated at the prow and rank upon rank of gun decks along the flanks. The great majority are of patterns that were old before the Heresy. The ships fall into five broad classes.

The ships' principal weapons are macro-batteries, which fire great volumes of heavy shells, lances, which fire beams of focused energy through enemy shields, and torpedoes, which strike shields at close range. The largest cruisers and battleships may carry a nova cannon, which fires an enormous explosive shell over many thousands of kilometres. Defences are void shields, which absorb fire until overloaded, and thick armour. [[Warp travel]] is guarded by the [[Geller field]] that wraps each ship and by the [[Navis Nobilite|Navigator]], whose Warp Eye finds the path.

The Navy's fighters and bombers fly from carriers and from the flight decks of larger ships. Fury interceptors and Starhawk bombers are the common craft of the void, and atmospheric craft of the Navy, from Thunderbolt fighters to Marauder bombers, support the regiments of the [[Astra Militarum]] from their airfields. Crews pass most of their short lives in these craft, and their losses are accounted for in the monthly tithe.

Relations with other institutions

Navis Nobilite

The [[Navis Nobilite]], the Navigator houses, give every ship of the Navy its Warp pilot. No vessel can cross the Warp without one, so the Navy depends on the houses for its very mobility and has no means of replacing them. The houses are bound by their own charters and by the [[Adeptus Terra]], not by the Navy's articles of command, and an admiral cannot discipline a Navigator as he would a rating.

The dependence has a price. Each house sets its own terms for service, and those terms rise in proportion to the Navy's need. In a time of crisis, as in the darkness that followed the opening of the [[Great Rift]], an admiral may find that his fleet has the guns to fight and no one to steer it.

Astra Militarum

The Navy and the [[Astra Militarum]] are partners and rivals. Since the post-Heresy reforms neither may command the other: the Navy carries the Guard's regiments between worlds and covers their landings from orbit, and the Guard garrisons and holds what the fleet cannot. Neither can fight a war without the other, a fact that each service knows well and has often resented.

The rivalry is real. Fleets are assigned by the [[Departmento Munitorum]] against the claims of every sector, and a regiment kept waiting for transport commonly blames the admiral, while an admiral kept waiting for a landing blames the general. The Navy's [[Commissar|commissars]] and the Guard's are drawn from the same Officio Prefectus, which gives both services a shared overseer.

Adeptus Mechanicus

The shipyards of the [[Forge world|forge worlds]] build the Navy's ships, and the priesthood of Mars keeps the [[Omnissiah|machine spirits]] of their engines content. A ship that leaves a forge world is never wholly the Navy's: it carries tech-priests of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] who maintain it and who report to the Fabricator-General as well as to the captain.

The Mechanicus also holds the knowledge of the oldest ship patterns, and it sets its own price for refits and new construction. A battlefleet that wishes to repair a relic hull must negotiate with the forge that holds its plans, and the Mechanicus has often refused to build a ship it considers an unnecessary innovation.

Ecclesiarchy

On many worlds the Navy is known as the Holy Fleet, and its ships are blessed at their launching by priests of the [[Imperial Cult|Ecclesiarchy]]. Chaplains and confessors serve aboard its larger vessels, and the crews' devotion to the Emperor is encouraged as a bond of discipline.

The Ecclesiarchy teaches that the light of the [[Astronomican]] that guides every fleet through the Warp is the light of the Emperor himself. Navy officers accept the doctrine more readily than the Mechanicus does, and a captain who lost his Navigator in a storm is likely to ascribe his survival to the Emperor.

Rogue Traders

The [[Rogue Trader|Rogue Traders]] descend from the scouts of the Great Crusade and carry the Warrant of Trade granted in that age. They operate independently of the Navy, beyond its authority, and often beyond its charts. The Navy has no right to command them.

In practice the two have long traded favours. The Navy asks Rogue Traders to scout unmapped space and to carry cargoes in the lanes it cannot patrol, and the Rogue Traders ask the Navy for protection or refuge when their ventures go wrong. Neither trusts the other entirely.
