A heretic is a person who holds, teaches or acts upon a doctrine that contradicts the beliefs of the [[Imperial Cult]] or the laws of the [[Imperium of Mankind]]. Under [[Imperial law]], heresy is among the gravest crimes, and it is a matter for the [[Ecclesiarchy]], the [[Inquisition]] and the [[Adeptus Arbites]] to judge. A heretic may be a single individual, a family, a cult or a whole world. The most serious cases pass beyond heresy into [[Traitor|treachery]], and the law then treats the offender as a [[Traitor]].

Imperial doctrine distinguishes between the opinion that is merely unorthodox and the act that is heretical. The Ecclesiarchy's records state that the commission of heresy is often not the expression of an unusual religious view, but the worship of the [[Ruinous Powers]] or the aid of Chaos against the Imperium.

Definition

The Imperial definition of heresy turns on the Imperial Truth and the Emperor's law. An opinion is heretical if it contradicts the theology of the Imperial Cult, the commandments of the Ecclesiarchy, or the civil law of the Imperium. What counts as heresy is decided by the high officers of the Ecclesiarchy. Massive divisions of the [[Adeptus Ministorum]] study the many religious sects of the Imperium and determine whether each has kept within the orthodoxy of the Imperial Cult.

Imperial law sets the test in practical terms. The question is whether the person's belief has led to an act that threatens the Imperium, its people or its institutions. Doctrine alone is usually corrected rather than punished, but a teaching that encourages rebellion, mutation, sorcery or the worship of Chaos is treated as an act.

Categories

Heresy of thought

Heresy of thought covers beliefs and teachings. It includes unorthodox readings of the Emperor's nature, claims to speak with the Emperor's voice without authority, and the teaching of the [[Imperial Truth]] in forms that the Ecclesiarchy has not approved.

Heresy of deed

Heresy of deed covers acts against the Imperial Cult or the civil order. It includes sacrilege, the use of forbidden knowledge, and the use of unsanctioned psychic powers. Under the [[Edict of Nikaea]], issued in 001.M31, the use of psychic power by the Astartes was forbidden, and such use is still treated as a heretical act.

Heresy by association

A person who aids heretics, shelters them or keeps silence about them may be judged a heretic in their own right. Inquisitors treat association as evidence to be weighed, and the [[Ordo Hereticus]] is the Inquisitorial body most concerned with it.

Punishment

Heresy is punished by the Imperial courts and by the Inquisition. The usual penalty is death, though the Ecclesiarchy offers penitence to those who repent. A penitent heretic may be assigned to the [[Penitent]] order, and in the most serious cases the penance is the pilot's seat of a [[Penitent Engine]]. The [[Excommunicate Traitoris]] designation is reserved for the gravest cases, in which the accused is banished from the Imperium and struck from its records.

Historical cases

The rolls of the Imperium record many heresies. The civil war of 005–014.M31, known as the [[Horus Heresy]], began with the defection of the [[Warmaster Horus]] and is the largest heresy in Imperial history. In the 36th millennium the [[Plague of Unbelief]] and the [[Age of Apostasy]] produced further heresies in the Ecclesiarchy and the Imperial government, and the [[Ordo Hereticus]] was founded in response to them.
