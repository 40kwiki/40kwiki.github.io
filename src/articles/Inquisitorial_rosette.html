An Inquisitorial rosette is a symbolic icon worn by members of the [[Inquisition]]. It is one of many badges worn by the servants of the [[Imperium of Mankind]], but it carries a far greater weight. Every [[Inquisitor]] is presented with a rosette on their elevation to rank. It is a small amulet in the shape of a stylised column, and it is recognised across Imperial society as a sign of ultimate authority exercised in the name of the [[Emperor of Mankind]].

The rosette is among an Inquisitor's most important items of equipment. Inquisitors are said never to surrender it willingly. Its verification technology and coded encryption let any cogitator confirm the bearer's clearance, and they make the rosette difficult to forge.

Design and use

The rosette is worn on an Inquisitor's clothing or wargear. It may also be worn by the people in an Inquisitor's employ, including retinues and [[Acolyte]]s, and it may be displayed on vehicles or on the forces that an Inquisitor commands. Its presence means that the bearer is working for the [[Inquisition]], and it is usually enough to secure the cooperation of most Imperial adepts and citizens who know the Inquisition's purpose.

Inquisitors use the rosette sparingly. Most prefer to keep themselves and their Acolytes inconspicuous. It is shown most often in the company of other members of the Inquisition, or when an Inquisitor wishes to strike fear into ordinary people.

Verification

A rosette carries verification technology that can be read by a cogitator. An official who doubts an [[Inquisitor]]'s authority can scan the rosette to confirm the bearer's clearance. The encryption is intended to make a forged rosette useless, because a false badge fails when it is checked.

History

Origins

The name "rosette" is traced to the opening days of the [[Horus Heresy]] in the early [[31st millennium]], during the hunt for Battle-Brother Severian, known as "The Wolf". Severian was the last of the Outcast Dead, a group of renegade Space Marines from the [[Luna Wolves]] who had served in a Terran honour guard. When the betrayal of the [[Warmaster Horus]] became known on [[Terra]], the [[Adeptus Custodes]] confined the suspect [[Adeptus Astartes|Astartes]] in the prison complex of Khangba Marwu, deep beneath the Himalayas. The Imperial record names the escape of Severian and the [[Astropathy|Astropath]] Kai Zulane as the event that led to the first use of the badge.

Development

The rosette became the mark of the [[Inquisition]] as the organisation grew from the small body formed by [[Malcador the Sigillite]] into the Holy Ordos. Its design was settled over the following millennia. [[Inquisitor|Inquisitors]] of the late [[41st millennium]] still wear it, and the rosette is the most widely recognised emblem of the Inquisition among ordinary citizens.

Cultural significance

The rosette is one of the most recognised symbols of Imperial authority. Its image appears in Imperial art, and its presence in a city is often enough to change the behaviour of its people. Imperial sources describe it as a symbol that means fear and cooperation at once, and that is the reason it is used sparingly.
