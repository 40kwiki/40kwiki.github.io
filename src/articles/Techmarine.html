A Techmarine (Frater Astrotechnicus in [[High Gothic]]) is a Firstborn or Primaris [[Space Marine]] technician and engineer, and a full battle-brother of his Chapter. He is also a fully initiated member of the [[Cult Mechanicus]]. In his Chapter he serves the role that a [[Enginseer|tech-priest enginseer]] serves in the [[Astra Militarum]], and without the ancient lore that Techmarines pass on, the Space Marines would eventually be unable to make war.

Techmarines hold a double loyalty. They are bound by blood to their Chapter, but in mind and spirit to the mysteries of the [[Omnissiah]]. Their Chapters hold them in great esteem for their expertise, yet regard them with awe and a deep mistrust. Most of the priesthood of Mars see them as mere enginseers, or worse as [[Heretek|hereteks]] who violate the sanctity of machine spirits in order to wage war more efficiently.

History

The pacts with Mars

Ancient pacts sworn between the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] and the [[Adeptus Astartes]] allow the Space Marines to send warriors with an affinity for technology to [[Mars]] to train and to begin the long journey of becoming a priest of the Machine God. Without the passing on of that lore the Space Marines would be unable to make war.

A Techmarine's calling is among the most arduous in the Imperium. He returns to his Chapter as an aloof and mysterious figure, and most Techmarines find solace only among their own number, retreating to the Chapter's forges and limiting their contact with other battle-brothers. Few ever hold authority in a Codex Astartes-compliant Chapter, and most such Chapters keep their Techmarines at arm's length from their traditions, ceremonies and rituals.

The Era Indomitus

Further information: [[Era Indomitus]]

The [[Era Indomitus]] has brought [[Primaris Space Marines|Primaris]] Marines into the mysteries of the Omnissiah as Techmarines. They act in much the same way as their Firstborn counterparts, striding through enemy fire to soothe the machine spirits of wounded war engines and peeling back damaged armour to repair them with servo-arm and mechadendrite.

Training

Every Chapter sends a number of its warriors with a demonstrated affinity for the use and maintenance of technology to Mars, where they are inducted into the Machine Cult by the Adeptus Mechanicus. The aspirants train for thirty standard years, steeping themselves in the rites of activation and the hymnals of maintenance, and learning how to call forth a machine spirit or placate its wrath. They are taught to feel the pain of a damaged machine and heal it. On their return they become custodians of the Chapter's armoury.

Duties

The most critical of a Techmarine's tasks is to operate, maintain and, when necessary, repair the war machines of the Space Marines. Every item of technology, from a round of ammunition to a [[battle barge]], is revered as an instrument of the Emperor's will, and a Techmarine regards neglect of such equipment as blasphemy. He oversees the rites by which the machine spirit of each weapon is appeased.

On the battlefield a Techmarine's main role is to repair damaged equipment so that it can return to the fight, using a great range of specialised tools, often mounted on a servo-arm or a mechadendrite. Some machines are so exceptional that only a Techmarine may attend them, such as the rare Conversion Beamer. A Chapter may have any number of Techmarines outside the company organisation, belonging to the Armoury, and the posts of vehicle driver, pilot and gunner are typically held by them. Despite their calling they remain fierce warriors, and if a vehicle is lost they will fight for its retrieval as stridently as their brothers would for a fallen comrade.

Machina Opus

In deference to their training on Mars, Techmarines incorporate red into their armour or heraldry, though they never fully obscure their Chapter's colours or badge. They also display the Machina Opus, the icon of a tech-priest adept, awarded as each Techmarine completes his training. The devotees of the Omnissiah accord the bearers of the Machina Opus great respect, and of all Space Marines they alone are allowed free passage through the Ring of Iron into the great workshops of the Martian hive cities.

Master of the Forge

The senior Techmarine of a Chapter is the Master of the Forge, who commands the Armoury and maintains the Chapter's fleet of armoured vehicles. His knowledge of the arcane sciences has been refined over centuries and rivals that of the senior tech-priests of Mars. He is always part of the Chapter Council, yet he is an outsider to all save his subordinate Techmarines. He is also charged with conserving any arcane relics the Chapter has inherited from the time before the Horus Heresy.

A Master of the Forge is a Space Marine first, and few hesitate to modify or overhaul a weapon or vehicle to better suit their Chapter, to the outrage of the more conservative tech-priests, who see the worst kind of heretek in him. Some Chapters, such as the [[Mentors]], the Praetors of Orpheus and the Astral Knights, embrace advanced technology without the usual superstition, and the [[Iron Hands]] do so with an enthusiasm nearly matching the Mechanicus. In these Chapters the Master of the Forge is honoured no less than the Chapter Master.

Deathwatch service

For most Chapters Techmarines are an irreplaceable resource, so it is exceptional for one to take the Apocryphon Oath and serve a Vigil in the [[Deathwatch]]. It is often the most freshly ordained who do so, to broaden their understanding of both lost Imperial technology and xenos technology. In a Watch Fortress the Deathwatch Techmarine keeps his portion of the Vault sealed to all but his fellow Techmarines, and he evaluates, and when necessary retrieves, alien technology met in the field.

Chapter variants

  • The [[Iron Hands]] lack Chaplains, and the Iron Father combines the roles of Chaplain and Techmarine, representing both the Emperor and the Mechanicus to the Chapter.
  • The [[Space Wolves]] call their Techmarines Iron Priests. They are trained on Mars like all Techmarines, but keep their original armour colour, and Iron Priests are traditionally attached to the Great Wolf's Company.
  • The [[Sons of Medusa]] call their senior Techmarines Forge-Wrights; the Chapter is governed by a council of its Techmarines.
  • The Chaos Space Marines' equivalents, the Warpsmiths, trace their origins to the priesthood of Mars and seek to subjugate technology rather than hold it sacred.
