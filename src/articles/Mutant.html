A mutant (in the vulgar [[Low Gothic]], a mutie) is a human being whose body differs from the human genetic pattern. In the [[Imperium of Mankind]] the word covers a vast range of conditions, from a minor deformity to a body changed beyond recognition, and it carries a heavy weight of law and religion. The [[Imperial Cult]] teaches that the human form was made in the image of the [[Emperor of Mankind]] and that mutation is a sign of spiritual corruption; Imperial law therefore treats mutants as less than citizens, and on many worlds as outlaws to be killed on sight.

Mutants are nonetheless extremely common. The poisoned environments of [[hive world]]s, the radiation of mining and [[forge world]]s, inbreeding on isolated worlds and exposure to the [[Warp]] all produce them in numbers that no Imperial authority can eliminate. On many worlds they form an underclass of forced labour; on others they live hidden in the depths of hive cities or in the wastes. Mutant populations have been a frequent source of rebellion and of recruits for [[Chaos]] cults, and the Imperium has fought many wars to suppress them.

Imperial law distinguishes ordinary mutants from [[abhuman]]s, stable variants of humanity such as [[Ogryn]]s and [[Ratling]]s that breed true and are accepted as citizens, and from the hereditary mutation of the [[Navigator]]s, which is protected by ancient privilege. Psychic ability is also a form of mutation, governed by its own laws (see [[Psyker]]).

Causes

Imperial records attribute mutation to several causes. The most common are environmental: radiation, industrial pollution, chemical and biological toxins. Others are hereditary, the result of long isolation and inbreeding. The most feared are spiritual: exposure to the Warp, which can twist flesh directly, and the favour of the [[Chaos Gods]], whose followers are often marked with gifts of mutation. Some mutations result from [[Xenos|xenos]] infection, such as the hybrids of the [[Genestealer Cults]], or from disease, such as the plagues of [[Nurgle]].

Classification

The official standard for distinguishing humans from mutants is the Godolkin Purebreed Guide, also known as the Godolkin Index. It scores a subject's deviations from the human phenotype by a system of points: the more severe the mutation, the higher its value. A subject whose total exceeds a set threshold is classed as a mutant. Planetary authorities set their own thresholds and penalties, so that a person counted human on one world may be put to death on another.The date of the Godolkin Guide is not recorded. It is cited in Administratum and Arbites documents of the 41st millennium as a long-established standard.

History

The Age of Strife

Further information: [[Age of Strife]]

Mutation spread widely among human populations during the [[Age of Strife]] (c. M25–M30), when worlds cut off by [[Warp storm|warp storms]] suffered war, radiation and the collapse of medical technology. On [[Terra]] itself much of the population of the [[Unification Wars]] era (c. M30) was mutated. The Emperor's armies purged the mutated warbands of the techno-barbarians, but also recruited stable strains, the forerunners of the abhumans, into the [[Imperialis Auxilia|Imperial Army]].

The Great Crusade and after

During the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31) the secular [[Imperial Truth]] judged mutants by their usefulness rather than their sin, and markedly divergent strains such as [[Abhuman#Beastmen|Beastmen]] served the Imperium. After the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31), in which mutant hordes fought for the [[Traitor|traitors]], and with the rise of the [[Imperial Cult]] in the [[32nd millennium]], hostility hardened into doctrine. The [[Liber Ecclesiasticus]] (M37) describes mutation as the outward sign of inward corruption.

The 41st and 42nd millennia

Mutant uprisings are among the commonest internal wars of the Imperium. They range from riots in the underhives to rebellions that topple planetary governments and draw in the [[Astra Militarum]]. The [[Adeptus Arbites]] maintain extensive records of such outbreaks, and the [[Inquisition]] investigates the cults that often stand behind them. In the [[Era Indomitus]], after the [[Great Rift]] opened in 999.M41, warp exposure and the plagues of the [[Plague Wars]] (c. 012.M42) produced mutation on an unprecedented scale in parts of [[Ultramar]] and the [[Imperium Nihilus]].

Life in the Imperium

The treatment of mutants depends on the world. On some, all mutants are killed at birth or as soon as they are discovered. On many hive and industrial worlds they are tolerated as a source of cheap labour, working in the most dangerous industries, forbidden to marry or hold property, branded or segregated and denied the protection of the law. Some worlds confine them to ghettos or penal colonies; others drive them into the wastes. Mutants are excluded from the Imperial priesthood and from most offices, and are often refused the rites of the Cult.

Excluded from society, mutants have repeatedly turned to cults that promise them dignity or vengeance. [[Chaos cult|Chaos cults]] in particular recruit among them, and the gifts of mutation that the [[Chaos Gods]] bestow can make outcasts into worshippers. The Imperium regards a mutant population as a standing threat for this reason, and the [[Inquisition]] and the [[Imperial Cult|Ecclesiarchy]] have preached purges in many [[Sector|sectors]].

Mutation and Chaos

Not all mutation is the work of [[Chaos]], but the followers of the Chaos Gods are frequently mutated, and mutation is held to be one of the clearest signs of Chaos influence. The servants of [[Nurgle]] bear the marks of disease, those of [[Tzeentch]] shifting and multiplying changes. The [[Heretic Astartes]] of the traitor Legions are often grossly mutated after ten thousand years in the [[Eye of Terror]]. A Chaos warrior who receives too many gifts may degenerate into a mindless [[Chaos Spawn|spawn]].
