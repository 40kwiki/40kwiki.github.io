A mutant (in the vulgar [[Low Gothic]], a mutie) is a human being whose body differs from the human genetic pattern. In the [[Imperium of Mankind]] the word covers a vast range of conditions, from a minor deformity to a body changed beyond recognition, and it carries a heavy weight of law and religion. Imperial authorities hold the baseline human form to be divinely perfect and treat mutation as a heretical crime against the [[Emperor of Mankind]], because of its link to sympathy for or worship of [[Chaos]]. Mutants are shunned by almost everyone and are often hunted down and killed.

Mutants are nonetheless numerous. Millions across the Imperium are born deformed by radiation, by the extreme environments their ancestors lived in for generations, or by the touch of Chaos. They become the outcasts and lower classes of Imperial society. On some worlds they live hidden in the undersumps and catacombs beneath great cities or in the radiation-ravaged wastes. Mutant populations have been a frequent source of rebellion and of recruits for [[Chaos]] cults.

The Imperium tolerates only a few kinds of mutant. These are the stable [[abhuman]] strains, such as [[Ogryn]]s and [[Ratling]]s, that serve in the [[Astra Militarum]], the [[Navigator]]s, who are vital to its commerce and defence and may have been bred deliberately in the Dark Age of Technology, and sanctioned [[Psyker|psykers]]. Psychic ability is itself a form of mutation, governed by its own laws.

Causes

Imperial records attribute mutation to several causes. Many mutants are born so, because their ancestors were exposed over many generations to mutagens such as radiation or to extreme planetary environments, and radiation can also act on a child before birth. The most feared cause is spiritual: exposure to the psychic energies of [[Chaos]], which can cause rampant mutation. Followers of the [[Chaos Gods]] are known to be granted mutations as gifts by their masters, and so physical mutation is taken as a sign of inner corruption.

Classification

The official standard for distinguishing humans from mutants is the Godolkin Purebreed Guide, also known as the Godolkin Index. It scores a subject's deviations from the human phenotype by a system of points: the more severe the mutation, the higher its value. A subject whose total exceeds a set threshold is classed as a mutant. On most worlds a tolerance of up to three points is allowed, but the [[Administratum]] may grant exemptions to worlds of strategic value or with highly mutagenic environments. On the industrial world of Kalidar, for example, emergent psykers are put to death only if they deviate by more than fourteen points from the baseline human genome.The date of the Godolkin Guide is not recorded. It is the official standard in the 41st millennium.

History

The Age of Strife

Further information: [[Age of Strife]]

Imperial scholars trace the danger of the warp to the end of the Dark Age of Technology, when uncontrolled human psykers began to appear. The tragedies they caused helped bring on the destruction of the [[Age of Strife]] (c. M25–M30), which consumed human space.

The Great Crusade and after

See also: [[Great Crusade]]

During the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31) mutants such as the [[Abhuman#Beastmen|Beastmen]] were accepted and used in the [[Imperialis Auxilia|Imperial Army]], formed into their own squads or mixed with the regular troops. After the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31) the [[High Lords of Terra]] outlawed mutants for the part they had played in the rebellion as favoured servants of the [[Dark Gods]]. The link between mutants, heresy and [[Chaos cult|Chaos cults]] has since hardened the hatred of them.

The 41st and 42nd millennia

The history of many human worlds is full of mutant uprisings, and such conflicts are bitter and merciless. Mutant populations are often vital to economic operations, yet they are also a problem for civil order and a ready source of riots and cult activity. In the [[Era Indomitus]], after the [[Great Rift]] opened in 999.M41, mutative warp storms washed over the galaxy. Some of the seeds they carried sprouted at once as mutation outbreaks and plagues. Others lay dormant, and many voidships lost in the warp during the Noctis Aeterna emerged deeply and often unknowingly changed.

Life in the Imperium

The treatment of mutants depends on the world. On many worlds mutants are quickly abandoned by their parents and must fend for themselves, and it is rare to find a world that tolerates them without discrimination. For every sanctioned mutant that serves the Imperium, a thousand are slain outright and another thousand hide in the shadows. Where they are allowed to exist, they are usually a legally discriminated underclass, forbidden to bear weapons for fear of rebellion, and often exiled to harsh work such as the rad mines of Sepheris Secundus or the chemworks of Solomon in the [[Calixis Sector]]. Those not counted fully human have no defence against brutality by those in power.

Excluded from society, mutants have repeatedly turned to cults that promise them dignity or vengeance. [[Chaos cult|Chaos cults]] in particular recruit among them, and the gifts of mutation that the [[Chaos Gods]] bestow can make outcasts into worshippers. Such cults are often led by rogue psykers and witches, who can hide more easily among the distortions of a mutant population.

Mutation and Chaos

See also: [[Chaos]] and [[Chaos Space Marines]]

Not all mutation is the work of [[Chaos]], but the followers of the Chaos Gods are frequently mutated, and mutation is held to be a sign of Chaos influence. Mutants who serve [[Khorne]] are common, since many turn their hatred of Imperial prejudice into battlefield fury, and their mutations, such as razor claws, tripled muscle or acid blood, favour combat. A Chaos warrior who receives too many gifts may degenerate into a mindless [[Chaos Spawn|spawn]].
