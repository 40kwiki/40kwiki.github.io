The Traitor Legions were the nine [[Space Marine Legions]] that turned against the [[Emperor of Mankind]] in the [[Horus Heresy]] of the [[31st millennium]]. Their [[Primarch|primarchs]] and their warriors followed the [[Warmaster]] [[Horus]], whose rebellion was declared at [[Isstvan III]] in 005.M31. The Legions that remained loyal, the Loyalist Legions, went on to found the [[Space Marine Chapter]]s of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] after the [[Second Founding]] in 021.M31.

The [[Traitor|Traitors]] lost the war at the [[Siege of Terra]] in 014.M31, when Horus was slain aboard the [[Vengeful Spirit]]. The surviving Legions fled into the [[Eye of Terror]] during the [[Great Scouring]]. There the Legions fought one another for decades in the [[Legion Wars]], and by the end of the 31st millennium they had broken into warbands. Their descendants have fought the Imperium ever since in the [[Long War]], and in the [[Era Indomitus]] they have been among the principal enemies of the [[Imperium Nihilus]] and the [[Imperium Sanctus]].

The name "Traitor Legions" is an Imperial term. The Traitors themselves rarely used it. The Legions that followed Horus called themselves by their Legion names, and many still do. Imperial teaching describes them as [[Heretic|heretics]], and Imperial records of the Heresy are sealed by the [[Inquisition]].

Background

The Great Crusade

Further information: [[Great Crusade]]

The Traitor Legions were raised in the [[First Founding]] of about 700.M30, when the [[Emperor of Mankind]] created twenty [[primarch]]s and gave each one a Legion.Estimated from the sequence of events; no surviving record gives the exact year. [[Space Marine Legions|The Legions]] conquered and reclaimed worlds across [[Milky Way|the galaxy]] in the [[Great Crusade]]. By the [[30th millennium]]'s close the Legions were the Imperium's chief military arm, and their primarchs were its most senior commanders.

The Legions were not uniform. Each had its own homeworld, its own doctrine and its own rivalries, and their primarchs had been separated as infants by the [[Warp]] before being found again between 801.M30 and 981.M30. Some Legions, such as the [[Ultramarines]], had settled into the Imperial system quickly. Others, such as the [[World Eaters]] and the [[Night Lords]], carried the marks of their primarchs' youths into the Legion. The gap between Legions was a constant source of friction long before 005.M31.

Davin and the Monarchia

See also: [[Davin]]

The Emperor's treatment of the Legions changed in the years around 004.M31. [[Horus]] was recovered from the [[Feral World]] of [[Davin]] in that year, after he had been wounded in a battle there. [[Remembrancer Order|Remembrancer]] accounts of Davin record that the [[Warmaster]]'s loyalty had begun to shift before he returned to the Great Crusade, and that his Legion, the [[Sons of Horus]], had been renamed for him. Some Imperial historians put Davin at the start of the Heresy, while others place the decisive change later.

The Emperor's administration of the Crusade was known as the [[Monarchia]], a system of [[Governance of the Imperium|Imperial governance]] that the Legions' commanders resented. Many Legionaries believed that the Emperor preferred to govern through the Terran administrators and the [[High Lords of Terra|Council of Terra]] rather than through the primarchs, and that the Legions were being prepared for disbandment. Imperial accounts describe this as the principal grievance that the [[Traitor|Traitors]] shared, though each Legion had its own.

The Warmaster had long been the most senior of the primarchs. His position gave him access to the Legions and the Emperor's plans in equal measure, and he used that access to win over his brothers.

Lorgar, Erebus and the Word Bearers

See also: [[Word Bearers]] and [[Lorgar]]

The first Legion to turn was the [[Word Bearers]], under their primarch [[Lorgar]]. Lorgar had been raised on the world of [[Colchis]] and had been a devoted missionary of the Imperial Truth before the Heresy. His Legion's [[Chaplain|Chaplains]] and Dark Apostles had worshipped the powers of [[Chaos]] in secret for some decades. The Word Bearers' Chaplain, [[Erebus]], was their chief architect of the betrayal. Imperial investigators hold that Erebus had made his choice long before Horus did, and that Lorgar's conversion was the decisive event of the early Heresy.

Erebus and the Word Bearers' Dark Apostles are recorded as the Legion's first faithful servants of the Dark Gods. Their Legion would later be among the most fervent of the Traitor Legions, and it was the Word Bearers who first preached the worship of Chaos openly to the other Legions.

The turn

Isstvan III

Main article: [[Isstvan III]]

The rebellion was declared on [[Isstvan III]] in 005.M31. The [[Warmaster]] had gathered loyal Legions on the world for a joint operation under the Emperor's orders. The Traitor Legions turned on their brothers during the engagement, and many loyal Legionaries were destroyed in the first hours.

[[Space Marine Legions|The Legions]] that took part in the betrayal were the [[Emperor's Children]], the [[Iron Warriors]], the [[Night Lords]], the [[World Eaters]], the [[Thousand Sons]], the [[Sons of Horus]], the [[Word Bearers]], the [[Alpha Legion]] and the [[Death Guard]]. Imperial accounts describe the Death Guard among the Legions that struck first at the loyal forces, although the Legion's exact role in the betrayal is disputed.

The Drop Site Massacre

Main article: [[Drop Site Massacre]]

The next stage of the betrayal was the [[Drop Site Massacre]] at Isstvan V, where loyal warriors who had landed on the surface were trapped and destroyed by their own Legions. Some [[Traitor]] Legionaries refused the order, and the Massacre was the act that ended any chance of reconciliation. The Loyalist Legions that survived Isstvan V, among them the [[Ultramarines]], the [[Imperial Fists]] and the [[Iron Hands]], swore to hunt the Traitors wherever they went.

Imperial accounts record the loyalist Legionaries of the Traitor Legions who were killed or driven out at Isstvan. They are sometimes called the "[[Chaos cult|Lost and the Damned]]" in later records. The term refers to the loyalist warriors of the Traitor Legions who were cut off, and it is one of the reasons why Imperial scholars speak of divided Legions rather than of nine simple traitors.

Course of the Heresy

The war between the Traitor Legions and the Loyalist Legions lasted nine years. It was fought on more than a hundred worlds, in the [[Segmentum Solar]], [[Ultramar]] and the [[Segmentum Obscurus]], and in the [[Warp]]. The campaign was marked by sieges, purges, planetary bombardments and the first wide use of Legion-scale warships against Imperial worlds.

Calth and the Shadow Crusade

See also: [[Battle of Calth]] and [[Shadow Crusade]]

In 007.M31 the Traitor Legions attacked [[Ultramar]] at the [[Battle of Calth]]. The attack failed, and the Ultramarines drove the [[Traitor|Traitors]] from the Calth system. Its failure did not end the Traitor campaign in Ultramar. The [[Word Bearers]] and the World Eaters launched a [[Shadow Crusade]] against the Five Hundred Worlds from 007.M31 to 009.M31, which was marked by mass atrocities against the civilian populations of the realm.

The Iron Warriors

Main article: [[Iron Warriors]]

The [[Iron Warriors]], under [[Perturabo]], fought mostly as besiegers and fortifiers. Their Legion's doctrine was to break fortresses and to hold them, and they built their Traitor strongholds in the Heresy as they had built Imperial ones. Their campaigns against the loyal forces were long and slow, and their Legion became among the most feared in the Traitor Legions for its discipline.

The Emperor's Children and the Night Lords

See also: [[Emperor's Children]] and [[Night Lords]]

The [[Emperor's Children]], under [[Fulgrim]], took part in the Traitor campaigns with a taste for ritual and spectacle. Their Legion had been changed by Fulgrim's Slaaneshi pacts, and the Legion became increasingly devoted to pleasure and cruelty as the war went on. The [[Night Lords]], under [[Konrad Curze]], were feared for their terror tactics, and their Legion had carried its [[Primarch|primarch]]'s violence into the galaxy since the Great Crusade.

The World Eaters and Angron

See also: [[World Eaters]] and [[Angron]]

The [[World Eaters]] followed [[Angron]], whose Legion had been conditioned with the [[Butcher's Nails]], neural implants that drove its warriors into battle-rage. Angron had been raised as a gladiator on [[Nuceria]], and he had carried a deep hatred of the Emperor since his rescue from that world. His Legion's rage made it one of the most effective and most destructive of the Traitor Legions.

The Thousand Sons and the Sorcerers

See also: [[Thousand Sons]]

The [[Thousand Sons]], under [[Magnus the Red]], were driven to rebellion in part by the [[Burning of Prospero]], which the Imperium had ordered after the Thousand Sons had been accused of sorcery and of breaching the [[Edict of Nikaea]]. The Legion's Sorcerers were the most accomplished [[Psyker|psykers]] among the Traitors. Their Legion was among those most deeply committed to the worship of [[Tzeentch]].

The Death Guard and Mortarion

See also: [[Death Guard]] and [[Mortarion]]

The [[Death Guard]], under [[Mortarion]], were the most resolute of the Legions in their loyalty to the Emperor before the Heresy. Their loyalty before the Heresy was long remembered, and their turn is among the most debated in Imperial records. Their Legion's later worship of [[Nurgle]] is one of the most complete of any Traitor Legion's.

The Alpha Legion

Main article: [[Alpha Legion]]

The [[Alpha Legion]], under [[Alpharius Omegon]], is the Traitor Legion about which Imperial records are least certain. Its warriors fought in many campaigns and in many guises, and its primarch is known to Imperial records mainly through rumour. The Legion's reasons for the Heresy are still debated, and Imperial scholars record that some of its warriors may have served the Emperor to the end.

Sons of Horus and Horus

See also: [[Sons of Horus]]

The [[Sons of Horus]] were the [[Warmaster]]'s own Legion. They were the largest of the Traitor Legions in the Heresy's early years and were at the centre of the rebellion's strategy. [[Horus]] led them from the [[Vengeful Spirit]] during the [[Siege of Terra]], and he was killed aboard it in 014.M31.

The Siege of Terra

Main article: [[Siege of Terra]]

The Traitor Legions converged on [[Terra]] in 013.M31, and the [[Siege of Terra]] began that year. The [[Traitor|Traitors]]' fleets and warbands attacked the [[Imperial Palace]] from orbit and from the surface, while the [[Loyalist Legions]] raced through the Warp to relieve the defenders. The defence of the Palace fell to the loyal [[Space Marine Legions]] and to the [[Adeptus Custodes]], and the Traitor assault was held back for a year.

In 014.M31 the Traitors made their last assault. [[Horus]] met the Emperor aboard the [[Vengeful Spirit]] and was killed there. His death ended the Traitors' unity and their hope of taking the Palace, and the surviving Traitor forces retreated from Terra. The Emperor himself was grievously wounded in the battle, and the Imperium's Cult holds that His sacrifice in that hour saved Mankind.

Retreat into the Eye of Terror and the Scouring

See also: [[Eye of Terror]] and [[Great Scouring]]

After Terra the surviving [[Traitor]] forces fled into the [[Eye of Terror]] and other warp rifts. The Loyalist Legions pursued them in the [[Great Scouring]], a campaign that lasted from c. 014.M31 to c. 100.M31.Estimated; no record gives an exact year. The Scouring destroyed many of the Traitor Legions' homeworlds and bases, and the loyal Legions are recorded as having destroyed several of them with the Emperor's authority. Imperial scholars continue to debate how much of this destruction was deliberate.

Once inside the Eye the Traitors were beyond the reach of the Loyalist Legions, and the Traitor Legions lost access to the Imperial worlds they had once commanded. Their survivors were cut off from their homeworlds and from the Imperial [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] banks, and the Legions began to fragment.

Legion Wars and the breaking of the Legions

See also: [[Legion Wars]] and [[Black Legion]]

In the Eye, the Traitor Legions turned on one another. The [[Legion Wars]] are recorded in Imperial and captured accounts as centuries of fighting over worlds, slaves, [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] and relics. The fighting was fiercest where the Legions' rivals shared a warp rift or a [[Daemon world|daemon world]].

The [[Emperor's Children]] and the [[World Eaters]] fought for the daemon world of [[Skalathrax]]. There [[Khârn the Betrayer]] broke his own Legion's oaths and was cast out of the World Eaters. The [[Sons of Horus]] were driven from their own stronghold, and [[Horus]]'s cloned body was destroyed in the fighting. From the ruin, [[Abaddon the Despoiler|Ezekyle Abaddon]] built the [[Black Legion]], a new Legion that absorbed warriors from every Traitor Legion.

By the end of the [[31st millennium]] the Traitor Legions no longer existed as single armies. Some Legions survived intact in name, among them the [[Death Guard]], the [[Thousand Sons]], the [[World Eaters]] and the [[Emperor's Children]], each following one god or one primarch. Others were absorbed into warbands, most of them mixing warriors of several Legions with renegades and [[Chaos cult|cultists]].

The Long War

Further information: [[Long War]]

The [[Traitor|Traitors]] have raided the Imperium throughout the 32nd to 41st millennia. Their most visible campaigns are the [[Black Crusade]]s. The first, launched by [[Abaddon the Despoiler|Abaddon]] in 781.M31, did not win lasting territory, but it established the [[Black Legion]] as the Traitors' leading force. The later Black Crusades were larger and more destructive, and each was judged by [[Planetary Governor|Imperial commanders]] by the Traitors' ability to hold the worlds they took.

Between the Black Crusades, warbands struck at Imperial worlds on their own account. The [[Badab War]] of 901–912.M41 saw the renegade Chapter of the [[Red Corsairs]] rebel against the Imperium, and its leader [[Huron Blackheart]] was among the Traitors' best-known allies of the period.

The Thirteenth Black Crusade and the Era Indomitus

See also: [[Thirteenth Black Crusade]] and [[Era Indomitus]]

In 999.M41 [[Abaddon the Despoiler|Abaddon]] launched the [[Thirteenth Black Crusade]]. The Traitor Legions, renegades and [[Daemon|daemons]] broke through the Cadian Gate, and the [[Fall of Cadia]] destroyed the pylons that had held back the Eye of Terror. The [[Great Rift]] opened across the galaxy, and the [[Imperium Nihilus]] and [[Imperium Sanctus]] were cut off from one another. The [[Traitor|Traitors]]' presence in the Imperium has grown ever since.

The [[Era Indomitus]] that followed has been marked by renewed war. The [[Plague Wars]] saw the [[Death Guard]] fight against [[Ultramar]], and the daemon primarch [[Mortarion]] led his Legion into the realm. The [[World Eaters]], under the daemon primarch [[Angron]], have been recorded on a crusade aimed at [[Terra]]. The [[Black Legion]] pursued the [[Arks of Omen]] campaign, in which Abaddon sought the daemon-smith [[Vashtorr the Arkifane]] and the hidden [[Blackstone Fortress]]es.

Disputed loyalties and the Loyalist counterpart

Not every warrior of a Traitor Legion served the [[Warmaster]]. Imperial records of the Heresy and the [[Era Indomitus]] describe a range of loyalist warriors inside the Traitor Legions. Some were captured and turned back. Others fought for the Emperor in secret, and some were executed by their own Legions for refusing the [[Chaos|Dark Gods]].

The most cited case is [[Nathaniel Garro]], a Dark Angels [[Adeptus Astartes|Space Marine]] who was captured and interrogated by the [[Traitor|Traitors]] and later returned to the Emperor's cause. Other loyalists who served inside the Traitor Legions are recorded in Imperial accounts. Their testimonies are contested, and some Imperial archivists doubt parts of them.

The loyal counterpart to the Traitor Legions is the [[Shattered Legions]], the Legions that were broken by the Heresy and whose survivors were absorbed into the Loyalist [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]]. The Shattered Legions' surviving warriors are recorded as having been given into the care of the [[Imperial Fists]], the [[Ultramarines]] and the [[Dark Angels]] after the [[Second Founding]].

The [[Alpha Legion]] presents the greatest difficulty. Its warriors have been recorded in service to the Emperor, in service to the Traitors, and in neither. Imperial scholars do not agree on whether the Legion was a Traitor Legion from the first, or whether its loyalties were concealed until the Heresy. Some sources hold that its primarch had a twin, while others deny that any such twin existed.
