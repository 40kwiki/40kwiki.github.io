An Aspect Shrine is a cult of [[Aeldari]] [[Aspect Warriors]] devoted to one specialty of the [[Asuryani Path|Path of the Warrior]]. Each [[Craftworld Aeldari|craftworld]] keeps at least one shrine. Each shrine is dedicated to one facet of the war god [[Khaine]], a Warrior Aspect, and each brings its own fighting techniques, weapons and abilities.

The Path of the Warrior teaches the arts of death and destruction, and the dark nature of the Aeldari psyche draws most of them onto it at some point in their long lives. In the distant past the [[Phoenix Lord]]s taught the arts of war to both men and women, so Aspect Warriors are as likely to be one sex as the other.

Organisation and war

When the Aeldari go to war, the Aspect Warriors of each shrine fight in a set role that matches their aspect. Each shrine has its own warrior garb, a ritual battle suit, and distinctive weapons. Their minds and bodies are trained by endless exercise until they embody the aspect of Khaine that their shrine represents. In battle, each squad plays its part with the precision of a virtuoso, and when war's call is loudest the shrines go to battle in three-pronged Aspect Hosts that combine their abilities.

The Aeldari are beset on all sides, and their generations are few. Every life lost in battle is a sacrifice made only because there was no other choice. Young Aeldari often believe they can rebuild the glory of their lost empire with fire and passion, but their shattered civilisation is in truth locked in a struggle for survival. For that reason more and more of them walk the Warrior Path with each passing Terran year, setting aside their peacetime crafts to learn the arts of Khaine.

Exarchs

When the Aspect Warriors put aside their ritual masks and wargear, they live at peace within their craftworld. Only the keepers of each shrine, the [[Exarch]]s, remain in the shrine at all times. Exarchs are the war aspects of Khaine embodied. They are the high priests of the Bloody-Handed God and teachers of his creed.

Exarchs leave their shrines only in times of war or high conclave. Even the smallest shrines are extensive structures or hidden otherworlds given over to training, instruction and ceremony. Each has its own armoury and an inner sanctum where the Exarchs administer the rites of war before the altar of Khaine, reciting old battle songs and marking their warriors' bodies with runes of blood before they arm themselves.

Only Aspect Warriors who become completely lost upon the Path of the Warrior can become Exarchs. The transformation is rare and cannot be reversed. It follows the repeated exhilaration of battle, a feeling so strong that it leaves an indelible mark on the psyche. In theory, Aspect Warriors learn to put on and cast aside their blood-hungry persona as they don or put down their ritual wargear, a practice called "putting on the war mask". An Aspect Warrior who loses the ability to separate himself from the killer within becomes an Exarch, feared and honoured by those he once called kin.

An Exarch wears an elaborate and often ancient version of the Aspect armour, and carries arcane wargear. His suit holds [[Spirit Stone]]s that contain the spirits of the suit's previous Exarchs. The Exarch takes the sacred name of the suit, and his spirit joins with those of its earlier wearers. This pooled psychic energy gives the suit and the warrior, who are indistinguishable, their special powers.

Phoenix Lords

The founders of the shrines, the [[Phoenix Lord]]s, have a stranger form of immortality. When they die, their suits lie empty until worthy Exarchs find them. An Exarch who dons such a suit becomes part of its gestalt soul, and the Phoenix Lord rises again to lead the Aeldari in war. [[Arhra]], the Fallen Phoenix, is the only Phoenix Lord lost entirely to Chaos.
