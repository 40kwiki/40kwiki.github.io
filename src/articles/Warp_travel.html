Warp travel, also called warp translation or the warp jump, is the means by which the [[Imperium of Mankind]] and most other starfaring powers cross the distances between stars. A ship with a warp drive opens a passage from realspace into the [[Warp]], also known as the [[Immaterium]], a dimension of thought and emotion in which distance, time and direction do not behave as they do in the material universe. It travels there under the protection of a [[Geller field]] and the guidance of a [[Navigator]], and returns to realspace at its destination. A voyage that would take thousands of years at sublight speeds may take weeks or months in the Warp.

The Warp is not a road. Its tides and storms shift without pattern, and no two crossings are alike. A ship may arrive years before it left, or centuries after, or not at all. A fraction of all vessels that enter it are never seen again, and the Imperium accepts the loss as the cost of holding a galaxy-wide domain together. Every part of the Imperium's government, from the collection of the [[Imperial tithe]] to the movement of armies, is shaped by the fact that travel is slow, uncertain and dependent on a small number of people with rare gifts.

Since the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 the Imperium has been divided between the [[Imperium Sanctus]] and the [[Imperium Nihilus]], and the safe crossing of the Warp has become harder than at any time since the [[Horus Heresy]]. The [[Webway]], the dolmen gates of the [[Necrons]] and the crude drives of the [[Orks]] provide other means of crossing space, and each has its own dangers.

Principles

The warp drive

A warp drive is a large engine that tears a hole between realspace and the Warp. The Imperial Navy builds its drives to many patterns, and the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] holds that the original design dates to the [[Dark Age of Technology]], when it was made together with the Geller field. A drive cannot be used deep inside a star's gravity well, where realspace is too firmly fixed to be breached. A ship must first travel under sublight power to a jump point, a region far from the system's sun and its largest worlds. For a system with a heavy primary star this journey may take days or weeks, and in that time a fleet is vulnerable to attack. The same restriction governs arrival: a ship leaves the Warp at the edge of a system, not at its worlds.

The moment of entry is called translation. The hull is sealed, the Geller field is raised, and the drive is brought up to power. The ship sinks into the Warp as a stone sinks into water. Inside the field the crew lives as it does in realspace, but outside the hull the Immaterium presses at the shield, and the sensors show what the Navigator describes as a sea of colour and faces.

Navigators and the Astronomican

A ship in the Warp cannot be steered by instruments, because the Warp has no fixed directions. The task falls to a Navigator, a member of one of the noble houses of the [[Navis Nobilite]]. A Navigator has a mutation, the third eye, that lets him look directly into the Warp without being destroyed by it. He finds the way by the light of the [[Astronomican]], the psychic beacon projected from [[Terra]] by the [[Emperor of Mankind]] on the [[Golden Throne]]. To a Navigator the Astronomican is a bright point in the Warp, and from its position and the shape of the currents around it he judges the course. Without it, the Navigator is blind.

Navigator houses hold their charters from the [[Adeptus Terra]], and without a Navigator no ship of the Navy will make a long voyage. Their art is hereditary and jealously held. A good Navigator can often feel the currents ahead and warn of storms, and a ship with a gifted one may cut weeks from a crossing.

Time in the Warp

Time in the Warp has no fixed relation to time outside it. A journey of the same distance may take a week one time and a year the next. Ships have left a system and arrived at their destination before they set out, and others have emerged into realspace centuries after their crews believed they had entered the Warp. The effect is stronger in storms. Crews also age at their own rate, so a returning ship may carry men who are older or younger than the date suggests. The Imperium keeps its records in Terran Standard time, and every report from a distant world is understood to describe events that are already past.

Routes and jumps

Short jumps

A ship without a Navigator may make a short jump, a hop of a few light-years across a stable region of the Warp, in which no steering is needed beyond the setting of the drive. Such jumps are used by small system craft and by merchants who cannot afford a Navigator, and they are exceedingly dangerous. A ship that jumps blind has no way to avoid a storm, and may emerge in the wrong place, or deep within a star.

Stable routes

Over the millennia the Navy and the Navigator houses have recorded the more reliable courses between worlds. These are called warp routes. They follow currents that stay predictable for decades or centuries, and an Imperial trade lane or pilgrimage route will often follow such a course for its whole length. A warp route is never safe: a stable current may fail in a night, and a route that served for a thousand years may close. Imperial charts therefore carry a date of survey, and ships are warned to treat any chart older than a century with suspicion.

Worlds that lie on a stable route prosper. Worlds that lie off it may be visited once in a century, or not at all. The [[Black Ships]] that collect the psykers of the Imperium follow such routes, and a world that falls out of its schedule through the shifting of the Warp may not be visited again for generations.

The Great Rift

Main article: [[Great Rift]]

The opening of the [[Great Rift]], also called the Cicatrix Maledictum, in 999.M41, was the greatest single blow to Warp travel since the Horus Heresy. The Rift is a wound in realspace and the Warp that crosses the galaxy from the [[Eye of Terror]] to the [[Hadex Anomaly]] and divides the Imperium in two. The light of the Astronomican cannot be seen across it, and in the [[Imperium Nihilus]] it can scarcely be seen at all. In the years that followed, thousands of worlds were swallowed by the [[Noctis Aeterna]], the long night, and a large share of the ships that entered the Warp in those regions were lost or scattered.

Navigators of the Imperium Nihilus have had to find their way without a beacon, by the memory of old charts and the guidance of the stronger houses. The fleets of the [[Indomitus Crusade]] crossed the Rift by routes that the Mechanicus and the Navy revised after the first losses, and the storms of the Rift, which are more violent than those of the old Warp, have forced changes in the settings of the drives and in the rites of the Geller fields.

The one reliable passage through the Rift is the [[Nachmund Gauntlet]], a narrow lane of calm between [[Vigilus]] on the northern side and Sangua Terra on the southern. It was found as the storms settled in the year after the Rift opened, and it has since been the Imperium's principal crossing. Its fortresses are the target of constant attack by the forces of Chaos.

Alternatives

The Webway

Main article: [[Webway]]

The [[Aeldari]] and the [[Drukhari]] travel through the [[Webway]], a labyrinth of tunnels that runs between realspace and the Warp and is not subject to its storms. The Webway allows rapid passage across the galaxy, and its gates are guarded by ancient powers. The Emperor built a Webway beneath the [[Imperial Palace]], whose unfinished state became one of the central matters of the [[Webway War]] in 004–014.M31. Imperial forces have rarely used the Webway since, and the Inquisition regards its use as a matter for the [[Ordo Xenos]].

Necron dolmen gates

The [[Necrons]] do not travel in the Warp. They use dolmen gates, portals that open on a network of tunnels through the structure of realspace itself, and they build their ships with sublight drives that move at great speed. Necron tomb worlds are linked by these gates, which allow an army to appear without warning. Imperial reports of such a gate state that it opens in a tall arch of black stone, and that an army may emerge from it within moments.

Ork travel

[[Orks]] cross the Warp in crude vessels with improvised drives and no Geller field that the Mechanicus can recognise. They have no Navigators, and the Navy has no explanation for their survival, beyond the belief that the collective psychic force of an Ork fleet, the [[Waaagh!]], holds off the daemons of the Warp. Ork fleets arrive in the wrong place or in vast numbers. The [[War of the Beast]] and the [[Wars for Armageddon]] both began with fleets that Imperial scouts did not see until they were at the edge of the system.

Notable disasters

The Imperium has lost many fleets in the Warp. The following are among the best recorded.

  • The Eisenstein, 005.M31. The frigate fled [[Isstvan III]] with a damaged Geller field and carried the first warning of [[Horus]]'s treachery. It survived, with daemons loose aboard.
  • The Ruinstorm, 007–014.M31. The storm raised by the [[Word Bearers]] at Calth divided the galaxy in two and made travel between Terra and the rest of the Imperium almost impossible for most of the war.
  • The Aletris, c. 656.M40.Estimated from the state of the wreck; no record gives an exact year. Hadex's flagship was lost to the [[Hadex Anomaly]]. Its wreck was found by the Navy in 779.M41 at the edge of the ring of derelicts.
  • The Noctis Aeterna, 999.M41. The long night that followed the opening of the Great Rift darkened the Astronomican across the greater part of the galaxy and wrecked or scattered many fleets, among them ships of the early Indomitus fleets.
