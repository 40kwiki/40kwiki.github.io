A purity seal is a seal of wax, usually red, stamped with a sacred device and hung with strips of parchment on which prayers, litanies or oaths are written. Purity seals are affixed to the armour of the [[Adeptus Astartes]] and the [[Adepta Sororitas]], to the weapons and vehicles of the [[Astra Militarum]], to the hulls of warships and war machines, and to holy books, relics and doors throughout the [[Imperium of Mankind]]. To the [[Imperial Cult]] a seal is a blessing: it marks its bearer or object as purified and protected against corruption, and declares that the rites required for it have been performed.

In the [[Space Marine Chapter|Space Marine Chapters]], a purity seal is not strictly an honour but a blessing given by the Chapter's [[Chaplain]]s before battle, recording the oaths a warrior has sworn and the rites performed over him. Some Chapters replace the wax seal with a permanent electrum casting after the battle, so that a veteran's armour may carry the record of many campaigns. Seals are among the most recognisable features of Imperial war-gear, and in the [[41st millennium]] they are found on almost every holy or martial object of the Imperium.

History

Seals of wax and parchment were used in the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31) to mark oaths, most famously the oaths of moment sworn by the warriors of the [[Space Marine Legions]] before a campaign, which were written on parchment and fixed to their armour. Under the secular [[Imperial Truth]], such seals were records of a promise, not religious objects. During the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31), believers of the [[Lectitio Divinitatus]] in the loyal armies began to treat them as charms against the powers of the [[Warp]].

After the Heresy, the spread of the [[Imperial Cult]] and the emergence of the Ecclesiarchy in the 32nd millennium gave the seals their present meaning. The [[Liber Ecclesiasticus]] (M37) lists the forms of the purity seal and the litanies proper to each, and the [[Adepta Sororitas]], whose orders became the Ecclesiarchy's armed force after the [[Decree Passive]] of c. 288.M36, adopted them as a standard part of their armour. By the 41st millennium the purity seal had become a universal sign of Imperial devotion.The date at which seals took on a religious meaning is estimated from the sequence of events. Records of the Great Scouring (c. 014–020.M31) already describe soldiers wearing them "against the witch and the daemon".

In the [[Era Indomitus]], the warriors of the [[Primaris Space Marines|Primaris]] Chapters of the [[Ultima Founding]], created by [[Belisarius Cawl]] and revealed in 999.M41, were sealed according to the customs of their parent Chapters when they joined them, and the Chaplains of the [[Indomitus Crusade]] have been recorded issuing purity seals by the thousand before the great battles of the crusade.

Form

A purity seal has two parts. The seal itself is a disc of wax, usually red, pressed with a device: most commonly the [[Aquila (symbol)|Aquila]], a skull, the letter I of the [[Inquisition]], a Chapter badge or a saint's sign. Below it hang one or more streamers of parchment or vellum, on which a priest or Chaplain has written litanies, verses of scripture, blessings, battle-oaths or a record of the bearer's deeds. Seals are fixed to armour or objects with wax, adhesive or rivets.

Some seals are made to last: a permanent seal is cast in electrum or another metal, with its litanies engraved on metal tabs. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] uses seals of its own on machines and weapons, inscribed with rites of maintenance and appeasement of the [[machine spirit]].

Use

Adeptus Astartes

Main article: [[Adeptus Astartes]]

Before a campaign, a Chapter's Chaplains pass through the ranks chanting litanies and invocations, marking chosen warriors for special blessings. The blessing is written on the streamers and fixed to the warrior's armour with the seal. Practices differ between Chapters. In some, a seal is earned by deed; in others every battle-brother receives one; in the [[Black Templars]] and other zealous Chapters, seals cover armour and weapons in great numbers.

Adepta Sororitas and Ecclesiarchy

The Sisters of Battle wear purity seals as a matter of course, often in large numbers, each bearing a prayer or the name of a martyred sister. Priests of the [[Adeptus Ministorum]] attach seals to the weapons and banners of the faithful before battle, and to the doors of churches, reliquaries and the tombs of [[Imperial saint|saints]].

Astra Militarum and the Navy

Regimental priests and [[Commissar]]s attach seals to tanks, artillery and banners, and individual soldiers buy or are given seals to wear. Warships of the [[Imperial Navy]] carry great seals on their hulls and on the doors of their sanctuaries, and the void-shield generators of many ships are sealed against warp intrusion.

The Inquisition

Main article: [[Inquisition]]

Inquisitors use seals to mark their property, to close forbidden archives and to seal the prisons of daemons and heretics. A seal of the [[Ordo Malleus]] on a door or a book is a warning that what lies behind it must not be opened.

Related honours

Purity seals are often worn together with other Imperial honours, which Imperial heraldry treats separately:

  • Crux Terminatus: the badge of a warrior entitled to wear [[Tactical Dreadnought Armour|Terminator armour]].
  • Iron Halo: a field generator and honour borne by Space Marine officers.
  • Imperialis: a winged skull, the badge of loyalist valour since the Heresy (see [[Aquila (symbol)#Forms and variants|Aquila]]).
  • Imperial Laurel, Marksman's Honour and Iron Skull: decorations for leadership, marksmanship and long service.
