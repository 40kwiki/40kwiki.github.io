The Moirae Schism was a religious schism and civil war within the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] in the [[35th millennium]]. It began on the minor [[forge world]] of Moirae, whose priesthood claimed to read the future of humanity in the fluctuations of the [[Astronomican]], and spread through the forges, the [[Titan Legions]] and the Space Marine Chapters bound to the Mechanicus. It was the most divisive doctrinal conflict to strike the Cult Mechanicus since the [[Horus Heresy]].

The orthodox priesthood of [[Mars]] declared the Moirate teachings heresy and destroyed Moirae with a rectification fleet, but the doctrine had already spread. The war that followed was fought in forge worlds, in Titan Legions and in the void, and its greatest battle, a war of salvage in the Gulf of Black Shadows, lasted for centuries. The schism also divided the [[Iron Hands]]. A third of the Chapter embraced the Moirate [[Ursarkar Creed|creed]] and was exiled; its descendants were later recognised as a Chapter in their own right, the [[Sons of Medusa]].

The schism coincided with the [[Nova Terra Interregnum]], when the Imperium was divided between two courts, and Imperial historians count it among the troubles of that age. Martian records say that more than two thousand years passed before the Moirate doctrine was considered purged.

Background

In the early [[35th millennium]] the [[Nova Terra Interregnum|Ur-Council]] of [[Nova Terra]] repudiated the authority of the [[High Lords of Terra]] and claimed the [[Segmentum Pacificus]]. The division of the Imperium ran through the [[Adeptus Terra|Adepta]] as well as between worlds, and the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]], which had never been wholly united in doctrine, was weakened by the rivalries of its forges. Several schisms arose in this period. The Moirae Schism was the most bitter of them.

Moirae was a minor [[Forge world|forge world]] of no great industrial weight. It was, however, the source of the [[Standard Template Construct|STC]] data from which most of the Imperium's Whirlwind Hyperios air-defence vehicles were then built, and its archives were consulted by many other forges.

The Moirate doctrine

The [[Ursarkar Creed|creed]] of Moirae was based on the work of a triad of tech-mystics enshrined within the forge's great data-looms. The triad claimed to have found predictive patterns in the minute fluctuations of the beam of the Astronomican. They called these the Gematric patterns, and taught that they held the words of the [[Omnissiah]], from which the future of the human race could be read.

The triad recorded the results of their calculations in encrypted archives, and copies spread quickly through the sects of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Cult Mechanicus]]. Their contents went beyond prophecy. The archives contained veiled references to the future overthrow of [[Mars]], and described a coming age in which the Cult Mechanicus and the [[Adeptus Ministorum|Ecclesiarchy]] would be merged into a single state religion of the Imperium. To the Martian priesthood this was sedition. To the Ecclesiarchy, which had no wish to share its [[Emperor of Mankind|God-Emperor]] with the Machine God, it was blasphemy.

Course of the schism

The destruction of Moirae

Civil war broke out between the orthodox priesthood and the radicals. The [[Fabricator-General]] sent a rectification fleet, and one of the first acts of the war was the destruction of Moirae, which was reduced to a cinder. Imperial records describe the destruction as an [[Exterminatus]]. The world is still a [[Planetary classification|dead world]].

The destruction of the source came too late. The doctrine had taken hold in sects of the priesthood, in [[Skitarii]] regiments and in [[Titan Legions]], and it spread from them to the [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] of the [[Adeptus Astartes]] that had close ties to the [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicus]]. Conflict between the Moirate adepts, the Martian orthodoxy and other radical sects flared up across the Imperium for the following centuries.

The Hyperios cipher

The Moirate tech-priests had built a hidden digital cipher into the Whirlwind Hyperios vehicles constructed from their STC data. On receiving the right code the machines powered down. The schismatics used the cipher against Imperial forces, and many Space Marine Chapters still refuse to deploy the Hyperios, though its pattern is known to most [[Forge world|forge worlds]] and Astartes forges.

The Gulf of Black Shadows

The largest and longest battle of the schism was fought in the nebula fields of the Gulf of Black Shadows. Mining wars between rival forge worlds there grew into great fleet actions. The wrecks of more than ten thousand ships, from mining craft to warships, came to drift among the gas clouds. The fighting did not end when the fleets were spent. Salvage crews fought on among the wrecks for centuries in boarding actions, each side trying to recover valuable technology, and whole maniples of [[Legio Cybernetica|battle robots]] were captured, reprogrammed and turned against their former masters.

Ghosaris

On the world of Ghosaris the [[Adeptus Custodes]] intervened against an army of the [[Dark Mechanicum]] and destroyed it, the venerable Contemptor [[Dreadnought]] Uriaxes fighting at their head. Whether the Dark Mechanicum host had any connection with the Moirate schismatics is not known. The Custodes' record of the battle names it as part of the schism without explaining why.

The Xarisians

Towards the end of the schism a radical sub-sect of the Moirate [[Ursarkar Creed|creed]], the Xarisians, turned to outright techno-heresy in an attempt to win the war. They were hunted as [[Heretek|hereteks]] even by their own creed, slaughtered and their creations destroyed. One survived: the great laser destructor called the Diamonas, which is said to have passed out of Imperial hands and to be fought over among [[Dark Angels|the fallen]] houses of the [[Chaos Knights]].

The Iron Hands

Main article: [[Iron Hands]]

No Chapter was more deeply affected than the [[Iron Hands]]. The Moirate doctrine was embraced by a large part of the Chapter's brethren and championed among its clan companies by the Omnissian mystic Iron Father Setol Sollex. The Chapter came close to civil war.

Clan Raukaan held to the Chapter's established beliefs. Under Iron [[Chaplain]] Furnous and Iron Captain Hekkan it was one of the few clan companies in which not a single battle-brother was corrupted by the new doctrine. At the Battle of Kamjada its Vindicators and Predators fought rebellious [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicus]] forces in [[Terminator armour|Cataphractii]] armour, and the presence among the rebels of a small cadre of warriors from Clan Borrgos remained a cause of bitterness between the two clans for centuries.

Open war within the Chapter was averted by the Great Clan Council, which imposed a settlement. The minority who had accepted the Moirate [[Ursarkar Creed|creed]] were exiled, and both sides swore a binding oath never to raise their hands against each other. About a third of the Chapter left, joined in time by a few brethren of Iron Hands Successor [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] who shared their beliefs. Not every Successor was so lenient; the [[Red Talons]] are recorded as having destroyed their own dissidents rather than let them live.

The exiles thought of themselves as Iron Hands still. They kept the Chapter's heraldry with small changes, organised themselves into three new clan companies, continued to fight the Imperium's enemies from their fleet and tithed their [[gene-seed]] to the Imperial authorities as before. They outlived the schism that had made them.

Aftermath

The schism was ended by the slow attrition of the Moirate forges and sects and by the reabsorption of those adepts who recanted. Martian records say that it took more than two thousand standard years before the doctrine was considered fully purged.

The exiled [[Iron Hands]] were judged at the beginning of the [[Age of Redemption]], when the [[High Lords of Terra|High Lords]] reassembled the Imperium after the [[Age of Apostasy]]. Their records and [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] were examined in detail and found loyal and untainted, and by an almost unique edict they were ratified as a Chapter of the [[Adeptus Astartes]] under the name [[Sons of Medusa]]. Parts of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] protested, and several [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] had grave misgivings. The Iron Hands treat the Sons of Medusa with cold civility at best. In the late [[39th millennium]] the Sons of Medusa built a chain of fortified void stations in the abandoned mining system of Taelus, one day's travel through the Warp from the [[Planetary classification|dead world]] of Moirae.

Legacy

The Moirae Schism left the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] more suspicious of prophecy and of any attempt to read meaning in the working of the Astronomican, and more hostile to any suggestion that the Cult Mechanicus and the [[Imperial Cult]] might be reconciled. It is one of several schisms, with the [[Schism of Mars]] in the [[Horus Heresy]], that Martian doctrine cites as proof of the danger of heterodox belief. The distrust of the Whirlwind Hyperios among the [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]], the enmity between the [[Iron Hands]] and the [[Sons of Medusa]], and the hulks of the Gulf of Black Shadows remain as its visible traces.
