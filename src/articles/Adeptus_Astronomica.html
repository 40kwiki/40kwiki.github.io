The Adeptus Astronomica is the [[Adeptus Terra|Adeptus]] of the [[Imperium of Mankind|Imperium]] charged with maintaining the [[Astronomican]], the psychic beacon by which the [[Navis Nobilite|Navigators]] steer the Imperium's ships through the [[Warp]]. It is an order of [[psyker]]s and their hereditary servants, based in the Forbidden Fortress within the [[Imperial Palace]] on [[Terra]], and led by the Master of the Astronomican, who sits among the [[High Lords of Terra]].

Its central duty is a sacrifice. The psykers it trains, the Chosen, join a choir of about ten thousand in the Chamber of the Astronomican, where the mind of the [[Emperor of Mankind|Emperor]] draws on their strength to project the beacon. A Chosen lives only a few months once called to the Chamber. The order is therefore fed constantly with new psykers by the [[Black Ships]] of the [[Adeptus Astra Telepathica]]. Since the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 the beacon has been hidden from half the galaxy, and the Astronomica's burden has grown heavier than at any time in its history.

History

Founding

Before the [[Great Crusade]] left the Sol System in the late 30th millennium, the Emperor ordered the construction of the Astronomican beneath the Himalazian mountains, as a focus through which his psychic power could be projected into the Warp as a navigational beacon. At first the Emperor sustained it alone. The duty was later shifted to a choir of psykers, and the body formed to train and serve that choir became the Adeptus Astronomica. It was founded alongside the astropathic corps that grew into the Adeptus Astra Telepathica, which the Emperor had created in the last months of the [[Unification Wars]].

Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

During the [[Horus Heresy]], the Astronomican's light failed for a time in parts of the galaxy, both through the [[Ruinstorm]] raised by the Word Bearers in 007.M31 and through the strain placed on the Emperor during the [[Webway War]]. When the Emperor rose from the Throne to fight in the last phase of the Siege, [[Malcador the Sigillite]] took his place on it and kept the beacon and the Palace wards alight at the cost of his life. Records of the Choir in those years are sealed. The Astronomican nevertheless survived the [[Siege of Terra]] in 014.M31, and the Astronomica was confirmed among the institutions of the Adeptus Terra after the Emperor's interment on the [[Golden Throne]].

The long millennia

For ten thousand years the Astronomica has kept the beacon burning without recorded interruption at Terra. Its psykers have died in their thousands each year in the Chamber, and its Masters have sat among the High Lords, usually silent on matters outside their charge. The Astronomica has rarely played a part in the factional struggles of the Senatorum; Imperial historians attribute this to the simple fact that no faction can rule an Imperium without the beacon.

Era Indomitus

Further information: [[Era Indomitus]]

The [[Fall of Cadia]] and the opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 hid the Astronomican from the [[Imperium Nihilus]] and made it dimmer and harder to follow across the [[Imperium Sanctus]]. During the [[Noctis Aeterna]] it could not be seen at all across much of the galaxy. The failing of the Golden Throne has made the beacon still more costly to maintain, and the Astronomica has had to call more psykers to the Chamber. Under the reforms of the Lord Commander [[Roboute Guilliman]], the Black Ships' tithes have been increased, and the protection of the Astronomican has been made a standing priority of the [[Indomitus Crusade]]. The suppression fields of the [[Pariah Nexus]] in the early 42nd millennium posed a further threat to the psykers on whom it depends.

Organisation

The Master

The Master of the Astronomican heads the order and represents it in the Senatorum Imperialis. Unlike most High Lords, the Master is outranked in the order's own hierarchy by the Chosen, whose sacrifice is regarded as the order's highest honour.

Acolytes, Chosen and Instructors

Young psykers judged too undisciplined for the Astra Telepathica, but strong enough to serve, are handed over to the Astronomica as acolytes. They are taught to control their powers, and they study the Lore of the Astronomican, philosophy and the nature of the Warp. Those who reach the mystic state the order seeks become the Chosen. Their heads are shaved, they wear yellow robes and a scarlet badge, and they spend the rest of their lives in prayer until they are called to the Chamber. Those who fall short become Instructors and High Instructors, who run the order's training, or join the administration of the Forbidden Fortress. Members of the order otherwise wear blue hooded robes.

The Chamber of the Astronomican

The Chamber lies beneath the Himalazian mountains, in the heart of the Palace. Ten thousand Chosen serve in it at a time. Their energies are drawn on by the Emperor's mind, and in a few months each is drained to a husk and dies. The Imperium also sacrifices, separately, hundreds of psykers a day to sustain the Emperor himself on the Golden Throne. The two sacrifices are often confused in popular belief; the Astronomica's own doctrine distinguishes them.

Servants and travel

The order's day-to-day work is done by functionaries and hereditary servants who are not psykers. Instructors and functionaries may travel off Terra; the Chosen never leave it. Even the judges of the [[Adeptus Arbites]] and the agents of the [[Inquisition]] must be invited to enter the Forbidden Fortress lawfully.

Relations with other Adepta

The Astronomica depends on the Adeptus Astra Telepathica for its recruits and on the [[Sisters of Silence]] and the [[Adeptus Custodes]] for the security of the Palace. Its closest client is the Navis Nobilite, whose Houses cannot cross the galaxy without the beacon. The [[Adeptus Ministorum|Ecclesiarchy]] regards the Astronomica as a holy order and the Chosen as martyrs; the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] treats the beacon as an engineering work beyond its understanding. The Astronomica itself, by long tradition, offers no doctrine on the Emperor's nature beyond its duty.
