A death world is a class of planet of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] whose environment is so dangerous that an unprepared human is likely to die there within days. The danger may come from predatory wildlife, poisonous or carnivorous plants, extremes of heat or cold, volcanic activity, radiation or disease. The class is recorded by the [[Administratum]] to warn travellers and to set the tithe of the world's inhabitants.

Death worlds are usually thinly populated, but the people who survive on them are among the hardest in [[Milky Way|the galaxy]]. The [[Astra Militarum]] raises some of its most feared regiments from death worlds, above all the [[Catachan Jungle Fighters]], and several [[Space Marine Chapter]]s recruit from them, among them the [[Space Wolves]] of [[Fenris]] and the [[Blood Angels]], whose aspirants come from the tribes of [[Baal Secundus]].

Characteristics

No single environment defines a death world. Imperial surveys distinguish several kinds:

  • Jungle worlds, where every plant and animal is hostile, such as [[Catachan]], whose creatures include the Catachan devil and carnivorous vines.
  • Ice and storm worlds, such as [[Fenris]], with long winters, seas that freeze and thaw, volcanic eruptions and predators such as the [[Hive Fleet Kraken|kraken]] and the Fenrisian wolf.
  • Radiation deserts, such as [[Baal|Baal Secundus]], poisoned by ancient wars and inhabited by [[Mutant|mutant]] beasts and scattered tribes.
  • Volcanic worlds, such as [[Nocturne]], scarred by earthquakes and a fiery sky whenever its moon draws close.
  • Ruined worlds, such as [[Krieg]], made deadly by human action, in Krieg's case a nuclear war of its own in M40.

The class may be held together with another. Fenris is also recorded as a [[feral world]], Nocturne and Baal Secundus as Chapter homeworlds, and [[Caliban]], before its destruction in M31, as a death world with a [[feudal world|feudal]] society.

Inhabitants

The people of death worlds survive by constant vigilance and by skills learned in childhood. On [[Catachan]] most children die before adulthood, and those who live become hunters and soldiers of great strength and endurance. On [[Fenris]] the tribes migrate between shifting islands and fight for every scrap of land. On [[Baal|Baal Secundus]], before the [[Devastation of Baal]] (999.M41), tribes of humans lived alongside [[Mutant|mutant]] clans in the radioactive deserts. Death-worlders are often distrustful of outsiders and of the authority of the Imperium, but are loyal to their own people.

Military value

The Imperium values death worlds chiefly for their soldiers. The tithe of a death world is usually paid in regiments rather than goods, and death-world regiments are prized for operations in hostile terrain. The [[Catachan Jungle Fighters]] are the best known, and the [[Death Korps of Krieg]] are renowned for their acceptance of losses. The [[Administratum|Departmento Munitorum]] also uses some death worlds as training grounds for other regiments and for the [[Militarum Tempestus]].

[[Adeptus Astartes|Space Marine]] [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] regard death worlds as the finest recruiting grounds. Their aspirants have already survived trials that kill most of their peers, which is held to make them more likely to survive implantation with [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]]. Some Chapters chose their homeworlds for this reason; others, such as the [[Space Wolves]] and the [[Blood Angels]], inherited them from their [[Primarch|primarchs]], who were raised on [[Fenris]] and [[Baal|Baal Secundus]].

History

Many death worlds were colonised in the [[Dark Age of Technology]], when terraforming and technology made them habitable, and became deadly when that technology failed during the [[Age of Strife]]. Others, such as [[Baal|Baal Secundus]] and [[Siege of Vraks|Krieg]], were made deadly by war. The class was used by the [[Administratum]] from the census of the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31) onwards. Since 999.M41 several death worlds have been fought over in the wars of the [[Era Indomitus]], and the recruiting worlds of [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] such as the [[Blood Angels]] have suffered severe losses.

Survival and study

The [[Administratum]] discourages settlement of death worlds by civilians, and those who must travel to them are expected to take guides from the native population. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] and the [[Inquisition]] sometimes maintain research stations on death worlds to study their fauna and flora, some of which yield valuable compounds or serve as weapons. The [[Ordo Xenos]] watches death worlds whose life is suspected to be of alien design, and several have been placed under quarantine. A few death worlds are thought to be lethal because of ancient weapons of the [[Dark Age of Technology]] still active in their soil or air.
