The Imperium of Mankind, usually called the Imperium of Man or simply the Imperium, is the vast interstellar empire of humanity in the fictional universe of [[Warhammer 40,000]], the science-fantasy wargame published by [[Games Workshop]]. Spanning more than a million worlds across the [[Milky Way]], it is the largest and most populous power in the setting and the faction to which most of the game's protagonists belong.

The Imperium was founded at the end of the 30th millennium by the [[Emperor of Mankind]], who united a war-torn [[Terra]] in the [[Unification Wars]], allied with the [[Mechanicum]] of [[Mars]] and then led the [[Great Crusade]] to reconquer the scattered worlds of humanity. The crusade ended in the [[Horus Heresy]], a galactic civil war in which half of the Emperor's [[Space Marine Legions]] turned to the [[Chaos Gods]]. Mortally wounded in the final battle, the Emperor was interred on the [[Golden Throne]], a life-sustaining machine on which he has sat, neither alive nor dead, for more than ten thousand years.

In the [[41st millennium]] the Imperium is a stagnant, superstitious and brutally authoritarian theocracy. It is governed in the Emperor's name by the [[High Lords of Terra]] and an immense bureaucracy, the [[Adeptus Terra]], worships the Emperor as a god through the [[Imperial Cult]], and survives by perpetual war against [[xenos]], heretics, mutants and the daemonic powers of the [[Warp]]. Its technology is preserved by rote rather than understood, and dissent, [[psyker|psychic mutation]] and alien contact are punished with death. At the close of the 41st millennium the opening of the [[Great Rift]] split the Imperium in two; the resurrected [[primarch]] [[Roboute Guilliman]] now leads the [[Indomitus Crusade]] to hold it together.

Out of universe, the Imperium is the setting's central faction and the clearest expression of its tone: a satirical portrait of a fascist, theocratic and bureaucratic state that is presented, without irony from its own citizens, as humanity's last hope. The setting's tagline describes it plainly: "In the grim darkness of the far future, there is only war."

Etymology

The name is Latin, in keeping with the setting's use of [[High Gothic]], a Latin-flavoured liturgical language, for official titles. Imperium denotes both "empire" and the authority to command; Imperial documents use the form Imperium of Man, while rulebooks and older sources favour Imperium of Mankind. Citizens rarely use either and simply call it "the Imperium". Inhabitants of the Imperium are "Imperials" or "Imperial citizens", though most identify far more strongly with their home world, regiment or hive than with the Imperium as a whole.

The empire built during the [[Great Crusade]] is sometimes distinguished in sources as the Imperium of the Great Crusade or "the Emperor's Imperium", to contrast its secular [[Imperial Truth]] with the theocracy that followed the [[Horus Heresy]].

History

Age of Strife and Unification

By the late 2nd millennium humanity had spread across the galaxy using [[Warp]] travel during a period remembered as the [[Dark Age of Technology]], when thinking machines and [[Standard Template Construct]]s let colonies build anything they needed. The rebellion of the [[Men of Iron]], the rise of [[psyker]]s and the birth of the Chaos god [[Slaanesh]], whose emergence tore open the [[Eye of Terror]] and wrecked the [[Aeldari]] empire, brought on the [[Age of Strife]] (also called Old Night). Warp storms isolated human worlds for some five thousand years, and Terra itself collapsed into warring techno-barbarian states.

Late in the 30th millennium the being later known as the Emperor revealed himself on Terra and waged the [[Unification Wars]], defeating the planet's warlords with his genetically engineered [[Thunder Warriors]]. He then concluded the [[Treaty of Olympus]] with the [[Mechanicum]] of [[Mars]], which supplied the weapons and ships of the coming crusade in exchange for autonomy and recognition of the Emperor as an aspect of their own [[Omnissiah|Machine God]]. The union of Terra and Mars is traditionally treated as the founding of the Imperium.

Great Crusade

With the [[Space Marine Legions]] created from the genetic template of his twenty [[primarch]]s, the Emperor launched the [[Great Crusade]] in about 798.M30. Over some two centuries the crusade rediscovered thousands of human worlds, brought them into compliance by negotiation or force, and exterminated alien empires deemed a threat. The primarchs, who had been scattered across the galaxy as infants, were found one by one and given command of their Legions.

The crusade imperium was officially secular. The Emperor promoted the [[Imperial Truth]], a doctrine of reason that rejected all gods, including himself, and banned religion. After the victory at [[Ullanor]] he named [[Horus]], primarch of the [[Luna Wolves]], Warmaster and returned to Terra, leaving the crusade in Horus's hands and creating the [[Council of Terra]], a civilian body of officials that would later become the High Lords.

Horus Heresy

Corrupted by the [[Chaos Gods]], Horus rebelled in 005.M31. Nine of the eighteen known Legions followed him: the [[Sons of Horus]], [[Word Bearers]], [[World Eaters]], [[Death Guard]], [[Emperor's Children]], [[Iron Warriors]], [[Night Lords]], [[Alpha Legion]] and [[Thousand Sons]]. The opening atrocities at [[Isstvan III]] and the [[Drop Site Massacre]] at [[Isstvan V]] were followed by seven years of war across the galaxy.

The war ended at the [[Siege of Terra]] in 014.M31. When Horus lowered his flagship's shields, the Emperor teleported aboard with his bodyguard and killed him, but was himself mortally wounded. The primarch [[Sanguinius]] died in the same battle. [[Rogal Dorn]] carried the Emperor's broken body to the Golden Throne, the machine originally built to support the Emperor's secret [[Imperial Webway|webway project]] beneath the [[Imperial Palace]]. [[Malcador the Sigillite]], the Emperor's regent, had already given his life holding the Throne during the siege.

Great Scouring and the Codex reforms

In the [[Great Scouring]] that followed, the loyalist Legions drove the traitors into the Eye of Terror. To prevent any one commander holding such power again, [[Roboute Guilliman]], primarch of the [[Ultramarines]], wrote the [[Codex Astartes]], which broke each Legion into [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] of about a thousand warriors in the [[Second Founding]]. Fleets and armies were separated into the [[Imperial Navy]] and the [[Imperial Guard]], and provinces were divided so that no governor controlled both troops and ships.

The Council of Terra became the [[High Lords of Terra]], and the worship of the Emperor, banned under the Imperial Truth, spread into the official [[Imperial Cult]]. Over the following centuries the [[Ecclesiarchy]] grew into one of the most powerful institutions in the Imperium. Guilliman held the office of [[Lord Commander of the Imperium]] until he was mortally wounded by his traitor brother [[Fulgrim]] and placed in stasis on [[Macragge]].

Age of the Imperium

The ten thousand years between the Heresy and the 41st millennium are poorly recorded and frequently contradictory, even in Imperial archives. Major events include:

  • The [[War of the Beast]] (M32), an [[Orks|Ork]] invasion that reached the [[Sol System]], killed most of the High Lords and led to the creation of the [[Third Founding]] Chapters.
  • The [[Nova Terra Interregnum]] (M35), a centuries-long schism in which a rival Administratum on [[Nova Terra]] claimed the [[Segmentum Pacificus]] and western reaches.
  • The [[Age of Apostasy]] (M36), when [[Goge Vandire]] seized both the [[Administratum]] and the Ecclesiarchy, beginning the [[Reign of Blood]]. He was overthrown by the [[Daughters of the Emperor]], the forerunners of the [[Adepta Sororitas]], and the preacher [[Sebastian Thor]]. Thor's reforms, including the [[Decree Passive]], barred the Ecclesiarchy from keeping "men under arms".
  • The [[Gothic War]] (139–151.M41), a twelve-year naval campaign against [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] for control of the [[Blackstone Fortress]]es.

Time of Ending

The final centuries of the 41st millennium, called the [[Time of Ending]], brought a series of existential threats. The [[Tyranids]] arrived from beyond the galaxy with [[Hive Fleet Behemoth]], defeated at the [[Battle of Macragge]] in 745.M41, followed by [[Hive Fleet Kraken|Kraken]] and [[Hive Fleet Leviathan|Leviathan]]. The [[Necrons]] awoke from their tomb worlds, the [[T'au Empire]] expanded on the [[Eastern Fringe]], and [[Ghazghkull Mag Uruk Thraka|Ghazghkull Thraka]] invaded the hive world of [[Armageddon]] for a second and third time.

In 999.M41 Abaddon's [[13th Black Crusade]] broke through the [[Cadian Gate]]. When Abaddon rammed a Blackstone Fortress into [[Cadia]], the fortress world that had guarded the Eye of Terror for ten millennia was destroyed, and a chain of warp storms spread across the galaxy.

Era Indomitus

The storms joined into the [[Great Rift]] (Cicatrix Maledictum), a scar of warp energy running across the galaxy from the Eye of Terror to the [[Hadex Anomaly]]. It cut the Imperium into the [[Imperium Sanctus]] on Terra's side and the [[Imperium Nihilus]] beyond it, where the Astronomican can no longer be seen and countless worlds have gone dark.

At the same time the [[Aeldari]] of the [[Ynnari]], working with [[Archmagos]] [[Belisarius Cawl]], revived Roboute Guilliman. Guilliman reached Terra, spoke with the Emperor in the Throne Room, and resumed the office of Lord Commander. He launched the [[Indomitus Crusade]] with fleets reinforced by Cawl's [[Primaris Space Marines]] of the [[Ultima Founding]], fought the [[Plague Wars]] against [[Mortarion]] in [[Ultramar]], and began reforming the High Lords. In the early 42nd millennium the primarch [[Lion El'Jonson]] of the [[Dark Angels]] also returned, and the Imperium faced new campaigns against Hive Fleet Leviathan, the Necrons of the [[Pariah Nexus]] and the Chaos-aligned forge-daemon [[Vashtorr]].

Geography

The Imperium claims the whole galaxy but controls only a scattered fraction of its stars. Its worlds lie along stable warp routes, separated by vast regions of unexplored space, alien empires, dead zones and warp storms. Distances are measured not in light-years but in the time a voyage takes through the Warp, which can vary from days to centuries.

For administration and defence the galaxy is divided into five [[Segmentum|Segmentae Majoris]], each governed from a fortress world that houses its fleet command. Segmentae are subdivided into [[sector]]s and sub-sectors.

Imperium Sanctus and Imperium Nihilus

Since the opening of the Great Rift the Imperium has been divided into two halves. The Imperium Sanctus, which contains Terra and most of the southern galaxy, still receives the light of the Astronomican and the attention of the High Lords. The Imperium Nihilus lies beyond the Rift, cut off from Terra; its worlds are isolated, besieged and forced to fend for themselves, and many are presumed lost. Crossing the Rift is possible only at a few unstable passages, such as the [[Nachmund Gauntlet]].

Government and politics

In theory the Imperium is an absolute monarchy: every law is issued in the Emperor's name and every official answers to him. In practice the Emperor has not issued a recorded command since his interment. Power is exercised by the [[High Lords of Terra]] and by the many [[Adeptus Terra|Adepta]], each of which has its own hierarchy, territories and agenda, and which compete for influence through intrigue, schism and occasionally open war.

The Emperor

The [[Emperor of Mankind]] is the head of state, supreme commander and, to the Imperial Cult, a living god. His body is a desiccated corpse sustained by the Golden Throne; his mind is said to remain active, guiding the [[Astronomican]] and shielding humanity in the Warp. Sustaining him requires the sacrifice of hundreds of [[psyker]]s each day, whose souls are consumed by the Throne.Sources differ on the number of daily sacrifices. Older material cites "a thousand"; most recent books say only "hundreds". The [[Adeptus Custodes]] guard him, and the [[Sisters of Silence]] guard the Imperial Palace's null vaults.

High Lords of Terra

The [[Senatorum Imperialis]], whose members are the High Lords of Terra, is the supreme governing body. Traditionally it has twelve members drawn from the heads of the great organisations. Some seats are permanent, such as the [[Master of the Administratum]], the [[Ecclesiarch]], the [[Fabricator-General]] of Mars and the Inquisitorial Representative; others rotate among commanders such as the [[Lord High Admiral]], the [[Lord Commander Militant]] of the Astra Militarum, the [[Grand Provost Marshal]] of the Adeptus Arbites and the [[Paternoval Envoy]] of the Navigators.

Since his return, Roboute Guilliman has presided over the council as Lord Commander of the Imperium, regent in all but name. He has used the office to remove corrupt High Lords and to force sweeping reforms, a process the entrenched bureaucracy resists.

Planetary government

Day-to-day rule of most worlds lies with a [[Planetary Governor]] (formally an Imperial Commander), often a hereditary noble. Governors enjoy wide autonomy so long as they pay the tithe, maintain the Imperial Cult, hand over psykers to the Black Ships, defend their world and raise regiments when called. Local laws, customs and even forms of government vary enormously. A governor who fails in these duties, or grows too independent, risks deposition by the Adeptus Arbites, the Inquisition or a Space Marine Chapter.

Law and justice

Imperial law is enforced by the [[Adeptus Arbites]], whose [[Judge (Warhammer 40,000)|Judges]] concern themselves with crimes against the Imperium itself: rebellion, failure to pay the tithe, heresy and the like. Ordinary crime is a matter for local enforcers. Punishments are severe and frequently capital; condemned citizens may be sentenced to [[Penal Legion]]s, mind-wiped into [[servitor]]s or converted into [[Arco-flagellant]]s. The [[Inquisition (Warhammer 40,000)|Inquisition]] stands outside ordinary law entirely, and an [[Inquisitor]] may requisition any resource or order the destruction of a world by [[Exterminatus]].

Military

The Imperium is permanently at war, and much of its economy exists to supply its armies. Its forces are split among separate commands, a deliberate legacy of the post-Heresy reforms intended to stop any one commander from rebelling with an entire army.

The Imperial Guard, a vast army of ordinary human soldiers raised by tithe from every world, numbering in the billions. Famous regiments include the [[Cadian Shock Troops]], the [[Catachan Jungle Fighters]] and the [[Death Korps of Krieg]].

The warfleets of the Segmentae, which carry the Astra Militarum and fight the Imperium's battles in space.

Economy

The Imperial tithe

The Imperium has no single economy. Each world runs its own affairs, and the central government's interest is limited to the [[Imperial tithe]]: the share of produce, materials and soldiers every world must surrender. The tithe is set by the Administratum according to a world's classification and value, using a scale of grades that runs from Aptus Non (exempt, typically for frontier or devastated worlds) through the Solutio and Decuma grades to Exactis Extremis, the heaviest burden. Tithes may be paid in kind (grain, ore, munitions), in [[psyker]]s for the Black Ships, or in regiments for the Astra Militarum.

Interstellar trade exists but is slow and dangerous. Much of it is carried by [[Chartist Captain]]s of the merchant fleet and by [[Rogue Trader]]s, privileged dynasties whose [[Warrant of Trade]] lets them operate beyond the Imperium's borders. Local currencies are common; the [[Throne Gelt]] serves as a general Imperial unit of account.

Technology

Imperial technology is advanced, but almost none of it is understood. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] treats engineering as religious ritual, maintaining machines by litany and forbidding innovation outside approved templates. The highest prize of the [[Quest for Knowledge]] is the recovery of [[Standard Template Construct]] fragments from the Dark Age of Technology. Archeotech weapons and vehicles may be thousands of years old and irreplaceable. Thinking machines are banned outright, a legacy of the Men of Iron; [[servitor]]s, lobotomised cyborgs, perform the roles robots would.

Transport and communications

Interstellar travel depends on the [[Warp]]. Ships protected by [[Geller field]]s can jump between systems, but only a [[Navigator]], a member of the mutant Navis Nobilite, can steer them over long distances by sight of the [[Astronomican]], the psychic beacon projected from Terra. Without it, ships can make only short, cautious jumps. Communication over interstellar distances relies on [[Astropath]]s, telepaths who send messages as symbolic dream-images that are often garbled or lost.

Demographics

Population

No reliable census of the Imperium exists. The largest hive worlds hold tens of billions of people, and estimates for the whole Imperium run into trillions. Life for most citizens is short, poor and hard: endless labour in hives, manufactoria or fields, service in the Guard, and a lifetime of devotion to the God-Emperor.

Abhumans and mutants

Humanity in the Imperium includes several stable [[abhuman]] strains descended from colonists adapted to extreme environments. The Imperium accepts these under sanction, notably the huge, dim [[Ogryn]]s and the small, sharp-eyed [[Ratling]]s, both used as Guard auxiliaries. The [[Squat]]s, once a third prominent strain, were long believed destroyed; their kin returned in the 42nd millennium as the independent [[Leagues of Votann]]. Unsanctioned [[mutant]]s face discrimination, enslavement or extermination.

Psykers

Psychic ability is the Imperium's greatest danger and one of its essential resources. Untrained psykers can become gateways for daemons, so all must be surrendered to the [[Black Ships]]. A few are trained as [[sanctioned psyker]]s or astropaths; others join the [[Astronomican]] choir; the rest are sacrificed to the Golden Throne.

Languages

[[High Gothic]] is the language of law, liturgy and the upper ranks of the Adepta, and is unintelligible to most citizens. [[Low Gothic]], the common tongue, varies so much between worlds that it is often mutually unintelligible across the Imperium. Many worlds keep their own native languages.

Religion

The state religion is the [[Imperial Cult]], which worships the Emperor as the God-Emperor of Mankind, protector of humanity and saviour against the powers of Chaos. It began during the Great Crusade as the underground [[Lectitio Divinitatus]], despite the Emperor's own rejection of divinity, and became the Imperium's official faith after the Heresy. The [[Adeptus Ministorum]], or Ecclesiarchy, headed by the [[Ecclesiarch]] on Terra, administers it through a vast hierarchy of cardinals, confessors, priests and missionaries.

Belief and practice vary greatly between worlds. Many local cults venerate the Emperor in forms that the Ecclesiarchy tolerates so long as they recognise his supremacy; a world that rejects him faces war. The [[Cult Mechanicus]] of Mars worships the [[Omnissiah]], whom it holds the Emperor to embody, and is tolerated under the Treaty of Olympus. The Imperium venerates thousands of [[Imperial saint|saints]], and pilgrimage to shrine worlds is a major feature of Imperial life.

The worship of any other god, especially the [[Chaos Gods]], is heresy punishable by death. The Imperium's ultimate enemy is the [[Ruinous Powers]] of the Warp, whose cults are its constant internal threat.

Culture

Art and architecture

Imperial culture is dominated by religious and martial themes. Its architecture, sometimes called [[Gothic architecture (Warhammer 40,000)|Imperial Gothic]], combines vast scale with cathedral forms: buttresses, spires, statuary and skulls, a style carried through from planetary cathedrals to the hulls of warships. Common motifs include the [[Aquila (symbol)|Aquila]], the skull as a symbol of mortality and devotion, [[purity seal]]s, [[servo-skull]]s and devotional scripture.

Calendar

The Imperium uses the [[Imperial Dating System]], which records a date as a check number giving its accuracy, a fraction of the year, the year within the millennium and the millennium itself. A date written 0 345 997.M41 means the 345th thousandth of the year 997 of the 41st millennium, recorded on Terra. Warp travel distorts time unpredictably, and since the Great Rift many worlds can no longer tell what year it is.

Symbols

The principal symbol of the Imperium is the [[Aquila (symbol)|Aquila]], a double-headed eagle whose heads look to the past and the future, or by some accounts represent Terra and Mars. It appears on armour, banners, buildings, documents and ships, and is made as a gesture of devotion by crossing the hands at the chest.

Foreign relations

The Imperium's doctrine holds that the galaxy belongs to humanity and that the alien must be destroyed. Formal diplomacy with xenos is rare, secret and almost always temporary. Its relationships include:

  • Chaos: total war with the [[Chaos Space Marines|traitor Legions]], daemons and cults of [[Chaos (Warhammer 40,000)|Chaos]], especially Abaddon's [[Black Legion]].
  • [[Orks]]: the most numerous enemy, forever invading Imperial space in vast [[Waaagh!]]s.
  • [[Tyranids]] and [[Genestealer Cults]]: extragalactic predators, and their infiltrating cults inside Imperial worlds.
  • [[Necrons]]: ancient androids reclaiming their former dominions.
  • [[Aeldari]]: an occasional, unreliable ally. The resurrection of Guilliman was the work of the Aeldari Ynnari, and Aeldari seers sometimes intervene on the Imperium's behalf.
  • [[T'au Empire]]: an expanding alien state on the Eastern Fringe that seeks to absorb human worlds into its Greater Good, opposed by the [[Damocles Gulf Crusade]] and later campaigns.
  • [[Leagues of Votann]]: a human-descended, technologically advanced power that trades and occasionally fights alongside or against the Imperium.

Creation and development

The Imperium first appeared in [[Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader]] (1987), the original rulebook written by [[Rick Priestley]]. The early setting was lighter and more anarchic than its later form, with Rogue Traders, explorers and an Imperium more comparable to a decaying space empire than a monolithic theocracy. Its tone drew on British science-fiction comics, especially [[2000 AD (comics)|2000 AD]] and [[Judge Dredd]], and on novels such as Frank Herbert's [[Dune (novel)|Dune]].[citation needed]

Over later editions Games Workshop developed the Imperium into its present grimdark form, and the [[Black Library]], founded in the late 1990s, expanded it in fiction. The [[Horus Heresy (novel series)|Horus Heresy novel series]], begun with Dan Abnett's Horus Rising in 2006, retold the Imperium's founding in detail. In 2017 the [[Gathering Storm]] campaign books moved the setting's timeline forward for the first time since 1987, destroying Cadia, opening the Great Rift and returning Roboute Guilliman.

Reception and analysis

The Imperium is widely discussed as a satire of totalitarianism, fascism and religious fundamentalism: a society whose propaganda insists it is righteous while it practises genocide, mass human sacrifice and thought control.[citation needed] Its appeal to players lies partly in that ambiguity: it is both humanity's protector and one of the setting's worst regimes.

That ambiguity has occasionally led to the Imperium's imagery being adopted by real-world extremists. In 2021 Games Workshop published a statement titled "Warhammer is for everyone", rejecting the use of its settings by hate groups and stating that such people were not welcome in its community.
