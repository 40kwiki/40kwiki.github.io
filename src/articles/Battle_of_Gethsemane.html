The Battle of Gethsemane was a naval engagement in the [[Gothic War]], fought in mid-151.M41 in the [[Gethsemane]] System of the [[Gothic Sector]]. It was one of the few occasions on which the Imperial fleet went on the offensive during the war. The engagement ended with the Imperial task force escaping a trap, though the Chaos fleets were also badly damaged.

Background

For the first seven standard years of the Gothic War, the Imperium fought mostly on the defensive across a long battlefront. As 150.M41 gave way to 151.M41, Lord Admiral [[Cornelius von Ravensburg]] decided to take the fight to the enemy and win back the worlds that had been lost. The Chaos warfleets outnumbered his, but they were divided into many smaller fleets. Ravensburg hoped to defeat each fleet separately by bringing the full weight of [[Battlefleet Gothic]] against it. The plan was risky, because it required him to concentrate his forces and leave other areas exposed.

The engagement

After several aborted attacks, Ravensburg saw his first real chance in mid-151.M41. Scout vessels reported a Chaos warfleet moving en masse towards the Gethsemane System. Ravensburg took personal command aboard his flagship, the Divine Right, and led a task force of seventeen capital ships, including two battleships and two battlecruisers, with twenty escorts. He pursued the enemy into the Gethsemane region.

The Chaos fleet tried to flee out of the system to get far enough from Gethsemane to regroup. As the Imperial ships followed, signals warned of a second Chaos fleet on a closing course. The Imperial task force had been lured into a trap. A further twelve Chaos warships joined the fleet, which then turned on the Imperial vessels. The Imperial ships could do little more than evade the bulk of the enemy. They lost three destroyers and four frigates in a series of small skirmishes, while destroying at least five escorts of the enemy, and perhaps as many as ten.

Outcome

The major warships of Ravensburg's command were not lost, and the Imperial task force was able to break away. Neither fleet achieved a decisive result.
