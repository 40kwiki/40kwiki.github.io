The primarchs (singular primarch) were the twenty genetically engineered superhuman sons of the [[Emperor of Mankind]]. Created late in the [[30th millennium]] in the gene-vaults beneath the [[Imperial Palace]] on [[Terra]], they were intended to serve as the generals, governors and living symbols of the [[Great Crusade]]. The [[Space Marine Legions]], the first [[Adeptus Astartes]], were raised from the gene-seed of the primarchs' genomes, and each Legion was commanded by the primarch whose template it carried.

Before the Emperor could present his sons to the Imperium, the powers of [[Chaos]] carried the twenty gestation capsules through the [[Warp]] and scattered them across the lost human worlds of the [[Age of Strife]]. Each primarch grew up alone, among strangers, in a culture that shaped his character, and each was found by the Emperor or by his brothers as the crusade advanced. When they were reunited with their Legions, the primarchs became the foremost military and political leaders of the Imperium, answerable only to their father.

The loyalty of the primarchs ended in catastrophe. In 005.M31 the [[Warmaster]] [[Horus]] rebelled, and eight of his brothers followed him. The [[Horus Heresy]] that followed cost the lives of the Emperor's closest sons, left him entombed on the [[Golden Throne]], and ended the age of the primarchs. Of the twenty, only two loyal primarchs are active in the early [[42nd millennium]]: [[Roboute Guilliman]], who rose from stasis in 999.M41 to lead the [[Indomitus Crusade]], and [[Lion El'Jonson]], who woke from his long sleep beneath the fortress-monastery of the [[Dark Angels]].

Terminology

They are called the "sons" of the Emperor, and each is the "gene-sire" of the [[Adeptus Astartes|Space Marines]] of his Legion. The two Legions struck from the record are still known only by their numbers.

The programme that created them is called the Primarch Project. It is unknown exactly when it began.

Creation

The Primarch Project

The date on which the Primarch Project began is unrecorded, and the Emperor never revealed how it was achieved. Imperial scholars hold that the work was carried out in two stages. First, a portion of the Emperor's own genetic code was refined into a pure, undifferentiated template, the primarch gene-stock. That stock was then differentiated into twenty separate genomes, each with its own gifts and weaknesses. The method did not use cloning. A [[Raven Guard]] Apothecary and a [[Adeptus Mechanicus|Mechanicus]] genetor who examined the surviving records during the Heresy found that some of the twenty strands had sequences deliberately removed from the base template, and that others carried additions that were not human. The strand listed as Subject VI held markers resembling those of a canine predator. Some savants connect this with the wolf-like temperament of [[Leman Russ]] and with the feral [[Mutant|mutations]] that afflict the [[Space Wolves]], though the numbering of the samples may not correspond to the Legion numbers.

Modern Imperial scholars believe that the work was not only a matter of flesh. They hold that the Emperor used knowledge gained in the Warp to shape the spirit of each primarch as well as the body, which would account for their personal magnetism. A soul so shaped burns brightly in the [[Warp|Immaterium]], and draws the notice of [[Daemon|daemons]]. The Emperor therefore protected the gestation capsules with inscribed wards and with the strongest [[Geller field]] ever built, set around the gene-vaults under the Himalazian mountains.

The date of the Project's completion is placed at about 792.M30. [[Perturabo]] later reckoned his own birth to that year, though he was speaking of his arrival on [[Olympia]], not of the capsule's creation. The [[Adeptus Custodes]] were derived in part from the same pure gene-stock, though their genetic code is far simpler than any primarch's.

The scattering

The powers of [[Chaos]] learned of the Emperor's work, and chose to strike at it before it could bear fruit. About 792.M30 a localised Warp rift opened in the gene-vaults and drew the twenty capsules out of the Palace. They were carried through the Immaterium and set down on human worlds that had been cut off from [[Terra]] since the [[Age of Strife]]. It may be that the first seeds of corruption were laid in those primarchs who later turned [[Traitor]] when the hand of Chaos first touched them, though it is unknown whether the Emperor could have prevented this.

The Emperor did not wait for his sons to be recovered. The [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] of the primarchs, already prepared, was implanted in the finest warriors of the [[Unification Wars]], and the first [[Space Marine Legions]] took the field when the Great Crusade began in about 798.M30. The Legions fought without their primarchs for decades. Their loyalty, and the sense of loss that they felt when the primarchs were found, shaped the character of every Legion.

Upbringing on the lost worlds

The twenty capsules landed in very different places, and each world shaped its primarch's personality, sometimes damaging it. The grave and withdrawn [[Lion El'Jonson]] grew up alone in the monster-haunted forests of [[Caliban]]; [[Angron]] was made a slave-gladiator on [[Nuceria]], where the Butcher's Nails were set into his brain; [[Sanguinius]] was found among the mutant tribes of [[Baal]] Secundus; [[Rogal Dorn]] was raised on [[Inwit]].

The Great Crusade

Further information: [[Great Crusade]]

Rediscovery

As the Great Crusade advanced, the Emperor used his powers of foresight to locate his lost sons. He took each in turn, revealed himself to him, and confirmed his place as the commander of the Legion made from his genome. Each was reunited with his Legion, and the return of the primarchs marked the height of the Great Crusade. The Emperor, according to the techno-archaeologist Arkhan Land, who aided him after the rediscovery of Angron, said that he felt no emotional attachment to the primarchs, who were made to serve Mankind as tools and weapons, and that it was only natural for any creation to call its creator father.Land's account is not accepted by every Imperial scholar.

Command of the Legions

Each primarch took command of an expeditionary fleet and the Legion that went with it. He was given the authority of a proconsul over every world his forces brought into compliance, and a primarch's word on such a world carried the weight of the Emperor's. The Legions bore the stamp of their commanders. Each primarch embodied an aspect of war: Guilliman was a great strategist, Curze the terror of war, Magnus the master of psychic power, and Dorn and Perturabo the greatest practitioners of fortification.

The campaigns of the Great Crusade, among them the [[Rangdan Xenocides]] and the triumph over the Ork empire at [[Ullanor]], were commanded by the primarchs. After Ullanor the Emperor named Horus the first Warmaster, giving him supreme command of the crusade, and returned to Terra to continue his secret work beneath the Palace. The primarchs never came to terms with the Emperor's absence.

Rivalry and the Council of Nikaea

The primarchs were proud, and their rivalries were old. They proved jealous and arrogant, competitive, and all too willing to hold grudges against one another and against their father. At the Council of Nikaea, called by the Emperor in 001.M31, the primarchs and the Imperium's leaders debated the use of psychic powers. The Emperor's Decree Absolute that followed banned sorcery and the Librarius of the Legions, a ruling that [[Magnus the Red]] opposed.

The Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

Corrupted by Chaos on the moon of [[Davin]] in 004.M31, [[Horus]] declared against the Emperor in 005.M31. Nine of the primarchs joined him: Horus himself, [[Fulgrim]], [[Perturabo]], [[Konrad Curze]], [[Angron]], [[Mortarion]], [[Magnus the Red]], [[Lorgar]] and [[Alpharius Omegon]]. The reasons differed. Lorgar had long believed that the Emperor should be worshipped; Magnus had been condemned for sorcery; Angron wanted vengeance for his slavery; Fulgrim and Perturabo were consumed by pride and bitterness; Curze and Mortarion nursed old wounds against the Emperor himself. The [[Alpha Legion]]'s motives remain the least certain of all.

The war opened with the atrocity at [[Isstvan III]] and the [[Drop Site Massacre]] on Isstvan V, where three loyalist Legions were almost destroyed. In the Massacre [[Ferrus Manus]] was killed by his brother Fulgrim, and the surviving loyal primarchs, [[Vulkan]] and [[Corvus Corax]], were gravely wounded. The [[Word Bearers]] and their allies struck at the Ultramarines on [[Calth]], and the Warp storm they raised, the Ruinstorm, cut the Imperium in two. [[Magnus the Red]] was struck down by [[Leman Russ]] in the [[Burning of Prospero]], and the shattered [[Thousand Sons]] threw in their lot with Horus.

Across the years of the war, the fighting spread from the eastern marches to the Solar edge. On the loyal side, [[Roboute Guilliman]], [[Sanguinius]] and [[Lion El'Jonson]] founded the stopgap realm of [[Imperium Secundus]] beyond the Ruinstorm; [[Rogal Dorn]] organised the defence of the [[Imperial Palace]]; [[Jaghatai Khan]] led the [[White Scars]]' mobile defence of the outer system. Alpharius led the [[Alpha Legion]] against the Solar defences and was killed by Dorn at [[Pluto]] in 010.M31.

At the [[Siege of Terra]] in 014.M31, Horus lowered the void shields of his flagship, the [[Vengeful Spirit]]. The Emperor teleported aboard with [[Sanguinius]], [[Rogal Dorn]] and his [[Adeptus Custodes]], and Horus scattered them through the ship. Horus killed Sanguinius and mortally crippled the Emperor, who then destroyed Horus, soul and all. Dorn, who had been separated from his father in the teleport, found the Emperor's broken body and placed it on the [[Golden Throne]].

Every [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapter]] of the [[Second Founding]] and later traces its [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] through the primarch whose Legion it was drawn from. The [[Black Templars]], for example, descend from the Imperial Fists.

The lost primarchs

Main article: [[Lost Primarchs]]

Two of the twenty, the Second and the Eleventh, and their Legions were struck from the Imperial record before the Heresy began. All data concerning them was deliberately deleted, and the Imperium knows nothing of what became of them. The circumstances of their removal are sealed by edict.

Daemon Primarchs

Main article: [[Daemon]]

After the Heresy the surviving [[Traitor]] primarchs fled into the [[Eye of Terror]]. Several became daemon princes, bound to the [[Chaos|Chaos Gods]] in return for power. [[Fulgrim]] became a prince of [[Slaanesh]], [[Angron]] of [[Khorne]], [[Mortarion]] of [[Nurgle]] and [[Magnus the Red]] of [[Tzeentch]]; [[Lorgar]] and [[Perturabo]] serve the powers collectively as primarchs of Chaos Undivided. Each rules a [[Daemon world|daemon world]] or a warband and wages an endless war against the Imperium. These beings are called Daemon Primarchs in Inquisitorial usage, to distinguish them from the lesser princes who were once Space Marines.

The Daemon Primarchs remain dangerous. Fulgrim dealt [[Roboute Guilliman|Guilliman]] his supposedly fatal wound in 121.M31, and Mortarion returned to Imperial space after ten thousand years to invade [[Ultramar]] in the [[Plague Wars]].

The loyal primarchs after the Heresy

With the Emperor on the Golden Throne, the surviving loyal primarchs reorganised the Imperium. [[Roboute Guilliman]] became [[Lord Commander of the Imperium|Lord Commander]] and wrote the [[Codex Astartes]], and the [[Second Founding]] divided each loyal Legion into [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] of about a thousand warriors, to prevent any one commander from again holding the power that [[Horus]] had held. Many of the primarchs railed against the division at first, and [[Rogal Dorn|Dorn]] deeply opposed it before he came to see its wisdom; the [[Imperial Fists]] then underwent the ordeal of the [[Iron Cage]] as a test of the Legion. The loyal primarchs then dispersed one by one. [[Jaghatai Khan|The Khan]] vanished in 084.M31; Dorn is said to have died in the First [[Black Crusade]]; Russ vanished in 211.M31; [[Corvus Corax|Corax]] withdrew after the Heresy and went into the Eye of Terror; Vulkan vanished about a thousand years after the Heresy; and Guilliman himself fell to [[Fulgrim]] and lay in stasis on [[Macragge]].

The Era Indomitus

Further information: [[Era Indomitus]]

At the close of the [[41st millennium]] the Imperium was cut in half by the [[Great Rift]]. In 999.M41 the [[Archmagos]] [[Belisarius Cawl]], aided by the [[Aeldari|aeldari]] prophetess [[Yvraine]] and the power of [[Rise of the Ynnari|Ynnead]], brought Roboute Guilliman out of stasis in the [[Macragge|Temple of Correction]] on Macragge. The first returned primarch took the title of [[Lord Commander of the Imperium]] once more, spoke with the Emperor in the Throne Room, and began the [[Indomitus Crusade]].

In the early [[42nd millennium]] a [[Lost Primarchs|second primarch]] returned. During the campaign known as the [[Arks of Omen]], [[Lion El'Jonson]] woke at last in the heart of the Rock, the mobile fortress-monastery of the [[Dark Angels]], after ten thousand years of sleep, and went on to found the Lion's Protectorate in the [[Imperium Nihilus]].

Imperial sources are divided on whether further primarchs are returning. The [[Space Wolves]] maintain that [[Leman Russ|Russ]] will come at the [[Wulfen|Wolftime]], the [[Salamanders]] that [[Vulkan]] will return when the nine relics he hid are found, and the [[White Scars]] that the Khan still hunts in the Webway.

Nature and abilities

Physiology

A primarch is larger, stronger, faster and cleverer than any ordinary man, and far beyond any Space Marine, whose abilities derive from his [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]]. Primarchs are untouched by disease and old age, and possess a charisma and martial prowess that made them seem like the gods of old.

Psychic and spiritual gifts

All primarchs are psychically active to some degree, although few use their gifts openly. [[Magnus the Red]] was a sorcerer of exceptional power, [[Sanguinius]] possessed a precognitive gift, and [[Konrad Curze]] was cursed with visions of the future.

Flaws

The primarchs had the emotional range and the weaknesses of men. Sanguinius lived under the shadow of an inherited flaw, a thirst that threatened to destroy him; [[Angron]]'s mind was damaged by neural implants; [[Mortarion]] nursed a lifelong resentment. The [[Inquisition]] records that the primarchs' unusual spiritual power made them more susceptible to Chaos than any other beings in the Imperium, and that most of the [[Traitor|traitors]] fell in ways that exploited an existing pride, bitterness or grief.

Relationship with the Legions

Every Legion looked to its primarch as father, commander and ideal. Space Marines inherited the primarch's gene-seed, and with it some of his temperament, his gifts and, in several cases, his flaws. The [[Blood Angels]] inherit the Red Thirst; the [[Space Wolves]] inherit the wolfish traits of [[Leman Russ|Russ]]; the [[Iron Hands]] inherit the hatred of the flesh that grew in [[Ferrus Manus]]. The [[Second Founding]] preserved that inheritance, and the Imperial Cult's veneration of the primarchs as saints rests on it.

Legacy

The reorganisation of the Imperium's forces after the Heresy, above all the division of the Legions into [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]], was made to prevent another [[Horus]]. The primarchs themselves are remembered as heroes and as warnings. [[Ultramarines|The Ultramarines]] honour [[Roboute Guilliman|Guilliman]] as their patron, the [[Blood Angels]] cherish the memory of [[Sanguinius]], and the [[Space Wolves]] look for the return of [[Leman Russ|Russ]] at the Wolftime.
