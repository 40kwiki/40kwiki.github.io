A battlecruiser is a heavy [[Cruiser|cruiser]] whose armament is greater than normal for its class. Cruisers form the backbone of any star-faring warfleet. They are more manoeuvrable than a [[Battleship]] and more powerful than an escort, and they are used for patrols, blockades, raids and fleet engagements. Battlecruisers are the most heavily armed of the heavy cruisers. No cruiser of any type can match a true battleship in an open fight on equal terms, and in a clash of the line cruisers support the battleships, using their speed for flanking attacks and finishing off wounded ships.

Cruisers come in several types according to their size and role. Light cruisers are fast enough to chase off escorts. Heavy or armoured cruisers favour defence and firepower, and the most over-gunned of them are called battlecruisers. Grand cruisers are the largest and are almost the equal of a true battleship, and they are often referred to as pocket battleships.

Origin of the battlecruiser

The battlecruiser arose from the failures of the older grand cruiser designs. Grand cruisers were built around experimental Warp drives that proved prone to catastrophic malfunction in transit, and the Navy came to regard many of them as cursed ships. During the internecine conflict of the [[Age of Apostasy]] in the [[36th millennium]] several of the skills needed to maintain their arcane technologies were lost, which made them hazardous to operate.

The fleet still lacked a heavy cruiser to fill the gap between a standard cruiser such as the Lunar class and a full battleship. Small task forces needed a powerful flagship, and the precious battleships could not be spared for every engagement. In the later centuries of the 36th millennium the tech-masons of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] answered with a hybrid that used the simpler technologies of the later cruisers and the power systems of the battleships. The battlecruiser gives the fleet the long-range punch of battleship weaponry in a cruiser-sized hull. It is made for heavy engagements between capital vessels, and it overpowers other cruisers with overcharged weapons. Battlecruisers are typically somewhat smaller and more lightly armoured than grand cruisers, but they are more economical, safer and more reliable. Battlefleet admirals guard them jealously, and it is rare for one to end up in a Rogue Trader fleet.

Imperial service

The conduct of the [[Great Crusade]] required warships by the thousands. Heavy and light cruisers are medium-sized Imperial vessels, ranging between 4 and 6 kilometres long, with crews of tens of thousands. They were intended for independent operations such as raids, outpost assaults, deep-range patrols and search-and-destroy missions. They were also the workhorses of the larger fleets and squadrons.

Grand cruisers were built to operate on their own rather than as part of a larger fleet. Many were designed for range, meaning the ability to stay deployed without resupply or refit, or to carry a specialised weapon that required particular tactics. A grand cruiser can use its range and speed to catch and destroy a target that a battleship cannot reach.

Rogue Trader houses and local defence navies also operate cruisers of their own design. Imperial records describe a Rogue Trader cruiser under attack by an unknown foe in a star system far from the Imperial heartland.

Other fleets

Each major power has its own equivalents. The [[Necrons]] use the Scythe-class Reaper ship as their battlecruiser, and the [[Orks]] build the Hammer-class battlekroozer. The [[Aeldari]] fly the Phoenix ship as their battlecruiser. The [[Tyranids]] build bio-ships at each size, and their Devourer is battlecruiser-grade.
