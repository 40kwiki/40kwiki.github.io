The Adeptus Astartes, known throughout the [[Imperium of Mankind]] as the Space Marines, the Astartes or the Angels of Death, are the Imperium's foremost body of transhuman warriors. Each is a human recruit who has been remade by a long course of surgery, [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] implantation and psycho-indoctrination into a being larger, stronger, faster and far longer lived than any ordinary man, clad in sealed [[Power armour|ceramite]] armour and armed with weapons that few other soldiers can carry. They are organised into self-governing brotherhoods called [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]], each of about a thousand warriors, and they are among the most honoured and most feared institutions of Mankind.

The Adeptus Astartes descend from the [[Space Marine Legions]] created by the [[Emperor of Mankind]] for the [[Great Crusade]], each Legion bred from the preserved flesh of one of his twenty [[Primarch|primarchs]]. Nine Legions followed [[Horus]] into rebellion during the [[Horus Heresy]]; the nine that remained loyal were broken into Chapters by the [[Codex Astartes]] of [[Roboute Guilliman]] in the [[Second Founding]], so that no single commander should ever again hold such power. Later [[Third Founding|Foundings]] have raised many hundreds of further Chapters from the same stock.

At the close of the [[41st millennium]] the Adeptus Astartes entered their greatest transformation since the Second Founding. The [[Archmagos|Archmagos Dominus]] [[Belisarius Cawl]] revealed the Primaris Space Marines, a stronger breed created in secret over ten thousand years, and Guilliman, returned to life as [[Lord Commander of the Imperium]], raised the Ultima Founding from them while reinforcing the older, or Firstborn, Chapters. By the [[Era Indomitus]] the Chapters fight as a mixed force of Firstborn and Primaris brothers, and the Firstborn Marines who undergo the surgery to become Primaris are said to have "crossed the Rubicon".

Names and terms

Astartes is a [[High Gothic]] word of uncertain origin, used in [[Lex Imperialis|Imperial law]] for the Emperor's warrior-sons; the full style Adeptus Astartes places them among the [[Adeptus Terra|Adepta]] of the Imperium, though they answer to no Adeptus Terra authority in the field. Popular speech prefers "Space Marines", a term from the early [[Great Crusade]] that survives in every dialect of [[Low Gothic]]. Folk tradition across the Imperium calls them the "Angels of Death", and the superstitious of many worlds believe them to be saints or spirits rather than men.

The warriors of the Great Crusade are called [[Space Marine Legions|Legiones Astartes]] and their ranks "Legionaries". The term "[[Primaris Space Marines|Firstborn]]" denotes the Marines created before the [[Ultima Founding]]; "Primaris" denotes those made with [[Belisarius Cawl|Cawl]]'s improved organs, and "Greyshields" is the name given to Primaris warriors who fought in the [[Indomitus Crusade]] without a Chapter. The Emperor's earlier, flawed gene-warriors, the [[Thunder Warriors]], are not counted among the Adeptus Astartes.

History

Origins

Imperial records of the first Space Marines are partial, and the surviving accounts disagree on details. All agree that during the [[Unification Wars]] (c. 797.M30) the Emperor created the [[Thunder Warriors]], a first generation of gene-engineered soldiers, and that the Thunder Warriors proved unstable, short-lived and prone to rage. When the wars ended the Emperor ordered them put down, and in their place he began the work that would become the Legions. The earliest Legion, the First, was formed on [[Terra]] from warriors who had fought beside him from the start, and it was followed by others as the Emperor perfected his methods and as his lost sons, the [[Primarch|primarchs]], were found among the stars.

The Emperor's genetic science is not understood today. The [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] holds fragmentary notes and the [[Administratum]] holds the surviving [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] stocks, but the techniques of the original creation, and in particular the means by which the primarchs were made, were never recorded in a form any later scholar could use. Several of the implants used by later [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] are copies of copies, and Mechanicus genetors have noted for centuries that the oldest vault stocks perform better than any seed made since.

Great Crusade and Horus Heresy

See also: [[Ullanor Crusade]] and [[Sons of Horus]]

Twenty Legions took the field in the Great Crusade from c. 798.M30. Each was commanded by a primarch and numbered in tens of thousands; in the later years a Legion could muster several hundred thousand warriors. [[Space Marine Legions|The Legions]] conquered and compelled, and they exterminated [[Xenos|xenos]] empires judged a danger to Mankind. The war was widely believed won when the Emperor returned to Terra after [[Ullanor Crusade|Ullanor]] and named [[Horus]] [[Warmaster]], but in 005.M31 Horus rebelled, and the Legions divided.

The Legions that followed Horus were the [[Sons of Horus]], [[Word Bearers]], [[World Eaters]], [[Death Guard]], [[Emperor's Children]], [[Iron Warriors]], [[Night Lords]], [[Alpha Legion]] and [[Thousand Sons]]. The loyalist Legions were the [[Dark Angels]], [[White Scars]], [[Space Wolves]], [[Imperial Fists]], [[Blood Angels]], [[Iron Hands]], [[Ultramarines]], [[Salamanders]] and [[Raven Guard]]. Two further Legions, the Second and the Eleventh, were struck from every record, and no Imperial source names them with certainty.

The war ended at the [[Siege of Terra]] in 014.M31, at the cost of much of the Legions' strength. Loyal Legions had been wrecked at [[Isstvan V]] and in the long campaigns afterwards, and the Imperial Fists, Blood Angels and Space Wolves bled heavily on Terra itself.

Second Founding

Main article: [[Second Founding]]

In the [[Great Scouring]] the loyalist Legions drove the [[Traitor|Traitors]] into the [[Eye of Terror]]. Guilliman, who had been given the task of reorganising the military of the Imperium, then wrote the [[Codex Astartes]]. Its central ruling was that no Legion should again exceed a thousand warriors under a single commander. The surviving Legions were divided into Chapters in the Second Founding, which began in 021.M31. Some primarchs accepted the change gracefully. Others, among them the Space Wolves' and the Dark Angels' leaders, kept parts of their old order, and a handful of Chapters never adopted the Codex fully.

Later Foundings

The Chapters have been added to in successive [[Third Founding|Foundings]]. The total number is disputed, but the Administratum records some twenty-seven. Most were intended to refill the Adeptus Astartes after periods of heavy loss, and each drew on the vault stocks held on Terra and [[Mars]]. Some Foundings, such as the Cursed Founding of 991.M35, produced Chapters with seriously flawed gene-seed, and the Imperium treats the Chapters of those Foundings with caution.

Creation of a Space Marine

Recruitment

Recruits are drawn from the worlds from which a Chapter takes its blood, and every Chapter has its own custom of choosing them. Several recruit from a single homeworld, among them the Ultramarines of [[Macragge]], the [[Blood Angels]] of [[Baal]] and the [[Space Wolves]] of [[Fenris]]. Others, such as the [[Dark Angels]], take warriors from many worlds, and some take their candidates from Imperial [[Commissar|schola progenium]] or from penal worlds. Most look for the same qualities: physical strength, intelligence, resistance to pain and an unusual degree of loyalty. Candidates are usually aged between ten and fourteen when first selected, though the practice varies. A candidate is called an Aspirant until he completes his trials and is chosen to become a Neophyte.

The trials differ widely. On [[Feral world|feral worlds]] they are tests of survival, hunts, ritual combat and endurance; among the noble houses of Macragge, a record of service and education is combined with trial by combat. A Chapter will usually take only a handful of recruits each year from each recruiting world, and a majority of Aspirants are rejected or die. The Chapter's [[Apothecary|Apothecaries]] then assess those chosen for their psychic aptitude, their reflexes and their resistance to the strain of the implants. A candidate who proves unable to survive the surgery may be returned to his people or retained as a Chapter serf.

Implantation

The making of a Marine takes years. A Neophyte receives a series of implants, nineteen in the [[Primaris Space Marines|Firstborn]] pattern, in a fixed order, by Apothecaries working with Chapter [[Techmarine|Techmarines]] and the [[Librarian|Librarius]]. Each organ must be accepted by the body before the next is introduced, and the surgery is accompanied by hypnotherapy and doctrinal instruction. The process often kills the Neophyte or leaves him unfit, and survivors are said to go through a process of mental reinforcement that makes them reliably obedient to their Chapter. The final organ, the [[Power armour|Black Carapace]], is implanted at the end of service among the Scouts, and the Neophyte becomes a Battle-Brother only when it has fully taken.

Several implants are known to fail to grow in some [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]], and the Imperium calls the condition "zygote deficiency". The Sus-an Membrane and the Betcher's Gland are the commonest to be missing, and the Mucranoid is absent in the gene-seed of a number of Chapters. The [[Imperial Fists]] have lost both of these organs, and the Blood Angels and Space Wolves each carry a defect passed on through the Progenoids.

Gene-seed

Main article: [[Gene-seed]]

The gene-seed is a Marine's inheritance. The Progenoid glands in the chest cavity produce the cells from which the Chapter makes new Marines, and the Chapter's Apothecaries must recover them from the fallen. A Chapter that loses its gene-seed stock, whether to destruction or theft, has lost its future. A Marine's death is therefore not the end of his service: the Progenoids of the fallen are harvested and, if sound, implanted into the next generation. Chapters that are unable to recover their dead for long periods can be reduced to a handful of warriors, and every Chapter's gene-seed is also tithed in part to the vaults of Terra.

The gene-seed carries its [[Primarch|primarch]]'s strengths and defects. The Blood Angels' inheritance includes the Red Thirst and the Black Rage, the Space Wolves bear the [[Wulfen|Canis Helix]], and most other Legions carry some lesser variation. A Chapter's "genetic stability", the degree to which its seed is free of [[Mutant|mutation]], is a matter of record in the [[Administratum]] and one of the matters an [[Inquisitor]] will examine closely.

Physiology and mind

A Firstborn Marine stands well over two metres in height and is correspondingly broad. His bones are denser than a man's, his muscle is more than twice as strong, he can breathe in a wide range of atmospheres and survive wounds that would kill many men. He heals with a speed that seems unnatural to observers, needs little sleep and can live for centuries. His body also responds to hypnotherapy and is trained for years to use his armour and his wargear as a single weapon.

The mind of a Marine is formed with equal care. Hypno-doctrination instils the beliefs and the discipline of the Chapter, and a Marine's faith, in the Emperor, in the Chapter and in the brotherhood of his squad, is intended to be absolute. Marines are generally celibate and are expected to hold no ties outside their Chapter. Most share an outlook that ordinary Imperials find alien: they are courageous, aloof and, in many cases, genuinely unable to understand the fears of the unaugmented. The same indoctrination makes them effective soldiers but leaves them vulnerable to certain types of corruption, and Inquisitors of the [[Ordo Astartes]] are tasked with monitoring those Chapters whose loyalty is in doubt.

Organisation and ranks

A Chapter is a sovereign body. It owns its fortress, its fleet, its recruits and its [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]], and it answers to the [[High Lords of Terra]] only through the [[Codex Astartes]] and the tithe of gene-seed. The standard Codex Chapter, described in full in the article on [[Space Marine Chapter|Space Marine Chapters]], has ten companies of one hundred warriors, led by a [[Chapter Master]]. Non-Codex Chapters, among them the [[Space Wolves]], [[Dark Angels]], [[Blood Angels]] and [[Black Templars]], are organised in their own way.

Four groups of specialists serve beside the Battle-Brothers. Apothecaries tend the wounded and recover the gene-seed of the dead. Chaplains maintain the Chapter's faith and discipline and lead it in war. Librarians are the Chapter's [[Psyker|psykers]], trained to fight the powers of the [[Warp]] with their minds and often to serve as scribes of its lore. Techmarines maintain the Chapter's armour and vehicles, and are initiates of the [[Cult Mechanicus]] as well as the Chapter. A warrior who is mortally wounded may be interred in the sarcophagus of a [[Dreadnought]], and so serve on for centuries more.

Wargear

Armour

See also: [[Power armour]] and [[Mark I Thunder power armour]]

A Marine fights in [[Power armour|power armour]], a sealed suit of ceramite plates over a layer of bodyglove, powered by a backpack reactor and driven by fibre bundles that magnify his strength. The suit is linked directly to the Marine's nervous system by the Black Carapace. It carries the Marine's helmet, with auto-senses, vox, targeting and vital-sign monitors, and it protects him from vacuum, heat and many hostile atmospheres for a period. Its pauldrons, helm and knee guards bear the heraldry of his Chapter and company, and its colours serve as the Chapter's identity.

Successive patterns of power armour, called Marks, were produced as the technology improved. The earliest, the [[Mark I Thunder power armour|Mark I]] Thunder, was an unpowered suit of the [[Unification Wars]]; later patterns include the Mark II Crusade, the Mark III Iron, the Mark IV Maximus, the Mark V Heresy, the Mark VI Corvus and the Mark VII [[Aquila (symbol)|Aquila]]. A good deal of the older equipment is still used by [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] that cannot replace it. The [[Primaris Space Marines|Primaris]] pattern is the Mark X Tacticus, a design that [[Belisarius Cawl|Cawl]] drew from the best of the old patterns, and its heavier variant is the Gravis armour. Marines facing the heaviest fighting wear Terminator armour, a tactical [[Dreadnought|dreadnought]] suit once reserved for the First Company, and the Primaris Terminator is the [[Terminator armour|Tartaros]] pattern. Scouts wear a light armour called a carapace.

Weapons

The signature weapon of the Adeptus Astartes is the bolter, a rocket-propelled mass-reactive shell weapon. Its Primaris successor is the [[Bolter|bolt rifle]] of the Cawl pattern. Standard Space Marine wargear also includes the bolt pistol, the combat knife, frag and krak grenades, the chainsword, the power sword and the power fist. Support weapons include the heavy bolter, the lascannon, the missile launcher, the plasma gun and the meltagun. [[Librarian|Librarians]] carry force weapons, and [[Chaplain|Chaplains]] the [[Crozius Arcanum|crozius arcanum]]. A broad range of specialist weapons has been produced for the Primaris, among them the plasma incinerator, the heavy bolt rifle and the gravis weaponry carried by the Aggressors and Eradicators.

Vehicles and ships

Chapter armouries field the [[Rhino]] armoured carrier, the Razorback, the Predator and [[Land Raider|Land Raider]] battle tanks, the Whirlwind, the Vindicator, the Land Speeder and the [[Thunderhawk|Thunderhawk gunship]], together with assault bikes and drop pods. The Primaris-era range includes the Repulsor and Impulsor transports, the Gladiator tank family and the Redemptor and Brutalis Dreadnoughts. A Chapter's fleet is made up of battle barges and strike cruisers, supported by smaller escorts. Several Chapters, among them the [[Imperial Fists]] and the [[Black Templars]], operate a fleet that is itself their home.

Primaris Space Marines

Main article: [[Primaris Space Marines]]

Origin

The newest Imperial record, based on the notes of [[Belisarius Cawl]], gives the following account. In the early [[32nd millennium]] [[Roboute Guilliman]], unsatisfied with the Space Marines' resilience after the Heresy, ordered the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] to study the creation of a new generation of warriors who could stand against the [[Traitor|Traitors]] if the Imperium were ever to be broken again. The task passed to Cawl, who spent ten thousand years refining the [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] of the loyal Legions, drawing on stocks of unknown origin and on the preserved fragments of the original genetic work. The product was the Primaris Space Marine.

An earlier, and still widely repeated, Imperial account held that Cawl's work was a Mechanicus experiment conducted without the Emperor's blessing; the [[Imperial Cult|Ecclesiarchy]] and a number of [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] regarded it as heresy. The newer record says instead that Guilliman himself commissioned it, and the [[Adeptus Custodes]], speaking in the Emperor's name, have declared the Primaris to be a gift of the [[Emperor of Mankind|Master of Mankind]]. The Imperial authorities have not published the Custodes' reasoning.

Organs and physique

A Primaris Marine carries the nineteen implants of a Firstborn and three more. The Sinew Coils are durametallic cables woven through the muscle that magnify strength and give an additional layer of defence. The Magnificat is a thumbnail-sized lobe in the cortex that stimulates growth and heightens the working of the other implants. The Belisarian Furnace is a dormant organ linked to both hearts that, in a crisis, floods the body with a mixture of stimulants and healing agents. The Magnificat is only half of an organ the Emperor made for the [[Primarch|primarchs]]; Cawl could find no plans for the second half, and it is not known whether they were lost or erased.

The result is a Marine noticeably taller and broader than his Firstborn brother, with faster reflexes and a greater capacity to survive. Primaris armour and weapons were developed alongside the warrior: the [[Power armour|Mark X]] Tacticus armour, the Cawl-pattern [[Bolter|bolt rifle]], and a range of new squad types: Intercessors, Hellblasters, Aggressors, Eradicators, Reivers, Inceptors, Eliminators, Bladeguard and others.

The Ultima Founding and the Indomitus Crusade

See also: [[Indomitus Crusade]] and [[Ultima Founding]]

The first host of Primaris Marines was woken from stasis aboard Cawl's ark mechanicus, the Zar-Quaesitor, at the start of the [[Indomitus Crusade]] in 999.M41. Each had been indoctrinated for a single role, and each was ready to fight at once. Some were formed into entirely new Chapters, the [[Ultima Founding]], and others were assigned to existing Chapters. The remainder served as Greyshields with the Unnumbered Sons until the crusade's fleets reached the home or the fleet of the Chapter whose colours they would wear. Examples of Ultima Chapters recorded so far include the Rift Stalkers and the Silver Templars, and many have been given the abandoned fortress-monasteries of lost Chapters, often on the borders of the [[Great Rift]].

Not every Chapter welcomed the newcomers. Several [[Chapter Master|Chapter Masters]] regarded the Primaris with suspicion, and a few declared them an offence to the Emperor's work. A shield host of the Adeptus Custodes, the Emissaries Imperatus, accompanied the crusade and told the doubters that the Primaris had the Emperor's sanction. Most Chapters complied, and the [[Imperial Fists]], [[Dark Angels]] and [[Blood Angels]] have each since inducted Primaris recruits from their own worlds.

Integration with the Firstborn

By the [[Era Indomitus]] the Firstborn and Primaris Marines fight in the same companies. Many Chapters have changed their organisation to put Primaris squads in the line, and the Firstborn who wish to do so may undergo the Rubicon Primaris, a surgery to implant the three extra organs. The procedure kills a significant proportion of those who attempt it. [[Marneus Calgar]] of the Ultramarines was among the first to cross it, in order to show that the Primaris were an inheritance and not a threat, and Chapters whose losses were heavy have performed the surgery on warriors who would otherwise have died.

The older Marines' tactical formations, such as Tactical Squads and Devastators, remain in the Chapters, which also continue to recruit Firstborn warriors in the traditional way. The accepted Imperial account, drawn from the Records of the Indomitus Crusade, is that the two breeds are intended to serve together, and an earlier prediction that the Firstborn would be retired has not come true. Chapters differ in their pace: the [[Space Wolves]] and Dark Angels have been slow to change their rituals, while the Ultramarines and Imperial Fists reorganised quickly.

Beliefs and culture

Almost all Space Marines revere the Emperor, though the nature of their devotion varies. Some [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]], such as the [[Black Templars]], treat him as a god; the Ultramarines are generally more restrained, and are in the [[Codex Astartes]] tradition of regarding the [[Imperial Cult]] as an institution of the unaugmented. Every Chapter keeps its own rites, relics and honours, and its own calendar of days of remembrance, and many Chapters keep warrior lodges, scrimshaw, honour markings and oaths that have no equivalent among other Chapters. The Chapter is a family: the Chapter's serfs, who work the fortress and the fleet, are born into its service and are its only contact with ordinary humanity.

The Adeptus Astartes are also, by long tradition, a force of last resort. The Codex requires that a Chapter's duties be to defend Mankind and the Imperium, and in practice the Chapters are asked to take on missions that the [[Astra Militarum]] cannot. The [[Inquisition]] has the right to call on them, as does the [[Lord Commander of the Imperium|Lord Commander]], and each Chapter lends warriors to the [[Deathwatch]], an Inquisitorial force that guards the Imperium from [[Xenos|xenos]] threats. Many Chapters also maintain their own rivalries and feuds, of which the oldest and best known are those between the [[Dark Angels]] and [[Space Wolves]], and between the [[Imperial Fists]] and the [[Iron Warriors]].

Non-Codex and Renegade Chapters

Several [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] do not follow the [[Codex Astartes]]: the [[Space Wolves]] and the [[Dark Angels]] retain their older structures; the [[Blood Angels]] use a modified order of battle; the [[Black Templars]], founded by [[Rogal Dorn|Dorn]]'s successors, are a crusading fleet of far more than a thousand warriors. The Codex permits such divergence, though it is regarded with suspicion by the [[Administratum]]. The Imperium's greatest concern is with Chapters that cease to answer to Imperial authority, or that turn to the [[Chaos|Ruinous Powers]]. A Chapter declared [[Excommunicate Traitoris]] loses its recognition and may be hunted by the [[Inquisition]] and by other Chapters. [[Chaos Space Marines|Renegade Chapters]] of Heretic Astartes exist in great number, as do the descendants of the [[Traitor Legions]] who fled into the Eye of Terror.
