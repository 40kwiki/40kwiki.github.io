The Ordo Redactus is an [[Ordos Minoris|Ordo Minoris]] of the [[Inquisition]] that deliberately obscures the Imperial past. It redacts records whose contents, in the Ordo's judgement, might corrupt the minds of weak men and women, and it takes pains lest the enemies of [[Mankind]] gain some advantage from knowing how the Inquisition began.

Purpose

The Ordo's argument is old. A fact may be true and still be a weapon. A [[Heretic|heretic]] who learns how the [[Inquisition]] arose might use it, and a [[Xenos|xenos]] who learns where an Imperial power is weak might do worse. The Ordo's duty is to decide what the Imperium must not remember, and to see that it does not.

The Ordo's work goes with a large apparatus of concealment. The [[Edict of Obliteration]], the Historical Revision Units and the sealing of archives are all tools of the kind the Ordo uses, but the records do not say that the Ordo is behind them.

Conflict with the Ordo Originatus

See also: [[Ordo Originatus]]

The Ordo Redactus is the declared opponent of the [[Ordo Originatus]]. The [[Inquisition]]'s article on its own origins records that they have reached opposite conclusions about where the Inquisition came from. Their dispute is, in effect, an argument inside the Inquisition about whether the truth is a danger or a defence.

The Era Indomitus

Further information: [[Era Indomitus]]

The Ordo's work became much harder after the return of [[Roboute Guilliman]] as [[Lord Commander of the Imperium]]. The [[Primarch]]'s insistence on the truth and on an accurate chronology since the [[Horus Heresy]] is disturbing, and to the Ordo even frightening. Its agents have moved quietly against the [[Ordo Originatus]] and the new [[Logos Historica Verita]], wherever they can do so without revealing themselves.

Whether they have done any harm is not recorded. The Logos is under the Lord Commander's own protection, and an open attack would not go unnoticed.

Rumour

Imperial rumour says that the Ordo is older than it admits and that it holds a library of the things it has removed from the record. This is perhaps the most natural story to tell about such an Ordo, and no source supports it. The reverse is at least as likely: that the Ordo has kept few copies, and that what it removed is gone.

Criticism

The Ordo's work is condemned by those who say that Mankind has paid dearly for its ignorance. The Imperium's reluctance to examine its own past, they argue, is part of what has left it unable to learn from its mistakes. The Ordo's reply, where one is recorded at all, is that the Imperium has survived ten thousand years by keeping certain things from the people who cannot bear them.

Members

None are named. An Ordo whose business is to conceal could not be expected to publish its roll.

Relations

The Ordo is opposed by the Originatus and the [[Logos Historica Verita]]. It has an uncertain relation with the [[Ordo Scriptorum]], which works to improve the quality of records and may or may not approve of what the Redactus does to them. The Chronostrife, which made the true date of every event uncertain, probably did the Redactus no harm.
