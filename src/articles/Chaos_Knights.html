The Chaos Knights, also called Renegade Knights, Daemon Knights and, in [[High Gothic]], Questor Traitoris, are [[Imperial Knight]] war machines and their Fallen Noble pilots, corrupted to the service of [[Chaos]]. Each is a walker that carries the firepower of a small army, and Imperial officers have long recorded that one Chaos Knight can break an armoured column or a regiment on its own.

The corruption works slowly. A Knight that falls to Chaos usually looks unchanged at first. Over years or centuries, Warp energy seeps into its [[Machine Spirit]] and alters its form, and its [[Throne Mechanicum]] fills with the minds of pilots who have turned to the Dark Gods. Imperial accounts describe the process as a rot that starts in the core and reaches the armour last.

Chaos Knights fight in three main forms: lone Dreadblades serving whoever pays them, Infernal households that serve the [[Dark Mechanicum]], and Iconoclast households that owe their loyalty to the gods or to a warlord. The Imperium treats all three as Traitoris, and the [[Ordo Hereticus]] hunts them as heretics. Imperial accounts stress that the Noble houses which turned were once among the most steadfast servants of the Emperor, which makes their fall harder for the Imperium to accept.

History

Colonisation and the Age of Isolation

The first Knight suits were made in the early millennia of human expansion. Colonists sailed from [[Terra]] aboard Long March generation ships, and some groups carried [[Standard Template Construct]] databases that could make Knight suits. The pilots were also the leaders of their settlements, and their bloodlines became the first noble houses. Their Thrones Mechanicum shaped them into strict and conservative rulers, and this habit of obedience later became the Code Chivalric.The Long March colonies are dated c. M15 from the sequence of expansion in the early Imperial records. No single document gives the first year.

The [[Age of Strife]] (c. M25–M30) cut most colony worlds off from one another. Many fell into war, and psykers and mutants were persecuted or drove their worlds into anarchy. The Knight worlds held out. Their houses purged mutation from their bloodlines, rejected thinking machines and used their Knights to defend their people. Their rulers' pride grew with their isolation, and this was later one of the causes of the Heresy.

Great Crusade

Further information: [[Great Crusade]]

The [[Great Crusade]] (798.M30 to 005.M31) found many Knight worlds in this state. A Rogue Trader named Jeffers was among the first to report one to the [[Administratum]]. Most noble houses swore fealty to the Crusade, and the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] of [[Mars]] secured the strongest oaths, since it wanted the archeotech of the Knight worlds. Other houses swore directly to the [[Emperor of Mankind]], and in return their Sacristan orders were taught Mechanicum techniques.

Horus Heresy

Further information: [[Horus Heresy]]

When the [[Horus Heresy]] began in 005.M31, many Imperial commanders believed the Noble houses could not be corrupted, since their Thrones were thought to make them loyal. The Heresy proved otherwise. Knight houses sworn to Mars followed the [[Dark Mechanicum]] when it turned to [[Horus]], and Knight houses bound to Traitor Legions followed their lords into rebellion. Some houses turned on their own kin, and some Nobles were bound to the Dark Gods by rituals.

The Knights fought on both sides at the [[Battle of Beta-Garmon]] (006–013.M31), and their duels there were among the bloodiest of the war. At [[Molech]] in 009.M31, [[House Devine]] turned on the Loyalist defenders during the Warmaster's attack. Its defection decided the battle and gave the Traitors the approach to Terra, and it is usually counted as the first house of Chaos Knights. At the [[Siege of Terra]] in 013–014.M31, Imperial accounts record Chaos Knights in the Traitor armies. One record names the Chaos Knights of House Vyronii, but the Imperial sources disagree about the number of Knights at Terra and the names of their houses.Records of the Siege of Terra are disputed. The Vyronii entry comes from a single Imperial summary and is not confirmed by the Solar War records.

Age of Degradation

After the Heresy, many Imperial officers believed the threat of the Chaos Knights had passed. It had not. Over the next centuries, Knight houses fell one by one, and in some years several worlds were lost in a single decade. The fallen houses became raiders, and their lords hired out lone Knights as mercenaries. The Imperium records their campaigns across the galaxy, from the Kreen Worlds to the Dysephamine System, but few of these accounts have exact dates.Dates for the Age of Degradation are estimated from the order of the Imperial reports.

Imperial scholars call the centuries of fighting after the Heresy the [[Long War]]. The Knight households of this period were drawn into the war against Chaos and the xenos, and many were lost to the Imperium on the same worlds where their Loyalist kin were still defending the Throne.

Era Indomitus

Further information: [[Era Indomitus]]

The opening of the [[Great Rift]] in 999.M41 cut many Knight worlds off from Terra and Mars, and some fell to Chaos. When [[Abaddon the Despoiler]] launched the 13th Black Crusade in the same year, Iconoclast and Infernal households and Dreadblades from across the galaxy joined his banner. Dread lances struck worlds where Imperial reinforcements were on their way, to hold the approaching armies in place.

Knight worlds on the Rift's edges have also been transformed directly. Some planets were enveloped by Warp storms, and when they emerged their populations had been devoured by [[Daemon|daemons]] and their war machines were remade. The [[Nachmund Gauntlet]] passage through the Rift led to the rediscovery of [[Dharrovar]], a Knight world lost since the Heresy. Renegade houses have also appeared along the Rift, including the household of Mandrakor on Dharrovar.

Households and Dreadblades

Infernal households

Infernal households are whole Knight houses that have joined the [[Dark Mechanicum]] or a [[Traitor Titan Legion]]. Their Knights are fitted with techno-sorcery, and the Throne Mechanicum of each is said to hold summoned daemonic entities as well as the spirits of earlier pilots. The Imperium recognises them by their dark-red and bronze heraldry and by the rune marks on their armour.

Iconoclast households

Iconoclast households have more varied allegiances. Some still swear oaths to a [[Heretic Astartes]] Legion or to a Chaos God, and others have broken with the Imperium entirely to build their own dread realms. Their heraldry is usually stripped of Imperial symbols and replaced with the marks of their new lords.

Dreadblades

A Dreadblade is a single Chaos Knight that has turned against its own household, often by killing its kin, and that now serves as a mercenary. Dreadblades work as lone wolves and sell their strength to warlords in return for relics, arcane knowledge or fief worlds. Their most notable members include Litany of Destruction, which fought beside the [[Iron Warriors]] at Tellerax Prime, and the Gilded King, which took up a quest for lost archeotech. Dreadblades are often followed by warbands of [[Chaos Space Marines]] and by [[Chaos cult|cultists]] who worship the machines.

Design and pilots

The Throne Mechanicum

Main article: [[Imperial Knight]]

A Chaos Knight is piloted from a [[Throne Mechanicum]] in the larger classes, or a Helm Mechanicum in the smaller ones. Neural jacks connect the pilot's nervous system to the machine, and the pilot can drive the Knight's limbs and weapons by will alone. The Throne holds the echoes of earlier pilots, whose suffering grows as the Knight is warped. Only the most resilient Fallen Nobles can bear this for long, and the others are burned out or consumed.

The Ritual of Becoming

Main article: [[Imperial Knight]]

Young nobles are bonded to a Throne in the [[Ritual of Becoming]]. Among the Chaos Knights this ritual is tainted by Warp entities and by the sorceries of their lords, so that a new pilot may come out of it changed. Imperial records describe Fallen Nobles as "wired into" their war machines in a way that gives the pilot's will a hold over the Knight's weapons.

Heraldry

Chaos Knights are marked with the eight-pointed star of [[Chaos]] and with the marks of the Dark Gods that they serve. The symbol of the Questor Traitoris is a black Chaos star with a skull-helm at its centre, and it is painted or cast onto the armour of most Chaos Knights.
