Excommunicate Traitoris is a [[High Gothic]] designation used by the [[Administratum]] and the [[Inquisition]] to declare an individual or organisation a [[Traitor]] to the [[Imperium of Mankind]]. It is the highest form of Imperial reproach, given to those who have committed extreme acts of heresy against the [[Emperor of Mankind]], among them [[Mutant|mutation]], apostasy, blasphemy, rebellion and corruption by [[Chaos]]. The sentence banishes the accused from the Imperium on pain of death and casts them out of the light of the Emperor's benevolence. Imperial jurists hold that no fate is graver for any who serve the Imperium.

The sentence can be pronounced by only two authorities: the High Lords of Terra, and an Inquisitor of the Holy Ordos of the Inquisition. When it is pronounced, the accused is struck from the annals of Imperial history. An Imperial servant's name is removed from the scrolls of honour in the Hall of Heroes in the [[Imperial Palace]] and from the archives of the Librarium Terra by the Deletions Teams of the Administratum. When the accused is a Space Marine, or an entire Chapter, the [[Gene-seed|gene-seed]] held in the vaults of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]] is destroyed, and any [[Traitor Marines|traitor Marine]] who is slain is burned so that his blood will not taint Mankind.

The sentence is seldom a trial in the ordinary sense. It names a condition: the accused is outside the law and outside the Emperor's protection, and any loyal servant of the Imperium may kill him on sight. A penitent who confesses his sins and repents is granted a quick and clean death, but few take that course. Most flee, and most flee towards the great Warp rift in the [[Segmentum Obscurus]] known as the [[Eye of Terror]], where they cannot readily be followed, and where many become [[Chaos Space Marines]] or the servants of the [[Ruinous Powers]].

The designation is best known from the Space Marine Chapters that have borne it. The [[Astral Claws]], whose Chapter Master led the [[Badab War]] in 901–912.M41, are the most famous, and their fall shaped the reading of the sentence in the [[41st millennium]]. The Imperial record also lists the [[Soul Drinkers]], the Relictors and the Sons of Malice, the Night Reapers and the Crimson Sabres. In each case the sentence deleted the Chapter from the rolls of the Adeptus Astartes while leaving its survivors alive, armed and hunted.

Nature and meaning

The words are High Gothic, the formal language of the Administratum, and read as a declaration that the subject is excommunicated as a traitor. The sentence is more than a punishment. It is a statement about what the accused is: a thing that has left the Imperial order, and may therefore be treated as an enemy, without the protections that even a condemned citizen holds. The Imperium has many sentences for heresy, and a heretic may be pardoned, reformed or put to the question. Excommunicate Traitoris ends those possibilities. It is final, and its force depends on that finality.

The sentence differs from the [[Edict of Obliteration]], though the two are often named together. Excommunicate Traitoris is the verdict: it declares the subject a traitor. The Edict of Obliteration, known also as a damnatio memoriae, or condemnation of memory, is the policy of destroying the icons, records and monuments that remain of a person or organisation once that verdict has been pronounced. The Edict can be ordered by the Emperor, by the High Lords of Terra or by the Inquisition, the same authorities that may pronounce the sentence. Its purpose is to preserve the honour of the Emperor by removing every trace of the traitor from the life of the Imperium, as though he had never existed.

Declaration

The two authorities that may declare a person or body Excommunicate Traitoris act in different ways. The High Lords of Terra pronounce it for the gravest cases, those that concern a whole Chapter or a body whose treason threatens the Imperium itself. They named the Relictors and the Sons of Malice, which belong to the Astartes Praeses, a brotherhood of twenty Chapters recorded in the writings of the Mythos Angelica Mortis, and they declared the Night Reapers and the Crimson Sabres traitors.

An Inquisitor of the Holy Ordos may also pronounce it, and the Inquisition used this power against the Soul Drinkers. That Chapter was declared Excommunicate Traitoris after it became obsessed with the recovery of its lost relic, the Soulspear, and began to show deep signs of mutation and corruption by Chaos. Its Chapter Master Sarpedon then taught that the Imperium, and not his battle-brothers, was the corrupt party, a position he kept to the end.

Consequences

Erasure of records

Main article: [[Historical Revision Unit]]

The records of an excommunicated servant are destroyed by the Historical Revision Unit, one of the departments of the Administratum, whose adepts are the indentured servitors and historitors who revise the record of Imperial history in the Imperial Palace. Its Deletions Teams carry out the Edict of Obliteration. Each team numbers ten adepts under an officer with the rank of captain. They wear black hooded bodysuits and rebreathers, and carry narrow-nozzled flamers fed by promethium canisters. Most of their work is done in the vaults of the Librarium Terra, which lie deep beneath the crust of the Throneworld.

When a team finds the records it was sent to destroy, its flamers burn the shelves and data-stacks until nothing remains. A scholar who meets a team in the stacks is killed, since no witness to a deletion may remain. The work is thorough, but it cannot be complete. A powerful person has friends and allies, and some keep forbidden keepsakes. When the High Lords condemned the memory of Lufgt Huron, the Tyrant of Badab, the noble House of Pyzentos hid one of the finest portraits of him in its manse on the world of Eshunna.

Chapters and individual Astartes

See also: [[Adeptus Astartes]]

When a Space Marine or a Chapter is declared Excommunicate Traitoris, the gene-seed stores held by the Adeptus Mechanicus for the Chapter are destroyed. This is the heaviest consequence of the sentence, for a Chapter lives by its gene-seed. Without it no new battle-brothers can be made, and the Chapter cannot recruit from its own lines. The Astral Claws, in the last days of the Badab War, created specialist Apothecaries to harvest gene-seed from the fallen of both sides.

The Space Marines themselves, however, are not always taken. Where the Imperium could not reach the Chapter, the sentence lies on it as a standing order, and every loyal force that meets it is to open fire. The Crimson Sabres learned of the order after they massacred the people of two worlds under the influence of the voices in their minds. Their home world of Drogsh was seized by the Inquisition and everyone connected with the Chapter killed, and only a handful of Scouts escaped the Brakatoa System. The Chapter later reappears in the record as the Crimson Slaughter.

Fate of the accused

Imperial law offers the accused a single exit. A heretic who confesses and repents is killed quickly and cleanly, so that his death is a mercy. Otherwise he is hunted. Because the sentence puts him beyond the protection of any Imperial authority, he can expect no shelter on a loyal world. The refuge that remains is the Warp. The Eye of Terror and the [[Maelstrom]] have both sheltered excommunicated Space Marines, among them the survivors of the Astral Claws, who became the [[Red Corsairs]].

Notable cases

The Lost Primarchs

The most complete application of the Edict of Obliteration that follows the sentence is the deletion of the records of the primarchs of the II and XI Legions and of those Legions themselves, at a date before the Horus Heresy that is not recorded. Imperial scholars of the 41st millennium know only that the two Legions are listed as deleted from the records, and the nature of their offence is known, according to Imperial tradition, only to the surviving primarchs and the Emperor.

The Astral Claws

After the Badab War the High Lords passed an Edict of Obliteration against the [[Astral Claws]], and the Chapter's records were ordered destroyed. The Chapter's own men completed the work, burning their archives during the siege of Badab Primaris. Its gene-seed origin is therefore unknown, and no records survive of its full history.

Other Chapters

The Night Reapers were declared Excommunicate Traitoris in 989.M41, six Terran years after last contact, when wreckage in the Guyathis Belt proved the Chapter's direct responsibility for the destruction of a Rogue Trader fleet. The Crimson Sabres were declared traitors after the massacre on Demetra in 929.M41. The Soul Drinkers were pronounced traitors by the Inquisition at a date the record does not give. The Relictors and the Sons of Malice were declared traitors by the High Lords of Terra, but their dates are not recorded.

Use in the Imperium

The sentence is rare, and the Imperium uses it with care, because a declaration cannot easily be undone and erases the accused from the record. The [[Horus Heresy]] produced the largest body of traitors in Imperial history, and the [[Great Scouring]] that followed hunted the remnants of the [[Traitor Legions]] across the galaxy. The sources consulted do not say whether the sentence was formally pronounced against the Legions, and the dated declarations they record are those of the 41st millennium.

In the Era Indomitus the practice has been challenged. The Lord Commander [[Roboute Guilliman]] founded the [[Logos Historica Verita]] to recover an accurate account of Mankind's past, because the Edicts of Obliteration and the work of the Historical Revision Unit had left it fragmentary and contradictory. The new organisation trains its own historitors, and its work runs against the long habit of erasure that the sentence represents. The Inquisition continues to use the sentence.
