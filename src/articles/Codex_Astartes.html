The Codex Astartes is the great treatise on the organisation, strategy and conduct of the [[Adeptus Astartes]], written by the primarch [[Roboute Guilliman]] of the [[Ultramarines]] in the first decades after the [[Horus Heresy]]. It is the foundational text of the Space Marines. Its purpose was to restructure the Imperium's most powerful warriors so that a civil war of the Heresy's kind could not happen again.

The Codex is best known for its most contentious decree, that the [[Space Marine Legions]] be divided into [[Space Marine Chapter|Chapters]] of a thousand battle-brothers. This was carried out in the [[Second Founding]]. Beyond it, the Codex sets out the internal structure of a Chapter, the manner of recruiting and training its members, the tactical doctrines it should follow, the colours and badges by which its warriors are known, and the principles by which a Chapter relates to the Imperium.

Few complete copies of the original text exist. Most of it, however, survives in the training of the Space Marines themselves, and a Chapter that follows its precepts is called Codex-compliant. Several of the most famous Chapters, including the [[Space Wolves]], [[Blood Angels]], [[Dark Angels]] and [[Black Templars]], follow its spirit but diverge from its letter.

History

Origins

The ultimate source of the Codex lies in the war on Calth. When the Word Bearers attacked the Ultramarines in 007.M31, the fighting devolved into a long struggle in the tunnels and holds of the poisoned world, in which small, mobile units of Ultramarines hunted down the Traitors. A veteran sergeant, Aeonid Thiel, formed one such task force, the Red Marked. When Thiel reported to Guilliman on Macragge after the battle, in about 007–008.M31,Date estimated from the sequence of events. the primarch is said to have destroyed the notes on which his old doctrine of the Legion as a horde under a warlord had been written, and to have resolved that the Ultramarines would henceforth be "hundreds of thousands of individual Legionaries each in support of the other".

Composition

After the Heresy, Guilliman was named Lord Commander of the Imperium and Imperial Regent, and carried out the reorganisation of the armed forces. The Codex Astartes was the principal product, and was written between about 014 and 021.M31 and completed in about 021.M31, the year that saw the division of the Legions. It was Guilliman's response to the Heresy as a whole: a detailed attempt to remove its causes. Chief among these was the concentration of power in the hands of a primarch, but a second concern was the quality of the Space Marines themselves.

The haste of the Great Crusade had caused some Legions to accept recruits and gene-seed of poor quality, and the Heresy exposed latent weaknesses. The Codex therefore ordered that Space Marines be created and trained slowly. Cultured organs were to be tested to the strictest standards, young initiates were to undergo trials to prove their suitability, and only those of the sternest character were to be chosen.

Contents

Organisation of a Chapter

The Codex requires a Chapter to consist of ten companies, each of a hundred battle-brothers, together with a headquarters, an armoury, and the specialist officers of the Librarius, the Apothecarion and the Reclusiam. The pattern is as follows.

The Codex expects most Chapters to avoid committing the whole force to a single war, since heavy losses could destroy the Chapter. Casualties in the Battle Companies are to be replaced from the Reserve, and those in the Reserve by promotion from the Scouts.

Doctrine

Tactical doctrine is built around the Tactical squad of ten battle-brothers, which is meant to be able to fight almost any foe. Specialised squads, such as Assault, Devastator and Scout squads, provide heavier firepower or manoeuvre, and the Dreadnoughts, vehicles and Terminators of the Chapter provide support. The Codex advocates speed, flexibility, the combination of arms and the strike at the weakest point of an enemy line. It teaches that a campaign should rarely be risked on a single battle.

Command and authority

Each Chapter is commanded by a Chapter Master. He answers directly to the Emperor, in practice to the High Lords of Terra, and to no other, even the Legion's former primarch. The High Lords alone may order the creation of a Chapter, and every Chapter must tithe five per cent of its gene-seed to the Adeptus Terra and the Mechanicum so that the Imperium may monitor and, if necessary, replace it. The Codex also requires a Chapter to be bound to a single base of operations, its fortress-monastery or fleet, and to support itself on its own recruiting world.

Heraldry

The Codex also prescribes the markings by which a Chapter's warriors are known. The company is identified by a colour on the pauldron trim, and the squad by a badge in a standard pattern, with the Chapter symbol on the left shoulder. Campaign honours, the Chapter's role in a war, and its vehicle numbers are recorded in the same system. Chapters that follow the Codex closely, such as the Ultramarines, use these markings almost to the letter; others, such as the Space Wolves, ignore them.

Reception

The Codex was resisted from the start. Some primarchs thought it an insult to their Legions, which they had built in their own image, and others saw in it a denial of the loyalty that had won the crusade. Lord Commander Guilliman nevertheless enforced it, and most of the surviving primarchs came to accept the need for it. Through the Age of the Imperium it became the standard of Space Marine conduct. A Chapter is judged by how closely it keeps to it, and even those that depart are expected to explain why.

Some Chapters hold it as sacred, others as an ideal to be adapted. The Space Wolves, whose primarch Leman Russ is said to have disliked it, follow their own customs. The Blood Angels and their successors, bearing the Legion's gene-seed flaws, deviate from its pattern in several ways. The Black Templars have been recorded keeping a very different structure, with a Chapter far larger than the thousand-warrior limit.

Revisions

The Codex has not been unchanged. The surviving editions differ, and the Apocrypha of Skaros, one of the earliest, is held to preserve an older form. Guilliman himself, restored to the Imperium in 999.M41, has overseen a revision of the text in the early Era Indomitus. The revised Codex accounts for the Primaris Space Marines of the [[Ultima Founding]] created by the Archmagos Belisarius Cawl. It adds Primaris squads, such as the Intercessor, Hellblaster and Aggressor squads, to the structure of a company, and permits a Chapter to be reinforced by Primaris warriors in numbers beyond the old limit of one thousand. Many Chapters have organised new Battle Companies in this pattern, and the Ultramarines have adopted it more thoroughly than any other.

The older chart of the Codex, still taught in the Chapters' libraries, is accordingly treated as a historical standard, no longer the sole pattern. Guilliman has said that the principle, not the numbers, is what matters, and that the Imperium's present need justifies departures that the Codex of the 31st millennium would have forbidden.
