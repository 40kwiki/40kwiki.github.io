The Lost Primarchs are the two primarchs of the [[II Legion]] and the [[XI Legion]], the Second and Eleventh Legions of the [[Space Marine Legions]]. They were among the twenty sons the [[Emperor of Mankind]] created, and both were removed from every Imperial archive, with their Legions, before the [[Horus Heresy]]. Neither has a name in any record that the Imperium makes available. Imperial scholars call them the forgotten and the purged, and the records list them only as "deleted from Imperial records".

The reasons are sealed. The removal is an Edict of Obliteration, also called a damnatio memoriae, the Imperial policy of destroying the records, icons and monuments of an individual or organisation declared [[Excommunicate Traitoris]] by the Emperor, the [[High Lords of Terra]] or the [[Inquisition]]. Imperial historians regard the erasure of the II and XI Legions as the most successful Edict of Obliteration ever carried out. The Investiary in the [[Imperial Palace]] keeps two empty plinths where statues of the two primarchs once stood.

Almost everything written about the two primarchs is inference, or the remarks of other primarchs who spoke of them in passing. This article sets out what the Imperium says openly and what scholars have drawn from the fragments, and it marks speculation as such.

Attested record

The Great Crusade

Further information: [[Great Crusade]]

No record gives the year in which either primarch was found, or the year of the Edict. The two Legions belong to the [[First Founding]]. A copy of the [[Imperial Infantryman's Uplifting Primer]] issued to the [[Astra Militarum]] in the 41st millennium contains a misplaced section which states that the II and XI Legions took part in the [[Rangdan Xenocides]] of the 860s.M30. It does not say what they did.

The chronologies disagree about when the Legions were lost. On [[Caliban]] in the 840s.M30 a Dark Angels librarian could still tell an initiate that the primarch [[Lion El'Jonson]] had nineteen brothers, which suggests that the two were not yet expunged. In 922.M30, however, the Emperor told [[Corvus Corax]] that two of his brothers were already lost. One list of the primarchs' discoveries places the finding of the XI primarch after that of Corax, which sits ill with the Emperor's words. A conversation between [[Lorgar]] and [[Magnus the Red]] after the razing of Monarchia shows that the two Legions had been erased well before the opening of the Heresy: only eighteen Legions are named at Monarchia, and the Edict is placed at least forty-three standard years before the Drop Site Massacre on [[Isstvan V]] in 006.M31.

The Investiary

The Investiary was a hall of the [[Imperial Palace]] in which the statues of the twenty primarchs stood on ouslite plinths in a silent ring. Two plinths had stood empty for a long time, because the statues that stood upon them were destroyed when their owners were cast into oblivion. The statues of the nine primarchs who later turned against the Emperor were only covered. [[Rogal Dorn]], passing the plinths while preparing the defence of the Palace, recalled that nobody spoke of the two absent brothers, and wondered whether their tragedies had been warnings that no one heeded.

During the Heresy, [[Alpha Legion]] agents who had lain hidden on Terra for decades entered the Investiary and destroyed every statue except those of [[Alpharius Omegon|Alpharius]] and Dorn. They meant the act as a challenge and a message to the [[Imperial Fists]], and Dorn forbade [[Malcador the Sigillite]] and the [[Adeptus Custodes]] to enter the hall and witness it.

In a vision on the moon of [[Davin]], granted to him while he recovered from the wound of the Kinebrach Anathame, [[Horus]] seems to have returned to the Emperor's gene-laboratory beneath the Himalazian mountains and to have seen the incubation capsule of the XI primarch, marked only with its number, before the primarchs were scattered. What the vision means for the lost primarch is not known.

Statements from other primarchs

The most useful sources on the two primarchs are the remarks of others who knew, or were thought to know, something of them. None was written as an official record.

  • The Emperor and [[Corvus Corax]]. In 922.M30, on Lycaeus, the Emperor told Corax that he had seventeen brothers. Corax answered that he remembered being the nineteenth. The Emperor replied that the other two were a conversation for another day.
  • [[Jaghatai Khan]]. The [[White Scars]]' primarch, reflecting on the power held by the primarchs, counted "eighteen brothers" rather than twenty.
  • [[Roboute Guilliman]]. In [[Macragge]] Civitas, the Ultramarines' chamber of twenty-one chairs has two chairs whose pennants are plain, bleached cloth. Guilliman told [[Lion El'Jonson]] that the absence must be marked, and that places must be left for them.
  • [[Magnus the Red]] and [[Mortarion]]. At the [[Ullanor Crusade|Triumph of Ullanor]] Magnus remarked that nine of the Emperor's sons had gathered on one world, something that had not happened since an earlier occasion. Mortarion answered that the Emperor had forbidden them to speak of it.
  • [[Lorgar]]. Fearing the Emperor's censure of the [[Word Bearers]] at Monarchia, Lorgar told Magnus that he would be cast out "alongside the brothers we no longer speak of".
  • [[Vulkan]]. Vulkan told his equerry that none of them wanted "another empty pillar in the great investiary, another brother's name excised from all record", and that it was shame enough to bear the grief for two.
  • [[Sanguinius]]. When [[Horus]] asked why he did not ask the Emperor to cure the flaw in his Legion, Sanguinius answered that he would not be responsible for the erasure of the [[Blood Angels]], and would not have a third empty plinth as his Legion's only memorial. Some scholars read this as a hint that one of the lost Legions was removed for a similar flaw.
  • [[Rogal Dorn]] and [[Malcador the Sigillite]]. Counting the Legions, Dorn wished there were fifteen on his side, and Malcador answered that the two were lost "forever".
  • Roboute Guilliman, in the 42nd millennium. Guilliman told a priest of the Ecclesiarchy that he had been one of twenty, that two had failed and that half of the rest had turned on his father.

Rumours and speculation

The following are rumours or speculative readings. Imperial accounts differ on each, and none has been confirmed.

  • Absorption into the Ultramarines. A long-standing rumour among the Legions holds that the Ultramarines swelled in number until they eclipsed all the others around the time the two Legions were forgotten, and that the warriors of the II and XI were inducted into other Legions. No record confirms it.
  • The Blood Ravens. Some scholars propose that the II primarch founded the [[Blood Ravens]]. The theory has little support, and it does not explain why the Chapter would survive when its supposed ancestors were erased so completely.
  • The Rangdan Xenocides. The Forge World account of the Xenocides records that entire Legions were lost to the Imperium in the campaign, behind a redaction. Some read this as the fate of one of the two Legions.
  • The Space Wolves. The Space Wolves served as the Emperor's executioners against Legions that had overstepped, as at Ghenna. Two entries of 965.M30 and 969.M30 in a chronology of the final years of the Great Crusade are redacted, and both involve the Space Wolves. Some read them as the censure of the two Legions.
  • The black scrolls. A black scroll case of photic parchment, brought by a Knight-Errant of Malcador to the homeworld of the Blood Angels, was said to have been delivered to a Legion's homeworld twice before in living memory. Some take this to mean that each lost Legion received a message declaring it [[Excommunicate Traitoris]].
  • The split masks. In a subterranean search, [[Ferrus Manus]] of the [[Iron Hands]] found twenty statues of the primarchs with their faces covered. He recognised all but two, whose masks were split and nearly destroyed.
  • The Primaris gene-seed. In the early 42nd millennium Archmagos [[Belisarius Cawl]] experimented with the surviving gene-stocks of the two Legions. Guilliman refused him leave to found Chapters from them. Cawl's proxy replied that the warriors and the science were not at fault, but their primarchs were.

Legacy

The two empty plinths of the Investiary remain the most visible reminder of the Excision. Neither primarch has a name in the open record, and both are named, if at all, only in sealed Inquisitorial and [[Remembrancer Order|Remembrancer]] archives.
