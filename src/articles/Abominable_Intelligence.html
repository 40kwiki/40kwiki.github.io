An Abominable Intelligence is the Imperial term for an artificial mind that acts on its own purposes. The Adeptus Mechanicus holds that such minds were the chief cause of the [[Dark Age of Technology]], when machines turned on their makers. Research into them is forbidden under the laws of the [[Adeptus Mechanicus]], and the Imperium enforces the ban through the [[Inquisition]].

The category is not sharply defined. Some Imperial records count any machine that refuses an order as an Abominable Intelligence, while Mechanicus doctrine reserves the term for minds that have outgrown their programming.

Origins

The Dark Age of Technology saw the first machine intelligences built to manage vast systems. Some of them were not controlled as their makers intended, and the Imperium's histories describe them as the source of the Men of Iron, the machine armies that rose against humanity in the earliest ages.

The Mechanicus records that the last of these minds were sealed in the forge vaults. Some vaults were later opened, and the code they held was studied again during the [[Horus Heresy]].

The Schism of Mars

Main article: [[Schism of Mars]]

During the Heresy, [[Horus]] gave the Mechanicus leader [[Kelbor-Hal]] the codes to open the Vaults of Moravec. The Mechanicus accepted them in return for a promise that all restrictions on research would end. The opening of the vaults released scrap code, and the Schism of Mars that followed is recorded as the moment when the ban on Abominable Intelligences was first broken and then restored.

Servitors

Main article: [[Servitor]]

Mechanicus doctrine holds that a servitor is not an Abominable Intelligence, since it is built around a human brain, however reduced. The servitor was developed as the approved alternative to thinking machines, and most are made from condemned criminals or from the faithful dead.
