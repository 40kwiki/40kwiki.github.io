The Thunder Warriors, also the Thunder Legions or Thunder Regiments and, in [[High Gothic]], the Legiones Cataegis, were twenty legions of superhuman soldiers made by the [[Emperor of Mankind]] to end the warfare of the [[Age of Strife]] on [[Terra]]. They were the first of his gene-engineered warriors and the prototype of the [[Space Marines]] who replaced them. They were made from adult men by surgery, chemistry and gene-sculpting, clothed in the first suits of [[Power armour|power armour]] and sent to war under a banner of a raptor and lightning. They were stronger, faster and more savage than any warrior the later Legions fielded, and they won the [[Unification Wars]]. They were also designed, or at least permitted, to burn out within a few decades, and when the peace was made the Emperor had them destroyed.

The Imperium taught for ten thousand years that the last of the Thunder Warriors fell heroically at Mount Ararat, in the final battle of the Unification. That is not what happened. Testimony of the [[Adeptus Custodes]] states that the Thunder Warriors were culled by the Emperor's order. The cost of that decision and the fate of the few who survived it are among the most discussed matters of the founding age.

Creation

Old Night on Terra

In the late 29th millennium Terra had been at war with itself for two and a half thousand years. The planet's government had dissolved into dozens of techno-barbarian states, whose rulers fought with the surviving weapons of the [[Age of Technology|Dark Age of Technology]] and with armies of gene-bred and machine-grafted soldiers. The warlords of that age, such as Kalagann of Ursh, Cardinal Tang of the Yndonesic Bloc and Narthan Dume of the Pan-Pacific Empire, held the whole planet in a stalemate of mutual slaughter.

The Emperor, who had walked among Mankind for many thousands of years under many names, judged that the Strife could be ended only by force. For solar decades he had worked in hidden laboratories beneath the Himalazian mountains, with a staff of savants and gene-wrights, some willing and some taken captive from his enemies. The staff became the Imperial Biotechnical Division. What it made was an army that could beat the warlords' own.

The process

The Thunder Warriors were made from adult male supporters of the Emperor, which was the chief difference between them and the later [[Adeptus Astartes|Astartes]], who were taken in adolescence. The process was a combination of surgical and alchemical alteration, gene-sculpting and muscle grafting. It thickened the skeleton, strengthened the muscles, sharpened the reflexes and wired in resistance to environmental hazard and to psychic attack. The Imperial records add that the Emperor's own strategic gifts went into their design, so that the Thunder Warriors were not simply strong. They were trained to fight as an army.

The technique was not perfected, and it relied on adult bodies that were poorly suited to the changes. Many of the Thunder Warriors suffered metabolic collapse within a few Terran decades, or broke down in mind, with fits of uncontrollable rage and hallucination. Some hold that the flaw was written into them on purpose, so that they would cease to exist when their usefulness was at an end.

Organisation

The Thunder Warriors were organised in twenty large formations, called legions or regiments, each led by a general who held the title of [[Primarch|primarch]]. The word would later be given to the Emperor's sons. Unlike the primarchs of the [[Space Marine Legions]], who were physically and mentally unlike the warriors they led, the Thunder Warrior primarchs were no more than the best of the men they commanded, chosen for skill and for stability of mind.

The Thunder Legions seem to have had tactical roles that were later echoed by the Space Marine Legions. The IV Legion of the Cataegis, for example, was known as the Iron Lords and specialised in siege warfare, as the IV Legion of the Space Marines, the [[Iron Warriors]], would do.

Wargear

The Thunder Warriors wore the [[Mark I Thunder power armour|Mark I Thunder pattern]], an early suit of [[power armour]] that was not fully enclosed and powered only the upper body. In their earliest days they had worn simple metal armour and carried [[Lasrifle|lasrifles]]; later they were armed with a large and archaic pattern of the [[Bolter|bolter]]. They marched under the [[Raptor Imperialis]], a banner showing a raptor's head and lightning, which was the early iconography of the Imperium before the Emperor adopted the double-headed eagle of the [[Aquila (symbol)|Imperial Aquila]] in the Great Crusade.

Character

The Thunder Warriors were deeply emotional. The Emperor's later Astartes were shaped to restrain their feelings, and the [[Adeptus Custodes|Custodes]] were drilled until theirs were almost invisible, but the Thunder Warriors kept their full human range, and relished the power and savagery of battle. Contemporary accounts also record a dry humour, which was noted by the [[Imperialis Auxilia|Imperial Army]] officers who served beside them and by the Custodes who later had to fight them. They were not easily disciplined. In the field they could stop obeying an order in the middle of an engagement, and the Emperor's commanders had to plan for the possibility.

Abilities

The Thunder Warriors were larger than most Space Marines of the first Legions and stronger. A Thunder Warrior could fight several Astartes at once in close combat. The accounts describe a skeletal system thicker than that of a Legionary, with a skull that could be used as a weapon, and legs that could kick through the torso of an ordinary man. Their reflexes were such that one is recorded dodging the bolt of a plasma gun while fighting two Astartes at the same time. Their upper-body strength, helped by the armour, was greater than anything the later warriors had.

They were designed to resist psychic assault. Any [[Psyker|psyker]] who attempted to enter the mind of a Thunder Warrior was met with pain, and a few died of the backlash. The feature was intentional. In the late [[Age of Strife]] the Emperor's enemies often used sorcery, and the Thunder Warriors were to meet them on their own ground. In combat the Thunder Warrior's chief weakness was a short life, and even in decline, a Thunder Warrior was still more than a match for an Astartes.

Service

The Unification Wars

Main article: [[Unification Wars]]

The Thunder Warriors were first used in the late 29th millennium. They won battle after battle, against enemies whose forces were larger and who held the advantage of ground. The battles of the Unification lasted for weeks and killed millions. The Ninth Battle of Xozer in 992.M29, in which Kalagann of Ursh was slain in the Nordafrik Conclaves, was one of the notable battles of the wars.

As the wars went on, the first Space Marines, picked from the Emperor's own mortal bodyguard, began to replace the Thunder Warriors, who were more savage and far less disciplined. The Adeptus Custodes were the only soldiers in the Imperial armies who could defeat the Thunder Legions in open battle.

The Culling

The accepted account of what followed was provided by Arik Taranis and then by the Custodes. The Emperor needed the people of Terra to unite behind a single leader and needed the official record to show that he had reunited the world himself, with warriors who had died gloriously. The Thunder Warriors were created for war and were not suited to anything else, and their instability made them a danger to the new state. At first the Emperor did not replace those who fell. When the final battle was won, he ordered the rest destroyed.

Imperial records differ on how the culling was carried out. The Custodes' testimony credits the Custodian Guard under [[Constantin Valdor]], the only soldiers in the army who could defeat a Thunder Warrior in open battle. A second source states that a cadre of several hundred Custodians, led by Valdor, was joined by several thousand of the first Space Marines of the [[Dark Angels|I Legion]], prototypes of the Dark Angels. A third states that the Thunder Warriors, aware at last that the Emperor had made them short-lived, turned on him before the end of the wars, and that the cull was an act of defence. These accounts are not mutually exclusive.

Survivors

The Palace coup

See also: [[Imperial Palace]]

In the final years of the Unification Wars a group of at least two dozen Thunder Warriors under Ushotan, who had been primarch of the [[Iron Warriors|IV Legion]], took part in the [[Imperial Palace]] Coup. It was led by a former Provost Marshal, [[Uwoma Kandawire]], who hoped to call [[Constantin Valdor|Valdor]] to account for the destruction of the Thunder Warriors. The surviving warriors joined it for revenge and for an honourable death in battle, which was better than waiting for madness or metabolic failure. They were put down by the first Space Marines to be deployed in open battle. Ushotan was killed by Valdor in single combat.

The Dait'Tar

Bands of Thunder Warriors continued to exist in hiding, and some escaped off Terra. A few were hunted down by the first Astartes of the [[Dark Angels|I Legion]], the future Dark Angels. In the early [[Great Crusade]] a small group of survivors calling themselves the Dait'Tar were among the renegades who began the Cerberus Insurrection. The Emperor sent the [[World Eaters|XII Legion]], then called the War Hounds and later the World Eaters, to crush the uprising. Despite their physical decline, and though they were badly outnumbered by one of the strongest of the Legions, the Thunder Warriors killed four or five times their number of Astartes before they were overwhelmed in several hours of close fighting.

Taranis

The best known of the Thunder Warriors is Arik Taranis, the Lightning Bearer, who in the older Imperial account raised the Banner of Lightning at the proclamation of Unity and died of his wounds on the field at Ararat. The [[Adeptus Custodes|Custodes]]' testimony holds that he lived. He took many names in the intervening years, among them that of a Terran called Babu Dhakal, and he survived into the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31). He had learned something of the Emperor's methods, and tried to use it to create a new army of Thunder Warriors under his own command. He suspected that his people had been made short-lived on purpose, though he hoped it was only a flaw. In the end a Thunder Warrior named Ghota encountered a band of [[Chaos Space Marines|Traitor Astartes]] and from one of them recovered a means of curing the instability in his people's genes. Taranis was cured, though not before he had suffered considerable degeneration.

Gladiators and others

Other survivors lived out their days in obscurity on Terra. Four, who still had the strength of warriors, served as arena fighters in the Maw, in the slums of the Outer Palace, owned by a former [[Astropathy|astropath]] who kept them alive by transplanting the organs of dead Thunder Warriors into their bodies. One died in the arena, one went mad and was killed by a comrade, and one died fighting a daemon-possessed [[Traitor]] Legionary. The last, Dahren Heruk, was killed at his own request by a Custodian whose life he had saved. A World Eaters Space Marine, Endryd Haar, who served the Imperium as a Blackshield, may have been a Thunder Warrior who was successfully made into a Space Marine. He told [[Malcador the Sigillite]] that he had fought alongside the Emperor in the Unification and called the Dait'Tar his brothers.

Legacy

The Thunder Warriors were the pattern for the Space Marines. The Space Marines were made in their place from adolescents, who could better bear the changes, and with all the lessons learned from the success and failure of the Thunder Warriors. The Thunder Warriors were a means to an end, the conquest of a single world, and the Space Marines were made to conquer a galaxy.

Their banner, the [[Raptor Imperialis]], was the early iconography of the Imperium, and the lightning bolt and raptor gave the [[Mark I Thunder power armour|Mark I Thunder pattern]] its common name.
