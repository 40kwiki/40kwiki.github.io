A shrine world is a planet of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] held sacred by the [[Imperial Cult]]. Most are associated with an [[Imperial saint]], a miracle or a relic: the place where a saint was born, preached, died or is buried, or where the Emperor is believed to have shown his favour. Shrine worlds receive countless pilgrims, and their economies, cities and customs are shaped around the worship of the [[Emperor of Mankind]].

A shrine world may be governed by a secular [[planetary governor]] or directly by the [[Adeptus Ministorum]]. Those ruled by the Ministorum are recorded by the [[Administratum]] as [[cardinal world]]s. The holiest shrine world of all is [[Terra]] itself, the Throneworld, which is the goal of the greatest pilgrimages in the Imperium.

Characteristics

The surface of a shrine world is commonly covered with cathedrals, chapels, mausolea, reliquaries and the inns and hostels of pilgrims. Many shrine worlds are also [[civilised world|civilised]], [[agri-world|agricultural]] or even [[hive world]]s, and their holy places stand beside ordinary industry. Their populations are swollen by pilgrims, some of whom never leave, and by the trade in devotional objects, prayer-scrolls and relics, genuine and false.

The Ecclesiarchy keeps the shrines, receives the offerings of pilgrims and maintains missions and schools. The [[Adepta Sororitas]] often keep a preceptory on important shrine worlds, and the [[Black Templars]] and other Chapters have at times guarded them. Some shrine worlds are the destination of great crusades or of whole pilgrim-fleets travelling for years.

History

The veneration of holy places began during the [[Great Crusade]] (c. 798.M30–005.M31), when followers of the [[Lectitio Divinitatus]] revered worlds where the Emperor had fought or where miracles were reported, though the [[Imperial Truth]] forbade such worship. After the [[Horus Heresy]] (005–014.M31) the Imperial Cult became the faith of the Imperium, and the sites associated with the Emperor, the loyal primarchs and the first saints were consecrated.

Shrine worlds multiplied over the following millennia as new saints were recognised. During the [[Age of Apostasy]] in the early 36th millennium the Ecclesiarchy's control of shrine worlds was one of the foundations of the power of [[Goge Vandire]]. Later crusades were often launched to recover shrine worlds lost to heretics or xenos; the [[Sabbat Worlds Crusade]], launched in 755.M41, was fought to reclaim the worlds sacred to Saint Sabbat.

In the [[Era Indomitus]] shrine worlds have been prime targets for the forces of [[Chaos]], who seek to desecrate them, and the Imperium has defended many at great cost. Reports of miracles and of the return of saints on shrine worlds have also increased since 999.M41.

Pilgrimage

Pilgrimage is one of the central acts of the Imperial Cult, and shrine worlds are its goal. Pilgrims travel alone or in great companies, often spending their whole lives and fortunes on the journey. The Ecclesiarchy organises pilgrim ships, and the [[Imperial Navy]] sometimes escorts them. Many pilgrims die on the way, and those who reach a shrine world may stay there for the rest of their lives, swelling the poor districts of its cities. The arrival of a great pilgrimage can strain a shrine world's food supply, and governors depend on nearby [[agri-world]]s to feed the visiting multitudes.

Shrine worlds also attract fraud. The trade in false relics, forged indulgences and spurious miracles is a constant concern of the Ecclesiarchy, and the [[Ordo Hereticus]] watches for cults that hide behind the forms of devotion. Some shrine worlds have been found to be centres of heresy, their saints false and their miracles the work of the warp.

Government

Where a shrine world is not a cardinal world, its secular governor shares power with the local hierarchy of the Ecclesiarchy, usually headed by a bishop or cardinal of the surrounding diocese. The governor keeps order and pays the Administratum's tithe; the Church keeps the shrines and collects the offerings of the faithful. Relations between the two are often uneasy, and the [[Administratum]] has at times reassessed a shrine world's class to settle a dispute between them. The [[Adeptus Arbites]] are frequently called upon to keep order among the crowds of pilgrims at the great feasts.
