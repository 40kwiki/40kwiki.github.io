The Imperium Nihilus, or Dark Imperium, is the name given to the half of the [[Imperium of Mankind]] that was cut off from [[Terra]] by the [[Great Rift]], the Cicatrix Maledictum, at the climax of the [[13th Black Crusade]] in 999.M41. The region includes the greater part of the Segmentum Obscurus and the Segmentum Ultima, in the north and east of the galaxy. It is a defining feature of the astrography of the Era Indomitus. The other half, the territory of Terra, where the light of the [[Astronomican]] returned soon after the end of the Noctis Aeterna and was held steady by the first phase of the [[Indomitus Crusade]], is called the Imperium Sanctus.

The name is accurate. Beyond the Rift, the light of the Astronomican is hidden from the Navigators and the Warp is rough with the rising power of the Ruinous Powers, so that travel and astropathic messages are all but impossible. Thousands of worlds have fought alone for decades. They are attacked by Chaos, by Orks, by Tyranids, by the Drukhari, by Necrons and by the Aeldari. The region is administered, so far as it is administered, by Commander Dante, who was named Lord Regent by [[Roboute Guilliman]] after the [[Devastation of Baal]].

Origin

The Great Rift

Main article: [[Great Rift]]

The Great Rift is a tear in reality and a galaxy-wide series of Warp storms, which stretches from the [[Eye of Terror]] in the west to the Hadex Anomaly on the Eastern Fringe. No one fully understands why it opened. The Imperial scholars name many causes: the breach of the Cadian Gate and the fall of Cadia with its blackstone pylons; the sorcery of the Daemon Primarch [[Magnus the Red]] at the invasion of Fenris; a catastrophe in the Webway; the partial awakening of Ynnead, the god of the Aeldari dead; the breaking of the craftworld Biel-Tan; the breaking of the Amethal daemon cage; the bloodshed between Imperium and T'au in the Damocles Gulf; and the resurrection of Roboute Guilliman. All of these, and other events, may have contributed to it. The Rift is known by other names among other species: the Crimson Path to the forces of Chaos, the Mouth of Ruin, and, to the Orks, Gork's Grin.

When it opened, a wave of empyric force and darkness, called the Noctis Aeterna, dimmed the Astronomican. It claimed countless worlds, fleets and armies. For the people of the Imperium Sanctus, the Rift is a scar in the sky. For those on the other side, it is much worse: the gates of Hell.

The Noctis Aeterna

No reliable account can be given of what happened in the first years in the Imperium Nihilus. Time itself was damaged. In some sectors it ran fast and in others slowly in relation to Terran time, and the historitors and chronotechs of Terra have found it hard to understand the reports that have reached them. As the effect faded and some communication returned, a picture became clear. Thousands of worlds had been engulfed. Planets and populations were destroyed or changed beyond recognition. Daemons came out of rifts to torment and kill. Heretical cults arose and prophesied the end of the world. Mutations spread and entire populations turned on one another in violence and cannibalism. Other worlds held out: the fortresses mobilised their garrisons and drove back the enemy from within and without, the agri-worlds raised militias that vanished into the wilds to wage a guerrilla war, and the industrial worlds drafted every man, woman and child and threw them against the horrors.

Imperial counts were made at the end of the first phase of the Indomitus Crusade. Slightly fewer than half of the one thousand Space Marine Chapters were still unaccounted for, and at least twelve Chapter homeworlds were reported destroyed.

Geography and passage

Crossing the Rift

To cross the Rift is hazardous, and was long considered impossible. There are two comparatively stable passages, which are called gauntlets, but both are haunted by phantoms of the Warp and by pirate wolf packs. Beyond them there are narrow channels that open between the storm belts and close again like a jaw on those who try to pass.

The better known is the Nachmund Gauntlet, which passes close to the Eye of Terror, in a region that Navigators have avoided since the Great Crusade. When the nebula of the Nachmund lulls, the Nachmund System is visible, close to Agripinaa on the near side. Its tyrant king, Kaligius, rules the household of House Mandrakor, a family of Renegade Knights on the Knight World of Dharrovar that had not been seen since the Horus Heresy. Kaligius said that Old Night had returned to plague the galaxy and refused all communication with the Imperium, and made pacts with Renegades and with pirate fleets instead. A number of Imperial vessels have run the Gauntlet. Many more have been destroyed on it, and the hulks of countless craft hang in the void there, a graveyard of ships. It was by this way that Fleet Primus of the Indomitus Crusade came to the relief of Baal.

At the end of the Gauntlet, on the Imperial side, is the hive world of Vigilus, and on the other side its counterpart, Sangua Terra. The two worlds have in their crusts altered deposits of blackstone, which help to hold the passage open. For this reason Vigilus has been fought over by every power in the region. In the War of Beasts, and later the War of Nightmares, Orks, Genestealer cults, Drukhari and the craftworld Aeldari assaulted it, and the Black Legion lord Haarken Worldclaimer was sent by Abaddon to take it. The planet was held by its garrison of the Astra Militarum and the Adepta Sororitas, and by relief forces under Marneus Calgar of the Ultramarines. It was then designated a Sentinel World and a War World. It remains a key route for resupply and communication with the Imperial forces that are trying to retake the Imperium Nihilus.

Communication

The Imperium Nihilus has been almost wholly cut off from the astropathic network. It takes a great and often fatal effort to force a message across it, and what arrives often arrives as recurring nightmares. Voidships may risk only the shortest jumps, so that travel is both perilous and slow. Many worlds are wholly isolated, and some believe themselves to be the last bastions of human civilisation in the galaxy. The Choral Engine, an artefact on Malakbael that might have served as a local Astronomican, was destroyed in the [[Arks of Omen]]. The hub-fortresses of the Indomitus Crusade have restored some of the network on the Terran side, but not beyond the Rift.

Life in the Imperium Nihilus

Each world in the Imperium Nihilus must stand alone. Many have taken monstrous measures to keep the lights of civilisation burning. Plagues of mutation and madness grow, and landscapes change beneath the sky. Malefic entities appear at will and prey on the unwary. The Great Rift is visible in the sky above every world, and corrupts all that its light touches.

The enemies of the Imperium have been affected differently. Some xenos species can cross the stars without the Warp, by technology, by biology or by sorcery, and they are not hindered. Others take pleasure in the madness and ride the tides of the Warp wherever they lead. The Warp that wrecks Imperial ships bears the Heretics and Traitors along on favourable currents, and surges of immaterial energy let renegade psykers perform rites that drag whole worlds into damnation. Ancient beings long absent from the galaxy are gathering strength to force their way through.

Yet the loyal worlds fight on. Chapter planets, Inquisitorial fortresses and Sororitas preceptories shine as beacons. The Knight Worlds light their fires as they did in Old Night. Rogue Traders, Mechanicus explorator fleets and Navy flotillas go into the storms to bring hope to lost worlds or to settle new colonies. The Imperial position is that the Dark Imperium may be endured by faith and held by hatred.

Regency of Dante

After the Devastation of Baal and the defeat of the Leviathan tendril there, Guilliman announced that the Chapter Master of the Blood Angels, Commander Dante, would be Lord Regent of the Imperium Nihilus, so that the Lord Commander could turn to the defence and strengthening of the Imperium Sanctus. Dante, who has lived for more than a thousand years, is among the most senior living commanders of the Adeptus Astartes. The Regent's authority is hard to exercise, since he can speak with few of the worlds he rules. His actions have been, in the main, those of a commander of the field: he led the Blood Angels out into the dark after the rebuilding of Baal and brought the Sanguinary Brotherhood with them. He was grievously wounded by Angron on Wyrmwood.

Notable events

  • [[Devastation of Baal]]: the assault of Hive Fleet Leviathan on the Blood Angels at the moment of the Rift's opening.
  • The War of Beasts: the multi-sided war for Vigilus, the keystone of the Nachmund Gauntlet.
  • [[Arks of Omen]]: Abaddon's search for the Key, the battle of Malak, the return of the Lion and the banishment of Angron.
  • The Fourth Tyrannic War: the renewed assault of Hive Fleet Leviathan, in three tendrils, on the western fringe and the Segmentum Pacificus.
  • [[Pariah Nexus]]: the Necron blackstone array in the Nephilim Sector and the Necron civil war around it.

Abaddon the Despoiler, who took the title of Warmaster of the Imperium Nihilus, has not made the Imperium Nihilus his own. His fleets and the Arks are active throughout it. The Black Legion is one of several dangerous powers, and some of the Fallen Angels have been reported gathering in the Somnium Stars.

The Imperium Sanctus

Main article: [[Imperium Sanctus]]

The other half is the Imperium Sanctus, the "Holy Imperium", which contains Terra, Mars, the Segmentum Solar, most of the Segmentum Pacificus and the western parts of the Tempestus. The light of the Astronomican has returned to it, and the crusades of Guilliman have repaired much of what the Noctis Aeterna destroyed. It is not safe, for the Rift is open on its border. It has also been the object of the Lord Commander's reforms. The Imperium Sanctus is the area in which he has been able to restore, by long effort, something resembling the order of the Imperium before the Rift.

Reckoning of time

The effect of the Rift on time has been so great that the Imperial Calendar was revised on the orders of Guilliman. The new reckoning counts the opening of the Great Rift as the point from which later years are measured, and dates of the 42nd millennium given here follow it. Dates from the Imperium Nihilus remain doubtful. Even the records of the battlegroups of the Indomitus Crusade, each of which keeps a private chronology, differ by years.
