    Warp storm
    The Ruinstorm as recorded by a Navigator of the Heresy era
    Artwork © Games Workshop

    Caused byEmotion, war and psychic power; Chaos; the fall of species
    EffectsBlinds the Astronomican; disrupts astropathic messages; scatters ships; opens passages for daemons

    Notable examplesEye of Terror, Maelstrom, Ruinstorm, Hadex Anomaly, Great Rift
    DangerExtreme; ships entering a storm are seldom recovered

    A warp storm, also written warpstorm, is a region of violent turbulence in the Warp, comparable to a tempest in an ocean. Warp storms are born of the emotions of living beings, and they swell with war, terror and psychic power. Some last a few days. Others have lasted thousands of years, and a few, such as the Eye of Terror, have torn the boundary between realspace and the Immaterium open. Within a storm the currents of the Warp are unreadable, Navigators are blinded, and ships are thrown far off course or destroyed.[1][2]

    A warp storm obscures the light of the Astronomican and cuts off astropathic messages, so that the worlds inside it are isolated for years, decades or centuries. It is also the means by which Chaos enters the material universe. Where a storm lies upon a world, the veil is thin, daemons may cross, and the minds of the population are open to corruption. The Imperium treats the approach of a storm as a grave threat, and the region around a persistent storm is often a place of Chaos cults, mutation and war.[3]

    The greatest warp storm in recorded history is the Great Rift, the Cicatrix Maledictum, which opened in 999.M41 and divided the Imperium of Mankind in two.[4]

    Nature and causes

    [edit]

    The Warp is formed of the thoughts and passions of living minds, and it responds to them. A great battle, a plague, a massacre or a mass of worshippers sends ripples through the Immaterium, and where the ripples meet they form a storm. The Adeptus Mechanicus compares the process to weather and the Ecclesiarchy to the wrath of the Emperor. The Inquisition regards both comparisons as insufficient, because some storms are made, not born, by the rites of sorcerers.[3][5]

    A storm can be caused by a single event. The Fall of the Aeldari gave birth to Slaanesh and opened the Eye of Terror, and the sacrifice performed by the Word Bearers at Calth raised the Ruinstorm. A storm may also grow out of a persistent source, such as a great concentration of psykers, a fortress of Chaos or a world on which a god of Chaos is worshipped. The storms of the Age of Strife are believed to have been caused by the psychic upheaval of the humans' own decline.[5]

    The effect of a storm on the Warp is strongest at its centre and fades with distance. The surrounding region, called the storm's margin, is turbulent but often passable.[1]

    Effects

    [edit]

    On travel

    [edit]

    A ship that enters a storm cannot be steered. The Navigator's third eye is dazzled by the storm, and the light of the Astronomican is lost. Ships may be thrown tens of thousands of light-years from their course, or held in the Warp for years, or destroyed outright. Those that survive may emerge in a different century. Even a ship that does not enter a storm may be diverted by it, because a storm changes the currents around it and ends the stable routes that crossed its region.[1]

    On communication

    [edit]

    Astropathic messages are scattered by storms, and a world inside a storm may hear nothing from the Imperium for generations. In the worst cases the astropaths of the world are driven mad by the noise of the Warp.[6]

    On worlds

    [edit]

    The inhabitants of a world under a storm suffer a rise in mutation and psychic outbreaks. Their dreams are disturbed, and cults arise with great speed. The Imperium's governors report spectral lights, the appearance of daemons in the streets and sudden changes in the weather and the seasons. A world cut off by a storm may revert to barbarism, fall to Chaos or be forgotten for ten thousand years.[3]

    Daemonic incursions

    [edit]
    Main article: Daemon

    Daemons cross into realspace most easily where a storm touches a world. The Imperium records frequent incursions on worlds near the Eye of Terror and the Maelstrom, and in the regions of the Great Rift. A world that suffers one may be reclaimed by the Inquisition or the Adeptus Astartes, or it may be declared lost and subjected to Exterminatus. Where a storm lasts for centuries, a world may become a Daemon world, in which the laws of realspace no longer hold.[3]

    Notable storms

    [edit]

    The storms of the Age of Strife

    [edit]

    In the late Dark Age of Technology, the rise of psychic power in the human species produced great storms that cut off the human worlds from one another. These storms, which are blamed for the Age of Strife (c. M25 – M30), did not clear until the Fall of the Aeldari opened the Eye of Terror and the Warp's energy drained into it. The Navigator houses and the Emperor's Astronomican later restored contact.[5][a]

    The Eye of Terror

    [edit]
    Main article: Eye of Terror

    The Eye of Terror is a permanent rift in the north-west of the galaxy, born of the Fall of the Aeldari c. 000.M30.[b] It is the stronghold of the Traitor Legions and the starting point of the Black Crusades. The region was kept under constant watch from the fortress world of Cadia until that world fell in 999.M41.[3]

    The Ruinstorm

    [edit]
    Main article: Ruinstorm

    The Ruinstorm was raised in 007.M31 by Erebus on the surface of Calth, at the command of Lorgar. It grew with the suffering of the Heresy and by 009.M31 had cut the galaxy in two, isolating Terra from Ultramar and the rest of the Imperium. It began to fail in about 011.M31, when the loyal primarchs destroyed the storm's anchor at Davin, and dispersed after the death of Horus at the end of the Siege of Terra in 014.M31.[7]

    The Storm of the Emperor's Wrath

    [edit]
    Main article: Reign of Blood

    The Storm of the Emperor's Wrath is a persistent storm in the Caradryad Sector that began in the Age of Apostasy, in the 36th millennium. The Ecclesiarchy holds that the Emperor raised it to show his anger at the tyranny of Goge Vandire and the Reign of Blood (c. 218–288.M36). The Administratum records only that it appeared in the years of the Reign and has since swallowed much of the sector.[8]

    The Maelstrom

    [edit]
    Main article: Maelstrom

    The Maelstrom is a great storm in the Ultima Segmentum that has been the domain of the Red Corsairs since the Badab War. It is a rift into the Warp, from which daemons and pirates raid the surrounding sectors, and the Imperium has fought to contain it for millennia.[3]

    The Hadex Anomaly

    [edit]
    Main article: Hadex Anomaly

    The Hadex Anomaly, in the Jericho Reach, appeared in 656.M40. Unlike other storms it disturbs time as well as space. Ships leave the Warp before they enter it, and worlds in its glow suffer days of changing length. It forms the eastern end of the Great Rift.[9]

    The Great Rift

    [edit]
    Main article: Great Rift

    The Great Rift opened in 999.M41, at the climax of the Thirteenth Black Crusade and the destruction of Cadia. It crosses the galaxy from the Eye of Terror to the Hadex Anomaly and is a chain of storms rather than a single one. It brought the Noctis Aeterna and the division of the Imperium, and it has caused the opening of many new daemon worlds.[4]
