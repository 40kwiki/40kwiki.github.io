    The Grey Knights are a secret Chapter of the Adeptus Astartes whose only purpose is to destroy daemons and the servants who summon them. They were created by the Emperor, with the aid of Malcador the Sigillite, in the last days of the Horus Heresy (c. 013–014.M31), and their gene-seed is drawn from the Emperor's own genome. They are psykers to a man, and they believe themselves incorruptible, since no Grey Knight has ever fallen to Chaos. They are the Chamber Militant of the Ordo Malleus of the Inquisition, and they fight in the places where the barrier between reality and the Warp has been broken.[1][2]

    The Grey Knights number a thousand in total. They are the greatest weapon the Imperium has against the denizens of the Warp. They are armed with blessed Nemesis force weapons, which hurt daemons, and with the psychic gifts that all of them have, and they are sealed from the corrupting power of the Warp by their training, their purity and their armour. Most of the Imperium has never heard of them. Those who have seen a Grey Knight in battle are normally mind-wiped or executed afterward, since the secrets of the Chapter are held to be more dangerous than anything it fights.[1]

    In the Era Indomitus the Grey Knights are in constant action. The Great Rift has let more daemons into the Imperium than in all the history of the Chapter, and the Grey Knights fly to every part of the Imperium Sanctus and the Imperium Nihilus. The Chapter's old secrecy has been relaxed in places, since daemons are now so common that the Chapters of the Astartes can no longer be kept ignorant of them.[3]

    History

    [edit]

    Origins in the Heresy

    [edit]

    The Chapter's origins are known only from the records of the Inquisition and the Chapter itself, most of which are sealed. The most recent evidence, gathered after the Siege of Terra, gives the following account.[2]

    When the Emperor learned that Horus had betrayed him on Isstvan III, he saw that even if the Warmaster were defeated, Chaos would remain. His Space Marines had proved corruptible, and the Emperor concluded that he must create a new kind of warrior, with a will to resist the Warp. He turned to Malcador, his Regent, and charged him to gather a company of servants whose loyalty was beyond doubt. Malcador's agents included a band of independent Astartes known as the Knights-Errant, who had either stayed loyal to the Emperor in the Legions or had left their Traitor Legions after the massacre at Isstvan III. Before the Siege began he presented twelve individuals to the Emperor in the Imperial Palace. Four were mortal. The others, eight in all, were Knights-Errant, and they were psykers whose gift had been suppressed under the edict of the Council of Nikaea.[2]

    The four mortals became the first Inquisitors. The eight Astartes, who had given up all ties to their Legions and their primarchs, were sent with Malcador to a hidden fortress on Titan, the moon of Saturn, which the Emperor had prepared. There they received a stock of gene-seed made from the Emperor's own blood, and a host of recruits taken from the worlds of the Imperium, tested for psychic talent and the absence of any trace of Chaos. Malcador appointed one of the eight, Janus, the first Supreme Grand Master of the Grey Knights, and wove a psychic ward about the moon that took it into the Warp. He then went back to Terra to face his last duty.[2]

    The eight Grand Masters were called the Titans, since each took the name of one of the Titans of ancient Terran myth. They were Ianius (Janus), Koios, Iapto, Satre, Epimetheus, Yotun, Ogen and Khyron. The Chapter's tradition holds that they came from a number of Legions, among them the Thousand Sons, the Ultramarines, the Luna Wolves, the Blood Angels, the Dark Angels, the Space Wolves and the Raven Guard. One is said to have been offered a place as a ninth and to have refused it.[2]

    The Second Founding

    [edit]
    Main article: Second Founding

    For many years Titan remained in the Warp while the Emperor was interred in the Golden Throne and the Loyalists hunted down the Traitors in the Great Scouring. Time moves differently in the Immaterium. Where years passed in the Imperium, whole decades passed on Titan, and the eight Grand Masters trained the host they had been given into a Chapter. When the Legions were being divided into Chapters under the Codex Astartes, Titan returned. Eight Astartes had gone in with hundreds of thousands of recruits. A thousand Grey Knights came out.[2]

    The Inquisitor Lords, the four mortals of Malcador's gathering, had by then become the masters of the new Inquisition. With great care they entered the Grey Knights in the records of the Adeptus Terra as the 666th Chapter of the Second Founding. The Imperium was full of new Chapters at the time, and few noticed one more. The Inquisitor Lords went to Titan and agreed a pact with Janus, the terms of which are written in the Chapter's annals. It is the oldest compact of the Inquisition.[2]

    The long secret war

    [edit]

    The Grey Knights spent their first millennia in a secret war. They destroyed a daemon on the twin moons of Yyrm, banished a warp-wyrm at Vause and a Khornate warlord at Korpolis, and they were remembered afterward only in the drawings of the dying and the tales of blind monks. In the 32nd millennium the Inquisition, whose single office had been unable to use the Chapter properly during the War of the Beast, divided in two. The Ordo Malleus took the original purpose of fighting Chaos and the Grey Knights as its Chamber Militant, while the Ordo Xenos was created to deal with aliens. A code chip left by the dying Inquisitor Veritus allowed another Inquisitor to travel to Titan to arrange it with Janus.[2]

    The campaigns of the following ages are largely lost. A record survives of the Hollow Cult, in which the whole of the 7th Brotherhood was nearly destroyed in the 34th millennium. In the 36th, the Brother-Captain Edeon and his squads were lost in the Warp for many centuries before word of them reached the Chapter. The Chapter hunted a Daemon Prince through a maze of ghost-glass, took the Astral Blades Chapter to the sword after its Chapter Master was possessed, and fought a Traitor force and Traitor Titans at Coriolanthe in 948.M38.[1]

    The Time of Ending

    [edit]
    Further information: Time of Ending

    In 444.M41 the first War for Armageddon brought the Grey Knights into the open. The Daemon Primarch Angron led a Chaos horde on to the planet and was held at bay by the Space Wolves of Logan Grimnar until a brotherhood of a hundred Grey Knights under Brother-Captain Taremar Aurellian arrived and cast the daemon back into the Warp. Afterward the Inquisition sterilised and quarantined the survivors of the world, an act known as the Months of Shame. The only Chapter that was granted knowledge of the Grey Knights was the Space Wolves, who had fought beside them.[1]

    In 799.M41 the young Kaldor Draigo became known for his part in banishing the Daemon Prince M'kar at Acralem, and in 901.M41 he became Supreme Grand Master after the death of Geronitan. In 941.M41 he led three brotherhoods against the Tzeentchian Ix'thar'ganix on Decimalus, and in 959–961.M41 the Chapter took part in the defeat of the Despoiler's gathering at Pandorax. In 999.M41, as the Thirteenth Black Crusade began, Draigo returned alone to Acralem to end the quarrel with M'kar. The Chapter's seers had meanwhile seen the Citadel itself under threat.[1]

    The Era Indomitus

    [edit]
    Further information: Era Indomitus

    The fall of Cadia and the opening of the Great Rift gave the Grey Knights more work than at any time in their history. In the early campaigns of the Era Indomitus Grand Master Aldrik Voldus, the Warden of the Librarius, joined Roboute Guilliman on his journey from Macragge to Terra, and was among the few who reached the capital. He then served in the Indomitus Crusade, leading his Grey Knights beside the Custodes of Captain-General Trajann Valoris at the Battle of Gathalamor.[3]

    Imperial sources differ on the leadership of the Chapter at this time. The Chapter's own annals name Draigo as Supreme Grand Master, and the Inquisition has treated the title as unchanged, though he has not been seen in the sight of other forces.[3]

    Organisation

    [edit]

    Brotherhoods

    [edit]

    The Grey Knights do not follow the Codex Astartes. They are organised in brotherhoods, each of about a hundred warriors, comparable to the companies of another Chapter. There are eight, one for each of the first Grand Masters, and each is led by a Brother-Captain, under the authority of a Grand Master. Beyond the brotherhoods there is the Order of Purifiers, never more than fifty, who are the Chapter's spiritual heart and are held to be the most incorruptible of all, and the company of Paladins, about a hundred, who act as bodyguards to the Grand Masters.[1]

    Squads and specialists

    [edit]

    A brotherhood is divided into squads of ten, led by a Justicar. The main types are the Terminator squads, who form the bulk of the Chapter and are armed with Nemesis force weapons and storm bolters, the Strike squads in lighter armour, the Interceptor squads, who carry personal teleporters and can shift across a battlefield in an instant, and the Purgation squads, who carry heavy weapons. The Chapter also maintains Librarians, Techmarines trained with the Mechanicus and Apothecaries who tend those infected by the Warp. Every Grey Knight learns every role, and moves between squads over his career.[1]

    A Grey Knight does not specialise as other Space Marines do. He must learn his psychic discipline again for each new type of squad, and the Justicar of a squad serves as the focus through which his brothers' powers are channelled.[1]

    Recruitment and the Citadel

    [edit]

    The Grey Knights recruit from across the Imperium. Candidates, selected by Inquisitors and the Chapter's own scouts, are tested for psychic potential and for the absence of any hint of corruption, and are taken to Titan for training. The tests are such that many die. The survivors are given the Emperor's gene-seed and the implantation of the Chapter's organs. They are sequestered in the 8th Brotherhood until they have proved themselves.[1]

    The Citadel of Titan is the Chapter's fortress-monastery, built on the moon that Malcador sent into the Warp. It holds the Chambers of Purity, the Sanctum Sanctorum in which the Dreadnoughts rest, and the Chapter's catacombs, in which the dead of ten thousand years are buried. The Chapter's vessels are strike cruisers, fast ships that carry a brotherhood into the heart of an incursion and withdraw before it can be understood.[1]

    Doctrine and wargear

    [edit]

    The Grey Knights are trained to fight daemons, which cannot be killed by an ordinary weapon. Their principal armament is the Nemesis force weapon, a blade, halberd or hammer forged from psychically conductive metal and engraved with wards. It is a conduit for the wielder's mind, and a daemon struck by it is hurt in the Warp as well as in the world. They also carry storm bolters and psycannons, whose rounds are blessed and inscribed with wards. The Chapter's armour is the Aegis, which protects its wearer from the psychic attacks of the Warp, and many Grey Knights wear it in Terminator form.[1]

    The Grey Knights have a large body of rites to use against daemons. Their librarians know the true names of daemons, which give a hold on them, and they speak Words of Banishment that may drive a daemon from its host. The Chapter's Tesseract Labyrinths trap daemons that cannot be banished. A Grey Knight's spirit is held to be a weapon in itself, and a daemon, it is said, recoils from the sight of one.[1]

    Relations with other institutions

    [edit]
    Inquisition
    The Grey Knights serve the Ordo Malleus and are bound to it by the pact of Janus. They obey a request of an Inquisitor of the Ordo, but their Grand Masters decide how it shall be carried out.
    Space Wolves
    The only Chapter whose whole complement was told of the Grey Knights, after the first War for Armageddon.
    Adepta Sororitas
    The Sisters of Battle and the Grey Knights have fought together on many occasions. The Grey Knights are called to the aid of the Orders at the greatest daemonic incursions, as at Van Horne in 876.M41.
    Other Chapters
    A Chapter that has fallen to Chaos is the Grey Knights' business, as the Astral Blades learned. The other Chapters do not ask where the Grey Knights came from.
