    The Imperial tithe, also called the Great Tithe, Terra's Due or simply the tithe, is the levy that every world of the Imperium of Mankind owes to the Imperium in return for its protection. It is assessed by the Administratum according to each world's tithe grade and may be paid in any resource the world produces: food, ore, manufactured goods, warships, soldiers and, by Imperial law, every psyker born on the world. It is the economic foundation of the Imperium, sustaining the Astra Militarum, the Imperial Navy, the Adeptus Terra and the Astronomican.[1][2]

    The responsibility for paying the tithe rests on the world's Planetary Governor, also called the Imperial Commander. As long as the tithe is met and the world remains loyal, a governor may rule as he or she pleases. Failure is punished severely: the usual consequence of a shortfall, even one caused by an accounting error, is the removal or execution of the governor. A world that does not pay may be cut loose from Imperial protection, and a world that rebels may be destroyed.[1][3]

    On many worlds the day of collection has become a festival, the Emperor's Gift, when the tithe is lifted into orbit and the world's newly raised regiments depart for the stars.[4]

    History

    [edit]

    The tithe originated in the Great Crusade (c. 798.M30–005.M31), when worlds brought into compliance were required to contribute resources and troops to the expansion of the Imperium. The Imperial Army of the crusade was raised largely through such levies, and the Administratum grew up as the office that recorded and assessed them. After the Horus Heresy (005–014.M31), the reforms of Roboute Guilliman in the Codex era split the Imperial Army into the Imperial Guard and the Navy, and the military tithe took its present form: a levy of regiments raised by each world and handed to the Departmento Munitorum.[1][5]

    The psychic tithe also dates from the crusade era, when the Black Ships first gathered psykers for the Scholastica Psykana. After the Emperor's interment on the Golden Throne in 014.M31 it took on a further purpose, since many of the psykers collected are consumed in sustaining the Emperor and the Astronomican.[6]

    The tithe grades in use in the 41st millennium are recorded in the Administratum's Tabularium of tithes, whose present form dates from the Time of Rebirth after the Age of Apostasy (early M36 to c. 310.M36).[2][a] After the opening of the Great Rift in 999.M41, the tithe could not be collected from much of the Imperium Nihilus, and Roboute Guilliman reassessed the tithes of the Imperium Sanctus to supply the Indomitus Crusade.[7]

    Tithe grades

    [edit]

    Every world is assigned a grade, which fixes the size of its tithe. The make-up of the payment, what proportion is owed in grain, metal, men or ships, is set separately by the Administratum office responsible for the world's sector and sub-sector. The grades, from the heaviest to the lightest, are:[2]

    Administratum tithe grades
    Grade classGrades (heaviest first)Typical worlds
    ExactisExactis Extremis, Exactis Particular, Exactis Median, Exactis Prima, Exactis Secundus, Exactis TertiusPopulous hive worlds, rich agri worlds, military recruiting worlds
    DecumaDecuma Extremis, Decuma Particular, Decuma Prima, Decuma Secundus, Decuma TertiusCivilised worlds, mining and industrial worlds
    SolutioSolutio Extremis, Solutio Particular, Solutio Prima, Solutio Secundus, Solutio TertiusPoor, young or remote worlds; feudal and feral worlds
    Aptus Non(also Adeptus Non)No tithe: forge worlds, Space Marine Chapter homeworlds, dead or exterminated worlds

    Forge worlds are graded Aptus Non because their production is already owed to the Imperium under the Treaty of Olympus, and Chapter homeworlds because their resources maintain the Adeptus Astartes who protect the Imperium.[2]

    Components

    [edit]

    Material tithe

    [edit]

    Most of the tithe is paid in goods. An agri world's tithe is usually stated as a proportion of its harvest; a hive world's as a quota of manufactures; a mining world's as tonnage of ore. The goods are loaded onto Administratum tithe fleets and carried to wherever the Munitorum and the Adeptus Terra need them. Imperial records note that tithe ships may take years to complete a circuit, and that errors in the records are common.[2]

    Military tithe

    [edit]

    Every world able to do so must raise regiments for the Astra Militarum. By the long-standing rule recorded by the Munitorum, a world gives up one tenth of its military forces at each founding. Governors are liable to death for supplying too few soldiers, or soldiers who are poorly equipped, badly trained or of doubtful genetic purity, and for that reason most send their best troops. Worlds choose their soldiers by many methods: caste, lottery, volunteers, conscription or custom. Vostroya gives up every firstborn son, and on some worlds competition to be chosen is fierce enough to cause local wars. In times of crisis the Munitorum may demand far more than the normal levy, emptying cities or continents.[3][8]

    Psychic tithe

    [edit]

    Imperial law forbids any world to harbour unsanctioned psykers. Each governor must find them, by whatever means, and hand them over when the Black Ships arrive, escorted by the Sisters of Silence. Those collected are taken to Terra, where they are tested: some are trained as astropaths or sanctioned psykers, a few join the Inquisition or the Grey Knights, and many are sacrificed to sustain the Emperor and the Astronomican.[6]

    Enforcement

    [edit]

    The Administratum audits tithe returns, and discrepancies may take decades to surface. When they do, the Imperium acts swiftly. A governor whose world falls short may be replaced by the Adeptus Arbites, by an Inquisitor or by an invading force. The Munitorum's cheapest sanction is simply to withdraw protection from a defaulting world. Rebellion against the tithe has been the cause of many of the Imperium's internal wars, including the secession of Vraks that led to the Siege of Vraks (813–830.M41) and the troubles of the Badab War (901–913.M41), which began when the Astral Claws withheld their gene-tithe and trade levies.[1][9]
