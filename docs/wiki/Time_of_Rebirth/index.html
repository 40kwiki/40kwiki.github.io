    The Time of Rebirth, also called the Age of Rebirth, was the first age of the Imperium of Mankind after the Horus Heresy. It began with the end of the Siege of Terra in 014.M31 and lasted until about the middle of the 32nd millennium, when the War of the Beast and the Beheading of the High Lords closed it. In these five centuries the Imperium recovered from the civil war, drove the Traitor Legions from its space and rebuilt its government around the absence of the Emperor, who sat silent on the Golden Throne.[1]

    Almost every institution of the later Imperium took its form in this age. Roboute Guilliman's Codex Astartes broke the Space Marine Legions into Chapters, the Imperial Army was divided into the Astra Militarum and the Imperial Navy, and the High Lords of Terra became the Imperium's ruling council. The age also saw the rise of the worship of the Emperor as a god, which the Emperor had forbidden in life. By the 32nd millennium the cults of the God-Emperor had been united into the Adeptus Ministorum, the state church of the Imperium.[2]

    Imperial historians name the age for its rebuilding, but it was not peaceful. The Great Scouring, the Iron Cage, the fall of Guilliman at Thessala, the First Black Crusade and the return of the Orks in strength all fell within it, and the primarchs who had survived the Heresy died, disappeared or withdrew one by one.[1]

    Name and extent

    [edit]

    The age is called the Time of Rebirth in the chronological tables of the Administratum and the Age of Rebirth in older Ministorum texts. Its beginning is fixed by the death of Horus and the Emperor's entombment in the Golden Throne. Its end is less certain. Most chronologies close it with the War of the Beast (544–546.M32) and the murder of the High Lords that followed, after which the rebuilt Imperium entered The Forging. Some Ecclesiarchy writers end it earlier, with the elevation of the Ministorum to the rank of state church.[1]

    Background

    [edit]

    The Imperium that survived the Siege of Terra was crippled. Nine of the eighteen Legions had turned traitor, and the loyal Legions had lost a large part of their strength at Isstvan V, Calth and Terra. Much of the Imperial Army and the Mechanicum had gone over to Horus. Countless worlds had been destroyed or had declared for the Warmaster, and many others had been cut off by the Ruinstorm for years. The Emperor, the source of all authority in the Imperium, could no longer speak or rule.[3]

    The government passed to the surviving members of the Council of Terra and to Roboute Guilliman, who as the senior surviving loyal primarch took the office of Lord Commander and acted as Imperial Regent. Malcador the Sigillite had died on the Throne during the Siege, and with him the old council. The new High Lords of Terra governed in the Emperor's name, but their powers were not yet fixed, and the first centuries of the age were spent deciding what they would be.[4]

    Reconstruction

    [edit]

    The Great Scouring

    [edit]
    Further information: Great Scouring

    The first task of the age was to drive out the traitors. The Great Scouring, conducted from 014 to about 021.M31, was a counter-offensive across the whole Imperium. The traitor forces that had survived Terra fled before the loyalist Legions, the Imperial Army and the Custodes. Most took refuge in the Eye of Terror and in lesser rifts such as the Maelstrom. Worlds that had sided with Horus were reconquered or, if they were judged beyond saving, destroyed. Isstvan V and Davin were scoured of life. Hunts for traitor bands continued for more than a century after the Scouring formally ended.[5]

    In the Eye, the traitors turned on one another. The Legion Wars destroyed the Sons of Horus as a Legion at Canticle City and saw Abaddon gather the remnants into the Black Legion, which would trouble the Imperium for the next ten thousand years.[1]

    The Codex and the Second Founding

    [edit]

    Guilliman's reforms were meant to make another Heresy impossible. His Codex Astartes, completed in about 021.M31, divided each loyal Legion into Chapters of about a thousand warriors, so that no commander could again hold the power that Horus had. The Second Founding, in about 021.M31, put this into effect. The Imperial Army was split into the Astra Militarum, which fought on the ground, and the Imperial Navy, which held the ships, so that no officer controlled both the soldiers and the means to move them. Sector and segmentum commands were reorganised along the same principle.[6]

    The reforms were resisted. Rogal Dorn refused at first to divide the Imperial Fists, and the quarrel between the two primarchs came close to fighting. It was settled only after the Imperial Fists were trapped in the Iron Cage, a battle against the Iron Warriors, and saved by the Ultramarines. Dorn accepted the Codex afterwards, although the Imperial Fists' Successors kept a number of their own customs.[6]

    The Inquisition

    [edit]
    Main article: Inquisition

    The Inquisition had its origin in the agents whom Malcador had recruited during the Heresy. In the Time of Rebirth it became a standing institution, charged with hunting the enemies of the Imperium wherever they were found, including within the Adepta themselves. Its records of the age are among the most heavily sealed in the Imperium.[1]

    The loss of the primarchs

    [edit]

    The age is also the age in which the primarchs passed out of the Imperium. In 121.M31 Guilliman was mortally wounded by the daemon primarch Fulgrim at Thessala. He was placed in a stasis field in the Temple of Correction on Macragge, which became one of the great pilgrim shrines of the Imperium. Lion El'Jonson disappeared after the destruction of Caliban, Leman Russ departed into the Eye of Terror, and Jaghatai Khan and Corvus Corax vanished in pursuit of their enemies. Vulkan, who could not die, withdrew from the Imperium's service.[4]

    Rogal Dorn was the last to fall. In 781.M31 Abaddon launched the First Black Crusade, which began with the First Battle of Cadia. Dorn is recorded as having been killed in the fighting; his hand and a few bones were recovered by the Imperial Fists and are kept as relics. The Black Crusade ravaged the Segmentum Obscurus for decades, and it confirmed Abaddon, already master of the daemon sword Drach'nyen, as the Warmaster of the forces of Chaos and Horus's successor. Earlier Imperial records held that he won the sword only at the end of this crusade, on the world of Uralan.[4][a]

    The departure of the primarchs left the Imperium without its greatest leaders. In the Time of Rebirth the High Lords came to depend on the Third Founding, which Dorn himself had urged, and on later Foundings to keep the Adeptus Astartes at strength.[1]

    Rise of the Ministorum

    [edit]

    The Emperor had preached the Imperial Truth, which denied all gods, but a cult that worshipped him as divine, the Lectitio Divinitatus, had spread through the Imperial Army and the expedition fleets during the Great Crusade. After his entombment the worship of the Emperor grew quickly. On thousands of worlds cults arose that taught that he had sacrificed himself for humanity and watched over it from the Throne.[2]

    Over the centuries most of these cults were gathered into one church by the largest of them, the Temple of the Saviour Emperor. In the 32nd millennium the High Lords recognised it as the state religion of the Imperium and gave it the title of Adeptus Ministorum. A few centuries later the Ecclesiarch Veneris II was given a seat among the High Lords, and three hundred years after that the seat was made permanent. The Ministorum's preachers carried the Imperial Creed to every world, and the faith became one of the bonds that held the Imperium together. It also became a political power, which in later ages would rival the Administratum itself.[2]

    Wars of the age

    [edit]

    Apart from the Scouring and the First Black Crusade, the records of the age list many lesser wars. Alien powers that had been defeated during the Great Crusade returned to attack the weakened Imperium, and traitor bands raided from the Eye. The greatest war of the age, the War of the Beast of 544–546.M32, came at its end. A new kind of Ork empire, led by warlords the Imperium called the Beasts, overran whole segmenta and attacked Terra itself. The Imperium survived only at great cost. In the aftermath the Inquisition was divided into the Ordo Xenos and the Ordo Malleus, the Deathwatch was raised, the office of Lord Commander was abolished, and the Captain-General of the Adeptus Custodes was given a seat among the High Lords.[7]

    The same year the Grand Master of Assassins had the High Lords murdered in the Beheading. The years of disorder that followed, until the Ultramarines Chapter Master Agnathio restored the council in 646.M32, are usually counted as the beginning of The Forging.[1]

    Legacy

    [edit]

    The Time of Rebirth gave the Imperium the shape it has kept for ten thousand years: Chapters rather than Legions, a divided military, a council of High Lords ruling in the name of a silent Emperor, and a state church that worships him. Ministorum writers have described the age as the Imperium's second founding in faith, in which humanity learned to serve the Emperor as a god. Administratum historians, more soberly, note that most of the problems of later ages, from the rivalry of the Adepta to the slow loss of the primarchs' knowledge, began in the same years.[1]

    The age ended with the return of one of its absent heroes in a later millennium. Guilliman's resurrection at the end of the 41st millennium, and his account of the years after the Heresy, have led the Logos Historica Verita to reopen many of the records of the period. Some of the age's received history has already been revised as a result.[8]
