    Khorne, called the Blood God, the Skull Lord or the Lord of Battle, is the Ruinous Power of rage, slaughter and the pride of the warrior. Imperial scholars place him first among the four named Powers in age and in the number of his mortal servants. He feeds on the passions of killing and being killed, and on the energies that rise from war: fury, hatred, the exultation of victory and the fear of defeat. He does not care for the cause of a battle or for the identity of the dead. To Khorne, every spilled drop of blood is an offering, whether it is that of a foe or of the one who shed it.[1]

    The Blood God is the most directly dangerous of the Powers. Where Tzeentch plots and Slaanesh seduces, Khorne simply sends his servants to kill, and his cults rise in every war and every place where men have grown used to violence. His mortal servants include the berserkers of the World Eaters, bloodied cultists on feral worlds, gladiators of the underhives and, in the worst cases, soldiers of the Imperium's own armies, who have turned on their officers in a frenzy and been given a wild, undying strength. His daemons, the Bloodletters and the Bloodthirsters, are among the most feared on any battlefield.[2]

    Khorne's loathing of sorcery and of those who use it is the oldest recorded enmity between the Powers. He regards the use of magic as cowardly, the mark of a fighter too weak to kill with his own hand, and his followers are forbidden to cast spells or to tolerate those who do. The same hostility extends to psykers in general, and Inquisitors note that the presence of Khorne's cults often coincides with the persecution of local psychic communities.[1]

    Nature and domain

    [edit]

    The Blood God

    [edit]

    Imperial scholars use the term aspect for the mortal passion from which a Power draws its strength, and the aspect of Khorne is wrath. The Ordo Malleus is careful to point out that this is not the same as violence in the abstract. Khorne does not feed on cruelty, which belongs to Slaanesh, nor on suffering, which belongs to Nurgle, nor on calculated slaughter such as that of a siege, which is the work of generals. He feeds on the heat of battle: the rage of men who are fighting, the strength of those who are winning and the despair of those who are not. A single berserker's duel is food for him, as is a war of ten thousand worlds.[1]

    Khorne is described by his cultists as a god who values honour of a kind. A warrior who fights bravely is honoured by the Blood God, and one who strikes down a worthy foe has a place in his hall. The Ordo Malleus regards this claim as bait. Khorne's honour is not mercy, and his rule is not justice. It is the sole law that blood is spilled, and the cultist who fails to fight is punished with the same ferocity as the one who flees.[3]

    The Brass Citadel

    [edit]

    The domain of Khorne in the Realm of Chaos is the Blood God's Domain, a landscape of battlefields, brass fortresses and rivers of blood. At its centre is the Brass Citadel, the Blood God's fortress, from which he surveys the endless war of his daemons. The Citadel is built, according to the accounts of those who have glimpsed it, from the armour and bones of the fallen, and rests on a throne of skulls whose number is without end. The throne is said to be fed by every death offered to the Blood God, and each skull carries the name of the one who died.[1]

    Daemons

    [edit]

    Bloodletters

    [edit]
    Main article: Daemon

    The common troops of Khorne's host are the Bloodletters, red-skinned daemons armed with hellblades and driven by a rage so great that they cannot retreat. They are born in numbers beyond counting, and are fielded in great hordes at the front of any daemonic assault. Their weapons are strong enough to cut through armour, and they are unaffected by fear. The Imperial Guard's manual describes them in four words: advance, kill, die, return.[2]

    Bloodthirsters

    [edit]
    Main article: Daemon

    The greatest of Khorne's daemons are the Bloodthirsters, winged giants with axes and whips of fire. A Bloodthirster is a general and a warrior in one: it leads a host of Bloodletters and Flesh Hounds and fights in the front rank. The Imperium records several named Bloodthirsters, of whom the best known is Skarbrand the Exiled, a daemon who once enjoyed the greatest favour of Khorne and was cast down for a crime recorded in the sealed archives. Other Bloodthirsters appear in the vanguard of every great Khornate invasion.[1]

    Other servants

    [edit]

    Khorne's host includes a variety of lesser beasts. The Flesh Hounds hunt psykers and sorcerers across the Warp, tracking them by scent. The Juggernauts are armoured daemonic beasts ridden by champions into battle. The Skull Cannons are war-engines of brass that fire the skulls of the Blood God's enemies. The most terrible of the Blood God's creations is the Lord of Skulls, a war-engine inhabited by a daemon, which stalks the battlefield and kills with an unquenchable fury.[4]

    Worshippers

    [edit]

    Cults and warbands

    [edit]

    Khorne is the most commonly worshipped of the Powers. His cults arise wherever the habit of war is strong: on feral worlds with tribal blood-feuds, in the underhives where gang violence is normal, in the fighting pits of the Imperium's penal worlds and in the ranks of its armies where the line between discipline and bloodlust is thin. The cults have a simple structure. A leader, usually the strongest or most violent member, rises by killing his rivals, and the rest follow him because they fear to do otherwise. The cult grows by taking converts, and then by war. A Khornate cult is rarely subtle, and it is the Inquisition's experience that it is rarely hidden for long.[3]

    Cultists of Khorne offer him blood in four principal ways: in battle, in duels, in sacrifice and in the building of skull-trophies. Certain marks identify them: a red or brass colour scheme, the display of skulls on banners and armour, and a rune cut into the flesh. In severe cases the cultist receives the "Gift of Khorne", a surge of strength that allows him to fight on after mortal wounds and that eventually leaves him unfit for any other purpose.[1]

    The World Eaters

    [edit]
    Main article: World Eaters

    The greatest Khornate force is the World Eaters, the XII Legion of the Great Crusade, whose primarch Angron was a gladiator-slave on the world of Nuceria. Angron's brain had been implanted with the Butcher's Nails, a device that sent him into a permanent rage and that was originally designed to keep him fighting. After the Emperor found him and brought him to the Legion, the Nails remained. The Legion adopted the savage, brutal arts of its primarch and was already notorious for its savagery before the Horus Heresy. Imperial records describe the Legion's fall into the worship of Khorne as a gradual process, driven by the Nails, by the Legion's own culture and by the work of the Word Bearers, who brought the Legion to Chaos during the Shadow Crusade.[5]

    Angron himself became a Daemon Primarch, a prince of the Blood God, in the course of that crusade. His Legion followed him, and in the Heresy it fought at Isstvan III and in the assault on the walls of the Imperial Palace at the Siege of Terra. In the ten thousand years since, the World Eaters have fractured into warbands led by champions such as Khârn the Betrayer, who is remembered for the madness of his rage and the slaughter he caused among his own brothers. Each World Eaters band follows its own war, and the Legion has taken part in many of the assaults on the Imperium, including the First War of Armageddon in 444.M41, in which Angron led a great Khornate host through the Warp.[5]

    Mortal champions

    [edit]

    A man who serves Khorne with distinction may rise in the Blood God's favour, and his rise is marked by visible signs: heavier armour, stronger limbs, a face that takes on the look of a beast. The most successful become Champions of Khorne, and the few who exceed even that rank become Daemon Princes. Khorne's champions are commonly found at the head of cults and warbands, where they are rarely more than a few years from death at a rival's hand. The Blood God does not care, for there is always another to take their place.[1]

    History

    [edit]

    Origins

    [edit]

    The origin of Khorne is unknown. Mechanicus records suggest that he is the oldest of the four Powers, and that he took shape from the great wars of the species who preceded man in the galaxy. The Aeldari, who were the first to observe the Warp's inhabitants, remember Khorne as the first of the Powers to grow strong in their civilisation, an entity whose name they did not know but whose rage they felt during the great war of their ancestors. The Aeldari add that their own god of war, Khaine, was broken by that same Power's rise, though their legends of the matter are contradictory.[3]

    The first human records of Khorne come from the Age of Strife, when the worlds of mankind were cut off from one another and fell into endless war. Cults of the Blood God arose among the soldiers of warring lords, and several of the Age's worst atrocities are attributed to his followers. The Emperor's unification of Terra and the Great Crusade appear to have suppressed these cults, but not to have destroyed them. The Imperial Truth treated them as superstition, and their followers were punished, not studied.[4]

    The Horus Heresy

    [edit]
    Further information: Horus Heresy

    During the Horus Heresy, Khorne was the Power that gave the Traitors their raw strength. The corruption of the World Eaters is the clearest case, but the Blood God's influence is recorded in many other Legions and in the hosts of auxiliaries that followed them. At the Siege of Terra the World Eaters took the Palace's outer defences at the cost of thousands of their own, and the Blood God's daemons appeared at the walls in numbers that the defenders had no way to counter. The daemons were banished when the Warmaster fell, but the memory of the Siege lives in the cults.[6]

    The long war

    [edit]
    Further information: Long War

    After the Heresy, Khorne's followers retreated to the Eye of Terror with the other Traitors and joined the long war of the Black Crusades. The Blood God's daemons were summoned in numbers during most of those campaigns, and the Blood God's Champions led many of the greatest assaults. The Imperium's own archives also record Khornate uprisings on hundreds of worlds far from the Eye.[2]

    In the Era Indomitus the Great Rift has given the Blood God greater access to the material universe than at any time since the Heresy. Reports from the Imperium Nihilus describe the arrival of Khornate hosts on worlds that were already at war, and of the Blood God's daemons drawn by the great slaughters that the Rift has made possible. The Lord Commander's war-council has designated the Blood God's hosts as a principal enemy in the southern reaches of the Imperium Sanctus.[7]

    Relations with other Powers

    [edit]

    Khorne is opposed to all the Powers, but his enmity is greatest toward Tzeentch, whose schemes he despises and whose sorcerers he hunts. The enmity is mutual: the daemons of Tzeentch, which feed on change and plans, are the natural prey of Khorne's wrath. The rivalry of the two Powers is reflected in the relations of their servants. The World Eaters and the Thousand Sons have fought each other with a bitterness that the Imperium has been able to exploit more than once.[1]

    Khorne's relations with Nurgle and Slaanesh are less direct. He regards Nurgle's endurance as a kind of weakness and Slaanesh's excess as a distraction, but he has at times allowed his daemons to fight alongside theirs. The Imperium has not found a way to turn those opportunities to its own use.[2]

    Imperial response

    [edit]

    The Imperium treats Khornate cults as a military problem, because they rarely wait to be discovered. When a cult is found, the Inquisition or the Arbites is likely to be too late to prevent a first uprising. The standard response is a combined assault by the local garrison and a Space Marine Chapter, followed by the purging of the cult's strongholds and the execution of its members. Chapters that have fought the Blood God's hosts maintain specific doctrines against them. The Black Templars and the Blood Angels, whose own fury in battle has often been compared to his, keep particular rites against the Blood God's influence.[8]
