    Interex
    Human star realm of the late Age of Strife
    The Luna Wolves in the field, as recorded by the Remembrancers of the 63rd Expedition
    TypeFederal interstellar polity
    ExtentAbout thirty star systems, in the western reaches of the galaxy
    Notable worldsXenobia, with the city of Xenobia Principis; the prison world later called Murder
    PeoplesHumans; the simian Kinebrach as allies and junior partners
    FoeThe Megarachnids; the Imperium of Mankind, from 003.M31
    Contact63rd Expedition at Murder, c. 001.M31; parley on Xenobia, 003.M31
    FateDestroyed by the Luna Wolves in the First War of Xenobia, 003.M31

    The Interex were a human civilisation of about thirty star systems that survived the Age of Strife and were found by the Great Crusade in its closing years. They differed from almost every other human realm the crusade encountered in one respect that proved fatal: they had decided to live alongside other intelligences rather than fight them. Their closest ally was the simian species known as the Kinebrach, whom the Interex counted as junior partners and had brought inside their society, and their science, in some respects more advanced than that of the Imperium, was devoted to the study of the Warp and of the means of resisting it. They called the Warp's corruption by a name that the Imperium transcribed as Kaos.[1]

    Contact was made by the 63rd Expeditionary Fleet under Horus. It ended in a war that began as a misunderstanding, in which the Interex took the visitors for servants of the thing they most feared, and in which the Luna Wolves destroyed the entire realm in a few months. The war was not the work of the Imperium alone. A weapon was stolen from the Interex during the negotiations, and the thief, Erebus of the Word Bearers, had been told by the powers he served that it was needed to win the Warmaster to Chaos. The weapon, the Kinebrach Anathame, came back to the Luna Wolves a year later in the hands of a Nurgle-tainted governor on Davin and wounded Horus, and the sequence of events that followed led directly to the Horus Heresy.[1][2]

    History

    [edit]

    Origins

    [edit]

    The Interex were descended from colonists of the Dark Age of Technology and had passed through the Age of Strife with fewer losses than most human realms. The reasons given by their own historians were geographical and political. Their thirty systems lay in a region that the Warp storms of the Strife had screened from the worst of the disruption, and they had formed a loose federation early enough to defend its worlds against the predation of raiders. Their institutions were a council of delegates chosen by the member worlds, a common defence and a shared doctrine, rarely spoken in public, that the Warp was a hazard to be managed by study rather than denied.[3]

    The Imperium would never learn a great deal about their history. Few Interex records survived the war. The Remembrancers of the 63rd Expedition set down what the fleet had seen on Xenobia, and the Luna Wolves' captains testified to what they had fought, and the Administratum's later survey of the region found the remnant of a once prosperous civilisation under a layer of ash.

    The Kinebrach

    [edit]

    The Kinebrach were a simian species, tall and long-armed, that had been native to a world of the Interex's region. By the time the Imperium met them, they and the humans had lived together for many generations. The Interex described them as allies and, in some documents, as wards, which the Kinebrach did not seem to resent. They were trained in a form of psychic discipline that the Interex considered safe, and their warriors fought with blades whose design was ancient.

    The Interex considered the arrangement a model. It was held up in the council halls of Xenobia as an example of what Mankind might do if it did not meet each stranger with a gun. The Imperium, whose accepted policy was that nearly every alien species had proved hostile and that extermination was the usual remedy, saw it very differently. The Interex themselves, who had met the Imperium's fleet with open suspicion, regarded the attitude as an excellent reason for caution.[4]

    Murder

    [edit]

    The first sign of the Interex's presence was found on the world the Imperium called Murder. The Luna Wolves of the 63rd Expedition were sent there in about 001.M31, after the Council of Nikaea, to cleanse it of a vicious alien species, the Megarachnids, and found that the planet was in fact a prison. The Interex had defeated the Megarachnids in a war of their own, stripped them of the technology of space flight and left them on the world with a cordon of watching satellites. The Luna Wolves broke the cordon, which they had taken for an enemy defence, in the course of the campaign and then discovered the Interex's fleet watching from the edge of the system.[1]

    Contact with the Imperium

    [edit]

    Parley

    [edit]

    The Interex approached cautiously. They had met no human power before the Imperium for centuries, and they had reason to doubt that this one was what it claimed to be. Its warriors were genetically changed, its fleets carried priests of an unfamiliar faith (the Iterators and the Chaplains), and its commander was a being of impossible stature who spoke to them through a delegation. The Interex council suspected the Imperium of an allegiance to Kaos, and they said so. The Imperium's envoys, for their part, found the Interex's dependence on alien allies, their use of machine servants more capable than anything the Mechanicum would sanction, and their open study of the Warp, alarming.[3]

    Despite this, in 003.M31, a delegation led by Horus himself went down to Xenobia, the principal Interex world, to negotiate. The Interex received it in their capital, Xenobia Principis. Horus brought with him a party of the Luna Wolves' officers, several Remembrancers, and Erebus, First Chaplain of the Word Bearers, who had been attached to the expedition for several months.[3]

    The Hall of Devices

    [edit]

    Among the sights of the city was the Hall of Devices, a museum of weapons and artefacts from the Interex's allies and enemies. The visitors were shown it as a courtesy. In its vaults was the Anathame, a ceremonial blade of the Kinebrach, whose makers had treated it with a respect that the Interex's own curators did not fully understand. It was known to be tied to Chaos.

    During the talks the Anathame was stolen. The thief was Erebus, who had turned to the service of the Ruinous Powers long before and who had been told by his masters that Horus could not be taken without the blade. He rigged the building to explode as he left. The explosion, and the loss of the Anathame, were taken by the Interex as proof that the Imperium's envoys were what they feared, and fighting broke out in the streets of Xenobia Principis.[1][2]

    First War of Xenobia

    [edit]

    The fighting

    [edit]

    The Luna Wolves in the city were outmatched. The Interex had every reason to be formidable. Their warriors were hybrids of human and machine, mounted on a base that gave them the appearance of centaurs (the Luna Wolves' first reports took them for aliens). Their light bows, which the Luna Wolves had written off as ornaments of parade, fired bolts of coherent light that pierced Space Marine armour. Their drones moved without human control, and their Kinebrach-trained psykers brought down warriors with a thought. Legionaries died within hours of contact, and the Interex fought with a skill and a spirit that the Luna Wolves had met in few enemies.[1]

    Horus was evacuated from the world, and the fleet withdrew to regroup. It did not withdraw for long. The Warmaster, as he was by then, was not minded to lose a war to a human realm and was persuaded by the report of the Hall of Devices that its people had meant mischief. The expedition returned in force.

    The destruction

    [edit]

    The destruction of the Interex took only a few months. The Luna Wolves took each of the realm's major worlds in turn, using the methods that had succeeded at Ullanor, striking at the centres of command before the Interex could organise a defence in depth. The Interex's technology could not compensate for the difference in numbers, and its Kinebrach allies, their discipline broken, fell in the ruins of their own cities. By the end of 003.M31 the realm had ceased to exist. Its worlds were put under Imperial garrison, its libraries were seized, and the Mechanicum took what it wanted from the wreckage.[1][4]

    The Imperial Truth had a ready explanation for the outcome. The Interex had allied themselves with xenos, had used artificial minds and had dabbled with the Warp. By each of these counts the Imperium considered them to have forfeited their right to survive.

    Aftermath

    [edit]

    The Anathame

    [edit]
    Main article: Kinebrach Anathame

    The blade stolen from Xenobia did not stay with Erebus. It passed to a world that the Luna Wolves were then bringing into compliance, Davin, where the governor, Eugen Temba, an agent of the Chaos god Nurgle, used it against Horus in 004.M31. The wound it made could not be closed by the Legion's apothecaries, and the Mournival carried their dying primarch to the Serpent Lodge on Davin's moon, where the priests of a cult of Chaos healed him by sorcery. During the healing, Erebus showed Horus a vision of an Imperium in which the Emperor was worshipped as a god, and Horus came back from it changed.[1][2]

    A little more than a year after the war on Xenobia, then, the Imperium had destroyed a human civilisation devoted to resisting Chaos and had armed Chaos with the weapon that began the corruption of its own Warmaster. The irony was lost on no one who learned of it, and the Inquisition has kept the matter closed.

    The Interex in the record

    [edit]

    The Imperium preserved the Interex mainly as a warning. They appear in the lectures of the Iterators as an example of a human realm led astray by sympathy for aliens, and in the books of the Mechanicum as a civilisation that built thinking machines. The Ecclesiarchy, when it came to write its own histories, passed over them.

    A few scholars of the Ordo Xenos have taken a different view. They note that the Interex held off the Megarachnids and had preserved a body of knowledge about the Warp that the Imperium later spent millennia recovering by worse means.

    Technology

    [edit]

    The Imperium recorded the Interex's weaponry and machines with a mixture of admiration and distaste. The most striking were the following.

    • Mobile platforms. Interex warriors entered a four-legged mobile base and fused with it to form a figure that resembled a centaur, which gave them greater mobility than a Space Marine.
    • Light bows. Weapons that resembled ceremonial bows and crossbows, which fired bolts of laser light through ceramite.
    • Drones. Automated machines that moved through Interex society and performed most of its menial work. The Mechanicum considered them to be thinking machines in the sense of the ban on the Silica Animus and noted that the Interex treated them as free.
    • Warp defences. Shielding and warding technology, which the Interex developed because their civilisation expected to be attacked by the powers of the Warp.

    Whether the Mechanicum learned anything from the wreck is disputed. The Martian records state that nothing of value was recovered. Other records, which the Mechanicum does not confirm, suggest that its Explorators worked over the Interex's worlds for some years.[5]
