    The Imperial Fists are the VII Legion of the Space Marine Legions and, since the Second Founding, a Space Marine Chapter of the Adeptus Astartes. They were led by the primarch Rogal Dorn and served the Emperor of Mankind as his praetorians during the Great Crusade. They built the defences of the Imperial Palace and held it at the Siege of Terra, and they are unrivalled masters of fortification and siegecraft. In the Era Indomitus they are known as the Sentinels of Terra.[1][2]

    The Imperial Fists have no fixed homeworld. Their fortress-monastery is the Phalanx, an ancient starship of the Dark Age of Technology, the largest ever built by human hands, which Dorn brought to the Emperor and which has served the Legion and the Chapter ever since. The Chapter recruits from a number of worlds, among them Terra, through fortress-chapels maintained across the Imperium. Of all the Chapters it is among the most devoted to the Codex Astartes, and among the most stubborn in defence, to the point that its obstinacy has often cost it far more than other Chapters would pay.[1][3]

    In the Era Indomitus the Imperial Fists have been transformed by the changes that have come to the Imperium. The Chapter Master Vorn Hagan fell defending Terra from a Khornate daemon assault early in the era, and Gregor Dessian was chosen to succeed him. The Phalanx was gravely damaged in the fall of Cadia. The Chapter has accepted Primaris reinforcements from the Indomitus Crusade, over some of its captains' objections, and has raised its auxiliary force with thousands of Cadian refugees.[4]

    Legion era

    [edit]

    The Praetorians

    [edit]

    The VII Legion was created from Terran warriors during the Unification Wars and was among the earliest of the Emperor's Legions. It was originally based on Terra, and it served as the Emperor's personal praetorians, accompanying him in his campaigns and often striking the decisive blow. The Emperor's primarch, Dorn, was found in 835.M30 at the Ice Hives of Inwit, where he presented himself with one of the greatest ships of the Dark Age of Technology as an offering of fealty. The Emperor welcomed him and gave him command of the Legion, and returned the ship, the Phalanx, from which Dorn and sometimes the Emperor himself commanded many of the Great Crusade's campaigns.[2][3]

    Dorn was a stern and reserved primarch, cautious and calculating, relentless in pursuit of an end and terrible in his anger. His Legion took on his character. The Imperial Fists mastered every aspect of war, but they excelled at siegecraft, whether in the attack or in the defence, and this brought them into a bitter rivalry with the Iron Warriors of the primarch Perturabo, which was fanned by Fulgrim of the Emperor's Children. Fulgrim is said to have asked Dorn whether a fortress of his own design could withstand an assault by the Iron Warriors; Dorn said that it could, and Perturabo, hearing of it, took it as a boast. The enmity of the two Legions would only grow.[2][3]

    Horus Heresy

    [edit]
    Further information: Horus Heresy

    Before the Heresy Dorn had been entrusted with the Emperor's own security, and as the rebellion unfolded he made Terra a fortress with two walls: the wall within, the gate to the Emperor's secret project beneath the Palace, guarded by the Emperor and the Custodes, and the wall without, the defences that extended from the Palace's walls to the edge of the Sol System. Dorn, as Praetorian of Terra, commanded the latter, and divided it into spheres under senior Imperial Fists called Lord Castellans, among them the First Captain Sigismund, Halbrecht and Efried. Sigismund also led the mission to Mars in 005–006.M31,[a] which secured the northern forges for the Imperium, and Dorn's captains took part in the blockade of Cthonia, kept from 006 to 014.M31, the homeworld of the Sons of Horus.[5][3]

    The Imperial Fists held the outer defences against the Alpha Legion, which crept into the Sol System in stasis; at the Battle of Pluto in 010.M31, in the Imperial Fists' own account, Dorn killed his brother Alpharius, though the Ultramarines make the same claim of Guilliman, and Imperial accounts differ. At the Siege of Terra in 014.M31 the Imperial Fists, with the White Scars and the Blood Angels, held the Palace's walls until the end of the war, and they accompanied the Emperor in his last battle aboard the Vengeful Spirit. It was Dorn who found the bodies of the Emperor, Horus and Sanguinius, and who bore the broken Emperor to the Golden Throne. The Legion lost the greater part of its strength. The Chapter remembers its dead in the Column of Glory, a crystal pillar embedded with the broken armour of the fallen near the throne room, and in the Pillar of Bone.[5][6][3]

    Great Scouring

    [edit]
    Further information: Great Scouring

    Dorn, now dressed in black for mourning, led the Imperial Fists in the Great Scouring, razing the fortresses of the Traitors with a pitiless thoroughness. He saw the Emperor's death as his own failure. It was in this period, in c. 021.M31,[a] that the Imperial Fists and the Iron Warriors fought the battle called the Iron Cage on Sebastus IV, in which both Legions suffered horrendous losses and both later claimed to have won. Dorn did not at first accept Guilliman's Codex Astartes, and the Imperial Fists' captain Oriax Dantalion, who argued in favour of it, was treated by the primarch with hostility; but Dorn eventually saw that his vengeance had distorted his judgment, and accepted the Codex. Under his direction the Legion was reorganised in the Second Founding of 021.M31, and it was thoroughly retrained to follow the Codex.[7][3]

    Chapter era

    [edit]

    Rebuilding and the loss of Dorn

    [edit]

    The Imperial Fists' Successors include the Black Templars, who were founded by the First Captain Sigismund as a perpetual crusading fleet, and the Crimson Fists, the Executioners and many others. In the centuries that followed Dorn's death the Chapter went through a long period of reflection. For some fifteen centuries its warriors, in memory of the Siege, held the Imperial Palace's walls in ritual vigil, and renamed their companies after the gates they had held. They took wall-names, and called each other Wall Brothers. The practice ended after 544.M32, when the Chapter was nearly wiped out on Ardamantua by a race of metallic xenos, the Chrome, which were fleeing the Beast's Waaagh!; only one Marine survived. The Chapter was then rebuilt under Maximus Thane, the former Chapter Master of the Fists Exemplar, who was named Lord Commander of the Imperium and ordered the Fourth Founding.[3][8]

    Dorn himself, by the Chapter's tradition, was lost in c. 781.M31, in the fighting against a Chaos fleet that emerged from the Eye of Terror under a returned Warmaster in the First Black Crusade. He is believed to have died on the bridge of a Despoiler-class battleship, the Sword of Sacrilege, after leading a desperate attack. All that the Imperial Fists recovered was a skeletal fist, and an escape pod with his armour and weapons. The hand, which the Chapter believes to be the primarch's, has been scrimshawed with the heraldry of all the Chapter's former Chapter Masters, and the present Chapter Master has the right to engrave his own name on it. Whether the hand is Dorn's, and whether he died at all, is unknown.[3]

    Era Indomitus

    [edit]
    Further information: Era Indomitus

    In 999.M41, as the thirteenth Black Crusade of Abaddon the Despoiler opened, the Imperial Fists' Third Company under Captain Tor Garadon was defending the Phalanx from a daemonic boarding action led by the Iron Warriors Warsmith Shon'tu and the Daemon Prince Be'lakor. The enemy sought to use the starship to bombard the Imperial Palace. Garadon sent the Phalanx into the Warp to protect Terra, and the Legion of the Damned arrived to turn the battle. Roughly a tenth of the vessel was left unusable, and the Phalanx went on to destroy a Blackstone fortress at Cadia. Cadia fell soon afterwards, and the Imperial Fists helped to evacuate its survivors.[3][4]

    The opening of the Great Rift at the turn of the 42nd millennium brought the Chapter's greatest loss in centuries. A Khornate daemon host fell on Terra, and Chapter Master Vorn Hagan, who had taken command in 970.M41 after Vladimir Pugh, was killed defending it. The Imperial Fists fought at the Battle of Lion's Gate beside the resurrected Roboute Guilliman, and the Chapter elected Gregor Dessian, formerly captain of the Seventh Company, as the new Chapter Master. Dessian, with his company, had embarked on a penitent crusade to the leading edge of the Rift, and returned to find Hagan dead. The First Captain Darnath Lysander, who had earlier refused the Chapter Mastership, remains one of the Chapter's most prominent warriors.[3][4]

    Guilliman's Primaris warriors arrived at a moment of crisis. Some of the Chapter's captains, among them Captain Dysorian of the Fourth Company, rejected them as unproven and as a meddling with the Emperor's work, but the Chapter has since formed Primaris squads, and the Firstborn who have become Primaris serve beside them. The Imperial Fists have continued their wars against old enemies: in the Ironhold campaign they attacked a newly fortified world of the Iron Warriors in vengeance for the damage to the Phalanx, with the aid of Cadian armies and Knight households that had escaped Cadia, and they broke all eight layers of its defences. The Phalanx hangs for the most part above Terra, and goes out only on vital missions before returning to the Throneworld.[3][4]

    Organisation

    [edit]

    The Imperial Fists are a model Codex Chapter, as strict in their observance of the Codex Astartes as the Ultramarines themselves. They keep ten companies and the four branches under the Chapter Master, and the Chapter's wall-names and custom of naming companies after the gates they defended survive as a tradition. The Chapter has no fixed recruiting world, and keeps fortress-chapels staffed by veterans on many worlds, including Terra and Necromunda, where its Neophytes are drawn. It makes few demands on the worlds from which it recruits beyond the right to test those who wish to join, and on some hive worlds it purges the down-hive slums and takes the best fighters from among the captives. Aspirants spend six months aboard the Phalanx learning Low Gothic and are tested in a series of severe trials.[3]

    The Phalanx is defended by a security force called the Auric Auxilia, which in earlier centuries was filled with Imperial Fists aspirants who had failed the trials. Dessian has increased it to thousands, with Cadian veterans who joined in gratitude for the Chapter's help during the evacuation of Cadia. The Chapter also fields a company of Phalanx Warders, who fight behind boarding shields and are renowned for the severity of their training.[3]

    Combat doctrine

    [edit]

    The Imperial Fists are masters of the art of the siege, and are celebrated for the immovability of their defence. They are quick to find weak points in an enemy's defences and relentless in reducing them. They are willing to fight in open battle and do not look for sieges above any other kind of war, but their stubbornness means that they will not accept that a position cannot be held or an enemy cracked, and on many occasions they have taken far greater casualties than other Chapters would have accepted. They favour heavy weapons such as autocannons and lascannons, and were among the strongest proponents of Terminator armour. In the Era Indomitus they are among the first to field Indomitus-pattern Terminator suits.[3]

    Beliefs and culture

    [edit]

    Dorn's legacy, and the Siege of Terra, shaped the Chapter's culture. The Imperial Fists revere rituals, ceremony and honour. Battle-brothers fight honour duels with tungsten epees on magnetised floors, and may practise only when they are fully-fledged Marines. The Chapter is known for scrimshaw, the carving of the bones of the dead, which Imperial Fists perform as a solemn discipline and as penance; the practice is a genetic compulsion that grows with age. Officers wear scrimshaws as jewellery, and examples are displayed throughout the Phalanx. Imperial Fists are regarded by other Chapters as proud, stern and loyal, and as the Chapter that stood unbroken in the Heresy.[3]

    Gene-seed

    [edit]
    Main article: Gene-seed

    The Imperial Fists' gene-seed is very stable and has never mutated. However, the Chapter has lost the use of two organs: the Betcher's Gland, which lets a Marine spit acid, and the Sus-an Membrane, which allows suspended animation. The Chapter's Apothecarion records suggest that the loss followed Dorn's decision in the last years of the Heresy to speed up the implantation process, reordering the sequence to shorten it by about a third, which raised the death rate among Neophytes by well over a hundred per cent. Dorn regarded the two organs as vestigial in a war in which no prisoners were taken. Later data suggests that the misordered implantation permanently damaged the code for both. The Chapter's obsession with scrimshaw is the other recognised quirk.[3][9]

    Successor Chapters

    [edit]

    The Imperial Fists' Successors are among the most numerous. The best known are the Black Templars, a crusading Chapter; the Crimson Fists of Rynn's World; the Executioners, founded under Fafnir Rann in the Third Founding to seek out and kill Mankind's foes; the Excoriators, the Exorcists, the Hammers of Dorn, the Sons of Dorn, the Invaders, the Iron Knights, the Astral Knights, the Fists Exemplar and others. A few, among them the Crusaders of Dorn and the Honoured Sons, are only suspected to be of the same line, and the Venom Thorns are recorded as Traitoris Perdita.[8]
