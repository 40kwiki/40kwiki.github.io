    Mechanicum Taghmata
    Taghmata Omnissiah, "that which is divinely ordered for war"
    Seal of the Taghmata Omnissiah
    ActiveAge of Strife (estimated M25–M29) to the present
    TypeFeudal war host of a forge world
    AllegianceThe forge world's ruling archmagos and the Fabricator-General; divided in the Horus Heresy (005–014.M31)
    CommandArchmagos Intendant or Archmagos Archimandrite; Magi of the synod
    ComponentsTaghma of each magos; Myrmidons; Thallax; Secutors; cybernetic cohorts; Skitarii and Titans when attached
    Notable battlesSchism of Mars; Battle of Tallarn; Ryza (013.M31); Indomitus Crusade

    A Taghmata, in full the Taghmata Omnissiah, is the combined war host of a forge world of the Mechanicum. The name is Lingua-technis and is rendered in High Gothic as "that which is divinely ordered for war". A Taghmata is both an army and a mirror of the forge world's government: each magos of the ruling synod raises the forces of his own domain, his taghma, and the forge world's master calls them together for war.[1]

    In the Great Crusade (c. 798.M30 – 005.M31) the Taghmata was the most numerous of the Mechanicum's armed forces, and with the Skitarii and the Titan Legions it formed the Triad-Magna, the three great powers of Martian war. In the Horus Heresy the Taghmata split along the feudal loyalties of the forges, and Mechanicum armies fought one another across the galaxy. The word is still used in the Era Indomitus for the full war host of a forge world, which now joins Cult Mechanicus battle congregations to its Skitarii legions.[1][2]

    History

    [edit]

    Origins

    [edit]

    Each forge world of the Great Crusade was a kingdom in its own right. Some were bound to Mars as closely as satrapies; others, far away or proud, owed little more than doctrine. Within each, power was divided among the magi, every one of whom held a share of the world's industry, its secret knowledge and its soldiers. The Taghmata was the military form of this order. When the forge's master called it into being, each magos was bound to send what his oath required, and the host that resulted was as varied as the magi themselves.[1]

    The Great Crusade

    [edit]
    Further information: Great Crusade

    Under the Treaty of Olympus Mars and its forges owed the Emperor troops and arms. The Taghmata of Mars, Ryza, Stygies VIII and dozens of other worlds marched with the Expeditionary Fleets, guarded the Mechanicum's own Explorator fleets, and held the forges against raiders. Their soldiers ranged from indentured thralls to Myrmidon war-priests who rebuilt their own bodies as weapons, alongside heavy cyborg troops such as the Thallax and the Ursarax, and the robots of the Legio Cybernetica.[1]

    The Horus Heresy

    [edit]
    Further information: Horus Heresy

    The Heresy broke the Mechanicum as it broke the Legions. When Kelbor-Hal declared for Horus in 005.M31, the Schism of Mars set the forges of the Red Planet against each other, and the Taghmata of every forge world chose its side by old loyalty or new ambition. Some forges fought for the Emperor, some for the Warmaster, and some for themselves. Mechanicum armies fought Mechanicum, and both fought the Adeptus Astartes. At Ryza in 013.M31 the loyal Taghmata held its forges against three traitor Titan Legions. In the Battle of Tallarn (010–011.M31) Taghmata armour fought on both sides.[3]

    When the war ended the loyal Taghmata passed, with the rest of the loyal Mechanicum, into the new Adeptus Mechanicus in 012.M31. For some years its place in the Imperium was unclear; the Titan Legions formed the Collegia Titanica at once, but the forge armies kept their old feudal form. The traitor Taghmata became part of the Dark Mechanicum.[4]

    Later use

    [edit]

    The Taghmata survived the end of the Heresy because the forge worlds did. In the 41st and 42nd millennia the word names the full war host of a forge world: its Skitarii legions, its Cult Mechanicus battle congregations of Electro-Priests, Kataphron Servitors and Kastelan robots, and any Titans or Knights the forge lends to the war. Belisarius Cawl's fleets in the Indomitus Crusade (999.M41 – c. 012.M42) carried Taghmata drawn from many forges.[2][5]

    Organisation

    [edit]

    A Taghmata had no fixed size or table of organisation. Its smallest unit was the taghma, the household army of one magos, shaped by that magos's specialisation: a master of energies might send Thallax, a lord of the forges might send heavy armour. Above the magi stood the forge world's ruler, the Archmagos Intendant or Archmagos Archimandrite, who answered in turn to the Fabricator-General of Mars.[1]

    The Skitarii, the Titan Legions and the Knight houses kept their own commands and traditions and were attached to a Taghmata rather than part of it. Smaller orders, such as the Legio Cybernetica and the Ordo Reductor, the Mechanicum's artillery and siege order, served with it in the same way.[1]
