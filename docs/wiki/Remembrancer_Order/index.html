    The Remembrancer Order was the corps of civilian artists, writers and chroniclers that accompanied the Expeditionary Fleets of the Great Crusade. A Remembrancer was a human poet, historian, painter, sculptor, composer, actor or imagist, chosen on Terra and assigned to a fleet, with the duty of recording the Legions' victories so that the Imperium's posterity would know how Mankind had come to rule the stars. The Order's output, in verse, image, sculpture and prose, formed the main body of the Great Crusade's contemporary record. Most of it is now lost.[1][2]

    The Remembrancers were rarely welcome. The Astartes of the Legions, the primarchs and the line troops of the Army viewed them with the disdain that fighting men have for those who watch war from a distance. They were nonetheless indispensable to the Emperor's design. A crusade that recorded itself in an idiom of reason and progress was a crusade that did not need the temples it was displacing, and the Imperial Truth was spread in part by the pictures, poems and monuments that the Remembrancers produced. In the Heresy the Order's loyalties split along those of the Legions it served, and the greater part was killed.[3]

    Purpose

    [edit]

    The record

    [edit]

    The Great Crusade was too large to be known from the centre. The Administratum registered worlds and tithes, and the War Council on Terra received reports from the fleets, but nothing in the system gave an account of the crusade as an experience, or fixed it in the memory of the people who were not on the front. The Emperor, who held that a state without a story was a state without a future, decided that the crusade should have chroniclers of its own. Tradition holds that he authorised the creation of the Order in the first decade of the crusade and set its terms: Remembrancers were to be attached to every fleet, to have the freedom of its decks and to answer to the Council of Terra and not to the fleet's commander.[4]

    The Truth in images

    [edit]

    The Order had a second purpose. The Imperial Truth was a doctrine with no mythology, and the Imperium needed a picture of itself to put in place of the saints and heroes it had abolished. The Remembrancers were to provide one. Their paintings of the Triumph at Ullanor, their sculptures of the primarchs and their heroic verse on the Luna Wolves' victories were designed as a secular iconography, a reason for pride that did not mention a god. A fleet's Remembrancers and its Iterators often worked together, and in several fleets the two offices were held by the same person.[2]

    Organisation

    [edit]

    Recruitment

    [edit]

    The Remembrancers were overwhelmingly natives of Terra. Appointment to a fleet was a coveted honour, and the preeminent artists of the age competed for it. The Order drew on every branch of the arts, and its members are classed by their specialties.

    • Poets, who composed verse for the Legions' victories and the fallen.
    • Imagists, who made pict-records, the Order's equivalent of photographers.
    • Historiographers, who wrote the narrative accounts of campaigns.
    • Documentarists, who were the journalists of the fleets and filed reports to Terra.
    • Painters and sculptors, who produced the great works commissioned for the triumphs.
    • Actors, singers and composers, who gave performances for the troops and made the fleet's ceremonies.

    In the last years of the crusade the Order numbered some 4.3 million, a figure that includes the support staff. The Remembrancers were accommodated in fleet vessels, usually in the lesser decks of the flagship, and were quartered apart from the troops.[5]

    Relations with the Legions

    [edit]

    The Legions did not like the Remembrancers and did not hide it. Leman Russ suggested that every Remembrancer ought to be armed and ready to fight if he wanted to see the front. Fulgrim, by contrast, took the opposite view, and welcomed the Order among his Emperor's Children and the mortal troops of his fleets, in the belief that a feeling for art was as necessary to the life of a warrior as training for war. The arrangement brought the Emperor's Children a succession of brilliant Remembrancers, and some of the most striking works of the age, and it would end in tragedy.[1]

    In the Luna Wolves the Remembrancers were tolerated and, among some captains, befriended. The 63rd Expedition's complement included several of the Order's best members, and its Legion's own Chronicle was the work of one of them, Mersadie Oliton.[6]

    Notable Remembrancers

    [edit]

    The Order's most famous members are known chiefly through their involvement in the events of the Heresy.

    • Kyril Sindermann, of the 63rd Expedition, whose Remembrances of the 63rd Expedition is the finest surviving first-hand account of a single fleet. He was also an Iterator.[2]
    • Mersadie Oliton, of the same fleet, author of the Chronicles of the Luna Wolves, the Legion's history from the circle of the primarch.[6]
    • Ignace Karkasy, poet of the 63rd Expedition, a drinker and a satirist who made himself the protege of the Luna Wolves captain Garviel Loken, and who was known for always telling the truth as he saw it. His poem The Truth Is All We Have condemned the Mournival for the death of twenty-seven civilians trampled in the rush to carry the wounded Horus to the Apothecarion after the wounding on Davin. It earned him the enmity of many of the Legion.
    • Serena d'Angelus and Ostian Delafour, painter and sculptor of the 28th Expedition, attached to the Emperor's Children. Delafour, who had been patronised by the Emperor, fell from Fulgrim's favour by criticising the primarch's own sculpture as too flawless. He was murdered after making a statue of the Emperor. D'Angelus, corrupted by the sorcery of Slaanesh on Laeran, painted a portrait of Fulgrim that later housed the daemon of his blade and, for a while, the primarch himself. She killed herself on the sword that had killed Delafour.
    • Kallista Eris, a historiographer and a psyker, assigned to the Thousand Sons on Prospero. She was destroyed by the Warp when the Thousand Sons used her gift to foresee the coming of the Space Wolves.
    • Mahavastu Kallimakus, called the Scribe of Magnus, who served the primarch of the Thousand Sons personally and was present at the Council of Nikaea. He wrote the Book of Magnus, a work of esoteric rites. He fled Prospero before the Wolves arrived and escaped the Rune Priests' scrutiny. Ahriman found his works in the Athenaeum of Kallimakus, on the world of Appollonia, a millennium and a half later, and burned the library after he had taken what he wanted.

    The Great Crusade

    [edit]

    Chronicle and propaganda

    [edit]

    The Remembrancers' work was one of the Great Crusade's achievements, and one of its mistakes. They recorded real victories, and in the best of their accounts, such as Sindermann's, the writing combines candour and sympathy. They also made the war look easier than it was. The Rangdan Xenocides, which cost the Legions more than eighty thousand dead, were later given a legend by Remembrancers and ideologues who wished to promote the glory of the crusade, a legend composed of as much invention as fact. The glorification of the Triumph of Ullanor, in which the Legions were shown in the splendour of unity, hid the quarrels that were already dividing the primarchs.[1][7]

    At the front

    [edit]

    The Remembrancers were present at some of the great moments of the crusade. They stood on the balcony at the Triumph and watched the Warmaster's elevation. They were on Xenobia when the Interex were met, and at Davin when Horus was wounded. Some were at the Council of Nikaea. A few attended the councils of war of the primarchs. Their testimony records the tensions of the late crusade with a precision that the official reports do not, and the quarrels between the Remembrancers and the Legions are among the main sources for the mood of the fleets in those years.[2]

    The Heresy

    [edit]

    When Horus rebelled, the civilians of the Expeditionary Fleets that followed him were in an impossible position. In the fleets that turned to Chaos, the Remembrancers were for the most part killed by the Traitors to prevent them from documenting the betrayal. The exceptions were the Remembrancers of the 28th Expedition, many of whom had been exposed to the corruption of Laeran and turned, like the Emperor's Children, to the service of Slaanesh. In the loyal Legions the Remembrancers served as before, and their narratives, written at the time, are among the most important sources for the Heresy.[3]

    The Order's field records were scattered, and the fate of its central archives on Terra is not recorded. It is not known what became of the great mass of the Great Crusade's records created by the Order. They are not in the thin chronicles that survive in the 41st millennium, and the Logos Historica Verita, the Imperium's archival authority, has recovered only a small part.[3]

    Legacy

    [edit]

    The Order did not survive the Heresy as an institution. The work of the Remembrancers was taken up in a different form by the chroniclers of the Adeptus Administratum, the Logos Historica Verita and the scribes of the Chapters, but the Order's idea of a free civilian corps with access to the front did not return. The Imperium that emerged from the Heresy preferred its history to be official. The Remembrancers' works, where they survive, are read with a mixture of gratitude and caution, since the writers were committed to a doctrine, the Imperial Truth, that the Heresy proved false.[1]

    A few of their texts have a continued life in the Imperial Cult, which values the testimony of those who walked beside the primarchs. The Chronicles of the Luna Wolves and the Remembrances of the 63rd Expedition are standard sources for the history of the Great Crusade, and the surviving rolls of the Triumph of Ullanor are among the treasures of the Imperial Palace's archives.
