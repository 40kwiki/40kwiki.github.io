    The Webway, also called the Labyrinth Dimension, is a network of tunnels and passages that lies between realspace and the Warp and connects distant parts of the galaxy. It was made by the Old Ones in the remote past, and inherited by the Aeldari, who used it to bind together their empire. Within its passages a traveller can cross the galaxy without exposure to the Warp and without the need for a Navigator or the light of the Astronomican.[1][2]

    The Webway was badly damaged by the Fall of the Aeldari, and many of its passages are broken, sealed or open to the Warp. It nonetheless links the craftworlds of the Asuryani, the hidden Black Library of the Harlequins, and Commorragh, the vast city of the Drukhari. It is the chief reason that the aeldari, though few, can strike anywhere in the galaxy without warning.[1]

    The Webway is of the greatest importance in the history of the Imperium of Mankind. The Emperor of Mankind attempted to open it to humanity in a secret project beneath the Imperial Palace, and the ruin of that project at the start of the Horus Heresy bound him to the Golden Throne. The opening of the Great Rift at the end of the 41st millennium tore further through the network.[3][4]

    Nature

    [edit]

    The Webway is both a physical and a psychic structure. Its walls are said to be made of a psychoplastic substance akin to the wraithbone of the aeldari, and they generate a shield that keeps out the energies and the inhabitants of the Warp. A traveller within it moves through something like a tunnel, whose appearance may be of glowing crystal, of an endless corridor, or of shifting light, depending on the region and on the observer. Distances within the Webway do not correspond to distances in realspace, and a short walk may cross many light years.[1]

    The network varies greatly in size. Its main arterial passages are wide enough to carry starships and even, in the time of the aeldari empire, whole fleets. Most passages admit only travellers on foot or in small vehicles. It is joined to realspace by gates of many kinds. Some are built into the infinity circuits of craftworlds, some stand on maiden worlds and the ruins of the old aeldari empire, some hang in orbit and a few, long forgotten, lie beneath the surface of human worlds. Every gate is sealed by runes of psychic power that only the aeldari know how to open, and many have not been opened for millennia.[1][2]

    The Webway's speed is its great advantage, but it is also a limitation. The aeldari can move swiftly between places joined by the network, but find it hard to reach worlds that have no gate. Because their craftworlds travel only with difficulty through the Warp, the Webway is the main means of contact between them.[1]

    History

    [edit]

    The Old Ones

    [edit]
    Main article: Old Ones

    The Webway was made by the Old Ones, an ancient and advanced race that held an interstellar empire tens of millions of years before most other species arose. They built it as a conduit that let them reach distant worlds at will without risking the tides of the Warp.[1]

    The aeldari empire

    [edit]

    The Old Ones taught the young aeldari the art of the Webway, and the aeldari quickly mastered it and spread their realms into the furthest corners of reality. At the height of their empire the network joined their worlds so closely that they could move between them without need of the Warp. The Emperor of Mankind later envied this unity.[1]

    The Fall

    [edit]

    The Fall of the Aeldari, which gave birth to the Chaos god Slaanesh and created the Eye of Terror, ruptured the Webway in countless places. Whole regions were swallowed by the Warp, and the passages that led to them became doorways into the realm of daemons. The surviving aeldari sealed these doors where they could, but many paths now lead to dead ends, to abandoned worlds or to places inhabited by Warp entities. Even the aeldari no longer know the network's full extent. Their seers guard the knowledge of each craftworld's place in it, and the Harlequins, servants of the Laughing God Cegorach, are said to know secret paths that no one else may walk.[1]

    The Imperial Webway Project

    [edit]
    Main article: Webway War

    During the Unification Wars the Emperor discovered, buried beneath a desert of Terra, an ancient alien device that gave access to the Webway. He set the Mechanicum to building around it the machine that would become the Golden Throne, and for centuries he directed in secret what he called his Great Work: an Imperial passage into the Webway, through which humanity could travel between its worlds without Navigators and communicate without astropaths. He believed that the Imperium could only survive if it possessed the same unity as the aeldari had once enjoyed. The project was known only to a few, among them Malcador the Sigillite and the Legio Custodes.[3][5]

    The Emperor maintained, by his own power, the psychic shield that protected the human-built gateway and conduit from the Warp. In 004.M31, just before the Heresy began, Magnus the Red, seeking to warn his father of Horus's treachery, sent a sorcerous message to Terra that broke through the Palace's wards and through the shield of the Imperial Webway. The daemons of the Warp poured into the passages. Many of the Legio Custodes and the Sisters of Silence gave their lives to hold the daemons back in the war that is described in the article Webway War.[3]

    The breach could not be repaired. To keep the gate beneath the Palace closed, the Emperor had to sit upon the Golden Throne and hold it shut with his own mind. He was relieved for a time by Malcador, who gave his life to the task, and after the Siege of Terra the Emperor's broken body was entombed in the Throne, where it remains. The Webway Project was abandoned, and its existence was concealed from all but a few. It is not known whether the Emperor's protection of individual humans from Chaos is connected to the destroyed gate or to the unknown alien device at the heart of the Throne.[3][5]

    Places within the Webway

    [edit]

    Commorragh

    [edit]
    Main article: Drukhari

    Commorragh, the Dark City, is the largest settlement in the Webway and the home of the Drukhari. It began as a port of the aeldari empire and grew, in the millennia before the Fall, into a refuge for those who wished to indulge their appetites beyond the reach of aeldari law. Its isolation in the Webway allowed it to survive the Fall. It is now a city of countless spires and sub-realms, lit by stolen suns, from which the Drukhari raid realspace for slaves and souls.[1]

    The Black Library

    [edit]
    Main article: Harlequins

    The Black Library is the best-hidden place in the Webway, a repository of the aeldari's knowledge of Chaos guarded by the Harlequins. It is said to hold a map of the whole Webway, made thousands of years ago. The aeldari no longer share the secrets of the network with humans.[1][2]

    Era Indomitus

    [edit]
    Further information: Era Indomitus

    The opening of the Great Rift, which the aeldari call the Dathedian, shattered many of the Webway's arterial passages. Sections were cut off, gates were ruptured, and some passages now open directly into the Warp. The aeldari who survived abandoned large parts of the network. The Drukhari, however, have used the confusion of the Rift to raid human worlds in the Imperium Nihilus on an unprecedented scale.[4]

    The Ynnari, followers of the aeldari god of the dead, have used the network to move between the craftworlds, and occasionally to fight alongside Imperial forces.[4]
