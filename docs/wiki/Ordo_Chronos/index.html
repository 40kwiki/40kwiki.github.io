    Ordo Chronos
    Ordo Minoris of the Inquisition
    The Inquisitorial I, the symbol of the Holy Ordos
    Organisation
    Known as"The Unseen Ordo" (Imperial rumour)
    RemitTemporal anomalies, time travel through the Warp, the integrity of the Imperial Dating System
    FoundedUnrecorded; established in the Jericho Reach before the 41st millennium
    StatusDisappeared at an unrecorded date; active again by 999.M41
    Chamber MilitantNone recorded
    Notable eventThe Chronostrife, c. 000–012.M42[a]
    Sibling ordosOrdo Malleus, Ordo Xenos, Ordo Hereticus, Ordo Originatus, Ordo Redactus

    The Ordo Chronos is one of the Ordos Minoris of the Inquisition. It exists to investigate the consequences of travel through time by way of the Warp, and above all the danger that someone might manipulate it on purpose. It is among the most obscure bodies of the Holy Ordos. Few Inquisitors have heard of it, and fewer still know what it is for.[1][2]

    The Ordo is said to have been raised in the Jericho Reach to deal with the temporal accidents of Warp travel. It studied them for several centuries, then vanished. Every Inquisitor linked to it disappeared or went into hiding in the same moment, and no record says why. By 999.M41 the Ordo was active again, either returned from wherever it had gone or rebuilt from new members. Its best-known act in the Imperial record is not an investigation but a quarrel: the Chronostrife, its internal dispute over the true date.[1][3]

    Remit

    [edit]

    Warp-induced time slips

    [edit]

    Time does not pass in the Warp as it does in realspace. A vessel becalmed in the Immaterium may emerge after its crew have lived a few solar months, while centuries have gone by outside. Rarer, and more feared, are voyages that end with the ship expelled from the Warp at a time before it departed. The Ordo's founding concern was that such travellers, appearing in the past, might alter the proper course of events and so the fate of Mankind and the intentions of the Emperor of Mankind.[1]

    Deliberate manipulation

    [edit]

    Over the centuries its inquiries widened from accidents to cases that might have been intended, and the Ordo came to watch for any attempt to bend time on purpose. Imperial records of what it found are almost wholly absent.[1]

    The Imperial calendar

    [edit]
    Main article: Imperial Dating System

    The Imperial Dating System is listed among the matters the Ordo maintains alongside the Administratum. After the Great Rift opened in 999.M41 the subjective flow of time on many worlds departed wildly from that on Terra, and the Ordo took part in the decision that ended the single Imperial calendar (see below).[3]

    Matters outside the record

    [edit]

    Imperial rumour links the Ordo to two further concerns. The first is the manipulation of time by the daemons of Tzeentch, the Changer of Ways, and by the sorcerers who serve him. The second is the time-keeping arts of the Necrons, whose tomb worlds are said to hold devices that slow, fold or stop time. No sealed or open record confirms that the Ordo has ever studied either. They are given here as rumour only.[2]

    Secrecy and disappearance

    [edit]

    The Ordo is secret even by the standard of the Inquisition. Its numbers are unknown and no Inquisitor of the Ordo is named in the open record, which is itself a mark of how thoroughly it has kept its work from the other Ordos. When it vanished, no body of Inquisitors claimed to know why. Imperial rumour holds that Renegade factions of the Adeptus Mechanicus were involved, pursuing technologies the Cult Mechanicus has long forbidden. The rumour is unsupported. It is repeated chiefly by those few who remember that the Ordo ever existed, and some of them say that its members remain watchful, ready to return if the doom they feared should come.[1]

    The Chronostrife

    [edit]
    Main article: Logos Historica Verita

    When the Ordo returned, it came with a feud. The Chronostrife, also called the Chronostrife of Terra, was a bitter and continuing dispute among its Inquisitors over the Imperial calendar. In the ten thousand years since the Great Crusade the dating system had been worn by dogmatic adherence on the one side and thoughtless revision on the other, and rival calendars had grown out of it.[3]

    The dispute became known beyond the Ordo after Roboute Guilliman returned and founded the Logos Historica Verita to recover Mankind's history. Near the end of the first phase of the Indomitus Crusade, he calculated the year by the five principal calendars and found that they set it anywhere from the early 41st millennium to a full millennium later, not counting the lesser and more heretical reckonings. After conference with his historitors and with the Ordo Chronos, he put before the High Lords of Terra a decree that ended the demand for a single reckoning. Each region now keeps a chronology anchored to the moment the Rift opened over it.[3] The Vigilus Template is the best known of these local calendars.

    Relations with other Ordos

    [edit]

    The Ordo Chronos has no Chamber Militant and no recorded dealings with a Chamber Militant. Its link with the Ordo Malleus, which studies the Warp, is natural but unrecorded. The Chronostrife touches the work of the Ordo Originatus, which tries to establish the Imperium's true history, and of the Ordo Redactus, which tries to bury it, since neither task is possible without a date. The Ordo Machinum would be the Ordo to ask about the forbidden technologies named in the rumour. All of this is inference, and the Ordos themselves are silent.[1]

    Known members

    [edit]

    None are named. The roll of the Ordo is sealed, lost, or never written.
