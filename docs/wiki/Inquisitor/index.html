    An Inquisitor is a member of the Inquisition, the secret order charged with protecting the Imperium of Mankind from heresy, the alien, the daemon and every other threat within or without. Inquisitors hold the authority of the Emperor of Mankind himself. They may command any servant of the Imperium, from a planetary militia to a Space Marine Chapter, requisition any resource, try and execute anyone below the rank of High Lord and, in the last resort, order the destruction of whole worlds by Exterminatus.[1][2]

    An Inquisitor answers to no superior. He is bound only by his own conscience and by the scrutiny of his peers, who may judge and destroy a colleague who has fallen to heresy. Most Inquisitors belong to one of the three great ordos, the Ordo Malleus, the Ordo Hereticus and the Ordo Xenos, or to one of many lesser ordos, and senior Inquisitors may be raised to the rank of Inquisitor Lord or Lord Inquisitor. Apprentices are called Interrogators, and the agents of an Inquisitor's retinue Acolytes or Throne Agents.[1]

    Inquisitors are marked by no uniform, but by the Inquisitorial Rosette, a seal in the form of the Inquisitorial I, which proves their authority and which every Imperial servant is bound to obey.[2]

    History

    [edit]

    Origins

    [edit]

    Tradition holds that the first Inquisitors were the agents of Malcador the Sigillite during the Horus Heresy (005–014.M31). Malcador recruited warriors from the Space Marine Legions, some of them loyalists from the traitor Legions, along with members of the Adeptus Custodes, the Sisters of Silence and others, and sent them on secret missions as his Knights-Errant. After the Siege of Terra in 014.M31 their work continued, and the institution that grew from it became the Inquisition. The Grey Knights were founded in the same years from the same circle of agents.[3][4]

    The ordos

    [edit]

    The great ordos took their form over the following millennia. The Ordo Malleus, which hunts daemons, was the first, with the Grey Knights as its Chamber Militant. The Ordo Hereticus, which hunts heretics, witches and mutants, rose to prominence after the Age of Apostasy of M36, when the Reign of Blood of Goge Vandire showed the danger of a corrupt Ecclesiarchy; the Adepta Sororitas became its Chamber Militant. The Ordo Xenos guards against the alien, with the Deathwatch as its Chamber Militant.[4][5]

    The Era Indomitus

    [edit]
    Further information: Era Indomitus

    The opening of the Great Rift in 999.M41 and the return of Roboute Guilliman reshaped the work of Inquisitors. Many Inquisitors in the Imperium Nihilus were cut off from their ordos. Guilliman, as Lord Commander of the Imperium, has relied on Inquisitors such as Katarinya Greyfax, while many Inquisitors distrust a primarch's return to power. A new minor ordo, the Ordo Maledictum, studies the Rift itself.[6]

    Powers

    [edit]

    An Inquisitor's power is limited only by what he can persuade or compel others to do. The Inquisitorial Rosette gives him the right to:[1]

    • command the forces of any Imperial institution, including the Astra Militarum, the Imperial Navy, the Adeptus Arbites and Space Marine Chapters, though the Chapters' cooperation is not always given willingly;
    • investigate, arrest, try and execute any citizen or official, including Planetary Governors;
    • call upon the Chambers Militant of the ordos, and upon the Officio Assassinorum;
    • declare Exterminatus, the destruction of all life on a world.

    An Inquisitor who abuses these powers may be judged by others. Inquisitors may declare a colleague excommunicate, which strips him of his authority and makes him a target for the whole order.[1]

    Ranks

    [edit]

    Inquisitor Lord

    [edit]

    The Inquisition has no formal hierarchy, but its most senior and respected members are recognised by their peers as Inquisitor Lords, also styled Lord Inquisitors or High Inquisitors. An Inquisitor Lord has no subordinates by right, but may recognise new Inquisitors, call conclaves of his ordo and command great resources. The Inquisitorial Representative among the High Lords of Terra is chosen from them.[1]

    Interrogator

    [edit]

    An Interrogator is an Inquisitor's apprentice, who serves in his retinue and learns his methods. When his master judges him ready, the Interrogator is given his own Rosette and becomes a full Inquisitor. Most Inquisitors are chosen in this way from among the Interrogators of others.[1]

    Acolytes

    [edit]

    The members of an Inquisitor's retinue, the Acolytes or Throne Agents, are drawn from every part of Imperial society: soldiers, scholars, priests, psykers, assassins, servitors and even criminals. They serve as investigators, bodyguards and agents, and some become Interrogators in time.[1]

    Philosophies

    [edit]

    Inquisitors disagree deeply about how the Imperium is best protected. Puritans hold that the enemy must be destroyed by any means but that the Inquisitor must never use the enemy's own weapons; Radicals hold that the weapons of the enemy, including daemons, psychic sorcery and alien technology, may be turned against it. Among Radicals, Xanthites hold that the power of Chaos can be harnessed, and Recongregators hold that the Imperium itself must be overturned and rebuilt. Each faction regards some of the others as heretics.[1]
