    A Techmarine is a Space Marine of the Adeptus Astartes who has been trained by the Adeptus Mechanicus to maintain, repair and command his Chapter's war machines. Techmarines serve in the Chapter's Armoury, which keeps its tanks, aircraft, Dreadnoughts, wargear and the systems of its fortress-monastery. The Armoury is led by the Master of the Forge, who sits in the Chapter Command.[1][2]

    Techmarines hold a double loyalty. They are sworn brothers of their Chapter but have also been initiated, on Mars, into the mysteries of the Cult Mechanicus, whose god is the Omnissiah. The arrangement rests on the ancient alliance between Terra and Mars, the Treaty of Olympus of 739.M30, and on the Codex Astartes, which made the Armoury a part of every Chapter. Most Chapters regard their Techmarines with respect and some unease, as men who are not wholly of the Chapter.[3][1]

    Techmarines wear red armour, or red markings on their Chapter's colours, and are recognised by the servo-harness they bear, a set of mechanical arms, tools and weapons mounted on the back of their armour.[2]

    History

    [edit]

    The Legions and the Mechanicum

    [edit]
    See also: Space Marine Legions and Dark Mechanicum

    Under the Treaty of Olympus, signed in 739.M30, the Mechanicum of Mars agreed to arm the Emperor's armies, and the Space Marine Legions were among the first to benefit. The Legions' own artificers, called Techmarines or forge-masters in several Legions, were trained on Mars and returned to their Legions with the knowledge to maintain their vehicles and armour. The Iron Hands under Ferrus Manus and the Iron Warriors under Perturabo were famed for their close bonds with the forges, while the Salamanders kept their own tradition of smithcraft from Nocturne.[3][4]

    The Schism of Mars, which began in 005.M31, split the Mechanicum between loyalists and the traitor Dark Mechanicum of Kelbor-Hal. Techmarines of the loyal Legions fought against the traitor forge worlds, and some of the traitor Legions' Techmarines became the first Warsmiths and Hereteks of the Chaos Legions.[5]

    The Codex Astartes

    [edit]
    Main article: Codex Astartes

    The Codex Astartes of c. 019.M31 confirmed the arrangement. Each Chapter was to send chosen battle-brothers to Mars, where they would spend years in the forges learning the rites of the machine before returning to serve in the Armoury. In return the Chapters owed the Mechanicus respect for its doctrines and a share of their gene-seed for its vaults. The Chapters' Techmarines thus became one of the few lasting bonds between the Astartes and the priesthood of Mars.[1]

    The Era Indomitus

    [edit]
    Further information: Era Indomitus

    The Ultima Founding of 999.M41 was itself a work of the Mechanicus, overseen by Belisarius Cawl, and brought with it new vehicles, weapons and armour that the Chapters' Armouries had to learn. Primaris Techmarines, trained in Cawl's own forges, joined the Chapters from the first years of the 42nd millennium.[6]

    Training

    [edit]

    A battle-brother chosen for the Armoury is usually one who has shown an affinity for machines. He travels to Mars, or to a forge world allied to his Chapter, and spends years in apprenticeship to the Tech-priests. He learns to tend machine spirits, to perform the rites of repair and activation, and to understand the technology of the Standard Template Construct. He receives bionic implants and the servo-harness, and is initiated into the lower mysteries of the Cult Mechanicus. Imperial records note that some Techmarines return to their Chapters changed in manner and speech, and that the Chapters' Chaplains watch them closely for signs of divided loyalty.[2]

    Duties

    [edit]
    • Maintenance. Techmarines perform the rites that keep a Chapter's Rhinos, Land Raiders, Thunderhawks and Dreadnoughts in service, and the repairs to its power armour and weapons.
    • Battle. In the field they repair damaged vehicles under fire, command batteries of artillery such as the Thunderfire cannon, and lead the servitors of the Armoury.
    • Fortress. They tend the systems of the fortress-monastery, its defences and its void shields.
    • Relics. They are the keepers of the Chapter's relic armour and weapons, whose machine spirits they must placate.

    Master of the Forge

    [edit]
    See also: Forge world

    The senior Techmarine of a Codex Chapter is the Master of the Forge, a member of the Chapter Command beside the Chief Librarian, the Master of Sanctity and the Chief Apothecary. He commands the Armoury and all its Techmarines, servitors and serfs, and he is responsible for the Chapter's whole stock of war machines. Many Masters of the Forge are also the Chapter's chief liaison with the Mechanicus and with its allied forge worlds.[1]

    Chapter variants

    [edit]
    • The Iron Hands give their Techmarines, the Iron Fathers, great authority; the Chapter is ruled by an Iron Council in which they hold many seats.
    • The Space Wolves call their Techmarines Iron Priests. They are trained on Fenris and not on Mars, in an arrangement the Mechanicus has tolerated rather than approved.
    • The Salamanders hold the craft of the forge as a sacred duty of every warrior; their Techmarines lead a Chapter of smiths.

    Standing in the Chapter

    [edit]

    A Techmarine's double oath is a frequent cause of tension. The Mechanicus regards him as an initiate of its own cult, owing obedience to Mars on matters of technology, while his Chapter regards him as a brother whose first loyalty is to his Chapter Master. Most Chapters resolve the matter by custom: the Techmarine keeps the rites of the Omnissiah in the Armoury but takes his orders in battle from his commanders. In the Schism of Mars of 005.M31 and in later disputes between the Chapters and the forge worlds, some Techmarines have had to choose between the two.[5]

    Techmarines are also among the few Space Marines who regularly deal with outsiders: tech-priests, the Titan Legions, the Navy's shipwrights and the magi of the forge worlds that supply their Chapters.[2]
