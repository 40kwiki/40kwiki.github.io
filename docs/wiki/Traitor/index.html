    From 40,000 Wiki, the Imperial encyclopedia
    "Traitors" redirects here. For the Space Marine Legions that turned against the Emperor, see Traitor Legions. For the designation applied to the worst traitors, see Excommunicate Traitoris. For the crime of belief, see Heretic.

    A Traitor is, in the law of the Imperium of Mankind, a person or organisation that has betrayed the Emperor or the Imperium. Treason is the highest civil crime, and the Lex Imperialis treats it as the gravest offence that a citizen can commit. A traitor is judged by the Imperial courts, by the Inquisition, or by the authority of the High Lords of Terra, and the penalty is death.

    The status of Traitor is distinct from that of Heretic. A heretic holds or spreads a doctrine that opposes the Imperial Cult or the law. A traitor acts against the Imperium, by rebellion, by aiding its enemies, or by serving the Ruinous Powers. Imperial law holds that a heretic who acts against the Imperium becomes a traitor, and that the two charges are often brought together.[1]

    Legal status

    [edit]

    Treason

    [edit]

    Treason is the act of betraying the Emperor, the Imperium or its institutions. It includes rebellion, desertion in the face of an enemy, the disclosure of secrets to outsiders, and any service to the enemies of Mankind.[1]

    Traitor status

    [edit]

    An individual who is judged a traitor loses the protection of the law. Their possessions are forfeit, and their names may be struck from the records. A captured traitor is subject to execution, and loyal servants of the Imperium are held to be expected to act against known traitors. The sentence is harsh because the Imperium regards treason as a threat to all of Mankind, not only to the people who are betrayed.

    Excommunicate Traitoris

    [edit]

    The most severe form of the status is the Excommunicate Traitoris designation. It is given by the High Lords of Terra or by an Inquisitor, and it removes the accused from the Imperium and from its records. The designation is used against the greatest traitors, including whole Chapters that have turned against the Emperor.

    Traitor Legions

    [edit]

    The largest body of traitors in Imperial history is the Traitor Legions of the Horus Heresy. The Heresy began in 005.M31, when the Warmaster Horus rebelled against the Emperor. Several Legions followed him, and their warriors were declared traitors. The Imperial account of the Heresy treats their betrayal as a crime against Mankind and not only against the Emperor.[2]

    The Legions' descendants are still treated as traitors by Imperial law. The Chaos Space Marines are the successors of the Traitor Legions, and they are hunted accordingly.

    Trial and proof

    [edit]

    Imperial law requires that the accused be judged before the sentence is passed, except where an Inquisitor or the High Lords have declared an Excommunicate Traitoris. In practice, evidence is weighed by the Inquisition, which may set aside ordinary procedure where heresy or Chaos is suspected. The Arbites and the military courts apply the ordinary procedure to lesser cases.

    Imperial sources treat the proof of treason as a difficult matter. An accused person may be a victim of the Warp, of a cult or of a corrupt official, and the law allows for that possibility. Those who confess and repent may be spared the full penalty, and some are sent to the Penitent order to atone.[3]

    Historical cases

    [edit]

    The Heresy is the largest case of treason in the Imperial record. After the Heresy, the Great Scouring hunted the surviving traitors across the galaxy, and the Black Crusade and later Chaos incursions brought new traitors to the Imperium's attention. The Siege of Terra, which ended the Heresy in 014.M31, is the final stage of the same crime.
