    A sub-sector is the astrographical and political division of an Imperial sector that gathers a cluster of inhabited star systems close enough to be served by the same squadrons of the Imperial Navy. Each sector holds a variable number of them, and each sub-sector usually comprises between two and eight star systems within a radius of about ten light years, although some hold more and a few hold only one. Below it lie the individual systems and the planetary governors who rule their worlds.[1][2]

    The size of a sub-sector is set by the practical patrol range of Imperial starships, and not by an equal volume of space. It is a division of worlds. A typical sector might occupy some seven million cubic light years, a cube with sides of almost two hundred light years, but only a fraction of the star systems inside it belong to any sub-sector. The rest are called inter-sector space, and are better known as wilderness space, forbidden zones, empty space and frontier space.[1][3]

    Many sub-sectors take the name of their principal system or world, and their governors are drawn from the same noble houses, Ecclesiarchal prelates and Administratum officials who supply the rulers of sectors. In the Era Indomitus the division survives in the registers of the Administratum, although the opening of the Great Rift in 999.M41 has cut many sub-sectors off from their sector capitals and from the Astronomican, and the Imperium has not mapped the damage.[4][5]

    Definition

    [edit]

    Size and shape

    [edit]

    The rule that sets a sub-sector at two to eight systems within ten light years is a guide, not a law, and the records say expressly that some sub-sectors hold more systems and others only one. The limit comes from the practical patrol range of Imperial starships through the Warp, so a sub-sector is in effect the group of inhabited systems that the same squadrons can reach and that share the same warp routes. The label therefore says more about the pattern of Imperial settlement than about the shape of space, and two sub-sectors of equal importance may occupy very different volumes.[1][2]

    The worlds of the Imperium are not scattered at random. They are clustered around the regions that Mankind settled in the first wave of its expansion in the Age of Technology, and worlds once colonised for their position or for some natural resource have grown into the cores of sectors, many of which now hold two hundred or more star systems. The sub-sector is the Administratum's way of dividing such a cluster into units that a governor and a fleet can manage.[5]

    Inter-sector space

    [edit]
    Main article: Wilderness space

    Because a sub-sector is a division of worlds, there are vast numbers of systems in every sector that fall within none. Imperial charts group them as inter-sector space. It may hold gas or dust nebulae, regions that ships cannot enter, systems inhabited by xenos, unexplored systems, systems with no inhabitants at all, and worlds that cannot be lived on. Some of these systems are known only by rumour. Others are shown on the charts and are simply left unregistered because no governor, fleet or tithe-collector has reached them.[1]

    Names and boundaries

    [edit]

    Most sub-sectors are called by the name of their chief system or world, as the Veritus Sub-sector is named for Veritus and the Helican Sub-sector for the Helican region around Thracian Primaris, though some bear the name of a geographic feature, such as a reach, a march, a sound, a nebula or a cluster. The names appear on the charts of the Administratum and the Navy and are used in tithe registers and fleet orders. A boundary is a fact of registration. It marks the worlds that a governor answers for, and it does not stop ships, trade or heresy from crossing it.[5][1]

    Position in the Imperial hierarchy

    [edit]
    Main article: Governance of the Imperium

    The sub-sector is the lowest level of Imperial territory that has its own administrative character. It stands under the sector, which in turn lies in one of the five segmenta, and above the single star system and the planet. Each sector is assigned a battlefleet of the Imperial Navy, usually named after the sector, such as Battlefleet Gothic or Battlefleet Calixis, and its squadrons are the forces that patrol the sub-sectors within.[1][5]

    Government and defence

    [edit]

    Civil rule

    [edit]

    The ruler of a sector or of a single sub-sector is a sector lord, also called a sector governor, who is formally addressed as "Lord" followed by the name of the territory, for example Lord Calixis. His duties resemble those of a planetary governor but reach across several systems. Most sector lords also rule the capital or principal world of their region. Some are hereditary nobles of the region. Others are high prelates of the Ecclesiarchy, such as the Cardinal Morst Bolifax, who governed the Veritus Sub-sector from the cardinal world of Benediction. Marius Hax, the sector lord of Calixis, ruled from Scintilla, which he governed as well.[6]

    Naval and military forces

    [edit]

    Sub-sectors are patrolled by squadrons drawn from the sector battlefleet, and the sub-sector governor depends on them for its defence. The regiments of the Astra Militarum are raised by the Departmento Munitorum from the worlds of the Imperium, and each planet keeps its own defence force for the first hours of any attack.[5][1]

    History

    [edit]

    Origins

    [edit]

    The Administratum's own records do not say who first divided Imperial space into sub-sectors, or when. The grouping of worlds by their shared warp routes and tithe is older than the registers now held on Terra, and in the Great Crusade (c. 798.M30 to 005.M31) the expeditions of the Imperium did group the worlds they won by the routes that joined them. The term itself appears in the surviving charts of the later Imperium.[2][a]

    The sub-sector in war

    [edit]

    The sub-sector is the unit in which the Navy and the Administratum commonly describe campaigns. In the Gothic War, which opened in 139.M41, the fleets of the Gothic Sector fought for the seven sub-sectors of the region (Bhein Morr, the Cyclops Cluster, Gethsemane, Port Maw, Quinrox Sound, Lysades and Orar) against the fleets of Abaddon the Despoiler. In the late 41st millennium the Helican Sub-sector of the Scarus Sector was the scene of the Helican Schism, and the Veritus Sub-sector became the battlefield of the Talledus War after 999.M41.[1][5]

    Era Indomitus

    [edit]

    The opening of the Great Rift split many sectors into two parts, one lit by the Astronomican in the Imperium Sanctus and the other cut off in the Imperium Nihilus. A sub-sector that was whole in 998.M41 may now be divided between them, with some of its systems in the dark and some within reach of the Indomitus Crusade. The Administratum keeps the old boundaries in its registers while its officials sort out which sub-sectors still have a living governor.[2]

    Notable sub-sectors

    [edit]

    The sub-sectors known best to the Imperium's scholars are those that have been the scene of famous wars or of famous investigations. The Veritus Sub-sector in the Segmentum Solar is a prosperous domain of the Ecclesiarchy and the home of the Talledus System. The Helican Sub-sector holds the hive world of Thracian Primaris. The Armageddon Sector has the Armageddon, Justicar and Golgotha sub-sectors, and the Gothic Sector has seven, of which Port Maw holds the sector capital. The Calixis Sector is divided into the Golgenna Reach, the Drusus Marches, the Adrantis Nebula, the Hazeroth Abyss, the Malfian, the Josian Reach, the Markayn Marches and the Periphery. The Askellon Sector holds the Asphodel Depths, Cyclopia, Rubicon, the Stygies Cluster and Thule.[5][3]

    Smaller sectors are named for a single sub-sector at their heart. The Acteron Sector of the Eastern Fringe is known for the Pandraxx Sub-sector, the Chiros Sector of the Segmentum Tempestus for the Kalidar and Agritha sub-sectors, the Korianis Sector for Sub-sector Aurelia, the Forsarr Sector for Kastorel-Severus and the Barbarus Sector for Urphir. Only a few of the sub-sectors of the Imperium are named in the open records, and the rest are known to the Administratum alone.[5]

    The Veritus Sub-sector

    [edit]
    Main article: Veritus Sub-sector

    The Veritus Sub-sector shows how a sub-sector can take its character from its governor. It lies near the centre of the Segmentum Solar and is ruled by a prelate, not by a noble, so that its worlds keep the festivals and tithes of the Imperial faith and its capital, Benediction, is a cardinal world. The sub-sector was a prosperous bastion of the Church for centuries until the opening of the Great Rift, when the Chaos forces of the Dark Apostle Kor Phaeron attacked it in the Talledus War and made it a battlefield for the faith of its people.[6][5]
