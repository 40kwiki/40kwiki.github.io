    The World Eaters are the XII Legion of the Space Marine Legions. They were raised as the War Hounds in the last years of the Unification Wars, and they took their later name from their primarch, Angron, who had been a gladiator and the leader of a slave army on the world of Nuceria. Of all the Legions of the First Founding none was more dreaded in the Great Crusade: to be sent against an enemy meant extermination, not by virus bomb or firestorm but by chainaxe and bolter, with the world drowned in the blood of its people. In 005.M31 they followed Horus into rebellion, and in the course of the Horus Heresy they gave themselves to Khorne, the Chaos god of rage and slaughter.[1][2]

    The Legion that survived the Heresy was a host of berserkers. It withdrew with the other Traitors into the Eye of Terror, and was broken at Skalathrax as a coherent force by the madness of its own champion, Khârn, who earned there the name of the Betrayer. Its warbands have since gone to war independently, each under its own champion, and in the thirty-eighth millennium Angron himself led fifty thousand of them across seventy sectors in the Dominion of Fire.[2][3]

    In the Era Indomitus the World Eaters are as powerful as they have been since the Great Crusade. The Great Rift has returned Angron to the material universe in a form that cannot be banished for long, and the warbands that follow him are drawn by his call.[4]

    Legion era

    [edit]

    The War Hounds

    [edit]

    The XII Legion was formed on Terra with no particular preference for any region of recruitment, and Imperial scholars suspect a programme of psychological screening to choose the most aggressive and competitive candidates. Its first Legion Master was one of the most promising of them, Ibram Ghreer. Its first recorded engagement was the Sa'afrik Liberation, in which a small force took the lead against fortified positions by sheer fury. The Emperor then kept the Legion largely in reserve for the rest of the Unification Wars, in constant readiness and training, to be loosed where the fighting was worst. It is thought that in these years he called it his War Hounds, after the white hounds that the Yeshk warriors of northern Terra had once used in battle, and in memory of its tenacity in the pacification of the narcotic sprawls of the Cephic Hives. The Legion took a red hound for its badge.[1]

    As the Great Crusade expanded, the Legion was divided into independent commands of several thousand, the largest of which, the Bloody 13th Expeditionary Fleet, was about eight thousand strong. These served as a mobile reserve on the frontier, fought beside the Space Wolves, Iron Warriors and Dark Angels, and gave the killing blow to larger formations of the Imperial Army in wars that had reached a stalemate. Every assault they made, it was said, ended in victorious slaughter or simple slaughter, and either left the enemy without any means of resistance. Others who fought beside them found them unpredictable and dangerous to anything within their reach.[1]

    Angron

    [edit]

    The Legion was one of the last to find its primarch. Angron, who had been the champion of the arena of Desh'ea on Nuceria and the leader of an army of escaped slaves, the Eaters of Cities, was taken from the world against his will by the Emperor when his army was about to be destroyed, and it was in this condition that he came to his Legion at the muster on its garrison world of Bodt. His first acts were murders: he killed at least seven senior officers of the Legion, among them its Legion Master. It was only when Khârn, captain of its Eighth Assault Company, went to the primarch's quarters alone, and was beaten almost to death without yielding, that Angron decided the Legion was worthy of him. He renamed it the World Eaters, in memory of the army of Nuceria and after the promise of a company captain that the Legion would become eaters of worlds.[1][5]

    Angron remade the Legion in the image of the arena. Conflict became its only measure and its only judge. Training was live-fire, in fighting pits, with blades and bare hands, and each warrior bore the scars of his own battles as a kind of record. Knowing what the Butcher's Nails, the cortical implants that the High-Riders had hammered into his own skull, had done for his prowess, he ordered the Legion's Apothecaries to fit the same devices to every Legionary. The implants heightened aggression and tolerance for pain beyond the power of even the transhuman body, and took in exchange every joy and peace but that of battle. The Legion's Techmarines could not copy the device, which was a relic of the Dark Age of Technology that they did not understand and could not take from the primarch without killing him. Early attempts under the Apothecary Gahlan Surlak killed every volunteer. Angron insisted, and in time the Legion found a way.[1][5]

    Ghenna and the Night of the Wolf

    [edit]

    The new Legion left a trail of ruined worlds, and its methods were censured by several primarchs. Roboute Guilliman, who fought beside it in the cleansing of Arigatta, saw the ramps of World Eaters corpses and the bloodbath that the Legion left at the Basalt Citadel, and said that they were excessive. The massacre of the whole population of Ghenna ended the Emperor's patience. The Legion was publicly censured and ordered to stop using the implants, and Leman Russ was sent with the Space Wolves to bring Angron to heel.[1][5]

    The two Legions met at Malkoya, outside the dead city of the same name. Russ told Angron that the surgeries and the massacres had to end, and that the World Eaters were to go to Terra under escort, where everything would be done to remove the Nails. No one afterwards could say who fired the first shot, and the Space Wolves and the World Eaters each blamed the other. Without an order from either primarch the Legions fought. Imperial archives call the incident the Ghenna Scouring and omit the moment at which blood was drawn. Both Legions claimed victory and kept the memory as a source of pride and of secret shame.[1]

    Horus Heresy

    [edit]

    Joining the Warmaster

    [edit]

    The Emperor's secret that the Nails were killing his son, and could not be removed without killing him, was kept from the Legion for the whole of the Crusade. Horus' conspirators learned it, and the approach to Angron was made through Lorgar, who offered him an end to the pain. In 005.M31 the World Eaters joined the Warmaster's Legions at the opening of the Heresy.[5]

    They were present at Isstvan III, where the loyalists of four Legions, among them a part of their own, were destroyed by the virus bombardment and by those whom the bombs did not kill. Angron led the ground assault on the survivors, who held out for nearly two months, and then took part in the Drop Site Massacre at Isstvan V.[2][5]

    The Shadow Crusade

    [edit]

    After Isstvan V, Lorgar proposed a joint campaign by the Word Bearers and the World Eaters in the realm of Ultramar. The Shadow Crusade, as Imperial archives call it, destroyed the populations of twenty-six worlds in rapid succession. At one point the World Eaters were lured by the Ultramarines into a trap in the ruins of Armatura, a war-world of the Five Hundred Worlds, and lost many of their number beneath collapsing buildings. It was in the Shadow Crusade that Angron passed from mortality to daemonhood. Lorgar's rite ended the primarch's pain, but it did so by giving him to Khorne, and the Legion, which had fought as an army of gladiators, fought thereafter as the host of the Blood God. Surviving accounts of the rite disagree on whether Lorgar acted from compassion or from calculation.[2][5]

    Ullanor and Terra

    [edit]

    After the Shadow Crusade the World Eaters went on a rampage through the Imperium and disregarded the Warmaster's order to muster. Perturabo of the Iron Warriors was sent to bring Angron to the muster at Ullanor. The brothers fought. Angron prevailed in strength, but Perturabo, though wounded, told him that he had been born a slave and would remain one for ever. The primarch then joined the muster.[5]

    At the Siege of Terra, Angron demanded to be sent at once against the Palace. The Emperor's psychic defences, which would have been fatal to a daemon, were still intact, and he was held in orbit. When he heard that the Death Guard were to have the first assault, he killed many of the crew of his own flagship, the Conqueror. The ship's captain, Lotara Sarrin, and Khârn, with the Night Lords captain Gendor Skraivok, contrived to move him into a maze that Perturabo had built aboard the Nightfall, where he was held until the Traitors' rituals had weakened the barrier around Terra. He was then fired to the surface like a meteor.[5][2]

    On Terra, Angron fought his way to the Palace walls and challenged Sanguinius, who saluted him and declined, saying that they would fight, but not that day. When the Lion's Gate Spaceport fell and the defence was reduced to the Inner Palace, the World Eaters took the Eternity Wall and led the assault on the Palace. The Legion's own annals state that it was Angron's blade that broke the great gate, a claim that the Sons of Horus dispute. When Horus died the Traitor army collapsed, and Angron was the last of the primarchs to leave.[5]

    The Long War

    [edit]

    Skalathrax

    [edit]

    Once inside the Eye of Terror the Legion took a Daemon World that Khorne had made into a realm of constant battle. Its unity ended in the Legion Wars at the Battle of Skalathrax, on a daemon world of storm-lashed cities of black rock and ice, where the World Eaters fought the Emperor's Children, the devotees of the rival power Slaanesh. City after city fell to the berserker assault, and the Legion halted only when the night fell. Khârn, who had been the primarch's equerry and was now the champion of Khorne, screamed with fury at the pause and demanded to be allowed to continue killing. He took up a flamer and turned it on the warriors of his own Legion, and cut down any who tried to stop him with his chainaxe.[2][3]

    As the fire spread through the city, the Legion tore itself apart. Khârn burned and hacked through the ruins and became, according to those who saw him, the living incarnation of the Blood God. The captains who had led the Legion before the battle were all killed, so that in future no warband leader could claim command by his old rank rather than by his martial ability, which was the way of Khorne. The Legion never recovered as a unified force. It broke into warbands, each seeking skulls, and Khârn, who had earned the name of the Betrayer, has wandered the Eye of Terror ever since.[3]

    The Red Angel in the Imperium

    [edit]

    Angron's appearances in the material universe in the following millennia were few and are well recorded. In the middle of M38 he overran some seventy sectors in two hundred years, at the head of about fifty thousand Chaos Space Marines and other forces. The Imperium replied with a crusade of four Chapters, two Titan Legions and more than thirty regiments of the Astra Militarum, which recovered ninety per cent of the lost territory. Imperial archives call the war the Dominion of Fire.[3][5]

    In 444.M41 his space hulk, the Devourer of Stars, emerged from the Warp above the hive world of Armageddon, where he led an army of cultists, daemons and World Eaters in the First War of Armageddon. The defenders held, and Angron was banished, though not before the Grey Knights had been called to the world.[5]

    The Thirteenth Black Crusade

    [edit]

    In the 13th Black Crusade, in 999.M41, the Imperium's records list Khârn among the most savage of the champions who followed the Warmaster, though the accounts of his conduct are confused and contradict one another. The records of the Legion's other warbands in the war are scattered and hold little more than the names of the worlds that they ravaged.[4]

    Era Indomitus

    [edit]

    The Great Rift has made the World Eaters a greater danger than at any time since the Great Crusade. In the days of the Noctis Aeterna, reinforcements were cut off from both sides in the Third War for Armageddon, and the hive world was overrun by the daemons of Tzeentch and Khorne, who fought each other in the Great Game. The Imperial relief force, with elements of nine Chapters under the Salamanders, arrived to find half the planet changed into a daemon world, and succeeded in halting a ritual that would have brought Angron back to it.[4]

    The ritual failed on Armageddon, but Angron has returned elsewhere. The Inquisition holds that he can no longer be truly banished to the Warp for long: voidship-class weapons only slow him while he re-forms. The Ordo Malleus believes that the cause is the quantity of Warp energy that pours from the Rift. If he is cast back he returns, unharmed, exactly eight weeks, eight days and eight hours later, heralded by eight crimson omens. The accounts of the Arks of Omen are the best-recorded. Angron emerged on the bridge of the Conqueror over the world of Kalkin's Tribune, killed the four lieutenants of the Chaos Lord who had been using him, and destroyed its garrison and a fleet of two thousand warships in a few weeks. He was drawn to the Imperium Nihilus world of Malakbael by the Choral Engine, a psychic beacon of the same kind as the Astronomican, and destroyed it, releasing a discharge that turned an Imperial fleet to berserkers. At Idolatros, a fragment of the ruined world of Caliban, he defeated the Blood Angels and their Chapter Master Dante, and was then confronted by the Lion, who had lately woken beneath the Rock. The duel ended with a sword through the throat and the removal of the primarch's head, and Angron was banished.[4][5]

    The warbands follow his lead and have gathered around him as they have not done since the Heresy. They differ in their methods. Gladiator Cadre 331 still wears the blue and white of the Great Crusade Legion, the Voidbutchers fight aboard ships, and the Bloodstalkers, who are derided by the other warbands, are patient and rely on gunnery, reasoning that a warrior who lives longer claims more skulls. The 66th Armoured Company have become one with their vehicles. The champions are of the same variety: the Eightbound have bound eight daemons of Khorne into their souls; Lord Invocatus is a fast raider; and the Daemon Prince Vorrakh leads a host of berserkers.[4]

    Organisation

    [edit]

    The Legion before the Heresy was organised in companies. Its Assault companies, led by captains such as Khârn of the Eighth, were its strength, and its Terminator-armoured veterans and its fleet were the reserve of the Great Crusade's commands. Like its brothers, it had a Librarius, which was disbanded under the Edicts of Nikaea.[1][3]

    The Legion today has no order of battle. Its warbands, of which the Imperium has recorded many dozens, among them Angron's Fury, Black Feast, Endless Murder, Skullhunt and the Wrathful Dead, are led by the champion who is strongest, until he is killed by a stronger one. The core of each is the Berzerker, a Chaos Space Marine fitted with the Nails who fights in frenzy with a chainaxe, and the Legion's veteran Terminators, the Red Butchers, are the elite. The Legion also fields its own vehicles, among them Land Raiders, Defilers and the Lord of Skulls, a titanic engine of Khorne, and is supported by Chaos Cultists and by daemons of the Blood God.[3][4]

    Beliefs and combat doctrine

    [edit]

    The Legion's creed is the creed of the arena: that a warrior is worth what he can kill, and that there is no worth in anything else. Its warriors score the record of their victories on their bodies in a rope of scars, the Triumph Rope, which they adopted from Nuceria, wear chains in memory of slavery, and take the heads of those they kill as tokens of their victories. Since the Heresy, the creed has become a worship of Khorne. The World Eaters do not draw a distinction between the blood of an enemy and that of a comrade, since all blood is the god's. The Butcher's Nails, which they believe a gift, deliver them into the rage that the god demands.[3][5]

    In battle the Legion charges. It prefers to close with its enemy and kill with the chainaxe, with the least possible regard to its losses, and the great majority of its warriors count a battle in which they were not at the front as a battle lost. Some warbands have learned patience, but even these seek the moment of contact, and the Legion has always been ready to take the worst of a campaign: it has been said that no Legion was faster to destroy an enemy and none more willing to be destroyed.[3]

    Gene-seed

    [edit]

    The gene-seed of the World Eaters is a strong one, and until the Heresy its only flaw was a high tolerance for aggression. The Butcher's Nails, which the Legion's Apothecaries fit to every initiate, work on the brain stem, and over time the warrior's reason is lost to rage. The Legion's veterans are seldom whole in mind. After the Heresy the corruption of Khorne added the changes that follow his favour: enlarged musculature, horns and thickened bone.[6][3]

    Heraldry

    [edit]

    The Legion's Great Crusade badge was a red hound. After the coming of Angron it became a world held in a mouth of teeth, which is still the Legion's mark and which is set on the eight-pointed star of Chaos. In the Great Crusade the Legion's armour was blue and white, and since the Heresy it has been red, with brass and bone-white trim. Its warbands paint the names of their gods and the tallies of their victims on their armour in blood.[7]
