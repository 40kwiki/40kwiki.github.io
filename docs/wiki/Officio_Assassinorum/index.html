    The Officio Assassinorum is the secret order of assassins of the Imperium of Mankind. It is a department of the Administratum in name and of the High Lords of Terra in practice. Its operatives, the Imperial Assassins, are the hidden blades of the Imperium, sent out singly to kill a ruler, a rebel, a heretic or a monster when the cost of using an army would be too high or the target is beyond the reach of one. They are the hand of last resort. Few know the names of their temples, and fewer have seen them.[1]

    An Assassin is a lone killer, trained for a decade on Terra and fitted with the most advanced augmentations the Imperium can supply. Each belongs to a temple, a school of killing that teaches one art: the sniper's, the infiltrator's, the berserker's or the psychic null's. The order is held in check by the Imperium's fear of its own weapon. An Assassin cannot be sent out without the vote of the High Lords, his record is open to the Inquisition, and, apart from the Eversor, he undergoes regular psycho-indoctrination to keep him loyal.[1]

    The Officio has been in existence since the first years of the Great Crusade (c. 799.M30). In ten thousand years it has killed primarchs, daemon princes, xenos warlords and governors, and it has turned on the Imperium at least once. It keeps scrupulous records of every killing, and the failures in its history are very few.[1]

    History

    [edit]

    Founding in the Great Crusade

    [edit]

    The Officio's own tradition traces its origin to the declaration the Emperor made on the summit of Mount Vengeance, on the world of Thor, in the early years of the Great Crusade: that no world would be beyond his rule and no enemy beyond his wrath. A number of the Emperor's servants who were skilled in stealth and killing heard it and resolved to give the Emperor what his armies could not. They moved unseen after the Crusade, making sure that rulers newly brought into Compliance kept their word, that newly installed governors remained loyal and that treaties were enforced. They shunned the Emperor's praise.[1]

    In time they understood that they would die, and that their skills would be lost. They revealed themselves to the Emperor, who is said to have regarded their existence with sorrow and to have accepted its necessity. He ordered temples built and the most promising youths found to train there. The six clades, Callidus, Venenum, Culexus, Vindicare, Eversor and Vanus, were established on Terra, each under a director primus. A seventh, the Clade Adamus, which specialised in the decapitation strike and the use of the sword, is recorded in the Great Crusade and the Heresy, and it is uncertain whether it survives.[1]

    The Officio was created by Malcador the Sigillite, the Regent of Terra, who became its first Grand Master of Assassins and gave it its charter on the Emperor's order. The clades answered to Malcador and carried out the Crusade's secret business in places where a Legion would have been too visible or too costly.[2]

    The Horus Heresy

    [edit]
    Further information: Horus Heresy

    The clades came into their own in the Heresy. When the Emperor learned of Horus's treachery, the Officio was ordered to destroy him. An Execution Force of four assassins, one from each of the major clades, was sent against the Warmaster. All four failed, for Horus was beyond the reach of mortal men and his fate lay elsewhere.[2]

    The Callidus Temple was more successful in a different mission. Konrad Curze, the primarch of the Night Lords, had been tormented since his youth by visions of his own death at the hands of an assassin sent by his father. When he rebelled and became the traitor Night Haunter, the Callidus assassin M'Shen was sent to kill him. When she entered his throne room she found him waiting. Curze made no attempt to resist, and is said to have regarded his death as proof that the Emperor was as callous as he had always claimed.[2]

    The clade Vanus worked less visibly. An infocyte of that clade, Fon Tariel, is recorded as writing in the middle of the war that "the cleanest kill is one that another performs in your stead with no knowledge of your incitement."[1]

    The Beheading

    [edit]
    Main article: Beheading

    In the middle of the 32nd millennium, after the War of the Beast, the Officio turned on its masters. The Grand Master of Assassins, Drakan Vangorich, had spent decades planning a coup. When he struck, the High Lords of Terra were murdered to a man, and the Imperium entered a century of anarchy known as the Beheading. A Space Marine force finally brought Vangorich and his followers to justice, and more than fifty Space Marine leaders came to Terra to supervise the appointment of twelve new High Lords.[1]

    The reforms of the Age of Apostasy

    [edit]

    The damage done by Vangorich was repeated in smaller form after the death of Goge Vandire. In the Wars of Vindication that followed the end of the Reign of Blood a renegade Callidus assassin, Tziz Jarek, tried to kill the Grand Master of Assassins. His attack, and the horror it caused in the Imperial Palace, convinced the High Lords that the temples were too dangerous to keep together. They were scattered across the galaxy, so that, should one be corrupted by an alien or by Chaos, the others would not be.[1]

    A hidden ordo of the Inquisition, the Ordo Sicarius, was set to watch the Officio. A kill order now requires a two-thirds vote of the High Lords. All assassins, except the Eversor, are subject to regular psycho-indoctrination, and the Inquisition may examine their records.[1]

    The Era Indomitus

    [edit]
    Further information: Era Indomitus

    The Great Rift has made the work of the Officio both more difficult and more urgent. The assassins were sent in great numbers against Abaddon the Despoiler before his Thirteenth Black Crusade and failed to reach him. In the wars since the Rift opened the temples have been used more than at any time in memory, and the Callidus Temple in particular has been seen more often on the battlefields of the Imperium.[1][3]

    Organisation and training

    [edit]

    The Officio is headed by the Grand Master of Assassins, who commands the whole order and is appointed by the High Lords. Each temple has a director primus at its head, assisted by Lord Assassins. Four of the temples are major, and the others are small, secret or lost. The temples are in hiding on Terra, except the Eversor, which keeps its operatives in cryo-stasis on ships and stations spread across Imperial space.[1]

    The Officio recruits almost entirely from orphans, taken from the death worlds, feral worlds and hive cities of the Imperium and passed through a Schola Progenium. A few of the most dangerous are taken by figures who attend each Selection Day, and are never seen again by their fellows. The training that follows lasts about ten years. The recruits are starved, deprived of light and air, forced to fight one another in every conceivable condition and subjected to a long programme of surgery, drugs and conditioning. Neural nodes are stitched into the brain and acoustic surgery prepares the muscles for the punishment ahead. Most do not survive, and the temples sometimes find that none of a year's candidates are worth keeping.[1]

    When an Assassin is sent out, he must act alone. He must be able to cross the galaxy by any means, pilot a bike or a small ship, speak the languages of the places he visits, and know the Imperium's history, institutions and the anatomy of its enemies. His mission is set by the High Lords, and he returns afterward to be debriefed and examined before being given a new mark.[1]

    The temples

    [edit]

    Vindicare

    [edit]

    The Vindicare Temple trains marksmen. A Vindicare can strike a target through the eye at extreme range, and will wait for weeks in a perfect firing position before taking the shot. He slows his metabolism almost to hibernation, his heart beating once in a minute, and wakes on the instant the target appears. The temple's speciality is the kill that is visible to all the target's followers, a demonstration of the Emperor's reach.[1]

    Callidus

    [edit]

    The Callidus Temple teaches infiltration. Its assassins use the drug polymorphine to change their appearance to that of any humanoid, and they take the places of aides, advisers and servants close to their targets. They may spend weeks or months in a role before they strike. They are used where overt action by the Imperium would upset the balance of power that the High Lords maintain, and they are sometimes asked to do more than kill: to influence a strategy, to steal a person or to throw a rebellion into confusion.[1]

    Culexus

    [edit]

    The Culexus Temple is the most feared. Its operatives are bearers of the Pariah Gene, a rare defect that leaves them with no presence in the Warp. A Culexus has no soul that the creatures of the Immaterium can see, and psykers and daemons suffer terribly in his presence. The temple teaches him to emit negative psychic force that can destroy a psyker's mind. Culexus assassins are used against rogue psykers, warlocks and daemons. The Aeldari describe them as pure evil, and the Necrons are reported to have made servants of their own out of them.[1]

    Eversor

    [edit]

    The Eversor Temple trains shock assassins. An Eversor is a berserker, driven into a killing frenzy by drugs and neural implants, and he is used where the aim is not one death but a rebellion's collapse. He cuts a path of destruction through an enemy force, destroying its leaders and sowing terror among the survivors, and he is rarely sent to a target of one. Eversors are held in stasis between missions and are not subject to the psycho-indoctrination of the other temples.[1]

    Other temples

    [edit]
    • The Venenum Temple trains poisoners. Its operatives sow delayed toxins that leave no trace.
    • The Vanus Temple deals in information and manipulation. Its assassins arrange for their target to be destroyed by his own folly or by a trusted friend, and are used when exposing a prominent heresy would do more harm than the death.
    • The Clade Adamus were swordsmen who specialised in decapitation strikes against an enemy's command. Whether it survives is not known.
    • The Maerorus Temple was created in the early 40th millennium to kill many targets at once, using illegal hybrids made with alien biology. It was destroyed after its one successful assassin, Legienstrasse, went rogue. She was slain on Opis by a force of the Imperial Fists under Captain Darnath Lysander.

    Notable missions

    [edit]
    • The Horus Heresy (005–014.M31). The Execution Force failed against Horus. The Callidus Temple killed Konrad Curze.
    • A Ghost Amongst Devils (990.M32). A Culexus assassin walked unseen through the ranks of a Chaos Sorcerer's daemons and burned out his brain.
    • The Cull of the Goliaths (372.M35). Six Eversor assassins were sent in error against the abhuman Goliaths of Thugrock Secundus and killed all of them. The Astra Militarum was displeased, and the incident is quoted as an argument against sending more than one Assassin.
    • The Stargazer's Demise (003.M38). A Vindicare assassin waited six days hanging upside down from a promethium pipe, then abandoned his first position, climbed a hive's highest spire and shot a heretical astronomer through the lens of his telescope.
    • Statuesque (243.M39). A Vindicare spent six years in the eye socket of a colossal statue waiting for a Drukhari pilot called the Skyknife, and killed him within half a second of his appearance.
    • The Tigers Caged (231.M41). A Callidus assassin impersonated the astropath of a rebel colonel and engineered the destruction of the entire regiment's command.
    • The Foe Untouchable (c. 999.M41). The Officio sent its agents against Abaddon the Despoiler and his lieutenants as the Thirteenth Black Crusade began. Abaddon survived.
