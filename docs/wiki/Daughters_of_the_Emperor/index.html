    The Daughters of the Emperor were an order of warrior-women who lived in a convent on the agri world of San Leor in the 36th millennium, devoted to the worship of the Emperor of Mankind and to the discipline of arms. Early in his tyranny, the High Lord Goge Vandire tricked them into accepting him as the Emperor's chosen servant, renamed them the Brides of the Emperor and made them his bodyguard. At the end of the Reign of Blood, about 288.M36, they learned the truth and their leader, Alicia Dominica, beheaded Vandire.[1][2]

    After Vandire's fall they resumed their old name. Because the Decree Passive of 288.M36 forbade the Ecclesiarchy to keep "men under arms", and the Daughters were women, they could be kept as the church's own fighting force. They were divided between two new convents on Terra and Ophelia VII and became the Orders Militant of the Adepta Sororitas, the Sisters of Battle. Every Sister of Battle in the Imperium regards the Daughters as her spiritual ancestors.[1]

    Origins

    [edit]

    Little is known of the Daughters before Vandire. San Leor was an obscure agri world, and the convent appears in no surviving record before his visit. Its sisters lived ascetic lives of prayer and labour and trained in arms as an expression of devotion. They acknowledged no authority but that of the Emperor himself and kept their gates closed to all visitors.[1][a]

    The Brides of the Emperor

    [edit]

    San Leor

    [edit]

    Early in his rule, about 220.M36, Goge Vandire, who held the offices of Master of the Administratum and Ecclesiarch, learned of the convent from his agents and decided to make a state visit.[b] Thousands of his retainers marched into the towns of San Leor, and the procession was recorded by hundreds of servo-skulls and shown on every world that could receive it. When he reached the convent, the gatekeeper told him that the Daughters would open their gates to no one but the Emperor.[1]

    Vandire replied calmly that he would prove that he spoke with the Emperor's authority. Admitted with a small escort, he told the sisters that the Emperor's grace protected him, and ordered one of his own guards to shoot him. The guard fired at his heart, and Vandire vanished in a blaze of light and stood unharmed. The Daughters knelt. In truth the shot had been turned aside by a Rosarius, a protective field generator concealed in an ornamental medallion, a device the sisters had never seen. They swore themselves to his service and, at his command, took the name Brides of the Emperor.[1]

    Bodyguard of the tyrant

    [edit]

    Vandire brought the Brides to Terra as his bodyguard, attendants and executioners. Together with the Frateris Templar they enforced his rule. In the last years of the Reign of Blood, as Vandire became increasingly fearful, the Brides were the only people he trusted: they tasted his food, guarded his chambers and carried out his sentences.[2]

    The fall of Vandire

    [edit]
    See also: Goge Vandire

    When the preacher Sebastian Thor raised the Confederation of Light against Vandire, the Ecclesiarchal Palace on Terra was besieged by Imperial forces, including the Adeptus Mechanicus and several Chapters of Space Marines. The Brides defended the palace and drove back the first assaults.[2]

    The deadlock was broken by the Adeptus Custodes. A party of Custodians came by secret ways into the palace and met the Brides. The Daughters had sworn to obey only the Emperor, and the Custodians took Alicia Dominica and five of her sisters into the presence of the Emperor on the Golden Throne. What passed there is not recorded. When they returned, Dominica went to Vandire's chambers with her sisters, pronounced sentence on him for his crimes and beheaded him with a single stroke of her power sword, which also cut in two the Rosarius that had deceived them on San Leor. The Reign of Blood was over.[2][1]

    The five sisters who went before the Throne with Dominica were Silvana, Mina, Lucia, Katherine and Arabella. With Dominica they are venerated as the six founding saints of the Orders Militant.[1][c]

    Legacy

    [edit]

    The Adepta Sororitas

    [edit]
    Main article: Adepta Sororitas

    After Vandire's death the Brides renounced the name he had given them and became again the Daughters of the Emperor. About 4,000 survived. Under the reformed Ecclesiarch Sebastian Thor, they were divided between two new convents: the Convent Prioris on Terra and the Convent Sanctorum on Ophelia VII. The sisters still at San Leor were transferred to them. The Decree Passive of 288.M36 forbade the Ecclesiarchy to keep men under arms, and so the Daughters were made into the Orders Militant of the new Adepta Sororitas, which also took on the Ecclesiarchy's work of healing, teaching and record-keeping.[1]

    Alicia Dominica led the Sisterhood in its first wars of faith and founded the Order of the Ebon Chalice. She was killed in battle on Frideswide's World in 650.M36, by the Ecclesiarchy's reckoning. The orders founded in memory of her companions include the Order of Our Martyred Lady, of Saint Katherine; the Valorous Heart, of Saint Lucia; and the Argent Shroud, of Saint Silvana. The Orders of the Bloody Rose and of the Sacred Rose were founded in mid-M38 in honour of Saint Mina and Saint Arabella.[1]

    San Leor

    [edit]

    San Leor remains a place of pilgrimage for the Adepta Sororitas. In 799.M41 a strike force of the Red Corsairs attacked the world and was destroyed by a counter-attack of nine Orders Militant.[1]

    Notes

    [edit]
    1. ^ The date of the convent's founding is unknown. Ecclesiarchy tradition holds that it was very old. The estimate of M35 is based on the state of the convent buildings described by Vandire's chroniclers.
    2. ^ The visit is dated to "the early years of the Reign of Blood". The year given is an estimate from the sequence of events, between Vandire's assumption of power and the birth of Sebastian Thor in c. 238.M36.
    3. ^ Some older Ecclesiarchy accounts give the number of sisters who entered the Throne Room as seven. The tradition of six founding saints is now the most widely held.
