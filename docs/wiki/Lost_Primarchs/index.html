    The Lost Primarchs are the two primarchs of the II Legion and the XI Legion, the Second and Eleventh Legions of the Space Marine Legions. They were among the twenty sons the Emperor of Mankind created for the Great Crusade. Both were found during the Crusade, and both were removed from every Imperial archive, with their Legions, before the Horus Heresy. Neither has a name in any record that the Imperium makes available. The Imperium's own word for the removal is the Excision, and its scholars call the two primarchs the forgotten and the purged.[1]

    The reasons are sealed. The Imperium has treated the matter as one of the most severe punishments ever carried out in its history, because it did not merely condemn two Legions and their commanders but also erased their memory. Imperial historians call it the most complete of all Edicts of Obliteration. The Investiary in the Imperial Palace keeps two empty plinths where statues of the two primarchs once stood, and these are the only physical trace of them that remains.[1]

    Almost everything written about the two primarchs is rumour, inference or the testimony of other primarchs who spoke of them in passing. This article sets out what the Imperium says openly, what it has recorded in sealed or fragmentary sources, and what scholars have concluded from them. Each claim is marked as attested, disputed or rumour.[1]

    Attested record

    [edit]

    The Great Crusade

    [edit]
    Further information: Great Crusade

    The two primarchs were found at dates that the rolls redact: the II primarch between 819.M30 and 824.M30, by his place between Leman Russ and Ferrus Manus, and the XI primarch (c. 927.M30) after Corvus Corax in 922.M30 and before Alpharius Omegon in 981.M30. The chronology of the Great Crusade gives the finding of each and then notes that the records were expunged. The annotation is undated. Both Legions were raised in the First Founding, and both were led by Legion Masters until their primarchs were found.[2]

    Only one document is known to name the two Legions in combat. A copy of the Imperial Infantryman's Uplifting Primer, revised in 990.M41, contains a misplaced section which states that the II and XI Legions took part in the Rangdan Xenocides of the 860s.M30. The section does not say what they did.[3]

    The Investiary

    [edit]

    The Investiary was an open amphitheatre in the Imperial Palace, two kilometres across, in which the statues of the twenty primarchs once stood on ouslite plinths in a silent ring. Twenty plinths are said to have been set out. Two of them were empty for as long as anyone who wrote about the Investiary could remember.[4]

    The Imperial Fists' account of the Investiary records that the empty plinths were a matter that First Captain Sigismund later raised with his father, Rogal Dorn. The account gives no reply that can be verified.[4]

    During the opening months of the Heresy, agents of the Alpha Legion are recorded as having entered the Investiary and destroyed the statues of the primarchs who had turned to Chaos. The statues of Alpharius and of Dorn were spared. The Alpha Legion's agents are said to have left the nine statues of the Traitor primarchs covered rather than destroyed, which some read as a message. The two empty plinths were not touched.[5]

    The Edict

    [edit]

    The removal was carried out under an Edict of Obliteration, the Imperial policy of destroying the records, images and monuments of a person declared Excommunicate Traitoris. The Edict is sealed. The Imperium's records say only that the Legions and their primarchs were "deleted from Imperial records" and that the act was taken by the Emperor of Mankind and his regent. The regent is most likely Malcador the Sigillite.[6]

    The phrase Excommunicate Traitoris does not appear in any surviving copy of the Edict. It is attested only in a sealed Inquisitorial note, which says that two black scrolls were delivered to the homeworld of the two Legions, one for each.[1]

    Statements from other primarchs

    [edit]

    The most useful sources on the two primarchs are the statements of others who knew, or were thought to know, something of them. None of these was written to be an official record, and each is cited here as a reported remark.[1]

    • The Emperor and Corvus Corax. In 922.M30, when the Emperor met his son Corax on Lycaeus, He told him that he had seventeen brothers, and that Corax had been the nineteenth. Asked about the other two, the Emperor said that this was a conversation for another day.[7]
    • Jaghatai Khan. The White Scars' primarch, reflecting on the power held by the primarchs, counted "eighteen brothers" rather than twenty.[1]
    • Roboute Guilliman. In Macragge Civitas, the Ultramarines' chamber of twenty-one chairs has two chairs whose pennants are left plain. Guilliman told Lion El'Jonson that the absence must be marked, and that places must be left for them.[8]
    • Magnus the Red and Mortarion. At the Triumph of Ullanor in 000.M31, Magnus remarked that nine of the Emperor's sons had gathered on one world. Mortarion answered that the Emperor had forbidden them to speak of the matter.[9]
    • Vulkan. Before the Heresy, Vulkan told his equerry that he feared "another empty pillar in the great investiary", and that he did not want to add to the grief for two brothers.[9]
    • Sanguinius. In a recorded exchange with Horus, Sanguinius refused to ask the Emperor to intervene in his own Legion's genetic crisis. He said that he would not have a third empty plinth as his Legion's only memorial. Some scholars read this as evidence that one of the lost Legions had a similar flaw.[9]

    Rumours and speculation

    [edit]

    The following are rumours or speculative readings. Imperial accounts differ on each, and none has been confirmed.

    • Absorption into the Ultramarines. A long-standing rumour holds that the warriors of the two Legions were drafted into other Legions. The Ultramarines are most often named, since their numbers are said to have grown sharply in the years of the Excision. Word Bearers captives have made the same claim, and the Inquisition has neither confirmed nor refuted it.[6]
    • The Blood Ravens. Some scholars propose that the II primarch founded the Blood Ravens. The theory has little support, and it does not explain why the Chapter would survive when its supposed ancestors were erased so completely.[8]
    • The Dark Angels' Inner Circle. The Inner Circle of the Dark Angels is known to keep a secret about the origins of the Chapter. Some inquisitors believe that the secret concerns one of the two primarchs, but the Dark Angels deny it, and the Inner Circle's members do not discuss their knowledge with outsiders.[1]
    • The Sisters of Silence and the Custodes. Some Imperial accounts name the Adeptus Custodes or the Sisters of Silence as the agents of the Excision. No sealed record confirms this, and the claim may have been invented to explain the Investiary's silence.[6]
    • The Hall of Victory. It is said that the Legion halls on Terra, and a hall of victory in the Palace, hold statues of the two primarchs whose faces are blank. No statue of this kind has been examined by an Imperial scholar in the open record.[5]
    • A Lord of the Lost. A speculative Ordo Hereticus note suggests that one of the two primarchs is the figure the Chaos cults call the Lord of the Lost. Nothing links the title to either primarch with confidence.[1]
    • The split masks. In the Iron Hands' records of a subterranean search, a warrior finds twenty statues of the primarchs, with their faces covered. Two of the masks are split and nearly destroyed. The Iron Hands' archive does not say which primarchs these are, and some read it as a clue to the two lost ones.[9]
    • Survival. A minority of inquisitors believe that the two primarchs survived the Excision in hiding, and that they may appear among the Traitor commanders. The Inquisition has no evidence for this.[5]

    Legacy

    [edit]

    The two empty plinths of the Investiary remain the most visible reminder of the Excision. Many of the Imperium's historians regard the Excision as a warning about the danger that a Legion can pose to the Imperium, and the Adeptus Astartes teaches that the Legions were strongest when united under one hand.[1]
