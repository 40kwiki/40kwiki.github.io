    Daemonhost
    A newly bound Daemonhost in its warded circle
    Artwork © Games Workshop
    NatureA Daemon bound into a living human body
    CreatorsChaos cults, sorcerers, and radical Inquisitors of the Ordo Malleus
    BindingWards inscribed in the skin, chains, locks and purity needles
    Levels of bindingOnce-, twice- and thrice-bound; the greater, the weaker the Daemon
    StatusHeresy of the highest order outside the Inquisition
    Notable examplesCherubael, Hedrodal, Kapeth-Shem, Suvfaeras, the Burning One, Karnak Zul

    A Daemonhost is a Daemon of Chaos bound by sorcerous rite into a living human, or another mortal, and enslaved to the will of its maker. The host's soul is condemned to torment, and the Daemon gains a lasting home in the material universe.[1]

    The practice is the work of Chaos cults and of mad students of forbidden lore. It is also, as the Inquisition's own records admit, copied by a few radical members of the Inquisition, who hope to use the creatures against the Imperium's enemies.[2]

    Nature

    [edit]

    A Daemon can force itself into the Materium only with difficulty, through weakened barriers between the Warp and realspace, and only for a short time. Possession is easier but is also short unless ceremonies prolong it. A Daemonhost is the form in which a Daemon is dragged out of the Warp and held in the flesh for long periods.[1]

    Even the most tightly leashed is stronger and tougher than a human and has psychic power. It also holds great knowledge of the Immaterium and can see into mortal souls. Normally the bound Daemon is on the level of a Herald or Daemon Prince. The bindings also fetter its power, so a Lesser Daemon is seldom worth the risk, while a Greater Daemon, even bound, is formidably powerful. The maker faces a choice: the tighter the binding, the weaker the Daemon, and the looser, the nearer its escape.[1]

    Some hosts volunteer, as members of cults who give their bodies to the powers they worship. Others are captives who wake with a Daemon in the mind, which drives most insane. An inquisitor may bind one as a weapon, believing only a Warp creature can match another, or in order to question it.[3]

    Creation

    [edit]

    The knowledge is rare and in a few very old tomes, some perhaps written when the Emperor still walked on Terra. Outside the Inquisition, even knowing of Daemonhosts can be a capital crime. Nearly every rite requires a living human victim.[2]

    The summoners must know the Daemon by name, its patron among the Ruinous Powers or lack of one, and its past deeds. Many bind a vanquished enemy, for revenge. The wards are written on the skin, often by tattoo or scarring so they cannot be smeared, and chains, locks, purity needles and sigils of authority are added. The work takes at least a full solar day. The chance of flaws depends on the skill of the summoners. For every degree of failure, one level of binding is lost, and all who work on it share in the corruption.[2]

    The summoner then calls the Daemon within a circle of sanctified salt and blood, usually with a live sacrifice, and fights it in a battle of will. If the summoner loses, the Daemon returns to the Warp, swearing vengeance. If the summoner wins, the Daemon is flung into the host. An unbound Daemonhost lashes out at its maker and anyone near.[2]

    Service and treachery

    [edit]

    A bound Daemonhost cannot directly harm its master. It obeys simple orders such as "protect me," but a specific order sets the wills against each other again. If the master wins, it obeys to the letter. A merciful master finds it reading the order creatively, to his harm.[2]

    No Daemonhost is a friend. It bends commands as far as it can without breaking them. Ordered to kill one man unseen, it may also kill everyone else in the building in a way that draws the authorities. All are among the most dangerous and fickle allies a cult or radical inquisitor can have.[2]

    Notable Daemonhosts

    [edit]
    • Cherubael: once a Daemon Prince worshipped as a god on the Feral world of Clanar II. Inquisitor Quixos enslaved it in the body of a warrior and it served him for many solar decades before being banished by other inquisitors.[1]
    • Hedrodal, the "Twice-bound Daemonhost": made by the false prophets of the Pilgrims of Hayte in the body of the fallen interrogator Cripon.[1]
    • Kapeth-Shem: made by Inquisitor Selech of the Ordo Malleus from his loyal guardsman Kapeth, and it tricked him.[1]
    • The Burning One: one of the most feared in the Calixis Sector, made by the radical Inquisitor Kal Xorn to store forbidden lore outside his retinue's minds.[1]
    • Karnak Zul: bound by Inquisitor Renthor and used for centuries to hunt heretics, until it plotted its revenge.[1]

    The records give no years for these cases.[a]
