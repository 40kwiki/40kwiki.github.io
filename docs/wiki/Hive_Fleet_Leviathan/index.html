    Hive Fleet Leviathan is the third and largest of the great Tyranid hive fleets to have invaded the galaxy. It was first detected in 997.M41, approaching from below the galactic plane, and its arrival began the Third Tyrannic War. That war was checked by the Inquisitor Lord Fidus Kryptman, who diverted much of the fleet into the Ork empire of Octarius, and ended, by Imperial reckoning, with the Devastation of Baal at the opening of the Era Indomitus.[1][2]

    Leviathan was not destroyed. In the 42nd millennium its largest mass yet recorded rose into the western reaches of the Segmentum Pacificus in three great tendrils, named by the Ordo Xenos Nautilon, Promethor and Grendyllus, beginning the Fourth Tyrannic War. The new swarms have consumed the hive world of Oghram, besieged the Chapter world of the White Templars, and shown adaptations aimed specifically at the Imperium's defenders, including bioforms bred to hunt psykers. The Ordo Xenos believes that the fleet's course leads, in the end, to Terra.[3][4]

    Third Tyrannic War

    [edit]

    Arrival

    [edit]

    In 997.M41, less than five years after the defeat of Hive Fleet Kraken, Inquisitor Lord Kryptman of the Ordo Xenos detected signs of a new invasion. He ordered the census that bears his name, burning out dozens of astropaths to contact worlds on every fringe of the Imperium. From the replies, and the silences, he charted the new fleet's course. Unlike Behemoth and Kraken, which had come from the galactic east, Leviathan was rising from below the plane of the galaxy, in two widely separated arms that closed like jaws and cut off whole regions from Warp travel and astropathic contact.[1]

    The Imperium's first victory came at Tarsis Ultra, where planetary defence troops, two Astra Militarum regiments, the Ultramarines, the Mortifactors and a Deathwatch kill-team under Kryptman destroyed the arm of the fleet nearest to Terra. But the fleet's other arm carried on. Its greatest prize was the forge world of Gryphonne IV, home of the Legio Gryphonicus.[1]

    Kryptman's cordon and Octarius

    [edit]
    See also: Octarius

    To slow the advance while Battlefleets Solar and Tempestus gathered, Kryptman ordered a cordon. Every world in the fleet's path was to be evacuated where possible and then destroyed by Exterminatus, so that the Tyranids would find no biomass. It was the worst act of self-inflicted destruction by the Imperium since the Horus Heresy. When migrating Orks occupied a score of the emptied worlds, Kryptman was denounced as a radical and expelled from the Inquisition.[1]

    He was not finished. With the help of loyal officers of the Deathwatch, he captured a brood of genestealers in stasis from a drifting space hulk and released them in the Ork empire of Octarius, close to Leviathan's line of advance. The crowded Ork worlds drew in almost the whole of the hive fleet, and the two races have fought each other since in the Octarius War. The Ordo Xenos warns that the Tyranids will absorb the Orks' hardy genetic stock, and that the victor will be more dangerous than either.[1]

    The Shield of Baal and the Devastation

    [edit]

    Another tendril, the largest then recorded, turned toward the Baal System, home of the Blood Angels. It broke through the Cryptus shield worlds and descended on Baal and its moons. The Blood Angels and their successors, the Sanguinary Brotherhood, held against nineteen successive waves before retreating into the ruins of their fortress-monastery for a last stand. Then the Great Rift opened. The Warp storms swallowed much of the encircling fleet, an Indomitus Crusade fleet under Roboute Guilliman arrived, and the daemon Ka'Bandha destroyed the remaining swarms in orbit, leaving their skulls piled in the shape of the mark of Khorne on Baal Prime. The Imperium counts this as the end of the Third Tyrannic War.[2]

    Fourth Tyrannic War

    [edit]

    The new invasion

    [edit]

    While the forces of the Imperium were engaged with the Rift and the Indomitus Crusade, the Hive Mind changed its approach. The largest mass of Leviathan yet encountered, greater than the swarm that had reached Baal, rose from beneath the galactic plane into the western reaches of the Segmentum Pacificus, the least defended region of the Imperium Sanctus. It came in three tendrils, named Nautilon, Promethor and Grendyllus, two of them advancing in parallel toward the Segmentum Solar.[3]

    The bioforms of the new tendrils differ from those seen at Tarsis Ultra and Baal. Their flesh is a deeper purple, and Magi Biologis report that the Hive Mind has redesigned many of its standard strains in light of its earlier wars, with improvements against the weapons and tactics of the Adeptus Astartes. New organisms have been recorded, among them the Psychophage, which seeks out and devours psykers, and the winged Tyranid Prime called the Terror of Vardenghast, which led a swarm that the Ordo Xenos classed as an alpha-level threat after battles along the Vardenghast Line.[3][5]

    The Bastior war zone and Formidyre

    [edit]

    The heaviest early fighting was in the Bastior Sub-sector, which controls a Warp route into the rest of the segmentum. In the Formidyre System the outer hive world of Oghram, home to billions, was struck by spore-falls that overwhelmed its garrison. The Adeptus Astartes launched a counter-offensive to save it, and for a time its fate hung in the balance. In the end the world fell, and its biomass was consumed until it was bare rock.[4][6]

    Hive Fleet Grendyllus then fell on Sanctum, the Chapter world of the White Templars. Its defence was shared between the Chapter Master Kruxor Stavro, who led every counter-attack, and Lord Solar Arcadian Leontus, who directed the defence from the fortress-monastery of Holdfast. The Imperial Fists brought their mobile fortress-monastery, the Phalanx, into orbit with a great Navy battlefleet, and Space Marine void squadrons fought a war of raids through the Dust Nebula to protect the astropathic beacon station of Vanmyre's Eyrie. The Tyranids broke on the walls of Holdfast.[4]

    Elsewhere in the sub-sector, in the Pyremoat System, a Genestealer Cult rose in a hive of Trygg as the fleet approached. Captain Sevastus Acheran of the Ultramarines Second Company and a task force of his company gave their lives to destroy it, and Demetrian Titus, recovered by the Chapter in the Recidious System, succeeded to command of the Second Company.[4]

    Solblades and the Larder Worlds

    [edit]

    The High Lords answered the invasion with a new kind of force. Rather than meet the swarms head-on, small elite strike fleets called Solblades were sent to strike at worlds behind the Tyranid advance and kill the synaptic organisms that direct the swarms. One of the first was led by First Captain Severus Agemman of the Ultramarines, with Terminators, Librarians, Sternguard and Apothecaries charged with taking samples of the new bioforms.[3]

    A Solblade under Epistolary Drakht of the Necropolis Hawks, following bioships back to their origin in the Othlor System, found the hive world of Shakar covered in a living membrane. Beneath it vast numbers of non-Tyranid creatures were held in induced stasis while bioships fed from the planet's biosphere. The Imperium calls such worlds Larder Worlds. The Solblade was driven off before it could learn more, and the purpose of the Larder Worlds is not known.[3]

    The Ultramarines' Tyrannic War Veterans, a formation raised by Chaplain Cassius after the Battle of Macragge in defiance of the Codex, received the Lord Commander's blessing, and other Chapters have raised their own Tyranid-hunting cadres on its model.[7]

    Course

    [edit]

    The Fourth Tyrannic War is still being fought. The Imperium has held Sanctum and slowed the advance, but has lost many worlds, and the Indomitus Crusade cannot spare the forces that the western front demands. The council of the Ordo Xenos called the Strategic Collective has long warned that the hive fleets met so far are parts of a much greater whole, and estimated that the Imperium would need to raise its mobilisation fivefold, drawing in every able person in three segmenta, to stop it. The scale of the new Leviathan has given that warning new weight.[3][1]

    Biology and appearance

    [edit]

    Leviathan's organisms are pale-skinned with purple carapaces and, in most strains, red weapon-organs. Like all Tyranids, the fleet learns from each war, absorbing the genetic material of its prey and reworking its bioforms in response to the tactics used against it. Its victories cost it nothing, since its dead are reabsorbed with the biomass of the worlds it consumes; its defeats only teach it.[5]
