    The Arks of Omen, also called the Arks of Omen Campaign, was a series of wars in the Imperium Nihilus in the early Era Indomitus, in which the Warmaster Abaddon the Despoiler and the independent daemon Vashtorr the Arkifane sent a fleet of daemon-wrought voidships, the Arks, to find the scattered fragments of an ancient artefact called the Key. According to a prophecy that Vashtorr believed, whoever reassembled the Key would be able to open the Lock and take up a weapon that could change the fate of the galaxy.[1]

    The campaign touched many parts of the dark half of the Imperium. It was fought against a Grey Knights detachment on Hyades, against the Dark Angels and the Blood Angels on the world of Wyrmwood, and against a T'au commander at the edge of the galaxy. It saw the return to the material universe of the Daemon Primarch Angron, who was banished again by the primarch Lion El'Jonson, who woke for the first time in ten thousand years. The Imperium prevented the Despoiler from destroying its forces. It did not prevent him from taking the fragments. At the end of the campaign, Vashtorr had the Key.[1][2]

    Background

    [edit]

    Vashtorr the Arkifane

    [edit]
    Main article: Vashtorr the Arkifane

    Vashtorr, called the Arkifane, the Master of the Soul Forges and the God in the Machine, is a greater daemon of Chaos Undivided. He claims no connection to the four Ruinous Powers. By his own account, and by that of the Imperial cryptoscholars, he came into being in the Warp out of the obsessive pursuit of technology by mortal minds, especially of inventions that had unexpected and terrible results. He is curiosity and invention without conscience, and he rules the Forge of Souls, a metaphysical foundry of dark iron and steam in which daemons are bound into war engines in exchange for a price in mortal souls. Soul Grinders, Calamity Engines and Infernal Bombards are its work. Unlike other daemons, Vashtorr does not lie. His contracts are explicit, and it is the nature of the daemons who sign them to be trapped, for every defeat adds to their debt. He is the arms dealer of the Great Game, and the powers of Chaos tolerate him because the engines of the Forge are useful, and because no god wishes to be the one to lose his allegiance.[3]

    Vashtorr has seldom appeared in the material universe. Imperial scholars have put his origins variously at some ancient galaxy-wide arms race, at the horrors of the Dark Age of Technology, and in the powers that grew in the Warp at the making of the Necrons and of the Primaris Space Marines. He is hated by Be'lakor, another independent daemon. In the Era Indomitus he has become far more active.[3]

    The Key and the Weapon

    [edit]

    Vashtorr learned in the long course of mortal lives that an artefact of great power had been broken into pieces and scattered across the galaxy. He could not tell what form the pieces took, but he knew that each had its own signature in the Warp, and that, if all were found, a ritual of Warp alchemy and inhuman ingenuity could restore them. The fragments varied in shape and size: ancient pillars, meteoric masses, banks of cogitators. To recover them he needed mortal help on a scale that his own cult could not supply.[3]

    He went to Abaddon, who commanded the Black Legion and many other forces of Chaos, and who had become the Warmaster of the Imperium Nihilus. The Despoiler was suspicious of him, as he was of all daemons, but accepted the alliance when Vashtorr told him that the Key could be used to destroy the Imperium and win the Long War. Abaddon, who had been planning for ten thousand years to win without depending on the gods, was persuaded. He told his followers only that, once refashioned, the Key would turn the servants of the Emperor into greater traitors than any Chaos Space Marine.[3][1]

    The weapon of which the Key is the means is called by the Chaos warbands the Weapon, and it opens, by their account, a lock. Vashtorr's own aim is more personal. He believes that the Key will raise him to become the fifth great god of Chaos. Imperial assessments agree that the Key's true nature is unknown.[3]

    The Arks

    [edit]

    The Arks were made by Vashtorr, with the help of the Hereteks of the Dark Mechanicum, from modified space hulks. Each is built around a wreck, fitted with daemonic machinery and surrounded by a fleet of ships called a Balefleet. Vashtorr gave them to Abaddon, who gave several of them to officers of the Black Legion and others to the Chaos lords who swore to aid him. They were designed to find the fragments and to destroy those who opposed their recovery. Abaddon's practice was to strike bargains with other warbands: a warband that completed a task for him received an Ark in reward.[3][1]

    Course of the campaign

    [edit]

    Kalkin's Tribune and Malakbael

    [edit]

    The first confirmed appearance of Angron, the Daemon Primarch of the World Eaters, in the material universe since the First War of Armageddon was in this campaign. He appeared on the bridge of his old flagship, the Conqueror, at the head of a World Eaters fleet under the Chaos Lord Kossolax the Foresworn, and his first act was to murder Kossolax's four chief lieutenants. Kossolax escaped. Angron led an army of Berzerkers and Khornate daemons onto the surface of Kalkin's Tribune and destroyed a garrison of billions and a fleet of two thousand warships in a matter of solar weeks. Little of his former mind remained. Kossolax and his warband, the Foresworn, hoped to use him to rally the scattered Legion and become its lord regent, but Angron proved hard to control.[1][4]

    He was drawn to an obscure world in the Imperium Nihilus called Malakbael by a psychic beacon similar to the Astronomican. A thirteen-strong party of Grey Knights under the Epistolary Graucis Telomane, who had seen the danger, set up a false beacon on Hyades with a captured psyker to draw him into a trap. Angron plunged into Hyades and massacred what he found, but the Grey Knights sacrificed themselves in a ritual that made a Warp explosion and destroyed the world and a great part of Kossolax's army. Angron survived, and was attacked again by the surviving Grey Knights, among them Graucis in a Dreadknight. The Grey Knights Paladin Dvorik used the primarch's True Name against him for a moment, and the ritual made a hole in the hull of the Conqueror, but Angron killed his enemies, though Graucis was able to project his soul into the Warp and escape the Daemon Prince.[4]

    At Malakbael itself, the beacon was the Choral Engine, an artefact able to act as a local Astronomican, powered by human psykers. It would have been of great value to the Imperium Nihilus, and the forces of Chaos had to destroy it. Angron, wounded, came to the world with eight Bloodthirsters as his honour guard and divided his forces into three pincers, in the shape of a trident. He fought through some sixty Grey Knights and the Tempestus Scions of Brother-Captain Drystann Cromm and reached the Engine. Khorne used him as a conduit into the material universe to destroy it, and the Engine released a wave of psychic force called the Murder-Curse. It corrupted nearly the whole of Fleet Quartus of the Indomitus Crusade, which was nearby, and turned its personnel into mindless Berzerkers. The fleet is now listed as Excommunicate Traitoris.[4][5]

    The Rock

    [edit]
    Main article: The Rock

    One fragment of the Key was held in the fortress-monastery of the Dark Angels, The Rock, where it had been hidden for ages. Abaddon and Vashtorr attacked it, and the Arkifane came in person with an army of the Cult of the Arkifane, the Cogs of Vashtorr. The attack failed, because the daemon Prince Be'lakor, who hates Vashtorr, interfered with it. The Dark Angels drove off the invaders, and, with the Unforgiven of their successor Chapters, pursued Vashtorr to his base.[1]

    Wyrmwood

    [edit]
    Main article: Wyrmwood

    In the Somnium Stars, the Dark Angels found Vashtorr's base: a rogue planet called Wyrmwood. It was a mobile world, made from a portion of the ruins of Caliban, the lost homeworld of the Legion, and warped by the powers of the Forge of Souls to a mockery of the original. Vashtorr had hidden the other fragments of the Key there. Abaddon and Vashtorr had lured the Dark Angels to the Somnium Stars, and the Chapter, under its Supreme Grand Master Azrael, landed on Wyrmwood and fought Chaos Space Marines and daemons on the surface.[1]

    The battle, called the Battle of Idolatros, went badly for the Imperials. World Eaters allied to Vashtorr, and cultists who had sacrificed themselves, summoned the Daemon Primarch Angron to the world, and he slaughtered the attackers. Reinforcements of the Blood Angels, led by their Lord Commander Dante, came to the field, and Angron mauled them. He defeated Dante, who was critically wounded, and he would have finished him.[1][4]

    The return of the Lion

    [edit]

    Before he could, Angron was confronted by Lion El'Jonson, the primarch of the Dark Angels, who had slept for ten thousand years in a hidden chamber at the heart of the Rock. The Lion had woken for the first time during this campaign, and he had a new power: he could move through the Warp at will. Before his return was known to the wider galaxy, a legend of him had spread among isolated worlds, where a hooded giant of the forests had come out of mist-wreathed passageways to cut down monsters. He was called the Cowled Giant, the Unforgiving Knight and, by mistake, the Emperor Incarnate. His coming was always heralded by an apparition of ancient forest fading from the air. It is thought that the dreams of the sleeping Lion carried him on the Warp to the places where he was needed, by the ghosts of the forests of Caliban.[6]

    The duel of the two primarchs was long. Angron's rage was unmatched, and the Lion bore the Emperor's Shield, an auramite shield that the Emperor himself once carried, and which turns the force of every blow back on the attacker. The Lion put his power sword Fealty through the Daemon Primarch's throat. As Angron lay in agony he cut off his head with the tip of the Shield. The Daemon Primarch was banished howling to the Warp.[4][6]

    The Imperial fleet bombarded Wyrmwood from orbit and destroyed the remaining Chaos forces on it, at a heavy cost in the lives of Space Marines. Wyrmwood broke up under the bombardment, and a tear in space was left where the planet had been.[1]

    Aftermath

    [edit]

    While the Imperials were occupied, Vashtorr went aboard the Rock in the confusion and took the fragment he had failed to seize before. Dark Angels records name it the Tuchulcha Engine. He escaped through the Warp with the fragments he held, and assembled them into the Key. In some summaries of the Inquisition the Key is called the Dissonance Engine. Abaddon's fleet and Vashtorr's were warped away. The Imperium does not know where the Lock is, and neither, so far as is known, do they.[1][2]

    The Imperial forces in the Imperium Nihilus were weakened. The Choral Engine, which might have restored communication, had been destroyed. Dante was gravely wounded, and the Imperial fleets of the dark half were drained at a time when the Tyranids were striking again at the west. The Lion took command of the Dark Angels in the field. The effect of his return on the authority of the Supreme Grand Master, and on the relations of the Dark Angels with the Lord Commander, is not discussed in the open.[6]

    Vashtorr has appeared since in the war of the Nephilim Sector, where he emerged from Wyrmwood's remains, according to some accounts, and tore a rift in the Pariah Nexus. See Pariah Nexus.[1]

    Notes on the other participants

    [edit]

    The campaign also involved Commander Farsight of the T'au Empire, who opposed Abaddon and Vashtorr. The records of this part of the campaign are few, and it is not recorded whether the T'au commander gained any of the fragments for himself.[1]
