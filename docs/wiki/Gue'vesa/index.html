    Gue'vesa
    Human auxiliaries of the T'au Empire
    Mark of the T'au Empire
    Artwork © Games Workshop
    Name"Human Helper" in the T'au tongue
    Origin745.M41, the withdrawal from the Damocles Gulf Crusade
    AllegianceT'au Empire
    CasteNone; outside the caste system
    Notable actionThe Taros Campaign (998.M41)
    Imperial statusTraitors and heretics; subject to Exterminatus

    The Gue'vesa ("Human Helpers" in the T'au tongue) are the humans who have chosen to join the T'au Empire. Some are descendants of Imperial soldiers left behind in T'au territory at the end of the Damocles Gulf Crusade, and others are citizens of Imperial worlds that the T'au have annexed or persuaded. Many serve in the Fire caste's cadres as auxiliaries, and they have shown that they will fight their fellow humans. The Ecclesiarchy holds that all Gue'vesa are heretics and that any association with xenos is a corruption, and the Inquisition treats any world on which Gue'vesa are found as lost.[1]

    Origin

    [edit]

    The T'au regard the absorption of Imperial worlds by agreement as the best means of defeating the Imperium. The Water caste sends missions to Imperial frontier worlds and offers them protection from Imperial retribution, trade without the Imperium's tithes, freedom of religion where it does not conflict with the Greater Good, and a place in a society in which every person is valued. The offer has been more successful than the Imperium likes to acknowledge.[1][2]

    The earliest Gue'vesa were Astra Militarum troops abandoned by the Imperial fleet when it withdrew from the Damocles Gulf in late 745.M41 to meet Hive Fleet Behemoth. Commander Farsight offered them the choice of the empire, and most accepted. They were allowed to keep their worship of the Emperor in a form purged of its xenophobia. Their children, raised in the empire, have in the Imperial view been wholly lost to it.[1]

    Status

    [edit]

    Gue'vesa live in the empire's western sept worlds and colonies, which border Imperial space, and some are found on T'au itself. They do not belong to a caste and it is rare for one to hold command in the Fire caste. They make their own tools and a copy of the lasgun, which they use to protect their colonies from raiders. The T'au show recordings of Gue'vesa at work to the population of Imperial worlds as a demonstration of the benefits of the Greater Good.[1]

    Notable actions

    [edit]

    In 998.M41, when the desert mining world of Taros was annexed by the empire, its Planetary Defence Force fought as Gue'vesa beside the T'au against the Imperial force sent to retake it, in the Taros Campaign. The Imperial regiments met a determined defence and suffered heavy losses, and Imperial commanders reported with alarm that the enemy used lasguns and Imperial vehicles.[3]

    Role in the T'au army

    [edit]

    Gue'vesa serve as light infantry and garrison troops, and Imperial scholars note that they are valued for their familiarity with Imperial tactics and equipment. They operate in cadres under a Fire caste officer, who is bound by the Code of Fire to protect them as he would his own caste. The T'au arm them with pulse weapons where they can, and with locally made lasguns where they cannot. Some are trained to pilot the empire's light aircraft. They are not given battle-suits.[1]

    The loyalty of the Gue'vesa is not uniform. The first generation, taken from the Astra Militarum, are reported to be more doubtful than their children, and Inquisitorial agents have used them as sources of information on conditions in the empire. The Ordo Xenos treats these reports with caution, because the Water caste is known to prepare such accounts for outsiders.[1]

    Imperial response

    [edit]

    The Imperium has ordered that Gue'vesa be killed or reported, and the Ecclesiarchy preaches against them. The Ordo Xenos observes that the order has not stopped the movement and that the Imperium's own neglect of its frontier worlds is a cause of it. Inquisitor reports describe Gue'vesa who still hold the aquila sacred and refuse the title of traitor.[2][4]
