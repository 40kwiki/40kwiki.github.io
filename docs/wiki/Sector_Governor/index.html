    A Sector Governor, also called a Sector Lord and addressed as Lord Sector, is an Imperial noble who serves as the governing Imperial Commander of an entire sector or sub-sector of Imperial space. Most are also the planetary governor of the sector capital or of its most important world. The duties of a Lord Sector resemble those of a planetary governor, but they extend over many star systems, or over several sub-sectors, and he is formally addressed as "Lord Sector" or as "Lord" followed by the name of the territory he rules, such as "Lord Calixis".[1]

    Sector governors come from a variety of backgrounds. Many belong to the nobility of their region of space, and some are drawn from the high prelates of the Ecclesiarchy, such as a Cardinal. The office is neither purely civil nor purely military, and it has no fixed term. Its holders are held to rule in the name of the Adeptus Terra and of the Emperor of Mankind, but the great distances of Imperial space leave them with a wide discretion.[1][2]

    Two holders are well recorded. Marius Hax is Lord Sector of the Calixis Sector, which he governs from the Lucid Palace on Scintilla, and Morst Bolifax, a cardinal of the Adeptus Ministorum, ruled the Veritus Sub-sector in the late 41st millennium and was named Imperial Commander in the Talledus War. Their records show the range of the office, from a ruler of unflinching order to a zealot whose severity helped to bring on the heresy he feared.[3][1]

    Office and authority

    [edit]

    Territory

    [edit]

    A sector is a division of Imperial space that forms one of the units of a Segmentum. A typical sector may cover seven million cubic light years, a cube almost two hundred light years on a side, though its size depends on local demand and on the density of the stars. Sectors are divided into a variable number of sub-sectors, each of which usually holds between two and eight star systems within a radius of about ten light years. The remaining space, which belongs to no sub-sector, is called Wilderness space and may contain nebulae, alien-held systems and unexplored or uninhabitable worlds.[4]

    A sector governor's real authority therefore reaches only the inhabited worlds that lie along the stable routes of the Warp. Beyond them lies the wilderness, where a governor has neither information nor power. The Imperium has an estimated million inhabited worlds, but the number of governors is smaller than that of worlds, since some governors rule an entire star system and some worlds have no governor at all.[4][2]

    Civil duties

    [edit]

    The governor's duties are those of any ruler in the Imperial system, enlarged to the scale of a sector. He sees that the tithes assessed by the Departmento Munitorum are levied and delivered, and he keeps the mutant and psyker populations of his worlds under control, so that the psykers can be sent to Terra in the Black Ships and the regiments and resources of each world's tithe reach their destination. He is expected to see that the planetary governors under him govern competently, and he decides disputes between them. The Imperium does not interfere with a governor who does these things, so far as the tithes are paid and the order of the Emperor is kept.[2][3]

    A governor also supplies the outward majesty of the Imperium. Marius Hax is said to be remote and austere, and he rarely holds public audiences, but his court at the Lucid Palace contains advisers and representatives from the worlds and institutions of the sector. The palace is hung with the banners of the noble houses of Scintilla, and the right to a banner on its dome is a distinction for which some will kill. The splendour is part of the office, for it reminds the sector that the Imperium exists.[3][5]

    Military affairs

    [edit]

    The Lord Sector is also the Imperial Commander of his domain, and he may call on the planetary militias of its worlds. Each sector also has a battlefleet of the Imperial Navy assigned to guard it, usually named for the sector, as Battlefleet Calixis is for the Calixis Sector. The fleet answers to its own admiral and not to the governor. When the forces of the sector cannot hold a threat, a governor may appeal to the governor of the segmentum for help, as Hax can request troops from the segmentum governor on Cypra Mundi. He is reluctant to do so, because the request implies that the sector cannot keep itself in order.[4][3]

    Relations with other bodies

    [edit]

    The sector governor is the most powerful man in his territory, but not the only power. The Inquisition keeps its own offices in the sector, and the Lord Inquisitor who directs them answers to no one but the High Lords of Terra, although he treats the Lord Sector with respect. The Adeptus Arbites, the Adeptus Mechanicus, the Ecclesiarchy and the Navy all have separate chains of command, and a governor who wishes to act against them must negotiate. In the Calixis Sector the Lord Inquisitor commands cabals of Inquisitors for special missions, and Hax does not dispute their authority.[5][3]

    History

    [edit]

    No record names the first sector governor or gives a date for the first appointment. During the Great Crusade (798.M30 – 000.M31), a world that was brought to compliance was set under an Imperial Commander who raised the militia to hold it, and the crusade fleets passed on. The sector as a formal unit and the lord who rules it belong to the structure that the Imperium built over the following millennia, in which the government of space was divided into segmenta, sectors, sub-sectors and worlds.[6][4]

    In the Imperium of the late 41st millennium the title is old, and in many sectors it is hereditary or customary within a noble house. Hax is recorded as descended from an ancient line of Terran stock, bred for leadership in the heartland of the Segmentum Solar. He was appointed to the governorship by the High Lords of Terra more than one hundred and fifty standard years before the records of his time were made, a date that can be estimated at c. 830.M41. At the time of the records he was over two hundred years old, though rejuvenat treatments kept him in the appearance of a man of fifty.[3][a]

    Where a sector has no suitable noble house, the title may pass to a prelate. The Ecclesiarchy has a long history of power in secular government, and the Age of Apostasy in the 36th millennium, when the Reign of Blood under Goge Vandire brought the Ecclesiarch to the head of the Imperium, is the best-known example of how far that power could reach. In the 41st millennium the cardinal Morst Bolifax ruled the Veritus Sub-sector as its lord and the Cardinal World of Benediction as its governor.[1]

    Notable holders

    [edit]

    Marius Hax

    [edit]
    Main article: Marius Hax

    Lord Sector of the Calixis Sector, a province in the Segmentum Obscurus on the northern edge of the known galaxy, next to the Halo Stars and the Eye of Terror. Hax is a tall man of granite features who wears plain dark robes. Popular opinion in the sector holds him to be an unsmiling hardliner, and rebels and cultists are the objects of his particular contempt, but he is a fair and even-handed ruler in his own estimation and in that of those who know him. He is said to sleep four hours a night and to review the daily fiscal and economic reports in person. He cares little for the fate of individual planets, including Scintilla, of which he is also the nominal planetary governor, and is concerned with tithes, psykers and rebels, and with the majesty of the Imperium.[3]

    Morst Bolifax

    [edit]
    See also: Talledus War

    Bolifax was a cardinal of the Adeptus Ministorum, governor of the Cardinal World of Benediction in the Talledus System, and the lord of the Veritus Sub-sector. He was a brutal traditionalist who ruled with an iron hand and took the deviations that had grown up in the faith of his domain for a threat. He began purges of subversive preachers and sent missionaries to cleanse the sub-sector. More than ten thousand apostates were crucified or stoned to death, and a dozen sub-cults were condemned as heretical by his decree. The sects that survived were infiltrated by demagogues who served the Dark Gods and the Word Bearers, who compared him to Goge Vandire and turned the population against him. When the Word Bearers attacked the Talledus System in the war that followed, Bolifax was given formal command of the Imperial defence as Imperial Commander but no real part in its strategic direction.[7]

    In the Era Indomitus

    [edit]

    The opening of the Great Rift in 999.M41 divided the Imperium into the Imperium Sanctus and the Imperium Nihilus, and cut many sectors off from the Throneworld. A governor in the Nihilus can no longer rely on the astropathic messages that once linked him to his segmentum, and he must govern without orders. The Talledus War, which began as the Word Bearers attacked the shrine worlds of the Talledus System in 999.M41, shows what can happen to a sub-sector when its lord is isolated and its faith divided.[7][2]
