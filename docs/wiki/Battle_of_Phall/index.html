    The Battle of Phall was a void engagement fought in 007.M31 between the fleet of the Iron Warriors Legion under Perturabo and an Imperial Fists squadron that was bound for Isstvan to reinforce the loyalists. It took place in the Phall system, on the margin of the Isstvan system, shortly after the Drop Site Massacre. The Imperial Fists fleet had been sent by Rogal Dorn as a Retribution Fleet. The Iron Warriors were ordered by Horus to destroy it.[1][2]

    The ambush went wrong because the fleet's commander, Captain Alexis Polux, had kept his ships at battle readiness for months. The Imperial Fists took heavy losses, but struck back, and Polux destroyed his own flagship with the boarders aboard. He then seized an Iron Warriors battle ship and fled in her through the Warp storms to the realm of Ultramar.[2]

    Background

    [edit]

    The Retribution Fleet

    [edit]

    When the Eisenstein reached the Imperial Fists' fortress-monastery Phalanx with Nathaniel Garro's report, Dorn was at first reluctant to believe it. The testimony of the Remembrancer Mersadie Oliton, who had escaped from Horus's flagship, convinced him. He wanted to go himself to bring his brother to account, but duty held him. He sent his fleet instead and returned to Terra with his First Captain Sigismund. The force he named the Retribution Fleet was made up of 30,000 Imperial Fists, in some 300 companies and more than 500 warships, a third of the Legion, and it bore his anger to Isstvan.[1][2]

    The Warp storm that had grown around the Isstvan system wrecked the fleet as it came out. Geller fields failed and hulls were sheared apart. Two hundred warships were lost, and nearly 10,000 Imperial Fists. The master of the fleet, Yonnad, was killed. His chosen successor was Captain Alexis Polux of the 405th Company, who had been his pupil. Polux was found in the wreck of one of the broken ships. He took command of 363 warships, a fifth of the Legion's strength, in a part of space where the Warp storm had somehow subsided.[2]

    The defence in depth

    [edit]

    Polux made his base in the Phall system. Its two habitable worlds were agri-worlds of no importance, and the Warp was calm there. He knew that his astropaths were blind and could not reach Terra or the loyalists on the surface at Isstvan, and that an attack was likely. He took the fleet to Phall II and arranged it in a sphere, a cage woven from the paths of comets. Each captain led a battlegroup of smaller craft on a looped course. The fleet was kept at battle stations and he drilled his shield-bearing breacher squads relentlessly. Several senior captains thought he was too cautious. Most could not fault the plan.[2]

    The battle

    [edit]

    Perturabo's decision

    [edit]

    After the Drop Site Massacre, the Iron Warriors fleet waited in the Isstvan system for orders. Horus sent word that more than 300 Imperial Fists ships were hiding at Phall and gave their dispositions in detail. He promised that the Warp would be passable. Perturabo found the intelligence too neat, and he wondered how the Warmaster had acquired it. The thought was a cold one. Yet the Imperial Fists were the Iron Warriors' oldest rivals, and the chance to break Dorn's sons was one he could not refuse.[2]

    Forrix, one of the Legion's Triarchs, warned that an enemy who suspected an attack would be prepared. Berossus, the Warsmith of the 2nd Grand Company, said that if Sigismund commanded, he would not sit still in a cage, and offered to take his fleet. Perturabo refused. The Imperial Fists were not to be broken, but ground to nothing, and Dorn's favoured First Captain would bleed for him. All of the ships under Berossus would go, and the Lord of Iron would command in person from his flagship, the Iron Blood.[2]

    The ambush

    [edit]

    The Iron Warriors broke into the system in a body, a hundred ships at once, with more behind them. The leading ships fired together at the Imperial Fists vessel Hammer of Terra. It blew up, and twelve of its consorts followed it. The grand cruiser Sulla fired once before its shields and hull were stripped. The Crusader and the Legate took vortex warheads and were gone. In close formation about the capital ship Contrador twenty-four grand cruisers and battle-barges drove through the debris of their kills. The method was patient and merciless, like a siege-drill through rock. Smaller ships crippled the targets and larger ones killed them.[2]

    The Imperial Fists' formation broke. A lone battleship, the Oath of Stone, an old vessel of forgotten make that had served the Legion long, turned to face the enemy and fired point blank.

    The counterattack

    [edit]

    The months of drill told. The Imperial Fists concentrated their fire on the leading Iron Warriors ships and tore them apart. Slow battleships drew Iron Warriors out of formation, and fast strike groups looped round and through the edge of the enemy fleet, striking at stragglers. The Iron Warriors began to pull their own fleet apart as they tried to chase them. The defence snapped into place like clockwork, an unfixed fortress of moving strike groups and punishing counter-attacks. The Iron Warriors still had more ships, but the margin shrank.[2]

    Captain Amandus Tyr, who commanded the 14th Company, took the strike cruiser Halcyon and two escorts, Unity and Truth, straight through the Iron Warriors. They killed four ships for each of their own. The Halcyon tore open the battle cruiser Dominator from below, and the escorts finished it. Tyr then saw a ship fifteen kilometres long, cold iron and blackened adamantium. It was the Iron Blood, the oldest daughter of the ship forges of Olympia, and he knew that only one being had ever commanded her. He sent word to Polux that Perturabo had come.[2]

    The gambit

    [edit]

    Polux gave Tyr fifty ships and one order: kill the Lord of Iron. Tyr took 53 veterans in Terminator armour. They were to attack a primarch of another Legion in his own flagship, which was something no Imperial Fist had thought to do, and they did not hesitate.[2]

    As the fleet's fire shattered the leading ships, the Iron Warriors broke off to regroup. At that moment the fleet's astropaths reached Terra. They received a direct order from Dorn, with the highest priority, that the Retribution Fleet return at once to prepare the defence of the Palace. A passage in the Warp storm seemed to have opened. It would not be open long. The Imperial Fists could counterattack and perhaps wound the Iron Warriors beyond recovery, or obey.[1]

    They obeyed. Polux ordered the fleet to withdraw. The smaller ships went first, frigates, gunboats and strike cruisers, a hundred and thirty of them. Every Imperial Fist knew what it meant. The ships that could survive were leaving, and those that remained were condemned. The heavy ships fired rolling salvoes to cover the escape. The Lacedaemon, the vessel that had carried the first Imperial Fists out of the Sol system, was run down by twelve Iron Warriors ships and killed. Most of those who followed her died with her. The Iron Warriors fell on the retreat, and the battle became a slaughter.[2]

    The death of the Tribune

    [edit]

    Polux's command ship, the Tribune, met the Contrador broadside to broadside. The Contrador boarded her with assault pods. When Polux knew that the Tribune was lost he ordered the surviving crew out. A hundred assault craft left the hull like fireflies. He and thirty of his own strike force remained, and the enginseers waited for the order. The teleport chamber took them away, and five seconds later the enginseers overloaded the reactor. The Tribune held her shape a moment and burst. The blast stripped the shields from the Contrador, burned out her sensors and killed the boarders. Imperial Fists attack craft followed the blast into her.[2]

    The Iron Warriors, believing that they had killed the commander, took little notice of the Contrador lingering at the site. Polux and his strike force had fought their way to her bridge and taken her. He ordered her out. By the time the Iron Warriors understood, she was beyond their range, and her engines trailed a comet's tail as she made for the edge of the system. They pursued her to the Warp, and lost her in the storms.[2]

    Tyr's force reached the Iron Blood. It was killed to a man. The last dozen Terminators who had reached her bridge were killed by the Lord of Iron himself, and their bodies lay round his throne.

    Aftermath

    [edit]

    The stolen Contrador spent an unmeasurable time in the Warp. Her crew divided. Some still held to their Iron Warriors masters, and the Imperial Fists suppressed them. Some starved. Then her Navigator found the faint light of the Astronomican, and she came out of the storm into what seemed to the survivors to be the Sol system. Weapon platforms and void stations ringed the planets in heavy chains, and the greatest force that they had seen since the war began lay at anchor. It was not Sol. They had reached the Realm of Ultramar, where Guilliman was gathering what remained of the loyalist strength.[2]

    The rest of the Retribution Fleet was gone. Some ships escaped to the Warp, but most of the Retribution Fleet drifted in the void, shattered and cold. The Iron Warriors suffered heavily and had lost ships and Legionaries to a rival they despised.[1]

    Legacy

    [edit]

    The Imperial Fists remember Phall as a battle in which discipline saved part of a fleet from destruction, and Polux's defence in depth, with fast strike groups and constant drill, is held up as a model. The Iron Warriors regard it as a stolen victory. Their rivalry with the Imperial Fists, already old, was made bitterer by it.[3]
