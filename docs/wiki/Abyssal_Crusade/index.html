    The Abyssal Crusade was a penitent crusade into the Eye of Terror by thirty Chapters of the Adeptus Astartes, who entered the Eye at the end of 321.M37 and whose survivors returned in 112.M38. The Chapters, known as the Judged, had been found wanting by the Ecclesiarchal saint Basillius, who had tested every Chapter whose homeworld had been touched by a great warp storm. Rather than live under suspicion they chose to seek redemption by carrying war into the realm of the Traitor Legions.[1][2]

    The crusade purged more than four hundred worlds within the Eye. Its cost was the loss of most of the thirty Chapters, many of which were destroyed or fell to Chaos. When the survivors returned, nearly eight centuries later, they denounced Basillius as a false saint. The Inquisition confirmed that he was a servant of the Ruinous Powers who had sent loyal warriors into damnation, and every trace of his cult was destroyed. The Abyssal Crusade is counted among the gravest episodes of the Age of Redemption, and the rise in Traitor Space Marine raids that followed it is attributed by many scholars to the warriors it lost.[1][3]

    Background

    [edit]

    Saint Basillius

    [edit]

    In the Age of Redemption that followed the Age of Apostasy, the Imperial Cult grew in zeal and its great preachers wielded great power. After the Ecclesiarchal Purges of 321.M37, the most powerful of them was Basillius, called the Elder. His Puritas Divisions, which included several Chapters of Space Marines, executed any mutant or cultist they found without regard to age or rank. For once the Ecclesiarchy, the Inquisition and the Adeptus Astartes worked together across the Segmentum Solar, and every citizen was required to inform on any neighbour suspected of the taint of Chaos. Countless accusations were false and the few liberties of the citizenry were further reduced, but insurrection against the Emperor fell almost to nothing.[1]

    The Dionys storm

    [edit]

    The peace was short. The warp storm called Dionys engulfed more than a dozen star systems, and its effects were felt along the spiral arms of the galaxy. Reports of mutation and cult activity quadrupled. Several Chapters whose homeworlds lay in the storm's path found that hidden flaws in their gene-seed were suddenly expressed in their new recruits, in a wave of disturbing physical and mental changes.[1]

    Basillius demanded that every Chapter whose homeworld had been touched by the storm be submitted to his judgement. His influence with the High Lords of Terra was such that within a year this was done. After a long series of tests and auguries, hundreds of Chapters were declared untouched. Thirty were found wanting.[1]

    The Council of Dismay

    [edit]

    The condemned Chapters met in an emergency gathering of their representatives, later called the Council of Dismay. The most militant demanded the right to purify themselves in battle. They offered to undertake a penitent crusade, and to the surprise of his advisers Basillius agreed, naming the destination himself: the Eye of Terror, and the daemon worlds of the Traitor Legions, which had once been human worlds before the birth of Slaanesh swallowed them. After a few hours of debate the Council accepted. Its members are recorded as judging martyrdom preferable to a life of suspicion.[1]

    The crusade

    [edit]

    Into the Eye

    [edit]

    In the last days of 321.M37 the battle barges and strike cruisers of the Judged passed through the Cadian Gate, their heraldry hidden under black plates and their signal relays silent. Within a single night they passed out of the range of Cadia's astropaths.[1]

    Less than an hour after the fleet entered the Eye, a Chaos war fleet emerged from the dust clouds to meet it. The battle was so fierce that the fleet lost all cohesion, and the violence tore at the fabric of space. When the enemy withdrew, the ships of the Judged were scattered across the Eye.[1]

    The lost years

    [edit]

    What became of the Chapters in the Eye is known only in fragments. Imperial chroniclers say that their stories would fill the Great Elucidan Library twice over, and that each ends in sorrow. Some Chapters fought on for centuries and purged world after world. Others were destroyed, and others again were corrupted by the powers they had come to fight and joined the warbands of the Eye. The Administratum denies any link between the loss of the Judged and the increase in raids by Traitor Space Marines from the Eye that followed, but the increase is recorded.[1][3]

    Return and judgement (112.M38)

    [edit]

    In 112.M38 the survivors of some of the Judged Chapters emerged from the Warp, having purged more than four hundred worlds of the Eye. They did not stop to claim the honours due to them. They were detained by the Inquisition and tested for the taint of Chaos, and passed every test.[1]

    Basillius, impossibly, still lived. Konvak Lann, Chapter Master of the Vorpal Swords, testified that the saint was a false idol, and that his judgement had sent loyal Chapters into the jaws of damnation in the service of the Dark Gods. Led by the Vorpal Swords, the returned Chapters broke through the defences of Basillius' palace and seized him, and his true nature as an apostle of Chaos was revealed. The Inquisition executed him and declared an Edict of Obliteration. Within a year every known shrine and sepulchre of the false saint had been destroyed. His bones, his relics, his books of doctrine and thousands of his living worshippers were loaded onto a derelict bulk freighter and fired into a star.[1][2]

    Aftermath

    [edit]

    An appendix to the Grimoire Hereticus lists the Chapters known to have been corrupted, in whole or in part, during the crusade, together with the names under which they were later declared Excommunicate Traitoris. Among them were the Altar Brethren, recorded afterwards as the Iconoclasts; the Bronze Gorgons, as the Crystal Wyverns; the Doom Legion, as the Vectors of Pox; the Lionguard, as the Death Shadows; the Sanctors of Terra, as the Blighted Claw; the Star Gryphons, as the Sons of Midnight; and the Viridian Consuls, as the Broken. The designation of the Blades Eternal remains pending.[4]

    The Ecclesiarchy has never fully established the extent of Basillius' influence. Imperial historians of the Ministorum treat the episode as a warning against the unchecked power of individual saints, and it is one of several cases, with the apostasy of Goge Vandire before it and of Cardinal Xaphan at Vraks after it, in which the Imperial Cult was turned against the Imperium from within.[2]

    Legacy

    [edit]

    For the Adeptus Astartes the Abyssal Crusade is a cautionary tale. The Judged had been loyal when they were condemned, and they were lost because they accepted judgement from an authority outside their Chapters. The Vorpal Swords are honoured for having returned and spoken the truth. For the Imperium at large, the loss of thirty Chapters at the height of the Age of Redemption contributed to the exhaustion of its armies that brought that age to an end and opened the Waning.[3]
