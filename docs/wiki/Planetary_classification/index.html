    Planetary classification, also called world classification or Imperial planet classification, is the system by which the Administratum of the Adeptus Terra sorts the worlds of the Imperium of Mankind into types according to their population, economy, environment and purpose. Each registered world is given a class, such as hive world, agri-world or forge world, and a tithe grade that sets how much it must pay towards the Imperium in goods, soldiers and psykers. Together the two entries decide what the Imperium expects of a world, how closely it is governed and how much aid it may hope for in time of war.[1][2]

    The system grew out of the census of the Great Crusade in the 30th and early 31st millennia, when the expeditionary fleets had to record what each newly compliant world could offer. It was codified by the Administratum after the Horus Heresy and the reforms of Roboute Guilliman, and has changed little in ten thousand years. In the Era Indomitus the opening of the Great Rift in 999.M41 has thrown the registry into disorder: thousands of worlds in the Imperium Nihilus have not reported since, and their classes are carried in the rolls only as "unconfirmed".[3][2]

    Classification is administrative rather than scientific. A world may hold more than one class, for example a Death world that is also a Feral world, or a Space Marine Chapter homeworld that is also a Fortress world. Classes are revised by Administratum order when a world's condition changes, as when a hive world is depopulated, a civilised world falls to Chaos and becomes a Daemon world, or a world is destroyed by Exterminatus and is reclassified as a Dead world.[1]

    History

    [edit]

    Origins in the Great Crusade

    [edit]

    The Imperium inherited no common registry from the Dark Age of Technology. The worlds of humanity had been scattered and isolated in the Age of Strife, and when the Emperor of Mankind began the Great Crusade in c. 798.M30, each expeditionary fleet assessed the worlds it brought to compliance by its own methods. Imperial commanders, the Imperial Army and the Mechanicum of Mars each kept separate rolls. Remembrancer accounts describe expeditionary fleets carrying Administratum adepts whose task was to count populations, survey resources and set the first tithes of newly compliant worlds.[4][5]

    By the end of the Crusade, in the decades before 005.M31, the Council of Terra had adopted a standard set of world types for the purposes of tithe and garrison, and the Mechanicum had agreed under the Treaty of Olympus that its own worlds would be recorded separately as forge worlds.[1][6]

    The Codex reforms

    [edit]
    See also: Codex Astartes

    After the Horus Heresy (005–014.M31) and the Great Scouring, the Administratum rebuilt its registry. The reforms that accompanied the Codex Astartes divided power across the Imperium so that no single governor could again raise a rebellion of the size of the Heresy. Planetary governors lost command of the fleets, and world classes were tied to fixed military duties: fortress worlds to garrison, hive worlds to the raising of regiments for the newly separated Astra Militarum, and agri-worlds to the feeding of both. The tithe grades took their present Low Gothic names in this period.[7][2]

    Later revisions

    [edit]

    The registry has been revised many times. During the Age of Apostasy in the early 36th millennium, the Adeptus Ministorum obtained the formal recognition of Cardinal worlds ruled directly by the Ecclesiarchy. In M38 and M39 the growth of the Departmento Munitorum led to finer sub-classes for worlds that supplied particular kinds of regiment. In the late 41st millennium the Administratum added classes for the worlds lost to Tyranid hive fleets and for Tomb worlds identified after the awakening of the Necrons, c. 700–999.M41.[1][8]

    Since 999.M41 the rolls of the Imperium Sanctus have been reconciled with the records of the Indomitus Crusade. Each Crusade fleet carries adepts who confirm or revise the class of every world it recovers. In the Imperium Nihilus, the Regent at Baal has authorised local reclassification, a power once reserved to Terra.[3]

    Method

    [edit]

    Classification is carried out by the Administratum's sector and sub-sector offices, which report to the segmentum headquarters and through them to Terra. The assessors consider the size and health of the population, the world's climate and resources, the state of its technology, its strategic position and its loyalty. The result is entered in the registry together with the world's tithe grade and the identity of its planetary governor.[2]

    Records of a class may be decades or centuries out of date. Astropathic messages are slow and unreliable, and the Administratum audits a world only when its tithe fails or when it is visited by Imperial forces. Many worlds have been found to bear a class that no longer describes them. Some agri-worlds, for example, are recorded as fertile long after their soils have failed, and a few worlds recorded as loyal had in fact been lost to Chaos for generations.[2][1]

    Worlds of the Adeptus Mechanicus are classified by Mars, not by the Administratum. Under the Treaty of Olympus they are recorded as forge worlds and given a separate production grade, from I-Maximus for the greatest forges to IV-Tertius for the least. Space Marine homeworlds are recorded by class but are exempt from the tithe.[6][9]

    Tithe grades

    [edit]
    Main article: Imperial tithe

    The Imperial tithe, also called Terra's Due, is the levy each world owes the Imperium. It may be paid in food, ore, manufactured goods, soldiers for the Astra Militarum, ratings for the Imperial Navy, or psykers for the Black Ships of the Adeptus Astra Telepathica. Its size is set by the world's tithe grade. In addition to the grade, each world able to do so must give a tenth of its armed forces to the Astra Militarum at each founding.[1][10]

    There are seventeen grades in three bands, Exactis, Decuma and Solutio, each divided into degrees, together with a final grade for worlds that pay no tithe:[1]

    Administratum tithe grades, from heaviest to lightest

    Failure to pay the tithe is punished severely. The governor of a world in arrears is usually removed or executed, and the Administratum may call on the Adeptus Arbites or the Astra Militarum to enforce payment. In times of crisis the Departmento Munitorum may demand foundings far above a world's grade, and in the wars of the Era Indomitus whole populations of some hive worlds have been raised as regiments.[10][3]

    Classification and government

    [edit]

    A world's class shapes the way it is governed. Most worlds are ruled by a planetary governor, also called an Imperial Commander, who has wide freedom provided the tithe is met and the world remains loyal. Some classes are governed differently. Forge worlds are ruled by the Adeptus Mechanicus, cardinal worlds by the Ecclesiarchy, fortress worlds usually by a military commander such as the Lord Castellan of Cadia, and many agri-worlds directly by the Administratum. Chapter homeworlds are governed by their Chapter or by a ruler it appoints.[1][2]

    Classification also governs the response to war. Hive and forge worlds are judged too valuable to lose, and the Imperium will send whole armies to hold them, as it did at Armageddon in 941.M41 and 998.M41. Lesser worlds may be abandoned. A world judged beyond saving may be destroyed by Exterminatus.[11]

    Disputes and errors

    [edit]

    The registry has never been complete. Imperial scholars estimate that a large share of entries were out of date even before 999.M41, and the Administratum itself acknowledges cases in which worlds were tithed for centuries after their populations had died, or in which whole systems were missing from the rolls. The so-called lost worlds, recorded as Imperial but not visited for millennia, are a recurring concern of the Adeptus Terra. Disputes between the Administratum and other Adepta over the class of a world are also common: the Ecclesiarchy may seek to have a world declared a shrine world to secure its offerings, while the Departmento Munitorum may resist any change that would lower a world's regimental quota.[2][8]
