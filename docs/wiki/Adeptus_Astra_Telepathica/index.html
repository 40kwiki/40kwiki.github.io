    The Adeptus Astra Telepathica is the division of the Adeptus Terra that finds, tests and trains the psykers of the Imperium of Mankind. Its most important charges are the astropaths, telepaths who can send messages across interstellar distances, on whom almost all communication between the worlds of the Imperium depends. It also trains the sanctioned psykers who serve the Astra Militarum, the Inquisition and the Adeptus Astronomica, and it gives up to the Golden Throne those whom it cannot train.[1][2]

    The Astra Telepathica was created by the Emperor of Mankind in the final years of the Unification Wars, before the Great Crusade, because the Emperor foresaw that an interstellar empire would need a means of speaking across the void. It is divided into two great departments: the League of Black Ships, whose Black Ships gather psykers from every world, and the Scholastica Psykana, which receives them on Terra. Both answer to the Master of the Adeptus Astra Telepathica.[2]

    The opening of the Great Rift at the end of the 41st millennium was a disaster for the Telepathica. Countless astropaths died or went mad in the psychic storm, and the worlds of the Imperium Nihilus lost contact with Terra. The Telepathica has since struggled to restore the Imperium's communications while its supply of psykers is consumed faster than ever by the failing Throne.[3]

    History

    [edit]

    Founding

    [edit]

    Although he was himself the greatest of psykers, the Emperor distrusted psychic power in others. The Age of Strife had been a time of witch-kings and the possessed, and in the Unification Wars he had destroyed many of them. He nonetheless understood that the Imperium he meant to build would need psykers, for travel through the Warp and above all for communication. Starships could take months to cross between neighbouring systems; a telepathic message could cross the galaxy in hours.[2]

    He therefore created a corps of telepaths, sanctioned by himself, whose minds were strong enough to resist the dangers of the Warp. At the same time he ordered the building of the Astronomican and gave its service to a separate body, the Adeptus Astronomica. The first astro-telepaths were bound directly to the Emperor's own mind in a ritual that strengthened them against possession, and the bond has been the basis of their training ever since.[2][4]

    The City of Sight

    [edit]

    During the Great Crusade the Astra Telepathica was based in the City of Sight, a district of the Imperial Palace in the mountains of the Himalazia. It was a gloomy place, built long before the Palace by an ancient sect of psychic architects, and its streets were walked only by the hooded adepts of the order. At its heart stood the Whispering Tower, where thousands of astropaths, seated in rising tiers, joined their minds in a gestalt that could reach the farthest expeditions of the crusade. Every message sent or received was recorded in its archives.[4]

    After the Council of Nikaea in 001.M31, the astropaths of the Whispering Tower were placed under the watch of the Black Sentinels, a force of guards and executioners charged with killing any astropath who showed signs of corruption.[4]

    The Horus Heresy

    [edit]

    The Telepathica was among the first casualties of the Horus Heresy. When Magnus the Red sent his sorcerous warning to Terra in 005.M31, it broke through the wards of the Palace, and many of the astropaths of the City of Sight were killed or driven mad. For the rest of the war the surviving choirs worked beyond their capacity, carrying the orders of the loyal Imperium to its scattered armies through a Warp churning with storms. The Telepathica's work during the Siege of Terra is recorded in fragments, and the City of Sight suffered heavily in the fighting within the Palace.[4]

    Later history

    [edit]

    In the Imperium that followed, the Telepathica became a permanent and powerful institution. Its Master often held a seat among the High Lords of Terra, and its sanction was required for any psyker to serve the Imperium. In the Age of Apostasy of the 36th millennium, the Master Phaedrus resisted the rule of the tyrant Goge Vandire and used his powers to stay one step ahead of him. Vandire had him captured by an assassin of the Culexus Temple, whose null power was used to sever Phaedrus's connection with the Warp, and kept him alive and powerless so that he could not be replaced. The Telepathica was then under Vandire's control until the end of the Reign of Blood.[5][2]

    Era Indomitus

    [edit]

    The opening of the Great Rift killed or drove mad countless astropaths across the Imperium in a single moment. Those in the Imperium Nihilus who survived found that their messages could no longer reach Terra, and the whole Imperium was plunged into the Noctis Aeterna, the long night in which the Astronomican could not be seen. In the Imperium Sanctus communication became slow and uncertain. The Lord Commander, Roboute Guilliman, made the restoration of astropathic contact one of the priorities of the Indomitus Crusade, and the Black Ships now follow the crusade fleets to collect new psykers from the worlds they reconquer. The demand for astropaths, and the hunger of the dimming Golden Throne, have never been greater.[3]

    Organisation

    [edit]

    League of Black Ships

    [edit]

    The League of Black Ships gathers the psykers of the Imperium. Its fleet visits every Imperial world about once a century to collect the tithe of psykers that each governor must provide, and carries them to Terra. Its operation is described in the article Black Ships.

    Scholastica Psykana

    [edit]

    The Scholastica Psykana receives the psykers brought by the Black Ships and others surrendered by the Inquisition, the Adeptus Arbites and the Schola Progenium. They are tested and sorted. The young are preferred, because they are the easiest to train.[2]

    • Primary psykers, those strong enough in power and character to resist possession, are given five years of basic training and then passed to other institutions. The youngest may become Librarians of the Adeptus Astartes; others are trained as Primaris Psykers of the Astra Militarum; the most gifted may enter the Inquisition or the Grey Knights. They are rarely soul-bound.
    • Secondary psykers, the majority, are trained as astropaths.
    • Some of both ranks are given to the Adeptus Astronomica to serve the Astronomican.
    • Those judged too weak, too old or too dangerous to train are sacrificed to the Emperor. Their lives sustain him on the Golden Throne and fuel the beacon of the Astronomican.

    Astropaths

    [edit]

    Soul-binding

    [edit]

    Every astropath undergoes the ritual of soul-binding, in which the psyker's mind is joined to that of the Emperor. The ritual hardens the psyker's soul against possession and gives him the strength to send and receive messages across the Warp. It is dangerous, and some psykers die in it. It also costs the Emperor a part of his strength each time it is performed. The survivors are usually left blind, their eyes burned out by the Emperor's presence, though they gain a kind of psychic sight in return. Soul-binding also shortens the astropath's life, and most die within a few decades of service.[2]

    Communication

    [edit]

    Astropaths do not send words. Their messages take the form of images, symbols and emotions, shaped by the astropath's own mind and interpreted by the receiver according to a vast and personal lexicon of meaning. A message from one astropath may therefore be misunderstood by another, and important messages are sent many times, by many astropaths, to be sure of their meaning. Messages travel through the Warp and are subject to its tides: they may arrive late, before they were sent, or not at all. Long-distance messages are relayed by astropathic choirs, groups of astropaths whose combined power can reach further than any one alone.[2][1]

    Service

    [edit]

    Astropaths serve on every Imperial world of importance, on every warship of the Imperial Navy, in the fortress-monasteries of the Adeptus Astartes, with the Inquisition and with the great merchant houses of the Rogue Traders. Each world's astropathic choir is housed in a fortified tower, and its astropaths are watched for signs of corruption. A world that loses its astropaths is cut off from the Imperium until replacements arrive, which may take decades.[1]

    Relations with other institutions

    [edit]

    The Telepathica works closely with the Inquisition, which has the right to inspect its psykers and frequently draws its own agents from them, and with the Adeptus Astronomica, its sister institution. It has been rivalled by the Navis Nobilite, whose Navigator Houses depend on the Astronomican. The Adeptus Mechanicus has long sought, without success, a technological means of communication that would free the Imperium from its dependence on psykers.[6][1]
